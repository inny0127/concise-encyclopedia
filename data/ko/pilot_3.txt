=== KEY: Tower_8f8b35
LEVEL: 4  PERSON: no
HEADWORD: Tower
ENTRY:
Tall structure, taller than it is wide, built to serve purposes other than habitation. Towers are distinguished from masts by their lack of guy-wires and, like tall buildings, are self-supporting. Height serves functions such as clock visibility, surveillance from a [[Castle|castle]], observation, leisure and telecommunications. The oldest known may be a circular stone tower in the walls of Neolithic Jericho (8000 BC). Ziggurats, such as the Sumerian [[Ziggurat of Ur]] built in the 3rd millennium BC, were among the earliest towers. The Chinese used towers as part of the [[Great Wall of China]] in 210 BC during the [[Qin dynasty]], and medieval fortified buildings made wide use of them. Modern examples include the [[Eiffel Tower]] in Paris, completed in 1889 as a temporary entrance arch for the World's Fair, and [[Cooling tower|cooling towers]] at industrial plants.

=== KEY: Umberto_Eco_905146
LEVEL: 4  PERSON: yes
HEADWORD: Eco, Umberto
ENTRY:
Italian medievalist, philosopher, [[Semiotics|semiotician]] and novelist, best known for *The Name of the Rose* (1980). Born in Alessandria, Piedmont, he wrote his thesis on the aesthetics of [[Thomas Aquinas]] at the University of Turin and later taught for much of his life at the [[University of Bologna]]. His 1980 historical mystery combined semiotics, biblical analysis and medieval studies, and *Foucault's Pendulum* (1988) explored the history of occultism. From 1985 he wrote a twice-monthly column, *La Bustina di Minerva*, for the magazine *L'Espresso*. His 1995 essay "Ur-Fascism" lists fourteen properties of [[Fascism|fascist]] ideologies. His *A Theory of Semiotics* (1975) became a foundational text in the field.

=== KEY: Vapor_8f5c72
LEVEL: 4  PERSON: no
HEADWORD: Vapor
ENTRY:
Substance in the [[Gas|gas]] phase at a [[Temperature|temperature]] below its critical temperature, so that it can be [[Condensation|condensed]] to a [[Liquid|liquid]] by increasing pressure alone. A vapor may coexist with its liquid or solid phase in equilibrium, and the vapor pressure of a liquid or solid is its equilibrium pressure at a specific temperature. Water vapor condenses into [[Fog|fog]] and clouds when its partial pressure is raised enough. Vapor is central to evaporation and [[Distillation|distillation]]. A vapor differs from an [[Aerosol|aerosol]], which is a suspension of tiny particles of liquid or solid in a gas. Mercury-vapor and sodium-vapor lamps produce light from atoms in excited states.

=== KEY: Wang_Wei_a27280
LEVEL: 4  PERSON: yes
HEADWORD: Wang Wei
ENTRY:
Chinese poet, painter, musician and statesman of the middle [[Tang dynasty]]. He is regarded as one of the most distinguished figures in Chinese arts and letters, renowned for his dual talents as a nature poet and landscape painter. About 400 of his poems survived, and 29 are included in the 18th-century anthology *Three Hundred Tang Poems*. No authenticated paintings survive, and none of his music is preserved. Born into an aristocratic family, he passed the palace examination in 721 with top place and first served as a court musician. He was demoted to overseeing a granary in the former province of Jizhou, now in [[Hebei]]. Later he was posted to the frontier at Liangzhou. Late in life he became a devout Chan Buddhist and a vegetarian. His poems were compiled at imperial command by his brother Wang Jin.

=== KEY: Watermelon_9aa80c
LEVEL: 4  PERSON: no
HEADWORD: Watermelon
ENTRY:
Flowering plant of the [[Cucurbitaceae]] family, grown worldwide for its large fruit. The watermelon (*Citrullus lanatus*) is a scrambling, vine-like plant whose fruit is a large [[Berry (botany)|berry]] with a firm rind and no internal divisions. Its sweet, juicy flesh is usually deep red to pink, with many seeds that are black when mature. The modern watermelon originated in [[Sudan]], where it was first domesticated, and it was cultivated in Egypt by 2000 BC. Dessert watermelons spread around the Mediterranean during Roman antiquity. Breeding has produced disease-resistant and seedless [[Cultivar|cultivars]] that bear mature fruit within 100 days of planting. In 2024, China produced 60% of the world's watermelons. The fruit is eaten raw or made into juice or wine, and the rind is edible when pickled or cooked. The sweet watermelon was first described by [[Carl Linnaeus]] in 1753.

=== KEY: Wedge_0e9bb3
LEVEL: 4  PERSON: no
HEADWORD: Wedge
ENTRY:
Triangular [[Tool|tool]], a portable [[Inclined plane|inclined plane]] and one of the six [[Simple machine|simple machines]]. It separates objects, lifts them or holds them in place by converting force applied to its blunt end into forces perpendicular to its inclined surfaces. Its mechanical advantage is the ratio of its slope length to its width, equal to 1/tan α for tip angle α, so a smaller angle gives a greater lifting force. Wedges have existed for thousands of years; the hand axe, made by chipping flint, may be the first example. [[Ancient Egypt|Ancient Egyptian]] quarries used [[Bronze|bronze]] wedges to split blocks of stone, as well as wooden wedges that swelled when soaked with water. Wedges split wood along the grain, and a narrow gib finely adjusts distances in machine tools. The tips of forks and [[Nail (fastener)|nails]] also act as wedges, and a blade is a compound inclined plane.

=== KEY: Whisky_498511
LEVEL: 4  PERSON: no
HEADWORD: Whisky
ENTRY:
Distilled [[Liquor|liquor]] made from fermented grain mash, usually [[Barley]], corn, [[Rye]] or [[Wheat]]. It is typically aged in charred white [[Oak|oak]] casks, and all classes share grain fermentation, distillation and aging in wooden casks. The name is an anglicization of the Gaelic word *uisce*, meaning "water"; the spelling whiskey is common in [[Ireland]] and the [[United States]]. Scotch whisky must be distilled and matured in oak casks in [[Scotland]] for at least three years. In the United States, bourbon must contain at least 51% corn in its mash. It is one of the world's most popular spirits, and Scotch exports were valued at £5.4 billion as of 2024.

HEADWORD: Wikipedia

Free online encyclopedia written and maintained by volunteer editors. It was founded by Jimmy Wales and Larry Sanger in 2001 as a [[Wiki|wiki]] complement to Nupedia, an expert-written encyclopedia project. It has been hosted since 2003 by the Wikimedia Foundation, an American nonprofit funded mainly by reader donations. It is the largest and most read reference work in history. As of November 2026 the English edition has more than seven million articles, and all editions together exceed 68 million. Its reliability was often criticized in the 2000s but has earned more praise since the late 2010s. Critics point to systemic [[Bias|bias]], including gender bias against women and geographic bias against the Global South. Some national governments have censored it, from specific pages to the entire site.

=== KEY: Zaha_Hadid_355479
LEVEL: 4  PERSON: yes
HEADWORD: Hadid, Zaha
ENTRY:
Iraqi-born British architect and designer. Hadid was born in [[Baghdad]] and studied mathematics at the American University of Beirut before enrolling in 1972 at the Architectural Association School of Architecture in [[London]]. She adopted painting as a design tool and abstraction as a method, drawing on Suprematism and the Russian avant-garde. Her major works include the London Aquatics Centre for the 2012 Olympics, the Broad Art Museum, Rome's MAXXI Museum and the Guangzhou Opera House. She was the first woman to receive the Pritzker Architecture Prize, in 2004, and won the Stirling Prize in 2010 and 2011. In 2012 [[Elizabeth II]] made her a Dame for services to architecture. [[The Guardian|The Guardian]] called her the "Queen of Curves". Several buildings, including the Daxing International Airport in [[Beijing]], were still under construction at the time of her death.

=== KEY: d_33c89d
LEVEL: 4  PERSON: no
HEADWORD: Łódź
ENTRY:
City in central [[Poland]] and capital of Łódź Voivodeship, a former industrial center (pop., 2025: 639,890). Granted town rights in 1423 by King [[Władysław II Jagiełło]], it was annexed by Prussia in the Second Partition of 1793 and joined Congress Poland, a Russian client state, after the [[Congress of Vienna]] in 1815. Rapid growth in [[Textile|textile]] manufacturing from 1850 drew migrants, including many Jews and Germans, which made the city multinational. The Muzeum Sztuki, founded in 1931, was the first museum in Europe dedicated to collecting and showcasing modern art. During [[World War II]] the German occupiers forced the city's large Jewish minority into a walled ghetto before sending its members to German concentration and extermination camps. After a sharp decline following 1989, the city began to revitalize its downtown in the 2010s. Its National Film School is a cradle for many of Poland's most renowned actors and directors, and in 2017 the city was named a [[UNESCO]] City of Film.
