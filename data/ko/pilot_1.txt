HEADWORD: Estrogen
ENTRY:
Category of steroid sex hormones that regulate the female reproductive system and secondary sex characteristics. The three major endogenous estrogens are estrone (E1), estradiol (E2) and estriol (E3); estradiol is the most potent and prevalent. Estetrol (E4) is produced only during pregnancy. === KEY: Euclidean_algorithm_bea54b
LEVEL: 4  PERSON: no
HEADWORD: Euclidean algorithm
ENTRY:
Efficient [[Algorithm|algorithm]] for computing the greatest common divisor of two [[Integer|integers]]. Named after the ancient Greek mathematician [[Euclid]], who described it in his *Elements* (c. 300 BC), it is one of the oldest algorithms in common use. It rests on the principle that the greatest common divisor does not change if the larger number is replaced by its remainder when divided by the smaller. Repeating this produces smaller pairs until the remainder is zero. The number of steps never exceeds five times the number of base-10 digits of the smaller integer, a result proven by Gabriel Lamé in 1844 that marks the beginning of [[Computational complexity theory|computational complexity theory]]. The algorithm reduces fractions, performs division in [[Modular arithmetic|modular arithmetic]], helps solve [[Diophantine equation|Diophantine equations]] and forms part of the cryptographic protocols that secure the [[Internet]].

ENTRY:
Mathematical formula in [[Complex analysis|complex analysis]] linking the trigonometric functions with the complex exponential function. For any real number x, eⁱˣ = cos x + i sin x, where e is the base of the natural logarithm and i is the [[Imaginary unit|imaginary unit]]. === KEY: Ground_tissue_432dbb
LEVEL: 4  PERSON: no
HEADWORD: Ground tissue
ENTRY:
Category of plant tissues that are neither dermal nor [[Vascular tissue|vascular]], forming the bulk of the plant body. Ground tissue is one of three main tissue systems, alongside the protective and vascular systems, and it provides structural support, storage, metabolic activity and [[Photosynthesis|photosynthesis]]. Its cells are classified by their [[Cell wall|cell walls]]. Parenchyma cells have thin primary walls, usually remain alive at maturity and form the filler tissue of soft parts; they store starch in roots, [[Potato|tubers]] and [[Cereal|cereal]] seed endosperm, and their large central [[Vacuole|vacuoles]] store water and ions. Collenchyma cells provide extra support in regions of new growth. Sclerenchyma cells have thick lignified walls, often die at maturity and provide the main structural support. Aerenchyma, with large air cavities, helps aquatic plants float.

=== KEY: Hilbert_space_e3e511
LEVEL: 4  PERSON: no
HEADWORD: Hilbert space
ENTRY:
Type of vector space that generalizes Euclidean space to any finite or infinite dimension. A Hilbert space has an inner product that allows length and angle to be measured, and it is complete, so that enough limits exist for the techniques of calculus. It was studied from the first decade of the 20th century by [[David Hilbert]], Erhard Schmidt and Frigyes Riesz, and it is indispensable in [[Partial differential equation|partial differential equations]], [[Quantum mechanics|quantum mechanics]], Fourier analysis and ergodic theory. [[John von Neumann]] coined the term for the abstract concept. Examples include spaces of square-integrable functions and of sequences. Exact analogs of the [[Pythagorean theorem]] hold, and perpendicular projection onto a subspace is central to optimization. These methods helped establish [[Functional analysis|functional analysis]] as a fruitful field.

ENTRY:
Development of organized physical competition from prehistoric activity to modern professional sport. Sport had early links with warfare and entertainment, and competition was used to determine whether individuals were fit for military service. Most of a lamprey's life is spent as a juvenile ammocoete, burrowing into sediment and filter-feeding. Only 18 species are carnivorous, boring into other fish to feed on flesh and blood. The sea lamprey, an introduced pest in the [[Great Lakes]], has harmed native fish stocks, and control programs use lampricides and barriers.

=== KEY: Late_Bronze_Age_collapse_884db6
LEVEL: 4  PERSON: no
HEADWORD: Late Bronze Age collapse
ENTRY:
Societal collapse in the eastern Mediterranean and Near East, from the late 13th to early 12th century BC. It affected [[Anatolia]], Egypt, the Aegean, eastern [[Libya]] and the [[Balkans]], and it sharply reduced the material wealth of the region's powers. The palace economy of [[Mycenaean Greece]] broke apart into small, isolated village cultures, which were followed by the Greek Dark Ages (c. 1200 to c. 800 BC). The Hittite Empire collapsed, while the [[New Kingdom of Egypt]] survived in weakened form and [[Phoenicia|Phoenicians]] enjoyed increased autonomy. Causes proposed since the 19th century include climate change, droughts, disease, invasions by the [[Sea Peoples]], and changes in military technology that ended chariot warfare. Later research found that many destructions once attributed to the period were misdated or never occurred, so the collapse was more limited than once thought.

=== KEY: Liberty_84e1da
LEVEL: 3  PERSON: no
HEADWORD: Liberty
ENTRY:
State of being free within society from restrictions imposed by authority on one's way of life, behavior, or political views. In U.S. constitutional law, ordered liberty means a balanced society in which individuals can act without unnecessary interference (negative liberty) and have access to opportunities and resources to pursue their goals (positive liberty). Some writers distinguish liberty from freedom, using "freedom" for the ability to do as one wills and "liberty" for the absence of arbitrary restraints, subject to the rights of others. The word comes from [[Latin]] *liber*.

The concept has long occupied philosophers. [[Thomas Hobbes]] held that a free man is one not hindered from doing what he has the will to do. [[John Locke]] rejected that definition: in political society, liberty consists of being under no lawmaking power except that established by consent, and freedom is constrained by laws in both the state of nature and political society. [[John Stuart Mill]]'s *On Liberty* (1859) was the first to recognize the difference between liberty as freedom to act and liberty as absence of coercion. [[Isaiah Berlin]] framed this as the distinction between negative liberty, protection from tyranny and arbitrary authority, and positive liberty, self-mastery over inner compulsions such as weakness and fear. Critics argue that positive liberty can justify coercion in the name of collective goals.

The political concept originates in Greek ideas of freedom and slavery: to be free was not to have a master. [[Aristotle]] linked this to democracy, though it applied only to free men. In the [[Maurya Empire]], citizens of all religions and ethnic groups had some rights to freedom and tolerance. The phrase "Life, Liberty and the pursuit of Happiness" appears in the [[United States Declaration of Independence]], and France's motto is "Liberté, égalité, fraternité."

Iron Age kingdom in western [[Anatolia|Anatolia]], with Sardis as its capital and only city, prominent in the 8th to 6th centuries BC. 16th-century Spanish maritime expedition that was the first to circumnavigate the Earth. === KEY: Map_projection_c9fa64
LEVEL: 4  PERSON: no
HEADWORD: Map projection
ENTRY:
Systematic method of representing the curved surface of a [[Globe|globe]] on a flat plane. Every projection of a sphere onto a plane distorts the surface in some way, so each one preserves some properties, such as area, shape, direction or distance, at the expense of others. The purpose of the map determines which projection suits it, and the study of projections centers on characterizing their distortions. The best-known is the [[Mercator projection]], which preserves angles but enlarges regions far from the equator; it was criticized through the 20th century and revived in the 21st for online mapping. Equal-area projections, such as the Gall–Peters projection, show the correct sizes of countries relative to one another but distort angles. In September 2026 the [[United Nations]] resolved to promote equal-area projections, notably the Equal Earth projection; 164 countries voted in favor, the [[United States]] voted against, and six abstained.

=== KEY: Melanin_448e72
LEVEL: 4  PERSON: no
HEADWORD: Melanin
ENTRY:
Family of biomolecules organized as oligomers or polymers, forming the pigments of many organisms. Melanin is produced by melanocytes in a process called melanogenesis, in which the [[Amino acid]] tyrosine is oxidized and then polymerized. There are five basic types: eumelanin, pheomelanin, neuromelanin, allomelanin and pyomelanin. Eumelanin is more abundant in tissues requiring photoprotection, such as the epidermis, and can dissipate over 99.9% of absorbed [[Ultraviolet|UV]] radiation. In human skin, melanogenesis is initiated by UV exposure, which is associated with increased risk of [[Melanoma]]. Pheomelanin is largely responsible for the red or yellow tint of some skin and hair. Neuromelanin, found in the brain, has been studied for possible treatment of neurodegenerative disorders such as Parkinson's.

=== KEY: Michelson_Morley_experiment_67a471
LEVEL: 4  PERSON: no
HEADWORD: Michelson–Morley experiment
ENTRY:
Experiment in 1887 to detect Earth's motion through the luminiferous aether, a medium thought to carry light waves. [[Albert A. Michelson]] and Edward W. Morley compared the speed of light in perpendicular directions at what is now Case Western Reserve University in [[Cleveland]], Ohio. They found no significant difference, a negative result generally considered the first strong evidence against some aether theories. The experiment initiated research that led to [[Special relativity]], which rules out motion against an aether. Later experiments with greater sensitivity found no aether wind; in 2009 optical resonator experiments confirmed its absence at the 10⁻¹⁷ level. Michelson–Morley type experiments were repeated many times with steadily increasing sensitivity and, together with the Ives–Stilwell and Kennedy–Thorndike experiments, form one of the fundamental tests of special relativity.

=== KEY: Mumbai_8ebaad
LEVEL: 3  PERSON: no
HEADWORD: Mumbai
ENTRY:
City (pop., 2011: almost 12.5 million), capital of [[Maharashtra]] state and financial capital of India. Also known as Bombay, it has a deep natural harbor and is the most populous city proper in India; the Mumbai Metropolitan Region has over 20 million people. The Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation, established in 1888, administers the city.

Originally an archipelago of seven islands on the [[Arabian Sea]], it has evidence of human occupation. The [[Maurya Empire]] ruled the islands from the 3rd century BC, and Ptolemy referred to them in AD 150. They were inhabited by the Marathi-speaking Koli people, whose deity Mumbā is believed to have given the city its name. Later the [[Delhi Sultanate]] and the Gujarat Sultanate ruled them. The islands were ceded to the Portuguese in 1535, transferred to the British Crown in 1661 as part of Catherine of Braganza's dowry, and leased to the [[East India Company]] in 1668. The Hornby Vellard land-reclamation project, begun in 1782 and completed in 1845, unified the islands; with new roads and railways and the 19th-century [[Cotton|cotton]] boom, it made Bombay a major seaport and commercial center. The city played a major role in the [[Indian independence movement]] and became capital of Maharashtra in 1960.

The Government of India officially changed the English name to Mumbai in November 1995, at the insistence of the Shiv Sena party, which argued that "Bombay" echoed British colonial rule. Some residents still use the older name. In the early 2010s the city contributed nearly five percent of India's GDP, one-fourth of its industrial output, and one-third of its tax revenues. It is the center of Bollywood and Marathi cinema.
