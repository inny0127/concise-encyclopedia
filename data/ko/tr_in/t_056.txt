Any of the four main compass directions: north (N), east (E), south (S) and west (W). The intercardinal, or ordinal, directions northeast, southeast, southwest and northwest lie midway between them. Their azimuths, measured clockwise from north, are 0°, 90°, 180° and 270° for the cardinal directions, and 45°, 135°, 225° and 315° for the ordinal ones. East corresponds to the direction of sunrise, and the cardinal directions underpin systems such as [[Latitude]] and [[Longitude]].

ENTRY:
Property of sets in mathematics that measures their size, roughly the number of objects they contain, which may be infinite. Two [[Set (mathematics)|sets]] have the same cardinality if their objects can be paired one-to-one, with none left unpaired. For finite sets, cardinality equals the usual count of elements. Infinite sets are measured by the [[Aleph number|Aleph numbers]], which extend the process of counting. The [[Natural number|natural numbers]] have cardinality ℵ₀, and a set is countably infinite if it can be paired one-to-one with them. The [[Real number|real numbers]] are uncountable, a result proved by diagonal arguments, which show that there are different sizes of infinity. [[Georg Cantor|Cantor]] introduced cardinality formally to mathematics in the late 19th century. Whether the reals have cardinality ℵ₁ is the continuum hypothesis, which is neither provable nor disprovable in standard set theories such as [[Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory]].

Any disease involving the heart or blood vessels; abbreviated CVD. It includes [[Coronary artery disease|coronary artery disease]], [[Heart failure|heart failure]], hypertensive heart disease, valvular heart disease (such as rheumatic heart disease), cardiomyopathy, arrhythmia, congenital heart disease, aortic aneurysms, peripheral artery disease, and venous thrombosis.

Mechanisms vary by disease. Coronary artery disease, [[Stroke|stroke]], and peripheral artery disease involve [[Atherosclerosis|atherosclerosis]], which may be caused by [[Hypertension|high blood pressure]], smoking, [[Obesity|obesity]], high cholesterol, [[Diabetes|diabetes mellitus]], poor diet, lack of exercise, excessive alcohol consumption, and poor sleep. Estimates attribute about 13% of CVD deaths to high blood pressure, 9% to tobacco, 6% to diabetes, 6% to lack of exercise, and 5% to obesity; dietary risk factors are associated with 53%. Rheumatic heart disease may follow untreated strep throat.

Up to 90% of CVD may be preventable through healthy eating, exercise, avoiding tobacco smoke, limiting alcohol, and treating blood pressure, lipids, and diabetes. The use of [[Aspirin|aspirin]] in otherwise healthy people is of unclear benefit.

CVD is the leading cause of death worldwide except Africa. It caused 17.9 million deaths (32.1%) in 2015, up from 12.3 million (25.8%) in 1990. Death rates from CVD have fallen in most developed countries since the 1970s but are increasing in much of the developing world. Coronary artery disease and stroke account for 80% of CVD deaths in males and 75% in females. At the same age, men are about 50% more likely to develop CVD and are typically diagnosed seven to ten years earlier than women.

Goods transported by land, water or air, while freight refers to their conveyance. In economics, freight is goods carried at a freight rate for commercial gain. Reusable multimodal containers are also called cargo, as are the goods stored in them. [[Merchant ship]] designed to carry goods, commodities and materials across seas and oceans. Container ships carry all their load in standardized intermodal containers and now carry most seagoing non-bulk cargo. Geopolitical region in the middle of the Americas, centered on the [[Caribbean Sea]] in the North Atlantic Ocean of islands, cays, reefs, banks, and adjoining mainland coasts. It largely overlaps the West Indies, a term often used interchangeably.

Island arcs mark its northern and eastern edges: the [[Greater Antilles]] in the north and the [[Lesser Antilles]], including the Leeward and Windward Islands, to the east and south. The northwestern Lucayan Archipelago, made up of [[The Bahamas]] and the Turks and Caicos Islands, and [[Barbados]] are counted as Caribbean though they do not border the sea. Mainland coasts from the Yucatán Peninsula to the Guianas are included. The region is organized into 33 political entities, including 13 sovereign states, 12 dependencies, and 7 overseas territories. It is named for the Caribs, an Amerindian people of the Lesser Antilles.

The oldest evidence of humans is at Banwari Trace in southern Trinidad, where remains date from 7,000 years ago. Spanish explorer [[Christopher Columbus]] landed in 1492, beginning colonization by Spain, France, Britain, the Netherlands, and Denmark. Plantation economies grew sugar, tobacco, and coffee using enslaved Africans brought by the [[Atlantic slave trade]]. Revolution in [[Haiti]] against France in 1804 was the first successful slave revolt in the Americas. Most British, French, and Dutch colonies gained sovereignty between the 1960s and 1980s.

European colonization, the slave trade, indentured servitude, and later immigration have made the Caribbean one of the most ethnically diverse regions in the world.

Organism that eats mostly or exclusively animal tissue. The term derives from the Latin *caro*, "flesh," and *vorare*, "to devour." Carnivores meet their energy and nutrient needs by consuming animal muscle, fat and other soft tissues, through predation or scavenging. Obligate carnivores, or hypercarnivores, derive more than 70% of their diet from meat and depend on animal flesh in the wild; cats, for example, appear unable to synthesize essential nutrients such as taurine.
