Illegal and overt attempt by a [[Military organization|military organization]] or other government elite to unseat an incumbent leader or government. From French, literally meaning stroke of state. By one estimate there were 457 coup attempts from 1950 to 2010, half of them successful, with most occurring in the mid-1960s. A soft coup overthrows a government without force.

ENTRY:
Measure of the joint variability of two [[Random variable|random variables]]. Covariance is positive when the variables tend to show similar behavior and negative when they tend to show opposite behavior. Its magnitude reflects the geometric mean of the [[Variance|variances]] shared by the two variables, so a larger magnitude indicates stronger mutual dependence. Its units are those of X times those of Y, so changing the units changes its value proportionally, which makes strength hard to judge from covariance alone. The [[Correlation|correlation coefficient]] normalizes covariance to a value between −1 and 1 by dividing by the product of the [[Standard deviation|standard deviations]]. For jointly distributed variables X and Y, covariance is the [[Expected value|expected value]] of the product of their deviations from their means: cov(X,Y) = E[(X − E[X])(Y − E[Y])]. Covariance is zero for independent variables, though the converse does not hold.

Third and last period of the [[Mesozoic|Mesozoic Era]], lasting from about 143.1 to 66 million years ago. At around 77.1 million years, it is the ninth and longest geological period of the [[Phanerozoic]]. Its name derives from the Latin *creta*, meaning [[Chalk|chalk]], which is abundant in deposits from the latter half of the period. The climate was relatively warm, with high sea levels that created numerous shallow inland seas. Dinosaurs continued to dominate the land, and the earliest relatives of placentals and [[Marsupial|marsupials]] appeared, as did the earliest crown group birds toward the end. [[Flowering plant|Flowering plants]] appeared in the Early Cretaceous and became the dominant plants worldwide by the end of the period, as widespread gymnosperm groups declined. The period ended with the [[Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event|Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction]], in which many groups, including non-avian dinosaurs, pterosaurs and large marine reptiles, died out.

Largest and most populous island of [[Greece]], in the southern [[Aegean Sea]]. Crete lies about 100 km (62 mi) south of the Peloponnese and is the fifth largest island in the [[Mediterranean Sea|Mediterranean]], after Sicily, Sardinia, Cyprus and Corsica. It has an area of 8,450 sq km (3,260 sq mi), is predominantly mountainous, and its highest point is Mount Ida. The region of Crete, which includes surrounding islands, had a population of 624,408 as of 2021, with its capital at Heraklion. Crete was the center of Europe's first advanced civilization, the [[Minoan civilization|Minoans]], from 2700 to 1420 BCE. Later rulers included Rome, the Byzantine Empire, the Venetian Republic and the [[Ottoman Empire]]. In 1898 Crete achieved autonomy under the Ottomans, and in December 1913 it became part of Greece. In Greek mythology it is best known as the birthplace of [[Zeus]], and the Minoan palace of [[Knossos]] lies near Heraklion.

Incurable, terminal neurodegenerative disease caused by a [[Prion|prion]]. Early symptoms include memory problems, behavioral changes and poor coordination, and later symptoms include [[Dementia|dementia]], involuntary movements and coma. About 70% of patients die within a year of diagnosis. About 85% of cases occur for unknown reasons, while 10–15% are inherited. A variant form, [[Variant Creutzfeldt–Jakob disease|variant CJD]], was caused by exposure to meat from cows with bovine spongiform encephalopathy. There is no specific treatment; opioids may relieve pain, and clonazepam may help with involuntary movements.

Bat-and-ball team sport between two teams of eleven players on a field. Each end of the 22-yard pitch has a wicket of two bails balanced on three stumps. The batting side scores runs by hitting the bowled ball and running between the wickets, while the fielding side tries to dismiss ten batters to end an innings. Forms range from Test matches of up to five days to Twenty20 (T20), in which each team bats for a single 20-over innings. The sport spread globally with the expansion of the [[British Empire]], and its earliest known definite reference is to play in southeast England in the mid-16th century. The International Cricket Council (ICC) has over 100 members, twelve of them full members who play Test matches. The sport is played mainly in [[India]], [[Pakistan]], Bangladesh, [[Sri Lanka]], [[Australia]], New Zealand, England and Wales, South Africa and the West Indies. Australia has won eight One Day International trophies, including six World Cups.

[[Orthoptera|Orthopteran]] insect of the family Gryllidae, related to bush crickets and, more distantly, to [[Grasshopper|grasshoppers]]. Crickets have cylindrical bodies, long antennae and enlarged hind legs for jumping. Males produce a loud, persistent chirp by rubbing their leathery forewings together, mostly at night. Crickets are distributed worldwide except at latitudes of 55° or higher, with the greatest diversity in the [[Tropics|tropics]]. The largest members of the family are the bull crickets, *Brachytrupes*, up to 5 cm (2 in) long. Crickets appear in literature, including *The Adventures of Pinocchio* and [[Charles Dickens|Charles Dickens]]'s *The Cricket on the Hearth*, and in poems by [[William Wordsworth]] and [[John Keats]]. They are kept as pets in countries from [[China]] to Europe and are eaten deep-fried in Southeast Asia.
