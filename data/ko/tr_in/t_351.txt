Genre of Japanese art that flourished from the 17th through 19th centuries, producing woodblock prints and paintings of beautiful women, [[Kabuki]] actors, sumo wrestlers and landscapes. Fundamental limit in [[Quantum mechanics|quantum mechanics]] on how precisely certain paired properties can be known together. Also called Heisenberg's indeterminacy principle, it states that the more accurately one property, such as position, is measured, the less accurately its complementary property, such as [[Momentum|momentum]], can be known. German physicist [[Werner Heisenberg]] introduced it in 1927. Earle Hesse Kennard derived the formal inequality, relating the standard deviations of position and momentum, later that year, and [[Hermann Weyl]] did so in 1928. In wave mechanics, the relation arises because the position and momentum wavefunctions are [[Fourier transform|Fourier transforms]] of each other, and a nonzero function and its Fourier transform cannot both be sharply localized. The principle extends to other pairs, such as energy and time. Its effects are significant only for microscopic objects and negligible for macroscopic ones.

Segment of the population occupying the lowest possible position in a class hierarchy, below the core of the [[Working class|working class]].
