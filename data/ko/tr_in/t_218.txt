ENTRY:
Cell division that produces haploid gametes, such as sperm and egg cells, in sexually reproducing organisms. Meiosis consists of two rounds of division that produce four cells, each with one copy of each [[Chromosome|chromosome]]. Before division, DNA is replicated and homologous chromosomes pair and exchange genetic material by recombination, which creates new combinations of genes. Because two haploid gametes fuse at [[Fertilisation|fertilization]], alternating meiosis and fertilization sustain sexual reproduction in animals, plants and fungi. Errors that produce an abnormal number of chromosomes, known as aneuploidy, are the leading known cause of miscarriage and the most frequent genetic cause of developmental disabilities, including [[Down syndrome]].

Family of biomolecules organized as oligomers or polymers, forming the pigments of many organisms. Melanin is produced by melanocytes in a process called melanogenesis, in which the [[Amino acid]] tyrosine is oxidized and then polymerized. There are five basic types: eumelanin, pheomelanin, neuromelanin, allomelanin and pyomelanin. Eumelanin is more abundant in tissues requiring photoprotection, such as the epidermis, and can dissipate over 99.9% of absorbed [[Ultraviolet|UV]] radiation. Neuromelanin, found in the brain, has been studied for possible treatment of neurodegenerative disorders such as Parkinson's.

The primary cause is [[Ultraviolet|ultraviolet]] light exposure in people with low levels of skin pigment; risk is higher for those with many moles, a family history, or poor immune function. Phase transition of a [[Solid]] to a [[Liquid]]. It occurs when the [[Internal energy]] of the solid increases, typically through heat or pressure, raising its temperature to the melting point. Melting is a first-order phase transition: at the melting point the change in Gibbs free energy is zero, while enthalpy and entropy change. Faculty of the mind by which data or information is encoded, stored, and retrieved when needed. It is the retention of information over time for the purpose of influencing future action. Without memory of past events, language, relationships, and personal identity could not develop. Memory loss is usually described as forgetfulness or as a disorder such as [[Amnesia|amnesia]].

Memory is often understood as an information processing system made up of a sensory processor, short-term (or working) memory, and long-term memory. Working memory serves as an encoding and retrieval processor, and long-term memory stores information through various categorical models or systems.

Declarative, or explicit, memory is the conscious storage and recollection of data and includes semantic memory, encoded with specific meaning, and episodic memory, encoded along a spatial and temporal plane. Non-declarative, or implicit, memory is the unconscious storage and recollection of information, as in procedural memory, the slow and gradual learning of skills, and priming.

Sensory memory holds information from the senses for less than one second and cannot be prolonged by rehearsal. In experiments published in 1963, George Sperling showed that its capacity is approximately 12 items but that it degrades within a few hundred milliseconds. Emotional, psychological, and social [[Well-being|well-being]], influencing cognition, perception, and behavior. The [[World Health Organization]] (WHO) defines it as a "state of well-being in which the individual realizes their abilities, can cope with the normal stresses of life, can work productively and fruitfully, and can contribute to their community." From the perspectives of positive psychology or holism, it is not merely the absence of mental illness but a broader state that includes the ability to enjoy life and to achieve psychological resilience. Cultural differences, personal philosophy, and competing professional theories all affect how it is defined.

Impairment is a risk factor for a [[Mental disorder|mental disorder]]; about 970 million people worldwide had one in 2019, anxiety and depression being the most common. Diet, exercise, stress, drug use, and social connections are associated with mental health. Constant online connection can increase stress, and cyberbullying has been linked to depression and anxiety.

In the mid-19th century William Sweetser coined the term mental hygiene. Dorothea Dix exposed sub-standard conditions for the mentally ill and, from 1840 to 1880, won federal support for over 30 state [[Psychiatric hospital|psychiatric hospitals]], which were nevertheless understaffed and accused of violating human rights. Emil Kraepelin developed a [[Taxonomy|taxonomy]] of mental disorders in 1896 that dominated the field for nearly 80 years. Clifford Beers, after publishing *A Mind That Found Itself* (1908), opened the first outpatient mental health clinic in the U.S. The mental hygiene movement was at times associated with advocating [[Eugenics|eugenics]] and sterilization. After World War II the term "mental health" gradually replaced it, shifting emphasis toward prevention.

Access to care remains uneven: the WHO African Region averages 2.2 mental health workers per 100,000 population against a global median of 13.5. Critics argue that diagnostic expansion has led to overmedicalization.

Innermost and smallest planet of the [[Solar System]], first from the [[Sun]]. It is a rocky [[Terrestrial planet|terrestrial planet]] with a trace atmosphere and a surface gravity slightly lower than that of Mars. The largest crater, Caloris Planitia, is 1,550 km across, about one-third the planet's diameter of 4,880 km. Because it orbits closest to the Sun, it always appears near the Sun in Earth's sky, as a "morning star" or an "evening star."

Mercury's sidereal year (88.0 Earth days) and sidereal day (58.65 Earth days) are in a 3:2 spin–orbit resonance, so one solar day, from sunrise to sunrise, lasts around 176 Earth days. Equatorial temperatures range from −170 °C at night to 420 °C in sunlight, making it the second hottest planet after [[Venus]], since it lacks an atmosphere to trap heat. Its very small axial tilt leaves the poles permanently shadowed, which strongly suggests that water ice may be present in the craters.

The planet is about 70% metallic and 30% silicate material. Its density, 5.427 g/cm³, is the second highest in the Solar System, implying a large iron-rich core. The core radius is estimated at 2,020 ± 30 km and occupies about 57% of the volume, against 17% for Earth. Mercury formed approximately 4.5 billion years ago and is expected to be destroyed when the Sun becomes a red giant in approximately seven or eight billion years.

The ancient Greeks had realized by about 350 BC that the morning and evening "stars" were the same body. Mariner 10 made the first successful flyby in 1974, and the planet has since been explored by the MESSENGER and BepiColombo orbiters.
