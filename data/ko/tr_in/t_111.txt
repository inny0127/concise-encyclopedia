ENTRY:
Change in the heritable characteristics of biological populations over successive generations. It occurs when individuals in a population vary in their genetic characteristics and in the numbers of their surviving offspring. [[Mutation]] introduces new genetic variation, and processes such as [[Genetic drift|genetic drift]], gene flow, and [[Natural selection|natural selection]] make some variants more or less common over time. Evolution is often defined as a change in the frequency of alleles, the variants of a gene found at a specific locus, in a population. In a strict biological sense it can occur even when the visible phenotype is unchanged. The process has given rise to biodiversity at every level of biological organization.

The scientific theory of evolution by natural selection was conceived independently by two British naturalists, [[Charles Darwin]] and [[Alfred Russel Wallace]], in the mid-19th century, as an explanation for why organisms are adapted to their environments. Their ideas were jointly presented in 1858, and the theory was first set out in detail in Darwin's 1859 book [[On the Origin of Species|*On the Origin of Species*]]. Natural selection rests on four observable facts: more offspring are often produced than can survive; traits vary among individuals; different traits confer different rates of survival and reproduction; and traits can be passed from generation to generation. Members of a population are therefore more likely to be replaced by the offspring of parents with favorable characteristics for that environment.

In the early 20th century, competing ideas of evolution were refuted and evolution was combined with [[Mendelian inheritance]] and population genetics to give rise to modern evolutionary theory. In this synthesis, the basis of heredity is in DNA molecules that pass information from generation to generation. Portions of DNA that specify a single functional unit are genes, and the different forms of a sequence at a locus are alleles. The complete set of genes in an organism is its genotype, and its set of observable traits is its phenotype. Some observable characteristics are not inherited; a suntan, for example, results from the interaction of genotype and sunlight and is not passed to children.

Variation comes from mutations in the genome, reshuffling of genes through sexual reproduction, and migration between populations. About half of the mutations in the coding regions of protein-coding genes are deleterious and the other half neutral, with a small percentage conferring a benefit. Duplication of large sections of a chromosome introduces extra copies of genes, a major source of raw material for new genes.

All life on Earth shares a [[Last universal common ancestor|last universal common ancestor]], which lived approximately 3.5–3.8 billion years ago. Existing biodiversity has been shaped by repeated formation of new species, changes within species, and extinction. Supporting evidence includes fossil records documenting transitional forms, comparative genomics revealing shared ancestry, and observable changes in real time, such as antibiotic resistance in bacteria. Evolutionary discoveries have influenced agriculture, medicine, and computer science.

Elimination of metabolic waste products by an [[Organism|organism]]. In vertebrates, the main excretory organs are the [[Lung|lungs]], kidneys and skin. Unicellular organisms discharge wastes directly through the cell surface. Excretion differs from secretion, in which a substance has specific tasks after leaving the cell.

Physical activity that enhances or maintains fitness and overall health. It is performed for reasons including weight loss or maintenance, developing [[Muscle|muscles]] and the cardiovascular system, improving [[Mental health|mental health]], or simple enjoyment. Exercise is a subset of physical activity: it is planned, structured, and repetitive, and aims to improve or maintain [[Physical fitness|physical fitness]].

Exercises are generally grouped into three types. Aerobic exercise uses large muscle groups and causes the body to use more oxygen than at rest, with the goal of increasing cardiovascular endurance; examples are running, cycling, and swimming. Anaerobic exercise, which includes strength and resistance training, can increase muscle mass and improve bone density, balance, and coordination. Flexibility exercises, such as stretching, improve joint flexibility and range of motion, which can reduce the chance of injury.

Regular exercise contributes to a healthy weight, bone density, muscle strength, and joint mobility, and strengthens the immune system, lowering the risk of [[Cardiovascular disease|cardiovascular disease]]. People who exercise at moderate to high levels have a lower mortality rate than those who are inactive. Physical inactivity causes approximately 6% of the burden of disease from coronary heart disease, 7% of type 2 diabetes, 10% of breast cancer, and 10% of colon cancer worldwide, and 9% of premature mortality overall.

The World Health Organization recommends that adults do at least 150–300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity or 75–150 minutes of vigorous-intensity activity per week, with muscle-strengthening activities on two or more days, and that children and adolescents do at least 60 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous activity daily. Even a small amount is healthier than none: for people who get the least activity, adding two or three minutes per day of moderate activity could reduce the risk of premature death by 10%.

Large-scale low-pressure system of the middle latitudes, also called a mid-latitude or wave cyclone. === KEY: Extraversion_and_introversion_e9925e

LEVEL: 4

ENTRY:
Central dimension of human personality, introduced into psychology by [[Carl Jung|Carl Jung]]. Extraversion is associated with sociability, talkativeness and high energy, while introversion is linked to [[Introspection|introspection]], reserve and a preference for solitary activities. Jung defined introversion as an orientation through subjective psychic contents, and extraversion as a concentration of interest on the external object. These traits are measured in degrees rather than fixed categories. Although often presented as opposite ends of a continuum, most people have elements of both, with one more dominant depending on the situation.

ENTRY:
Manufacturing process of pushing material through a die to form objects with a fixed cross-sectional profile. Its advantages include the ability to create very complex cross-sections, to work brittle materials, which encounter only compressive and shear stresses, and to produce an excellent surface finish. Drawing, which pulls material through a die using tensile strength, is the main way to produce [[Wire|wire]]. Commonly extruded materials include [[Metal|metals]], [[Polymer|polymers]], [[Ceramic|ceramics]] and [[Concrete]]. Joseph Bramah patented the first extrusion process, for pipe made from soft metals, in 1797. Harder materials such as [[Steel|steel]] began to be extruded in 1951, and extrusion can also increase the strength of metals.
