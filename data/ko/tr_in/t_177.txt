ENTRY:
Imperial dynasty of China (266–420), founded by Sima Yan. It succeeded the Cao Wei kingdom and in 280 conquered Eastern Wu, ending the [[Three Kingdoms]] period and reuniting China proper for the first time since the [[Han dynasty]] ended. Civil wars from 291 to 306, the War of the Eight Princes, weakened the state. Non-Han rebellions followed, ushering in the [[Sixteen Kingdoms]] era. The Han-Zhao state sacked [[Luoyang]] in 311 and executed Emperor Min in 318, ending the Western Jin. Sima Rui then re-established the dynasty at Jiankang, modern [[Nanjing]]. The Eastern Jin remained in near-constant conflict with northern states until General Liu Yu usurped it in 420.

City (pop. 9.2 million), capital of [[Shandong]] province in eastern China. Its name means "south of the Ji," referring to the Ji River, which flowed north of the city until the [[Yellow River]] took over its bed in 1852. The area has been inhabited for more than 4000 years, including during the [[Neolithic]] Longshan culture. Jinan is called the "City of Springs" for its 72 artesian springs. ENTRY:
Supernatural beings in [[Islam]], pre-Islamic Arabian religion and Arabic folklore. Like humans, jinn are accountable for their deeds and can be believers or unbelievers. They are naturally invisible and can shapeshift, usually appearing as [[Snake|snakes]], [[Scorpion|scorpions]], [[Lizard|lizards]] or humans. They rarely meddle in human affairs, but if harmed they may retaliate, sometimes by possessing the assailant, which requires exorcism. The word derives from a Semitic root meaning "to hide" or "to adapt."

Situated in the Thar Desert, the city has a [[Desert climate|hot desert climate]], and temperatures routinely exceed 40 °C from April to June. The highest temperature recorded was 48.8 °C on May 20, 2016. The old town, which circles the Mehrangarh Fort, is known as the Blue City for the dominant blue of its buildings. Jodhpur is a major tourist destination known for its forts, palaces and temples.
