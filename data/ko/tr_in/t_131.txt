Association of 39 mostly German-speaking states in [[Central Europe]] (1815–1866). Created by the [[Congress of Vienna]] to replace the [[Holy Roman Empire]], which dissolved in 1806 as a result of the [[Napoleonic Wars]], it had a single organ, the Federal Convention, presided over by [[Austrian Empire|Austria]] but without a head of state. Most important decisions required unanimity, and its purpose was largely limited to security, making it essentially a military alliance. Its effectiveness depended on cooperation between Austria and [[Prussia]], which often opposed each other. The revolutions of 1848–1849 sought a unified federal state under the Frankfurt Constitution, but the Convention dissolved and was reestablished in 1850 after the revolution was crushed. The Confederation ended after Prussia defeated Austria in the [[Austro-Prussian War]] of 1866. Most historians consider it weak and ineffective; its weakness was part of its design, as the great powers did not want a German nation-state.

German state, also called Imperial Germany or the Second Reich (1871–1918). Prussia held about two-thirds of its population and territory. [[Otto von Bismarck]] served as chancellor from 1871 to 1890 and introduced pioneering social programs that laid the foundation for the modern welfare state. The humiliation and economic devastation that followed the [[Treaty of Versailles]] are considered leading factors in the rise of [[Adolf Hitler]] and Nazism.

Medium to large German breed of working dog, known in Britain as the Alsatian. Developed in [[Germany]] from 1899 by Max von Stephanitz, using traditional German herding dogs, the breed was bred for working ability; Stephanitz founded the Verein für Deutsche Schäferhunde that year. West Germanic language of the Indo-European family, mainly spoken in Western and Central Europe. It is the majority and official or co-official language in [[Germany]], Austria, Switzerland, and Liechtenstein, an official language of Luxembourg, Belgium, and the Italian province of South Tyrol, and a recognized national language in Namibia.

German has nearly 100 million native speakers and over 130 million total speakers as of 2024. It is the most spoken native language in the [[European Union]] and, after English, the second most widely spoken [[Germanic languages|Germanic language]]. It is the fourth most commonly learned second language globally and has been influential in philosophy, theology, science, and technology.

German is most closely related to Afrikaans, Dutch, English, the Frisian languages, and Scots. Modern German developed from Old High German, which in turn developed from Proto-Germanic during the Early Middle Ages. The High German consonant shift separates the High German dialects of the south from the Low German and Low Franconian dialects to the north; an example is Standard German *machen* against Dutch *maken*. Standard German is based on Thuringian-Upper Saxon and Upper Franconian dialects, and its standardization was catalyzed by [[Martin Luther]]'s 16th-century Bible translation.

German is an inflected language, with four cases, three genders, and two numbers, and it has strong and weak verbs. Most of its vocabulary is Germanic, with a smaller share from Latin and Greek; English is the main source of recent loanwords. It is a pluricentric language with three standardized variants: German, Austrian, and Swiss Standard German. Its many dialects include some protected by regional or national governments, and it is closely related to [[Yiddish]] and Luxembourgish. The Council for German Orthography is the main international body regulating its orthography.

Literary works written in the German language. Medieval works include the *Hildebrandslied*, the heroic epic *Heliand*, and courtly epics such as the *Nibelungenlied*. Branch of the Indo-European language family. Spoken natively by about 515 million people, mainly in [[Europe]], North America, [[Oceania]] and [[Southern Africa]], the languages all derive from Proto-Germanic, spoken in Iron Age [[Scandinavia]] and northern Germany. The West Germanic branch includes English, the most widely spoken Germanic language with around 360–400 million native speakers; [[Germany|German]] with over 100 million; and Dutch with 24 million. Traditional religion of the [[Germanic peoples]]. It spanned at least a thousand years and an area covering Scandinavia, the British Isles, modern Germany and the Netherlands, and its beliefs varied. Few sources were written by pagans themselves; most were written by outsiders, complicating reconstruction. The Germanic peoples believed in many gods and supernatural beings such as giants, dwarfs, elves and dragons. Early medieval sources name the gods [[Odin]], [[Thor]], Tyr and Frigg. Grayish-white metalloid, chemical element with symbol Ge and atomic number 32. Germanium is lustrous and hard-brittle, similar in appearance to silicon, and ranks 50th in abundance in Earth's crust. [[Dmitri Mendeleev]] predicted it in 1869 as "ekasilicon". Clemens Winkler discovered it in 1886 in the mineral argyrodite at Freiberg and named it after Germany, his country of birth. It is mined mainly from sphalerite, the primary ore of zinc. Germanium was the basis of the first decade of [[Semiconductor|semiconductor]] electronics and was used in [[Transistor|transistors]]. Its major modern uses include fiber-optic systems, infrared optics, [[Solar cell|solar cells]] and [[Light-emitting diode|light-emitting diodes]]. Synthetic soluble germanium salts are nephrotoxic.

Charlemagne founded the Carolingian Empire in 800, and in 962 the Kingdom of Germany formed the bulk of the [[Holy Roman Empire]]. Northern German regions were the center of the Protestant [[Reformation]] in the 16th century. After the empire's dissolution in 1806, the German Confederation was formed in 1815, and unification led by [[Prussia]] established the [[German Empire]] in 1871.

Germany was among the Central Powers that lost World War I, after which the empire was replaced by the [[Weimar Republic]]. The Nazi rise to power in 1933 led to a totalitarian dictatorship under [[Adolf Hitler]], which resulted in World War II and the Holocaust. In 1949 the country was organized into two polities with limited sovereignty: West Germany and the communist East Germany. The former East German states joined the Federal Republic on October 3, 1990, in [[German reunification]], and full sovereignty was regained on March 15, 1991.

Germany is a developed country with the largest economy in Europe by nominal GDP and is the world's third-largest exporter and third-largest importer. It has the third-highest number of UNESCO World Heritage Sites, 55, of which 52 are cultural.
