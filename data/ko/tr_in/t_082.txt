Team [[Sport|sport]] played on ice in which two four-player teams slide heavy, polished [[Granite|granite]] stones toward a circular target called the house. Each team has eight stones, and points go to the stones resting closest to the center of the house at the end of each end. A game usually consists of eight or ten ends. Sweepers using brooms reduce friction in front of a moving stone, allowing it to travel farther and in a straighter line with less curl, so strategy and teamwork are central. The word curling first appeared in print in 1620. Curling has been an Olympic medal sport since the 1998 Nagano Games.

Standardized form of [[Money|money]] used as a medium of exchange. Government-issued fiat currencies include the U.S. dollar, the [[Euro|euro]], the yen, sterling and the [[Renminbi|renminbi]]. Currencies act as stores of value and are traded between nations in foreign exchange markets, where [[Exchange rate|exchange rates]] set their relative values. Originally currency was a receipt for grain stored in temple granaries in [[Sumer|Sumer]] and [[Ancient Egypt|Ancient Egypt]]. By the late [[Bronze Age|Bronze Age]], merchants traded under treaties guaranteeing safe passage, and oxhide-shaped copper ingots may have served as currency. Metals were weighed and stamped into coins so that the holder knew the weight of precious metal, which helped lead to banking. Gold coins were used for large purchases, silver for midsized transactions and copper for everyday exchange. Today over 180 national currencies exist, and fiat designs risk debasement through excessive issuance.

Unwritten body of community norms treated as binding law. Customary law exists where a legal practice is observed and the relevant actors consider it an opinion of law or necessity (*opinio juris*). Most customary laws concern community standards long established in a locale, but the term also applies in [[International law|international law]] to standards nearly universal in acceptance, such as laws against [[Piracy|piracy]] and [[Slavery|slavery]]. Many customary laws have supporting [[Court|court]] rulings and case law. The cyanide anion is extremely toxic and causes cyanide poisoning, binding to iron in cytochrome c oxidase and halting aerobic respiration. Soluble salts such as sodium cyanide and potassium cyanide are also highly toxic. Cyanide is also used in [[Mining|mining]] to extract gold and silver from ore.

Phylum of photosynthetic [[Prokaryote|prokaryotes]], informally called blue-green algae. Cyanobacteria are the first organisms known to have produced oxygen: they split water, release oxygen as a byproduct and use the energy to fix [[Carbon dioxide|carbon dioxide]] into carbohydrates. Over billions of years they changed the early atmosphere, contributing to the [[Great Oxidation Event|Great Oxidation Event]] in the early [[Proterozoic|Proterozoic]] eon. Their photosynthetic pigments include chlorophyll, carotenoids and phycobilins, and their internal membranes, called thylakoids, perform photosynthesis. Plant and algal [[Chloroplast|chloroplasts]] are thought to descend from cyanobacteria acquired through endosymbiosis. Cyanobacteria first appeared in the middle [[Archean|Archean]] eon and are probably the most numerous taxon ever to have existed on Earth. Settled about 13,000 years ago, Cyprus was seized from Persian control by [[Alexander the Great]] in 333 BC, and it was under Ottoman rule from 1571 to 1878. It was annexed by the United Kingdom in 1914 and became independent in 1960. Greek Cypriots sought union with Greece (*enosis*), while Turkish Cypriots pursued partition (*taksim*). After a coup on 15 July 1974 by Greek Cypriot nationalists and elements of the Greek military junta, a Turkish invasion displaced over 150,000 Greek Cypriots and 50,000 Turkish Cypriots. The division remains subject to an ongoing dispute. Cyprus joined the [[European Union]] in 2004 and the [[Euro|eurozone]] in 2008.

Egyptian Christian theologian and Patriarch of [[Alexandria]] from 412 to 444. He was a central figure in the Christological controversies of the late 4th and 5th centuries. As president of the Council of Ephesus in 431, he helped clarify the hypostatic union of Christ's divine and human natures in one person, against the views of Nestorius, who was deposed as Patriarch of [[Constantinople]]. [[Writing system|Writing system]] used for many languages across [[Eurasia|Eurasia]], including Slavic, Turkic and Mongolic languages. As of 2019, around 250 million people used it as the official script for their national languages, with Russia accounting for about half. When Bulgaria joined the [[European Union]] in 2007, Cyrillic became the third official script of the EU, after the Latin and Greek alphabets. The Early Cyrillic alphabet was developed in the 9th century at the Preslav Literary School in the [[First Bulgarian Empire]], probably by disciples of [[Cyril and Methodius]], and is named in honor of Saint Cyril. Derived from the Greek uncial script, it was augmented with letters from the Glagolitic alphabet for sounds absent in Greek. It spread among other [[Slavs|Slavs]] and non-Slavic Romanians, and came to dominate Glagolitic in the 12th century.

Persian king, founder of the [[Achaemenid Empire]] (r. 559–530 BC). Also known as Cyrus II of Persia, he came from Anshan, in Persis. Around 550 BC he revolted against the Median king Astyages. He defeated [[Lydia]] in 546 BC and captured [[Babylon]] in 539 BC with minimal resistance, gaining control of Anatolia and the Fertile Crescent. His conquests created what became the largest empire in history up to that time. It reached its greatest extent later, under Darius the Great.

Cyrus allegedly died in battle against the Massagetae along the Syr Darya, according to Herodotus; Xenophon says he instead returned to the capital, Pasargadae. His son Cambyses II succeeded him and conquered Egypt, Nubia, and Cyrenaica.

Greek writers praised his tolerance toward the customs and religions of conquered peoples. The Cyrus Cylinder records his restoration of local cults and the repatriation of exiles, including the Jews. Some modern assessments hold that this was pragmatic toleration for administrative efficiency rather than ideological benevolence. In Judaism he is exalted for ending the [[Babylonian captivity]]; the Hebrew Bible describes the return to Judah and the rebuilding of the Temple in Jerusalem. According to the Book of [[Isaiah]], he was anointed by Yahweh and designated "messiah," the only non-Jewish figure so revered.

Cyrus is credited with developing a central administration to govern the empire's satraps, a model said to have influenced later states. Later Persian empires viewed the Achaemenid era as an ideal to emulate, and his tomb at Pasargadae remains a site of reverence for many Iranians.

Material within a [[Cell (biology)|cell]], enclosed by the [[Cell membrane|cell membrane]] and excluding the nucleus in eukaryotes. Its main components are the cytosol, a gel-like substance, the cell's internal structures and various inclusions. About 80% water and usually colorless, the cytosol suspends larger structures such as [[Ribosome|ribosomes]], [[Mitochondria|mitochondria]], plastids, lipid droplets and vacuoles. Many cellular activities take place in the cytoplasm, including [[Glycolysis|glycolysis]], [[Photosynthesis|photosynthesis]] and cell division. The movement of [[Calcium|calcium]] ions into and out of the cytoplasm is a signaling activity for metabolic processes. Cytoplasmic streaming, the movement of the cytoplasm around organelles, occurs in large animal and plant cells, amoebae and slime molds. The term was introduced by Rudolf von Kölliker in 1863.

Dynamic network of [[Protein|protein]] filaments in the [[Cytoplasm|cytoplasm]] of all [[Cell (biology)|cells]], including those of bacteria and archaea. In [[Eukaryote|eukaryotes]], it extends from the [[Cell nucleus|nucleus]] to the [[Cell membrane|cell membrane]] and has three main components: microfilaments, intermediate filaments and microtubules. Its primary function is to give the cell its shape and mechanical resistance, and it can contract, allowing cells to migrate. It takes part in cell signaling, endocytosis, the segregation of chromosomes during [[Mitosis|mitosis]], and the intracellular transport of vesicles and organelles. Microfilaments are made of actin, the most abundant cellular protein, and during [[Muscle contraction|muscle contraction]] myosin motors exert force on parallel actin filaments. Tropomyosin inhibits this interaction until troponin, responding to rising calcium, releases the inhibition. Nikolai K. Koltsov proposed the cytoskeleton in 1903, and it was found in prokaryotes in 1992.
