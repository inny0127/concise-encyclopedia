Breakdown of large insoluble food compounds into small water-soluble components that can be absorbed into the blood plasma. It is a form of catabolism, usually divided into mechanical digestion, the physical breakdown of food into smaller pieces, and chemical digestion, in which [[Enzyme|enzymes]] break food down into compounds the body can use.

External digestion, in which enzymes are secreted into the surroundings and some products diffuse back, developed earlier in evolutionary history; most fungi still rely on it. Animals have a tube, the [[Gastrointestinal tract|gastrointestinal tract]], in which internal digestion occurs; it is more efficient because more of the broken-down products can be captured.

In humans, chewing and the wetting action of saliva begin digestion in the mouth. Saliva contains salivary amylase, which starts the digestion of [[Starch|starch]]; about 30% of starch is hydrolyzed into disaccharide there. The food, now a slurry mass called a bolus, travels down the [[Esophagus|esophagus]] to the [[Stomach|stomach]] by peristalsis. Gastric juice, mainly hydrochloric acid and pepsin, starts protein digestion; mucus and bicarbonates protect the stomach wall. Pepsin breaks proteins into peptides, which enzymes in the [[Small intestine|small intestine]] break down further into dipeptides and amino acids.

Partially digested food, called chyme, enters the duodenum, where it mixes with enzymes from the [[Pancreas|pancreas]] and bile juice from the [[Liver|liver]]. About 95% of nutrient absorption occurs in the small intestine. Water and minerals are reabsorbed in the colon, where bacteria produce some vitamins that are also absorbed. Waste material is eliminated from the rectum during defecation.

ENTRY:
Polynomial equation with [[Integer|integer]] coefficients for which only integer solutions are sought. A linear Diophantine equation, *ax + by = c*, has a solution if and only if the greatest common divisor of *a* and *b* divides *c*, and then it has infinitely many solutions. Because such equations define algebraic curves and surfaces, their study forms part of [[Algebraic geometry|algebraic geometry]]. The field traces to [[Diophantus|Diophantus]], and [[Fermat's Last Theorem|Fermat's Last Theorem]], which states that no positive integers satisfy xⁿ + yⁿ = zⁿ for n greater than 2, was proved by Andrew Wiles in 1994. In 1970 Yuri Matiyasevich showed that no general algorithm can decide whether an arbitrary Diophantine equation has an integer solution, resolving [[David Hilbert|Hilbert's]] tenth problem negatively.

Greek mathematician of [[Alexandria|Alexandria]], probably active in the 3rd century. His *Arithmetica*, originally thirteen books of which ten survive, is a collection of 290 algebraic problems solved through equations. Extinct [[Genus|genus]] of diplodocid [[Sauropoda|sauropod]] dinosaurs from the [[Jurassic|Late Jurassic]] of North America and Europe. The name, coined by Othniel Charles Marsh in 1878, means "double beam," referring to the double-beamed chevron bones under its tail. Its first [[Fossil|fossils]] were found in 1877. Its fossils are common in the Morrison Formation, where most specimens date to about 151.88 to 149.1 million years ago. *D. carnegii* is among the longest dinosaurs known from a complete skeleton, at 24–26 m, with an estimated mass of 12–14.8 metric tons. It had a small skull, peg-like teeth and a neck of at least 15 vertebrae. Its great size may have deterred predators such as [[Allosaurus|Allosaurus]].
