Location, physical or virtual, where people regularly gather to buy and sell goods. Such places are called souks, bazaars, mercados or palengkes in different regions. Some operate daily as permanent markets, while others meet weekly or on festival days. Open-air markets were known in ancient Babylonia, [[Phoenicia]], Greece and Egypt, and bazaars may have originated in Persia. [[Stochastic process|Stochastic process]] in which the probability of each event depends only on the state attained in the previous event. Named after the Russian mathematician Andrey Markov, Markov chains move between states at discrete time steps (discrete-time Markov chains) or continuously (continuous-time Markov chains). They serve as statistical models of real-world processes in fields such as [[Biology]], [[Economics]] and [[Physics]]. [[Google]]'s PageRank algorithm treats web links as transition probabilities, and hidden Markov models are used in speech recognition. Markov chains also appear in queueing theory in operations research.

ENTRY:
Culturally and often legally recognized union between people called spouses, also called matrimony or wedlock. Marriage establishes rights and obligations between spouses, between them and their children, and between them and their in-laws. It is nearly a cultural universal, but its definition varies across cultures, religions, and time. A marriage ceremony is called a [[Wedding|wedding]]. A marriage may be recognized by a state, an organization, a religious authority, a tribal group, a local community, or peers, and is often viewed as a legal [[Contract|contract]].

The word appeared around 1300, borrowed from Old French *mariage* and ultimately from [[Latin]] *maritātus*, "married". The earliest recorded evidence of marriage ceremonies dates to about 2350 BC in [[Mesopotamia]].

Anthropologists have proposed competing definitions. Edvard Westermarck, in 1936, defined marriage as a relation of one or more men to one or more women recognized by custom or law. Edmund Leach argued in 1955 that no single definition fits all cultures and listed ten rights marriage may establish, including rights over a spouse's labor and property, a joint fund for the children, and a relationship of affinity between the husband and his wife's brothers.

[[Monogamy|Monogamy]], one spouse at a time or in a lifetime, predominates across cultures, though some societies permit polygyny. Studies link marriage with better health outcomes, but debate continues over whether selection effects or causal benefits explain this. Worldwide trends include equal rights for women within marriage and greater tolerance of interethnic, interfaith, transnational, and same-sex couples, which have gained legal recognition in Western jurisdictions since the late 20th century. International norms stress free consent and a minimum age.

Fourth planet from the Sun, a desert-like rocky world known as the Red Planet for its orange-red appearance. Mars retains some water in the ground and thinly in the atmosphere, forming clouds, frost, and polar ice caps, but it has no bodies of liquid surface water.

German priest, theologian, and author, the seminal figure of the Protestant [[Reformation]]. A former Augustinian friar and professor, he was born in Eisleben and ordained to the priesthood in 1507. He came to reject teachings and practices of the Roman [[Catholic Church]], in particular its view of indulgences and papal authority. In 1517 he wrote the [[Ninety-five Theses]], sent to Archbishop Albert of Mainz; tradition holds that he nailed them to the door of the All Saints' Church in Wittenberg, though modern scholarship considers this unlikely. In 1520 Pope Leo X demanded that he renounce his writings, and when he refused, excommunicated him in January 1521. That year Emperor Charles V condemned him as an outlaw at the Diet of Worms, where he refused to recant. Protected by Frederick the Wise, he translated the New Testament into German at Wartburg Castle.

Luther taught that justification is not earned by human acts or merit but received as the free gift of God's grace through faith in Jesus, and that the [[Bible]] is the only source of divinely revealed knowledge. His beliefs form the basis of [[Lutheranism]], though he himself insisted on the names Christian or Evangelical. His German Bible (1534) made scripture accessible to laypeople, fostered a standard German language, and influenced the English Tyndale Bible. His hymns shaped Protestant congregational singing, and his marriage to the former nun Katharina von Bora set a model for clerical marriage.

Luther's writings also include harsh polemics against Anabaptists, Zwingli, the pope, and Jews. He called for the expulsion of Jews and the burning of synagogues, notably in *On the Jews and Their Lies* (1543), after having argued in 1523 against their mistreatment and for their conversion. He did not advocate murdering Jews, but some historians contend that his rhetoric encouraged antisemitism in [[German language|Germany]] and, centuries later, the [[Nazi Party]]. In 1525 he urged princes to suppress the peasants' uprising. His excommunication was still in effect at his death in 1546.
