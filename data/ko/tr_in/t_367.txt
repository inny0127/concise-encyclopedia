ENTRY:
English playwright, poet, and actor, widely regarded as the greatest writer in the English language and the world's pre-eminent dramatist. His extant works, including collaborations, consist of some 39 plays, 154 sonnets, 3 long narrative poems, and a few other verses, some of uncertain authorship. His plays have been translated into every major living language and are performed more often than those of any other playwright.

Shakespeare was born and raised in Stratford-upon-Avon, Warwickshire, the son of John Shakespeare, a glover, and Mary Arden. At 18 he married 26-year-old Anne Hathaway; their children were Susanna and the twins Hamnet and Judith. The years between 1585 and 1592, known as the "lost years," are poorly documented. By 1592 he was part of the [[London]] theater scene as an actor, writer, and part-owner of the Lord Chamberlain's Men, later called the King's Men. Around 1613 he appears to have retired to Stratford.

Most of his known works date from 1589 to 1613. His early plays were primarily comedies and histories. He then wrote mainly tragedies until 1608, among them [[Hamlet]], [[Othello]], [[King Lear]], and [[Macbeth]]. In his last phase he wrote tragicomedies, also called romances, such as *The Winter's Tale* and [[The Tempest]]. His sonnets were published in 1609, and the narrative poems *Venus and Adonis* (1593) and *The Rape of Lucrece* (1594) were his most popular works in his lifetime.

In 1623 fellow actors John Heminges and Henry Condell published the First Folio, a collected edition including 36 plays. Its preface has a poem by [[Ben Jonson]], a rival, who hailed him as "not of an age, but for all time."

ENTRY:
Natural movement of air or other gases relative to a planet's surface. Winds range from thunderstorm flows lasting tens of minutes, through local breezes lasting a few hours, to global winds resulting from the difference in absorption of solar energy between climate zones. The study of wind is called anemology.

Wind is caused by differences in [[Atmospheric pressure|atmospheric pressure]], primarily due to temperature differences; air moves from higher to lower pressure. On a rotating planet it is also deflected by the [[Coriolis force|Coriolis effect]], except exactly on the equator. The two major drivers of large-scale [[Atmospheric circulation|atmospheric circulation]] are differential heating between the equator and the poles and the planet's rotation. Thermal lows over terrain can drive [[Monsoon|monsoon]] circulations, and sea and land breezes define local winds on coasts.

Wind direction is the direction from which it originates, so a westerly wind blows from the west. Weather vanes and windsocks indicate direction, and speed is measured by anemometers, most commonly with rotating cups or propellers. Short bursts are gusts, and strong winds of around one minute are squalls; long-duration winds are named by average strength, such as breeze, gale, storm, and hurricane.

In outer space, the [[Solar wind|solar wind]] is the movement of gases or charged particles from the Sun, and planetary wind is the outgassing of light elements from an atmosphere. The strongest observed winds in the Solar System are on Neptune and Saturn.

Wind shapes landforms through aeolian processes such as erosion and the formation of loess, spreads wildfires, and disperses seeds. Humans have used it for sailing ships, hot air balloons, and as a source of [[Wind power|power]].

Use of wind energy to generate useful work, today mostly electricity. Historically used by sails, windmills, and windpumps, it is now generated almost completely by [[Wind turbine|wind turbines]], generally grouped into wind farms and connected to the [[Electrical grid|electrical grid]].

Wind supplied about 2,700 TWh of electricity in 2025, over 8% of world electricity. About 100 GW was added during 2021, mostly in [[China]] and the United States, and global installed capacity exceeded 800 GW. In 2024, 30 countries generated more than a tenth of their electricity from wind, and generation has nearly tripled since 2015.

The power in moving air is proportional to the cube of wind speed, so available power increases eightfold when speed doubles. A turbine's theoretical efficiency is capped at 59.3% by the Betz limit. Winter occurs when a hemisphere is tilted away from the [[Sun]] because of the [[Axial tilt|tilt of Earth's axis]]. Astronomical event when either pole of [[Earth]] reaches its maximum tilt away from the [[Sun]]. It is the shortest day and longest night of the year in its hemisphere, when the Sun is at its lowest arc in the sky. The date is December 21 or 22 in the Northern Hemisphere and June 20 or 21 in the Southern. Daylight hours then begin to increase again. Since prehistory, many cultures have marked the solstice with festivals and rites. Neolithic sites such as [[Stonehenge]] in [[England]] and Newgrange in [[Ireland]] are aligned with the solstice sunrise or sunset.
