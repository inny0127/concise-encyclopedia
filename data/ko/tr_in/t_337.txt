ENTRY:
1957 Swedish historical film written and directed by [[Ingmar Bergman]]. Set in medieval Sweden during the [[Black Death]], it follows the knight Antonius Block, played by [[Max von Sydow]], who returns from the [[Crusades]] and challenges Death to a game of [[Chess|chess]] to delay his fate. The title refers to the seventh seal in the [[Book of Revelation]], and the silence that follows its opening reflects the film's theme of "the silence of God." Its final "Dance of Death" sequence became one of the most recognizable images in film history. The film won the Special Jury Prize at the 1957 Cannes Film Festival, establishing Bergman's international reputation. It has a 93% approval rating on [[Rotten Tomatoes]], and in 1958 [[The New York Times]] critics voted it one of the year's ten best foreign films.

1980 psychological horror film produced and directed by [[Stanley Kubrick]], co-written with Diane Johnson. Adapted from a 1977 novel by [[Stephen King]], it stars [[Jack Nicholson]] as a recovering alcoholic who becomes winter caretaker of a remote mountain hotel with his wife and clairvoyant son. Released in May 1980 by Warner Bros., it received mixed reviews, and about 27 minutes was cut across its theatrical versions. American animated sitcom created by [[Matt Groening]] for the [[Fox Broadcasting Company]]. Set in the fictional town of Springfield, it satirizes American life through the Simpson family: Homer, Marge, Bart, Lisa and Maggie. The characters began as animated shorts on *The Tracey Ullman Show* in 1987, and the half-hour series premiered on December 17, 1989. It is the longest-running American animated series, sitcom and scripted primetime series, with 816 episodes broadcast. Its early 1990s seasons are generally considered its golden age, and [[Time (magazine)|Time]] named it the 20th century's best television series. Critics have described a decline in quality since then. The show has won 37 Primetime Emmy Awards. The Simpson family received a star on the [[Hollywood Walk of Fame]] in 2000, and Homer's catchphrase "D'oh!" has entered the [[English language]].
