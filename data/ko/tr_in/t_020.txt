ENTRY:
Matter composed of [[Antiparticle|antiparticles]], which have the same mass as their ordinary partners but opposite electric charge. When a particle meets its antiparticle the two annihilate, producing intense gamma rays, neutrinos and other particles, with energy proportional to the total mass involved in accordance with E=mc². Antimatter occurs naturally in [[Cosmic ray|cosmic ray]] collisions and some radioactive decays, and it is essential to applications such as positron emission tomography, [[Radiation therapy|radiation therapy]] and industrial imaging. Antiparticles can form antiatoms, but only a small total amount has been artificially produced, and no macroscopic amount has ever been assembled. The observable [[Universe|universe]] appears composed almost entirely of ordinary matter, an asymmetry that remains one of the great unsolved problems in physics.

Chemical element (symbol Sb, atomic number 51), a lustrous gray [[Metalloid|metalloid]] that occurs mainly as the sulfide mineral stibnite (Sb₂S₃). Antimony compounds have been known since ancient history, when they were powdered for use as cosmetics, known as *kohl*, and as medicine. China is the largest producer, with most output coming from the Xikuangshan Mine in Hunan. Its most common use is in alloys with [[Lead|lead]] and [[Tin|tin]] for solders, bullets and plain bearings, and it improves the rigidity of lead-acid battery plates. Its toxicity is comparable to that of [[Arsenic|arsenic]]. The element makes up about 0.2 parts per million of the Earth's crust.

Particle corresponding to each type of ordinary particle, with the same mass but opposite charges. The antiparticle of the electron is the [[Positron|positron]], while some particles, such as the [[Photon|photon]], are their own antiparticle. A particle and its antiparticle annihilate each other, producing photons. Because charge is conserved, antiparticles are produced by natural processes such as beta decay and cosmic-ray collisions, or by pair production in accelerators such as the [[Large Hadron Collider]] at [[CERN]]. The laws of nature are nearly symmetrical between particles and antiparticles, but the discovery of charge parity violation showed that this symmetry is only approximate, and the question of why matter dominates the universe remains unanswered. Carl D. Anderson discovered the positron in 1932 in cosmic-ray tracks, and Emilio Segrè and Owen Chamberlain found the antiproton in 1955 at the [[University of California, Berkeley|University of California, Berkeley]].

[[Personality disorder]] characterized by a chronic pattern of behavior that disregards the rights and well-being of others. French nobleman and chemist central to the 18th-century chemical revolution. Lavoisier transformed [[Chemistry|chemistry]] from a qualitative into a quantitative science by using more precise measurements than earlier experimenters. He demonstrated the role of [[Oxygen|oxygen]] in [[Combustion|combustion]], opposing the prior phlogiston theory, named oxygen in 1778 while recognizing it as an element, and recognized hydrogen as an element in 1783. He also confirmed that, although matter in a closed system may change its form, its mass remains the same, a principle now known as the law of [[Conservation of mass|conservation of mass]] that led to the balanced reaction equations used today.

Lavoisier wrote the first extensive list of elements, in which he predicted the existence of [[Silicon|silicon]], and helped reform chemical nomenclature (1787). He also helped construct the [[Metric system|metric system]]. His wife and laboratory assistant, Marie-Anne Paulze Lavoisier and worked with him on the metric system.

Born into a wealthy noble family in Paris, he took a law degree but never practiced. He received a provisional appointment to the Academy of Sciences in 1768. He devoted part of his fortune and effort to public causes, including Paris water supply, hospital ventilation, and prison hygiene.

His research was funded by his administrative role in the Ferme générale, a tax-collecting company that was one of the most hated parts of the Ancien Régime. During the [[French Revolution]] he was charged with tax fraud and selling adulterated tobacco, tried with 27 co-defendants, and guillotined despite appeals to spare him. A year and a half later the French government exonerated him.

Impairment of the ability to comprehend or formulate [[Language|language]], caused by dysfunction in specific brain regions. It can also result from [[Brain tumor|brain tumors]], [[Epilepsy]], multiple sclerosis, or neurodegenerative diseases such as [[Dementia]]. Small sap-sucking [[Insect|insects]] of the family Aphididae, order [[Hemiptera]]. Common names include greenfly and blackfly, though color varies widely within a species. A typical life cycle involves flightless females giving live birth to female [[Nymph (biology)|nymphs]] without males, and the nymphs may already be pregnant, an adaptation called telescoping generations. Females breed profusely, and winged females develop later in the season to colonize new plants. In temperate regions a sexual phase occurs in autumn, and the insects often overwinter as eggs. About 5,000 species have been described, around 400 of them on food and fiber crops. Aphids are among the most destructive insect pests of cultivated plants in temperate regions: they weaken plants by sucking sap, act as vectors for plant [[Virus|viruses]], and disfigure ornamentals with honeydew and sooty molds. Insecticide resistance and feeding on leaf undersides make large-scale control difficult.

Family of mostly aromatic [[Flowering plant|flowering plants]], also called Umbelliferae and known as the celery, carrot or parsley family. It is the 16th-largest family of flowering plants, with more than 3,800 species in about 446 [[Genus|genera]]. Most species are annual, biennial or perennial herbs, and their flowers are nearly always aggregated in terminal umbels. Economically important members include [[Anise|anise]], caraway, [[Carrot]], [[Celery]], [[Coriander]], [[Cumin]], dill, fennel, [[Parsley]] and parsnip. Many species are phototoxic, such as giant hogweed and parsnip, and some are deadly, such as poison hemlock and water hemlock. The seeds often contain essential oils that give anise, cumin and coriander their flavor.
