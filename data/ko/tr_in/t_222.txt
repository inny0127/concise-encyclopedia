[[Mid-ocean ridge]] along the floor of the [[Atlantic Ocean]], a divergent tectonic plate boundary. The ridge is part of the longest mountain range in the world. It separates North America from Eurasia and Africa north of the [[Azores]], and separates Africa from South America to the south. It runs from near Greenland to [[Bouvet Island]]. Underwater mountain system formed by [[Plate tectonics|plate tectonic]] spreading. A mid-ocean ridge is where seafloor spreading occurs at a divergent plate boundary. Mantle material rises as [[Magma|magma]] at the linear weakness between separating plates and emerges as [[Lava|lava]], creating new oceanic crust and [[Lithosphere|lithosphere]] on cooling. [[Hydrothermal vent|Hydrothermal vents]] along the ridges support ecosystems that do not depend on sunlight.

Period of European history lasting approximately from the 5th to the late 15th centuries, also called the medieval period. It is the middle of the three traditional divisions of Western history, between [[Classical antiquity]] and the modern period, and is itself subdivided into the Early, High, and Late Middle Ages.

The era began with the [[Fall of the Western Roman Empire]] and migrations of Germanic peoples, who formed new kingdoms. In the 7th century North Africa and the Middle East, once Byzantine, came under the [[Umayyad Caliphate]]. The Franks under the Carolingians briefly built an empire that succumbed to civil wars and invasions by Vikings, Magyars, and Saracens.

During the High Middle Ages, beginning after 1000, population grew as technological and agricultural innovations allowed trade to flourish. Society was organized through manorialism and [[Feudalism]]. The East–West Schism of 1054 divided the Christian church, and the [[Crusades]], first preached in 1095, were attempts by Western Christians to regain the Holy Land from Muslims. Achievements near the period's end include the theology of [[Thomas Aquinas]], the paintings of Giotto, the poetry of Dante and Chaucer, and Gothic cathedrals such as Chartres.

The Late Middle Ages brought famine, plague, and war; between 1347 and 1350 the [[Black Death]] killed about a third of Europeans.

The term first appears in Latin in 1469 as *media tempestas*. [[Petrarch]] regarded the post-Roman centuries as "dark" compared with classical antiquity, a view some historians challenge, citing continuity in legal traditions, technology, and monastic preservation of classical texts. Christoph Cellarius made the ancient–medieval–modern division standard in the 17th century.

ENTRY:
Geopolitical region generally defined as all of Egypt and all of West Asia except the Caucasus. It roughly corresponds to what Western Europe historically called the Near East. It follows political borders rather than continental ones, so it includes East Thrace as part of Turkey. The term has grown prominent since the early 20th century, but its definition is vague and changing; related concepts such as "Middle East and North Africa" and "Greater Middle East" extend it to include most Muslim-majority countries.

Thirteen of its 18 countries belong to the [[Arab world]]. The most populous are Egypt, Iran, and Turkey, and Saudi Arabia is the largest by area. [[Arabs]] are the most widespread ethnic group, followed by Turks, Persians, Kurds, Jews, and Assyrians. Major geographical features include the Arabian Peninsula, [[Anatolia]], the Levant and [[Mesopotamia]] (which together form the [[Fertile Crescent]]), and the Iranian plateau.

Located at the intersection of Africa, Europe, and Asia, the region was the first outside Africa settled by modern humans and the first in which writing systems were developed. All of the core [[Abrahamic religions]], Judaism, Christianity, and Islam, originated there and have their holiest sites there.

Most of the region is hot and arid, especially the Arabian and Egyptian areas. Major rivers, including the Nile, Tigris, Euphrates, and Jordan, support agriculture. The Levantine coast and most of Turkey have a temperate Mediterranean climate. The Gulf Arab states along the [[Persian Gulf]] hold vast reserves of petroleum, which has influenced regional politics. Historians traditionally date the period from the invasion of Europe by the [[Huns]] in about AD 375 to the Lombard conquest of Italy in 568.
