Pressure exerted by circulating blood on the walls of blood vessels. Most of this pressure results from the heart pumping blood through the [[Circulatory system|circulatory system]]. Unqualified, the term refers to pressure in a brachial artery, where it is most commonly measured. It is expressed as systolic pressure over diastolic pressure in millimeters of mercury (mmHg). A normal adult resting reading is under 120/80 mmHg, according to the American Heart Association and American College of Cardiology. Globally, the average has changed little since 1975, at approximately 127/79 mmHg in men and 122/77 mmHg in women. Baroreceptors regulate pressure in the short term by acting through the [[Nervous system|nervous system]] and [[Endocrine system|endocrine system]]. Consistently high pressure, called [[Hypertension|hypertension]], raises the risk of [[Stroke|stroke]], heart disease, and [[Kidney|kidney]] failure.

ENTRY:
Concentration of [[Glucose|glucose]] in the blood, also called glycemia. The body tightly regulates blood glucose as part of metabolic homeostasis. For a 70 kg (154 lb) human, approximately four grams of dissolved glucose is maintained in the [[Blood plasma|blood plasma]] at all times. Glucose not circulating is stored as [[Glycogen|glycogen]] in the [[Liver|liver]] and skeletal muscle. [[Insulin|Insulin]], a hormone produced in the [[Pancreas|pancreas]], primarily regulates the uptake of glucose into cells. The human brain consumes approximately 60% of blood glucose in fasting, sedentary individuals. Abnormally high levels, called hyperglycemia, characterize [[Diabetes|diabetes mellitus]], while low levels are called [[Hypoglycemia|hypoglycemia]].

Board games have been found at archaeological sites in many cultures. As of 2024, 14 sites reported 51 game boards from the [[Neolithic]] period, dating from the early 7000s BC to the mid-6000s BC. He is also popularly regarded as the founder of Shaolin kung fu, an idea popularized in the 20th century.

=== KEY: Body_dysmorphic_disorder_10cb88
HEADWORD: Body dysmorphic disorder = 신체이형장애
LEVEL: 5
GLOSSARY:
  DSM-5 = DSM-5
  Mental disorder = 정신 질환
  Muscle dysmorphia = 근육 이형증
  Obsessive–compulsive disorder = 강박 장애
  dysmorphophobia = 추형 공포증
ENTRY:
Mental disorder characterized by an overwhelming preoccupation with a perceived flaw in one's physical appearance. Also called dysmorphophobia, it is placed within the [[Obsessive–compulsive disorder|obsessive–compulsive]] spectrum by the DSM-5. It affects up to 2–3% of the population, usually beginning in adolescence and affecting men and women at roughly similar rates. Sufferers often check and compare the flaw repetitively and avoid social contact that exposes it. Muscle dysmorphia, in which the body is perceived as too small, affects mostly men. The disorder is commonly underdiagnosed and carries high rates of suicidal thoughts.

=== KEY: Body_language_86cfcf
HEADWORD: Body language = 보디랭귀지
LEVEL: 4
GLOSSARY:
  Nonverbal communication = 비언어적 의사소통
  Sign language = 수어
  kinesics = 동작학
  sign languages = 수어
ENTRY:
Type of [[Nonverbal communication|nonverbal communication]] in which physical behaviors, rather than words, convey information. Such behavior includes facial expressions, body posture, gestures, eye movement, touch, and the use of space. Most of it occurs without conscious awareness, and it often complements verbal communication. Some researchers conclude that nonverbal communication accounts for the majority of information transmitted in interpersonal interactions. Interpretation varies across cultures, although societies generally agree on the meanings of specific behaviors. Whether body language is universal is debated. Unlike [[Sign language|sign languages]], it is not a true language. The study of body language is known as kinesics.
