ENTRY:
Number representing an empty quantity, and the digit that marks an empty place in written numerals. Adding zero to or subtracting zero from any [[Number|number]] leaves it unchanged, so 0 is the additive identity of the [[Integer|integers]] and of the rational, real, and complex numbers. Multiplying any number by 0 gives 0. Division by zero is generally regarded as undefined in arithmetic, because no real number multiplied by 0 produces 1. Any number to the power of 0 equals 1, except that 0⁰ is sometimes regarded as undefined. Zero is the smallest nonnegative integer and the largest nonpositive one; it is even, neither positive nor negative, neither prime nor composite, and is usually shown as the origin of a number line.

As a digit in [[Positional notation|place-value notation]], 0 shows that a position adds nothing to a number's value: in decimal notation, 205 means two hundreds, no tens, and five ones. The same principle holds in bases other than ten, such as binary and hexadecimal. This use derives from Indian mathematics, where the earliest recorded use is dated to the 3rd or 4th century in the Bakhshali manuscript. It was transmitted to Europe by medieval Islamic mathematicians and popularized by [[Fibonacci]], and it was used independently by the [[Maya civilization]].

The English word *zero* came through French and Italian from the Venetian *zevero*, a contraction of *zefiro*, itself borrowed from [[Arabic]] *ṣifr*. That word meant "empty" and came to mean zero when used to translate the [[Sanskrit]] *śūnya*. Some sports have their own words for a zero score, such as "love" in [[Tennis|tennis]] and "duck" in cricket.

ENTRY:
Bright quasar in the constellation Virgo, identified in 1963 as the first quasar. It is the visually brightest quasar in the sky, with an apparent magnitude of 12.9, and lies about 2.4 billion [[Light-year|light-years]] away. Its central [[Supermassive black hole|supermassive black hole]] has a mass of roughly 900 million solar masses. The quasar is a prototype of an [[Active galactic nucleus]], powered by material falling onto that black hole. It lies at the center of a giant elliptical galaxy. Its jet, about 200,000 light-years long, was imaged by the [[Hubble Space Telescope]] and emits synchrotron radiation from relativistic particles. Its luminosity varies at nearly every wavelength.

ENTRY:
Construction of three-dimensional objects from digital models by adding material, usually layer by layer. Material is deposited, joined or solidified under computer control. In the 1980s the techniques were used mainly for prototypes, and by 2019 some processes had become viable for industrial production. Complex shapes, including hollow parts and internal lattices that reduce weight, can be made that would be impractical by hand. Fused deposition modeling (FDM), which extrudes a thermoplastic filament, was the most common process as of 2020.

ENTRY:
Second-largest object in the [[Asteroid belt|asteroid belt]], after [[Ceres (dwarf planet)|Ceres]]. Discovered by Heinrich Wilhelm Matthias Olbers on March 29, 1807, it has a mean diameter of 523 km (325 mi) and makes up about 9% of the belt's mass. It is the brightest asteroid visible from Earth. [[NASA]]'s Dawn spacecraft orbited it from July 2011 to September 2012. Meteorites from its surface, the howardite–eucrite–diogenite (HED) group, have fallen to Earth.

ENTRY:
Anonymous English-language imageboard website founded in October 2003. Created by Christopher "moot" Poole as a counterpart to the Japanese Futaba Channel, it hosts boards on topics from [[Anime|anime]] to video games and politics. Users typically post without registration, and threads are ephemeral. The site had more than 22 million unique monthly visitors as of 2022. It helped popularize [[Internet meme|memes]] such as lolcats and [[Rickrolling]], and it has been influential in hacktivist movements such as Anonymous. Its lax moderation has made it a focus of controversy over harassment and illegal content.
