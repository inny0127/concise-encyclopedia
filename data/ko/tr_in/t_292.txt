ENTRY:
Persian-language Sufi mystic and poet, founder of the Mevlevi Order.

Jalāl al-Dīn Muḥammad Balkhī, known as Rumi, came from a family of Balkh and was born to Persian parents in Wakhsh, in present-day [[Tajikistan]]. His name means "from Rum", the Anatolian region once held by the [[Byzantine Empire]]; in Iran he is also called Mawlānā, "our master". Mongol advances forced the family west, and after stays in cities across Iran, [[Baghdad]], and Damascus, Rumi settled in Konya, in the Seljuk Sultanate of Rum, at age 19.

Rumi was expected to follow his father as an Islamic scholar, but the arrival in Konya of the wandering dervish Shams Tabrīzī changed his life. The two became deeply attached, and Rumi neglected his duties. When Shams disappeared, Rumi's intense grief found expression in the *Divan of Shams Tabrīzī*, which marked the beginning of his poetic output.

His six-volume *Masnavi*, of over 25,000 rhyming couplets, is often called a "Qur'an in Persian" and is considered one of the greatest poems of the [[Persian literature|Persian language]]. Many Muslims rank it among the most important works of Islamic literature, behind only the [[Quran]]. His teachings present adversity, pain, and loss as paths to spiritual growth.

Rumi died in Konya and was buried beside his father, and his shrine became a place of pilgrimage. After his death his followers and his son Sultan Walad founded the Mevlevi Order, known as the Whirling Dervishes for its *Sama* ceremony. His poems have been translated into many languages, and he has been described as the "world's most popular poet" and, in recent years, as the best-selling poet in the United States.

ENTRY:
Hoofed herbivorous grazing or browsing mammal of the suborder Ruminantia. Ruminants digest plant fibers such as [[Cellulose|cellulose]] by fermenting them in a specialized stomach, principally through microbial action, a process called foregut fermentation. The fermented ingesta, known as cud, is regurgitated and chewed again in a process called rumination. The roughly 200 species include [[Cattle|cattle]], goats, sheep, giraffes, deer, gazelles and antelopes. Ruminants are the most diverse group of living [[Ungulate|ungulates]]. The suborder includes six families: Tragulidae, Giraffidae, Antilocapridae, Cervidae, Moschidae and Bovidae. The first fossil ruminants appeared in the Early Eocene. The word comes from Latin rūminārī, "to ruminate," from rūmen, the first stomach.

A miller boasts that his daughter can spin straw into [[Gold|gold]]. An imp spins it for her in exchange for her necklace, her ring and finally her firstborn child. ENTRY:
Letters of related alphabets, known as futharks or runic alphabets, native to the Germanic peoples. Runes primarily represented sounds, though some also stood for the concepts after which they are named. Runology is the academic study of runic alphabets and inscriptions. The earliest secure runic inscriptions date from AD 150 at the latest. Runes were generally replaced by the [[Latin alphabet|Latin alphabet]] as the cultures that used them were Christianized, by about AD 700 in central Europe and 1100 in northern Europe, though they persisted for specialized purposes. The three best-known alphabets are the Elder Futhark (c. AD 150–800), the Anglo-Saxon Futhorc (400–1100) and the Younger Futhark (800–1100). The script ultimately stems from the [[Phoenician alphabet|Phoenician alphabet]].

ENTRY:
Family of explicit and implicit iterative methods in [[Numerical analysis|numerical analysis]] for approximating solutions of [[Differential equation|differential equations]]. Developed around 1900 by the German mathematicians Carl Runge and Wilhelm Kutta. The best-known member, RK4, uses a weighted average of four slope estimates per step; its local truncation error is on the order of O(h⁵), and its total accumulated error is on the order of O(h⁴). If f is independent of y, RK4 is Simpson's rule.
