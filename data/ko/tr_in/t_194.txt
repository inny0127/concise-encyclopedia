Landlocked country in Southeast Asia, officially the Lao People's Democratic Republic (pop., 2025 census: 6,744,701). Its capital and largest city is [[Vientiane|Vientiane]]. ENTRY:
Ancient Chinese philosopher traditionally credited as author of the [[Tao Te Ching]] and a founder of [[Taoism]]. The name, also romanized Lao Tzu, is an honorific meaning "old teacher" or "venerable master," not a personal name. Traditional accounts give his personal name as Li Er, identify him with Lao Dan, and record his courtesy name as Boyang.

Those accounts place him in the 6th century BC state of Chu during China's [[Spring and Autumn period]] (c. 770–c. 481 BC), describe him as a keeper of archival records at the Zhou court, and remember him as an elder contemporary of [[Confucius]]. Disillusioned with social decay, he is said to have departed westward through the Hao Pass, dictating the *Tao Te Ching* to the gatekeeper Yin Xi before vanishing. [[Sima Qian]] used the name Lao Dan in his *Shiji* (c. 91 BC).

Modern scholars remain divided over his identity and dates. In the 1920s and 1930s many Chinese and Western scholars rejected the traditional dating in favor of the 4th or 3rd century BC. Some regard the traditional figure as largely or wholly legendary, while others consider a historical Lao Dan plausible. The Guodian Chu Slips, buried around 300 BC, contain approximately 2,000 characters corresponding to about forty percent of the received text, though scholars caution that they do not by themselves resolve how it was composed.

Laozi was claimed as an ancestor of the [[Tang dynasty]] (618–907) and honored as a progenitor of the surname Li. In religious Taoism he was increasingly deified and identified with Laojun, a divine manifestation of the Tao.

ENTRY:
Second-order partial differential equation, named after [[Pierre-Simon Laplace|Pierre-Simon Laplace]], who first studied its properties. Written ∇²f = 0 or Δf = 0, it states that the [[Laplace operator|Laplace operator]] of a function is zero. Its twice continuously differentiable solutions, called harmonic functions, are important in electrostatics, gravitation and [[Fluid dynamics|fluid dynamics]]. If the right-hand side is a given function, the equation becomes Poisson's equation, which generalizes it. Laplace's equation describes equilibrium, or situations that do not depend on time, and it is the steady-state [[Heat equation|heat equation]]. Its general theory is known as potential theory. The Dirichlet problem fixes boundary values, while the Neumann problem prescribes normal derivatives on the boundary.

Integral transform that converts a function of time into a function of a complex variable s. For suitable functions f it is defined by the integral ℒ{f}(s) = ∫₀^∞ f(t)e^(−st) dt. World's largest and highest-energy [[Particle accelerator|particle accelerator]], built by [[CERN|CERN]] near [[Geneva|Geneva]]. Built between 1998 and 2008 with more than 10,000 scientists, it lies in a tunnel 27 km (17 mi) in circumference beneath the France–Switzerland border. Device that emits coherent light through optical amplification based on the stimulated emission of [[Electromagnetic radiation|electromagnetic radiation]]. The word began as an acronym for light amplification by stimulated emission of radiation; the earlier microwave device was called a maser, and Gordon Gould first published the acronym "LASER" in 1959.

The first laser was built in 1960 by Theodore Maiman at Hughes Research Laboratories, using a synthetic [[Ruby|ruby]] [[Crystal|crystal]] pumped by a flashlamp. It drew on theoretical work by Charles H. Townes and Arthur Leonard Schawlow and the optical amplifier patented by Gould. The principle of stimulated emission was first theorized by [[Albert Einstein]] in 1917.

In a lightbulb or star, photons are emitted at random from many energy levels. In a laser, the release of a [[Photon|photon]] is triggered by the nearby passage of another photon of similar energy, and the emitted photon is identical to the first. Both can trigger further emission in a chain reaction. This requires an active laser medium, a material with metastable states that stay excited for a relatively long time, plus an energy source that continues to "pump" it.

Lasers differ from other sources in their coherence. Spatial coherence lets a beam be focused to a tight spot or stay narrow over great distances, and high temporal coherence permits a very narrow frequency spectrum or ultrashort pulses lasting attoseconds.

Mass extinction event of the [[Devonian]] period, around 372.15 million years ago, also called the Kellwasser event. It is one of the Big Five mass extinctions, though its severity is debated, with likely around 40% of marine species lost. A second extinction, the Hangenberg event, occurred about 13 million years later and ended the Devonian. Hard-hit groups included brachiopods, [[Trilobite|trilobites]] and reef-building organisms. [[Placoderm|Placoderms]] were hit hard by the Kellwasser event and died out in the Hangenberg event. Possible causes include changes in sea level and ocean anoxia, perhaps triggered by global cooling or volcanism, and a [[Comet|comet]] impact has also been suggested. Oceanic anoxia preserved organic matter, which, together with porous reef rock, made Devonian rocks an important source of oil in [[Canada]] and the [[United States]].

Classical language of the Italic branch of the [[Indo-European languages]], originally spoken in Latium (now [[Lazio]]) around [[Rome]]. Through the expansion of the [[Roman Republic]] it became the dominant language of the Italian Peninsula and then of the Roman Empire. Its grammar is highly fusional, with inflections for case, number, person, gender, tense, mood, voice, and aspect.

Scholars distinguish several phases. Old Latin, spoken until 75 BC, survives in inscriptions and the comedies of Plautus and Terence. Classical Latin, from about 75 BC to AD 200, was a conscious creation of orators, poets, and historians. Vulgar Latin denotes informal registers, which Cicero termed *sermo vulgi* ("the speech of the masses"); regional change in these between the 6th and 9th centuries produced the distinct [[Romance languages]]. Late Latin is the written form from the 3rd century AD onward.

Latin is often called a "dead language," but it did not undergo language death as formally defined. After the fall of the Western Roman Empire it remained the common language of international communication, science, and scholarship in Europe into the early 19th century. Medieval Latin was used across Western and Catholic Europe from the 9th century to the [[Renaissance]], which developed a classicizing form, the basis for Neo-Latin. Latin is now generally studied to be read rather than spoken.

Latin contributed many words to English, particularly after the [[Norman Conquest]], and its roots recur in theology, the sciences, medicine, and law. Ecclesiastical Latin remains the official language of the Holy See and the Roman Rite of the [[Catholic Church]].
