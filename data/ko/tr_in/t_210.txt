Adult male human. Before adulthood, a male child or adolescent is called a boy. A man's genome usually inherits an X chromosome from the mother and a Y chromosome from the father, and sex differentiation of the male fetus is governed by the *SRY* gene on the Y chromosome. During [[Puberty|puberty]], hormones that stimulate androgen production produce secondary sexual characteristics, including greater muscle mass, greater height, facial hair growth, and lower body fat composition. Male anatomy is distinguished by the male reproductive system, which includes the testicles, sperm ducts, prostate gland, epididymides, and penis. Men tend to be taller and heavier than women and, adjusted for height, to have greater lean and bone mass and lower fat mass.

The English word derives from Old English *mann*, which primarily meant "person" or "human being" and referred to men, women, and children alike; the distinct word for an adult male was *wer*, which survives today only in compounds such as "werewolf."

Throughout history, traditional gender roles have often defined masculinity and men's activities and opportunities, and many societies have been patriarchal, giving men considerable power, particularly over women. Men are over-represented as both perpetrators and victims of violence, are often directed into professions with high mortality rates, and are most often subject to conscription into military service. Many religious doctrines set rules for men, such as religious circumcision. Critiques of the impact of these roles have prompted movement toward [[Gender equality|gender equality]] and the diverse men's movement. Trans men have a gender identity that does not align with the female sex they were assigned at birth, while intersex men may have sex characteristics that do not fit typical notions of male biology.

Administration of organizations, whether businesses, nonprofit organizations, or government bodies, through business administration, nonprofit management, or public administration respectively. It is the process of managing the resources of businesses, governments, and other organizations.

Larger organizations generally have three hierarchical levels of managers. Senior management, including the board of directors and a chief executive officer (CEO) or president, sets strategic goals and policies. Middle management, including branch, regional, department, and section managers, directs front-line managers and communicates senior management's goals to them. Line management, made up of supervisors and frontline managers or team leaders, oversees regular employees or volunteers and performs the functions traditionally considered the core of management.

The English verb derives from the fifteenth-century French *mesnager*, which referred in equestrian language to holding a horse's reins, and ultimately from the [[Latin]] *manus* (hand) and *agere* (to act). Definitions vary. Henri Fayol stated: "To manage is to forecast and to plan, to organize, to command, to co-ordinate and to control." Peter Drucker saw the basic task of management as twofold: marketing and innovation. Frederick Winslow Taylor's scientific management applied empirical analysis of workflows through time-motion studies to improve efficiency, and it also prompted debate over worker autonomy.

Management is taught as both theory and practice at colleges and universities, with prominent degree programs in management, business administration, and public administration. Social scientists study it as an academic discipline, investigating social organization, organizational adaptation, and organizational leadership. In recent decades there has been a movement for evidence-based management.

Family of two-player count-and-capture [[Board game|board games]] played with seeds, stones, or beans in rows of pits. A turn consists of taking all the seeds from one pit and sowing them one per pit in sequence. A player who ends a turn in an empty pit on their own side captures the opponent's seeds in the pit directly across. The word derives from the Arabic root *naqala*, meaning to move. Its origins are disputed. Claimed boards from Jordan date to about 5,870 BC, a claim others dispute, while excavated boards and rock cuts in Eritrea and Ethiopia date to the 6th and 7th centuries AD. The game reached the Americas through the [[Atlantic slave trade]], and the traditional game Warra was still played in [[Louisiana]] in the early 20th century.

Small, rounded [[Citrus]] fruit, *Citrus reticulata*, usually eaten fresh or in fruit salads. The mandarin is oblate, unlike the roughly spherical sweet orange, which is a mandarin-pomelo hybrid. Its thin, loose peel makes it easier to peel and split into segments than most citrus. Ripe fruit is firm to slightly soft, heavy for its size, and pebbly-skinned. The tree is easily damaged by cold and grows in tropical and subtropical areas. Genetic studies indicate that the wild mandarin was one of the original citrus species and is an ancestor of many hybrids. Most commercial strains derive from hybridization with [[Pomelo|pomelo]], which gives them sweet fruit; the ancestral mandarin was bitter. The wild mandarin evolved in a restricted region of South China and [[Vietnam]].

ENTRY:
Chinese political doctrine of divine legitimacy for rulers, used in ancient and imperial [[China]]. Heaven was held to bestow its mandate on a virtuous ruler, the Son of Heaven, and to withdraw it if the ruler proved unworthy. Famine and flood were read as signs of Heaven's displeasure, and revolts often followed major disasters. The mandate did not require noble birth; dynasties such as the Han and Ming were founded by men of common origin but were seen as having succeeded because they gained it. The doctrine implied a right of rebellion against an unjust ruler. The classical statement of that right, in [[Mencius]], was often edited out of the text. The [[Zhou dynasty]] first used the concept to justify overthrowing the [[Shang dynasty]], and later dynasties, including the [[Qing dynasty]], invoked it.

Small plucked [[String instrument|stringed instrument]] in the [[Lute|lute]] family, usually with eight strings in four courses tuned in perfect fifths, like a [[Violin|violin]] (G3, D4, A4, E5). Mandolins evolved from European lutes, and the Neapolitan mandolin emerged in [[Italy]]. The three main styles are the round-backed Neapolitan, the archtop, and the flat-backed mandolin. [[Topological space]] locally resembling [[Euclidean space]] near each point. An *n*-dimensional manifold has a neighborhood around each point that is homeomorphic to an open subset of *n*-dimensional Euclidean space. ENTRY:
Style in European art, emerging around 1520 and lasting in Italy until about the end of the 16th century. Mannerists reacted against the ideals of the High [[Renaissance]], exaggerating the proportion and balance of [[Leonardo da Vinci]], [[Raphael]] and [[Michelangelo]] to create asymmetrical or unnaturally elegant compositions. The term derives from the Italian *maniera*, meaning style or manner. Its definition remains debated, and some scholars apply it to late 16th- and 17th-century poetry and music. The [[Baroque]] style largely replaced it in Italy, while in Spain, [[El Greco]] adapted it in his late works.
