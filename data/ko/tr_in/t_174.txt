Traditional stories, folktales, and beliefs that emerged in the islands of the Japanese archipelago. [[Shinto]] traditions are its cornerstone, and its pantheon holds uncountable kami, or spirits, tied to the archipelago's topography and to agriculturally based [[Folk religion]]. Contact with Chinese and Indian myths, including [[Buddhism]], also shaped its beliefs. The two oldest surviving sources are [[Kojiki|*Kojiki*]] (712) and *Nihon Shoki* (720). The Edo-period scholar Motoori Norinaga's commentaries on the *Kojiki* are still studied for their influence on the understanding of Japanese myths.

ENTRY:
Marine [[Crab|crab]] species, the largest crab found in waters around Japan. Its scientific name is *Macrocheira kaempferi*. Reaching up to 3.7 m (12.1 ft) from claw to claw, it has the largest leg span of any arthropod, and its carapace can grow to 40 cm (16 in) wide. It is the sole living member of its genus. Mixed writing system combining logographic [[Kanji|kanji]], adopted from [[Chinese characters]], with the syllabaries [[Hiragana|hiragana]] and [[Katakana|katakana]]. Island of Indonesia, one of the Greater Sunda Islands, bordered by the Indian Ocean to the south and the Java Sea to the north. It is the world's most populous island, with about 56% of the Indonesian population on only 7% of its land area. It is the world's 13th largest island. [[Jakarta]], Indonesia's capital, lies on its northwestern coast. Java dominates Indonesia politically, economically, and culturally.

The island was formed by volcanic eruptions caused by the subduction of the Australian Plate under the Sunda Plate. A chain of volcanic mountains forms its east–west spine, and the highest, Mount Semeru, reaches 3,676 m. Mount Merapi is the most active volcano in Java and in Indonesia. The mountains split the interior into relatively isolated regions suited to wet-rice cultivation, and its rice lands are among the richest in the world. Java was the center of powerful Hindu-Buddhist empires and Islamic sultanates, and the core of the colonial [[Dutch East Indies]]. It was also the center of the Indonesian struggle for independence in the 1930s and 1940s. Five of Indonesia's ten UNESCO World Heritage Sites are on Java, among them Borobudur, Prambanan, Ujung Kulon National Park, and Yogyakarta.

Music genre that originated in the African-American communities of New Orleans, Louisiana, in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Its roots lie in [[Blues|blues]], [[Ragtime|ragtime]], European harmony, African rhythmic rituals, spirituals, hymns, marches, vaudeville song, and dance music. It is characterized by swing and blue notes, complex chords, call-and-response vocals, polyrhythms, and improvisation. Since the 1920s Jazz Age it has been recognized as a major form of musical expression in both traditional and popular music.

The origin of the word is much researched. It is believed to be related to *jasm*, a slang term dating to 1860 meaning "pep, energy." The earliest written record, in a 1912 *Los Angeles Times* article, concerns a baseball pitch called a "jazz ball." The American Dialect Society named it the Word of the 20th Century.

Jazz is difficult to define because it spans more than 100 years, from ragtime to rock-infused fusion. [[Duke Ellington]] said, "It's all music."

Jazz did not begin as a single tradition. New Orleans jazz began in the early 1910s, combining brass band marches, French quadrilles, biguine, ragtime, and blues with collective polyphonic improvisation. In the 1930s the prominent styles were swing big bands, Kansas City jazz, and gypsy jazz. Bebop emerged in the 1940s, moving jazz from danceable popular music to a more challenging "musician's music" played at faster tempos. The mid-1950s brought hard bop, and the late 1950s modal jazz and free jazz, which explored playing without regular meter, beat, and formal structures. Jazz fusion, combining improvisation with rock's rhythms and electric instruments, appeared in the late 1960s and early 1970s; its commercial form, smooth jazz, succeeded in the early 1980s.

His *Discourse on Inequality* argued that private property is the source of social inequality, and *The Social Contract* outlines his ideas for a legitimate political order. The sentimental novel *Julie, or the New Heloise* (1761) contributed to preromanticism and [[Romanticism|romanticism]] in fiction. *Émile, or On Education* (1762) is a treatise on the place of the individual in society. His posthumously published *Confessions* and the unfinished *Reveries of the Solitary Walker* gave increased focus to subjectivity and introspection. Born in [[Geneva]], then a city-state, he was proud that his family had voting rights there.
