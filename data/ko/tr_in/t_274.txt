Literary form of poetry written in prose rather than verse. It is written as paragraphs, without line breaks, but uses poetic devices such as fragmentation, compression, repetition, rhyme, metaphor and figures of speech. Because the form has few rules, it is hard to define objectively. In 17th-century Japan, [[Matsuo Bashō]] originated haibun, a form combining [[Haiku]] with prose, best exemplified by his book *Oku no Hosomichi*. In the West, prose poetry arose in early 19th-century France and Germany as a reaction against the traditional verse line. Aloysius Bertrand's *Gaspard de la Nuit* (1842) is widely regarded as the pioneering collection, and [[Charles Baudelaire]]'s *Le Spleen de Paris* (1869) contains 50 prose poems. [[Arthur Rimbaud]] and [[Stéphane Mallarmé]] followed this example.

ENTRY:
Study of suprasegmental features of speech, such as intonation, stress, rhythm and loudness. Prosody occurs simultaneously with individual vowels and consonants, and it reflects a speaker's emotional state, the form of an utterance (statement, question or command), irony, sarcasm, and emphasis or contrast. Auditory measures of prosody include pitch, length, loudness and timbre. Acoustically these correspond to fundamental frequency, duration, intensity and spectral characteristics. English intonation is often analyzed in three aspects: the division of speech into units, the highlighting of particular words and syllables, and the choice of pitch movement, such as a fall or a rise. Prosody displays elements of language that are not encoded by [[Grammar]], punctuation or vocabulary, and prosodic features are found in all languages.

Malignancy of the [[Prostate]] gland, the most common cancer in men excluding skin cancer. Cancer arises in gland cells, most often as adenocarcinoma. Screening uses blood tests for prostate-specific antigen (PSA) and digital rectal examination. Diagnosis requires a [[Biopsy]], and the pathologist assigns a Gleason score, with higher scores indicating more dangerous tumors. Imaging assigns a stage from 1 to 4. Many tumors remain small and are managed with active surveillance, while more dangerous ones are treated with [[Radiation therapy]] or radical prostatectomy. Tumors that metastasize, most often to bones and lymph nodes, have five-year survival rates of 30–40%, while about 99% of men with low-risk tumors survive more than 10 years. The average age at diagnosis is 67.

[[Economic policy]] of restricting imports through tariffs, import quotas and other government regulations. Proponents argue that it shields domestic producers and workers from foreign competition and raises government revenue, while opponents argue that it reduces [[Trade|trade]] and raises the cost of imported goods for consumers. It has been advocated mainly by economic nationalist parties, and economically liberal parties generally support free trade. There is a consensus among economists that protectionism reduces [[Economic growth|economic growth]] and welfare. Large biomolecule consisting of one or more long chains of [[Amino acid|amino acid]] residues. Proteins catalyze metabolic reactions, replicate DNA, respond to stimuli, give structure to cells and organisms, and transport molecules. They differ mainly in their amino acid sequence, which is dictated by the nucleotide sequence of their [[Gene|genes]] and usually causes folding into a specific three-dimensional structure that determines activity. A linear chain of residues joined by peptide bonds is a polypeptide; chains shorter than 20–30 residues are rarely considered proteins and are commonly called [[Peptide|peptides]]. The genetic code specifies 20 standard amino acids, though some organisms add selenocysteine and, in certain archaea, pyrrolysine. During or shortly after synthesis, residues are often chemically modified, which alters folding, stability, activity, and function. Some proteins require a non-protein compound or ion, called a cofactor.

Synthesis, called translation, takes place on [[Ribosome|ribosomes]], where messenger RNA templates direct the assembly of amino acids. Many proteins are [[Enzyme|enzymes]] vital to metabolism. Others are structural, such as actin and myosin in [[Muscle|muscle]] and the scaffolding proteins of the cytoskeleton, or are important in cell signaling, immune responses, cell adhesion, and the cell cycle. Hemoglobin carries oxygen, and [[Insulin|insulin]] regulates metabolism. Proteins exist for minutes or years, averaging 1–2 days in mammalian cells; misfolded ones are degraded faster, often by the proteasome. Animals need dietary protein to obtain the essential amino acids they cannot make.

Proteins were first described by the Dutch chemist Gerardus Johannes Mulder and named by the Swedish chemist [[Jöns Jacob Berzelius]] in 1838, from the Greek *proteios*, "primary." Franz Hofmeister and Hermann Emil Fischer established in 1902 that proteins are chains of amino acids.

Stable subatomic particle (symbol p, H⁺) with a positive electric charge of +1 e. Its mass is slightly less than that of a [[Neutron|neutron]] and approximately 1836 times that of an electron; protons and neutrons, each about one dalton, are jointly called nucleons. One or more protons are present in the nucleus of every atom. Their number, the [[Atomic number|atomic number]] (Z), defines the element and, with the matching number of electrons, determines its chemical characteristics. The nucleus of the most common hydrogen isotope is a lone proton; deuterium and tritium contain one proton bound to one and two neutrons, respectively.
