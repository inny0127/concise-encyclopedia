ENTRY:
Apparatus designed for carrying out executions by beheading. A weighted, angled blade is released from the top of a tall frame and falls onto the neck of the condemned person. The guillotine is best known for its use in France during the [[French Revolution]], where supporters celebrated it as the people's avenger and opponents vilified it as the symbol of the [[Reign of Terror]]. It remained France's standard method of judicial execution until [[Capital punishment|capital punishment]] was abolished in 1981. The last person executed by a government via guillotine was Hamida Djandoubi, a Tunisian murderer, on September 10, 1977, in France.

ENTRY:
Moral emotion that arises when a person believes, accurately or not, that they have compromised their own standards of conduct or violated universal moral standards. Guilt is closely related to remorse, regret and [[Shame|shame]]. Understood as a collection of basic emotions, it is composed of self-directed anger, sadness for the victim, [[Anxiety|anxiety]] and fear of damaging relationships. Although unpleasant to feel, guilt can prompt efforts to restore relationships and make amends. [[Sigmund Freud]] attributed guilt to the [[Conscience|conscience]], or superego, and to a fear of loss of love that begins in childhood, and he rejected the role of God. Martin Buber distinguished Freudian guilt, based on internal conflicts, from existential guilt, based on actual harm done to others. Guilt is an important factor in perpetuating [[Obsessive–compulsive disorder]] symptoms.

Domesticated [[Rodent|rodent]], *Cavia porcellus*, from South America. Domestic guinea pigs typically weigh 700 to 1,200 g (1.5 to 2.6 lb) and measure 20 to 25 cm (8 to 10 in) in length. They are social animals that are skittish in response to noise or danger. The species was domesticated as early as 5000 BC in the [[Andes]], where it was raised for food and is still consumed, and it is also used in folk medicine and community religious ceremonies. European traders introduced guinea pigs to Europe and North America in the 16th century, and their docile nature has made them popular pets. Animal testing on guinea pigs has been carried out since the 17th century, and the animals were widely used as [[Model organism|model organisms]] in the 19th and 20th centuries. They have since been largely replaced in [[Laboratory|laboratory]] testing by mice and rats, though they are still used in research.

Soviet penal labor camp system, operating from the 1930s to the early 1950s. Mixture of [[Sulfur|sulfur]], [[Charcoal|charcoal]], and [[Potassium nitrate|potassium nitrate]] (saltpeter), and the earliest known chemical explosive. It is also called black powder. The sulfur and charcoal act as fuels, while the saltpeter is an oxidizer.

Gunpowder is classified as a low explosive: it deflagrates, burning at subsonic speeds, whereas high explosives detonate and produce a supersonic shockwave. When ignited behind a projectile it generates enough pressure to force the shot from the muzzle at high speed, but usually not enough to rupture the barrel. It has therefore served widely as a propellant in [[Firearm|firearms]], [[Artillery|artillery]], rocketry, and pyrotechnics, and as a blasting agent in quarrying, mining, and the building of pipelines, tunnels, and roads. Its low brisance suits it to blasting slate and monumental stone. Combustion converts less than half of its mass to gas; the residue fouls barrels and corrodes wrought iron and steel, so gunpowder arms need regular cleaning.

The first confirmed reference to what can be considered gunpowder in China dates to the 9th century, during the [[Tang dynasty]], in a formula of six parts sulfur, six parts saltpeter, and one part birthwort herb in the *Taishang Shengzu Jindan Mijue* (808). Daoist alchemists seeking an elixir of life probably discovered it by accident; its Chinese name, *huoyao*, means "fire medicine." It was first used for warfare around AD 904. The earliest chemical formula appears in the *Wujing Zongyao*, a [[Song dynasty]] military text written by Zeng Gongliang between 1040 and 1044. High explosives came into use in the second half of the 19th century, and smokeless powder replaced gunpowder in weapons, while [[Dynamite|dynamite]] and ammonium nitrate/fuel oil replaced it in industry.

Indian empire of the classical period, existing from the mid-3rd to the mid-6th century AD. At its peak the dynasty, founded by a king named Gupta, ruled much of the northern [[Indian subcontinent]].

The dynasty's homeland is uncertain. Eastern [[Uttar Pradesh]], Magadha (Bihar), and Bengal have each been proposed. Early rulers were Gupta, his son Ghatotkacha, and Chandragupta I; the Allahabad Pillar inscription calls the first two Maharaja ("Great King") and Chandragupta I Maharajadhiraja ("King of Great Kings"). The high points of the period came under [[Samudragupta]], [[Chandragupta II]], Kumaragupta I, and Skandagupta.

The era produced scholars including [[Kalidasa]], [[Aryabhata]], Varahamihira, and Vatsyayana, who made significant advances in many fields. The empire declined after its own former feudatories took much of its territory and authority, and after invasions by the Huna peoples (Kidarites and Alchon Huns) from [[Central Asia]]. After its collapse in the 6th century, India was again ruled by numerous regional kingdoms.
