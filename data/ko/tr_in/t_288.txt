Cycle sport discipline held primarily on paved roads. Road racing is the most popular professional form of bicycle racing in terms of competitors, events and spectators. The two most common formats are mass-start races, in which riders race to a set finish, and time trials, in which riders race a course alone against the clock. ENTRY:
Thoroughfare from one place to another, primarily used for movement of traffic, many of them paved. The words road and street are commonly considered interchangeable, but the distinction matters in [[Urban design|urban design]]: streets serve primarily local access, and stroads combine the features of streets and roads. Types include parkways, avenues, [[Controlled-access highway|controlled-access highways]] (freeways, motorways, and expressways), tollways, local roads, public roads, and private roads. Primary features include lanes, sidewalks (pavement), roadways (carriageways), medians, shoulders, verges, bike paths (cycle paths), and shared-use paths.

Definitions vary. The Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development ([[OECD]]) defines a road as a line of communication using a stabilized base other than rails or air strips, open to public traffic, primarily for road motor vehicles; this includes bridges, tunnels, junctions, interchanges, and toll roads but not cycle paths. The 1968 Vienna Convention on Road Traffic defines a road as the entire surface of any way or street open to public traffic. In the United Kingdom, the Highways Act 1980 treats a road as any length of highway or other road to which the public has access, including bridges.

Many early roads were simply recognizable routes. By about 10,000 BC human travelers used rough pathways. The world's oldest known paved road was built in Egypt between 2600 and 2200 BC. The Sweet Track, a timber causeway in England built in winter 3807 BC or spring 3806 BC, is one of the oldest engineered roads discovered. In 500 BC, Darius I the Great began an extensive road system for the [[Achaemenid Empire]], including the Royal Road connecting Sardis to [[Susa]]; the system reached as far east as [[Bactria]] and [[India]].
