Carthaginian explorer of the 5th century BC. Hanno led a naval expedition of 60 ships and 30,000 people along the western coast of Africa, founding cities as it went. His account, the *Periplus*, is one of the few surviving accounts of ancient exploration written by its leader, though only Greek abridgements survive of the lost Punic original. The account describes meeting the Lixitae, passing fires and a very tall mountain, and killing three [[Gorilla|gorillas]], whose skins were brought back to Carthage. Historians debate the route: some analyses suggest it reached as far south as [[Gabon]], while others argue it could not have sailed much beyond southern [[Morocco]]. Some historians question the account's accuracy because of its hyperbolic distances and dramatic elements.

German-American physicist. Bethe made major contributions to [[Nuclear physics|nuclear physics]], astrophysics, [[Quantum electrodynamics|quantum electrodynamics]] and solid-state physics. Positive mental state ranging from contentment to intense joy, closely linked to well-being and life satisfaction.

There is no single accepted definition, and usage differs by culture and context. The word is used mainly in two ways: for the current experience of an emotion such as pleasure or joy (Daniel Kahneman: "what I experience here and now"), and for an appraisal of life satisfaction (Ruut Veenhoven: "overall appreciation of one's life as-a-whole"). Physical system in which a restoring force is proportional to displacement from equilibrium. Harmonic oscillators occur widely in nature, and any mass subject to a force in stable equilibrium acts as one for small vibrations. They are exploited in devices such as [[Clock|clocks]]. If the restoring force is the only force acting, the system undergoes simple harmonic motion, with sinusoidal oscillations of constant amplitude and a frequency independent of amplitude. Friction, or damping, can make the motion underdamped, critically damped or overdamped. In quantum mechanics, the [[Quantum harmonic oscillator|quantum harmonic oscillator]] has discrete energy levels, and its [[Potential energy|potential energy]] at displacement x is U = ½kx².

Stringed instrument whose strings run at an angle to its soundboard and are plucked with the fingers. Harps are usually wooden and triangular, and some have multiple rows of strings and pedals. The earliest harps and lyres were found in [[Sumer]], c. 2500 BCE, with harps excavated from royal tombs in [[Ur]]. Angular harps appeared in the [[Iraq]]–[[Iran]] region around 1900 BCE, and harps later spread across Europe and the Americas, where they remain part of [[Folk music|folk music]]. Historically, strings were made of sinew from [[Tendon|tendons]], gut, plant fiber or silk, and later of nylon and wire. The modern double-action pedal harp, developed in the early 19th century by Sébastien Érard, has 47 strings.
