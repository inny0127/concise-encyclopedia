Form of government that completely controls public and private life, prohibits opposing political parties and suppresses opposition to the state. A totalitarian government uses an ideology to control the economy, education, the arts and sciences, and the private morality of its citizens. The difference from [[Authoritarianism|authoritarian]] rule is one of degree: totalitarianism features a charismatic dictator and a fixed worldview. The word was first used in the early 1920s to describe Italy's Fascist government. Some historians have challenged the concept, arguing that total control was never achieved and that Nazism and Stalinism differed substantially. In the 21st century the term has also been applied to [[Islamism|Islamist]] movements, a use that is also contested.

The family includes five genera and over 40 species, including aracaris and toucanets, and its closest relatives are the toucan-barbets. Toucans are arboreal and usually found in pairs or small flocks. They nest in tree hollows, often excavated by [[Woodpecker|woodpeckers]], and typically lay two to four white eggs. ENTRY:
[[Travel]] for pleasure, and the commercial activity of providing and supporting such travel. UN Tourism defines it more generally as people "staying in places outside their usual environment for not more than one consecutive year for leisure and not less than 24 hours, business and other purposes." The English words *tourist* (1772) and *tourism* (1811) derive from *tour*, from Old French *torner*, from Latin *tornare*, "to turn on a lathe."

Definitions have shifted over time. In 1936 the [[League of Nations]] defined a foreign tourist as someone traveling abroad for at least 24 hours, and the [[United Nations]] amended this in 1945 to include a maximum stay of six months. In 1994 the UN identified three forms: domestic tourism, by residents within their own country; inbound tourism, by non-residents entering a country; and outbound tourism, by residents traveling to another country. The terms tourism and tourist are sometimes used pejoratively to imply a shallow interest in the places visited, whereas traveler is often a mark of distinction.

International tourism receipts grew to US$1.03 trillion in 2005, and international tourist arrivals passed 1 billion for the first time in 2012. Numbers declined between the second half of 2008 and the end of 2009 because of the [[Great Recession]] and the 2009 H1N1 influenza outbreak, then recovered until the [[COVID-19 pandemic|COVID-19 pandemic]] ended the growth. The World Tourism Organization estimated that international arrivals might have fallen by 58% to 78% in 2020, a potential loss of US$0.9–1.2 trillion in receipts. Global tourism accounts for approximately 8% of greenhouse-gas emissions, and many organizations are shifting toward [[Sustainability|sustainable]] tourism to balance economic benefits with environmental and social responsibility.

Exchange of goods, services, or capital between parties, agreed in advance and made in return for something of value.

Trade involves the transfer of goods and services from a seller to a buyer. In one modern view, it exists because of specialization and the division of labor: individuals and groups concentrate on a small aspect of production and exchange their output for other needs. Trade between regions arises because regions may hold a comparative advantage in producing certain goods, and trading at market price can benefit both. The spice trade and the grain trade have both been important in the development of a global economy. [[Retail|Retail]] trade is the sale of goods from a fixed location, online, or by mail, in small lots for direct consumption.

Prehistoric peoples exchanged goods in a gift economy before the innovation of [[Currency|currency]]. Early humans built trade networks for [[Obsidian|obsidian]] 15,000 years ago and for ostrich egg shell beads 50,000 years ago. Until the fall of the [[Roman Empire|Roman Empire]] in the 5th century, a lucrative trade brought spice to Europe from India and China, and Rome imported grain from Sicily and Egypt. Afterward trade in Western Europe nearly collapsed, but it continued to flourish in Africa, the Middle East, India, China, and Southeast Asia. The first true maritime trade network in the Indian Ocean was built by the [[Austronesian peoples|Austronesian peoples]] of Island Southeast Asia; its Maritime Jade Road carried jade mined in Taiwan and processed mostly in the Philippines.

Openness to free trade increased substantially from 1815 until 1914, collapsed in Europe and North America during the [[Great Depression|Great Depression]] of the 1930s, and rose again from the 1950s onward. Economists and economic historians contend that current levels of openness are the highest ever. Supporters of free trade argue that it raises growth and productivity and lowers consumer prices. Controversy persists over tariffs: critics say they distort resource allocation and raise costs for consumers, while proponents appeal to the protection of particular industries.

Organization of workers formed to maintain or improve the conditions of their employment. Also called a labor or labor union, it seeks better wages and benefits, improved working conditions and safety standards, complaint procedures, and greater bargaining power for workers.

System of beliefs or behaviors passed down within a group or society, with symbolic meaning or special significance and origins in the past. A component of cultural expressions and [[Folklore|folklore]], traditions include [[Holiday|holidays]], socially meaningful but impractical clothing such as lawyers' wigs or military officers' spurs, and social behaviors such as [[Greeting|greetings]].

The word derives from the [[Latin|Latin]] *tradere*, "to transmit, to hand over, to give for safekeeping"; the related *traditio* was used in Roman law for legal transfers and [[Inheritance|inheritance]]. According to Anthony Giddens and others, the modern meaning developed during the Enlightenment, in opposition to modernity and progress. The unifying idea is that customs or beliefs originating in the past are taught from one generation to the next and are still practiced in the present. Traditions are often presumed ancient and unalterable, yet many were invented deliberately, for political or cultural reasons, over short periods.

Before writing, traditions were passed on orally, aided by devices such as rhyme, epic stories, and alliteration; the stories so preserved are called [[Oral tradition|oral tradition]]. Industrialization, globalization, and the assimilation or marginalization of cultural groups can erode them, and preservation initiatives, for example for traditional languages, now exist in many countries.

Views of tradition differ. Edmund Burke regarded it as an inheritance embodying the distilled wisdom of earlier generations, warning that abandoning it for abstract rationalism risks social disintegration. Modernist critics portray it as a barrier to innovation and rational progress.
