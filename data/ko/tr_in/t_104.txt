ENTRY:
Method for calculating the relative skill levels of players, originally devised for rating chess players. Self-conscious [[Emotion|emotion]] of mild to severe discomfort, usually involving a perceived loss of [[Dignity|dignity]]. It is typically felt when someone commits, or thinks of committing, a socially unacceptable act that is witnessed by or revealed to others. Frequently grouped with [[Shame|shame]] and [[Guilt (emotion)|guilt]], embarrassment can have a profoundly negative effect on thoughts and behavior. Causes include being caught in a lie, unwanted attention to private matters, and being made the focus of attention. Embarrassment is usually accompanied by blushing, sweating, nervousness and fidgeting, and may bring feelings of [[Anger|anger]]. The idea that embarrassment serves an appeasing function originated with Goffman, who argued that the embarrassed person shows he or she is disturbed by the act. Adolescent boys who displayed more embarrassment were less likely to engage in aggressive or delinquent behavior.

Complex physical and mental state arising from neurophysiological and neuropsychological changes and variously associated with subjective experience, behavioral response, and a degree of pleasure or displeasure. There is no scientific consensus on a definition. Emotions are often intertwined with mood, temperament, personality, disposition, and creativity.

Emotions are generally treated as multicomponential, involving subjective experience, cognitive processes, expressive behavior, psychophysiological changes, and instrumental behavior. At one time academics tried to identify emotion with a single component: [[William James]] with subjective experience, behaviorists with instrumental behavior, psychophysiologists with physiological changes. Later work has described emotion as consisting of all the components. The sociologist Peggy Thoits described emotions as involving physiological components, cultural or emotional labels (anger, surprise), expressive body actions, and the appraisal of situations. In Scherer's Component Process Model, five crucial elements must become coordinated and synchronized for a short period, driven by appraisal. Cognitive processes such as reasoning are often regarded as separate from emotional ones, a division between "thinking" and "feeling", though not all theories accept it. Emotions may be occurrences (panic) or dispositions (hostility), and short-lived (anger) or long-lived (grief).

Major theories differ on how the components relate. The James-Lange theory holds that physiological arousal precedes and causes the emotional experience; the Cannon-Bard theory holds that experience and physiological response occur simultaneously; the Schachter-Singer two-factor theory adds cognitive labeling of arousal. Appraisal theories, such as those of Richard Lazarus, stress ongoing evaluation of an event's relevance, while constructionist theories treat emotions as predictions assembled from core affect and conceptual knowledge. Paul Ekman identified six basic emotions (anger, disgust, fear, happiness, sadness, surprise) as universal on the basis of consistent facial expressions, a view contested by constructionists who emphasize cultural variability. Some cross-cultural studies indicate that categories such as "anger" and "sadness" are not universal; others argue for universal bases.

The word was coined in 1543 by the French humanist Étienne Dolet, in his translation of Cicero's *Tusculanae Disputationes*, and entered English in 1579. In the [[Age of Enlightenment]], [[David Hume]] argued that actions are motivated by "fears, desires, and passions". Theorizing about the evolutionary origin of emotion dates to [[Charles Darwin]]. Current research uses positron emission tomography and functional magnetic resonance imaging to study emotion in the brain, and clinical research examines the dynamics of emotions in daily life. An inability to express or perceive emotion is sometimes called alexithymia.

ENTRY:
Founder and first emperor of the [[Han dynasty]] (r. 202–195 BC). Personal name Liu Bang. Born into a peasant family, he entered the [[Qin dynasty]] bureaucracy as a minor law enforcement officer, then became a rebel leader after the death of [[Qin Shi Huang]]. He forced the surrender of the Qin ruler Ziying in 206 BC. After Xiang Yu divided the former Qin Empire, Liu was forced to accept the poor, remote region of Bashu, present-day Sichuan and Chongqing; he later won the Battle of Gaixia in 202 BC and established the Han dynasty. He reduced taxes and corvée labor, promoted [[Confucianism]], and suppressed revolts by vassal rulers outside his clan. He initiated *heqin*, a system of [[Arranged marriage|arranged marriages]] with the [[Xiongnu]] to keep peace after the Han defeat at the Battle of Baideng in 200 BC.

ENTRY:
Emperor of Japan (r. 1867–1912). Personal name Mutsuhito. His reign saw Japan transform from a feudal state under the [[Tokugawa shogunate]] into a major imperial power. His accession triggered the [[Boshin War]], in which [[Samurai|samurai]] defeated the shogunate and restored power in his name. The [[Meiji Restoration]] proclaimed the [[Empire of Japan]] in 1868, beginning the Meiji era. Documents of his reign include the Meiji Constitution of 1889 and the Imperial Rescript on Education of 1890. Military victories over China in the [[First Sino-Japanese War]] and over Russia in the [[Russo-Japanese War]] followed, and Taiwan and Korea were annexed in 1895 and 1910, respectively.

ENTRY:
Second emperor of the [[Tang dynasty]] of China (r. 626–649). Personal name Li Shimin. He is traditionally regarded as a co-founder of the dynasty for encouraging his father to rebel against the [[Sui dynasty]] in 617. His Reign of Zhenguan is considered a golden age, and his reign became the model by which later emperors were measured. He asked officials to be loyal to policies rather than people, to eliminate [[Corruption|corruption]]. In 630 his general Li Jing defeated the Eastern Turks, and Taizong took the title Khan of Heaven. Tang armies annexed Karakhoja in 640, Karasahr in 644 and Kucha in 648. The empire covered most territory once held by the [[Han dynasty]], as well as parts of modern-day Korea, Vietnam, Russia, Mongolia, Xinjiang and Central Asia.

Founding emperor of the [[Song dynasty]] of China (r. 960–976). Personal name Zhao Kuangyin. A distinguished general of the Later Zhou dynasty, he came to power after staging a [[Coup d'état|coup]] and forcing the last Later Zhou ruler to abdicate. He conquered Southern Tang, Later Shu, Southern Han and Jingnan, reunifying most of China proper. To strengthen his control he reduced the power of military generals and relied on civilian officials. He was succeeded by his younger brother, Zhao Kuangyi (Emperor Taizong).
