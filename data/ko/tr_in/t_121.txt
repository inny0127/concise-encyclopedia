Best known French poet of the late [[Middle Ages]], whose work draws on his own brushes with the law. Country primarily in Western Europe (pop., 2026: over 69.1 million), officially the French Republic. Its overseas regions and territories include French Guiana in South America, Saint Pierre and Miquelon in the North Atlantic, and many islands in Oceania and the Indian Ocean. Its 18 integral regions, five of them overseas, cover 632,702 km². [[Paris]], the largest city, is the main cultural and economic center, with a metropolitan population of over 13 million.

Celtic tribes known as [[Gaul|Gauls]] settled the territory in the Iron Age, and Rome annexed it in 51 BC. The [[Franks]] formed the kingdom of Francia, heartland of the Carolingian Empire; the Treaty of Verdun of 843 partitioned that empire, and West Francia evolved into the Kingdom of France. The kingdom fought England in the [[Hundred Years' War]] from the mid-14th to the mid-15th century and, under [[Louis XIV]], built Europe's strongest army. The [[French Revolution]] of 1789 overthrew the ancien régime. [[Napoleon]] Bonaparte established the First French Empire, bringing France to its political and military zenith in the early 19th century. The Second Empire collapsed in the Franco-Prussian War in 1870. France emerged victorious from World War I at great human and economic cost, was occupied by Germany in 1940, and was liberated in 1944. The Fourth Republic dissolved over the course of the Algerian War, and the Fifth Republic, a semi-presidential system, was formed in 1958 by [[Charles de Gaulle]]. Algeria and most French colonies became independent in the 1960s.

France has 54 UNESCO World Heritage Sites, the fourth-largest number, and received 58 million foreign visitors in 2025. A developed country, it is a permanent member of the UN Security Council and an official nuclear-weapon state.
