ENTRY:
Tough band of dense fibrous [[Connective tissue|connective tissue]], also called a sinew, that connects muscle to [[Bone|bone]]. It transmits the mechanical forces of muscle contraction to the skeleton while withstanding tension. Tendons, like [[Ligament|ligaments]], are made of collagen, but ligaments connect bone to bone while tendons connect muscle to bone. The adult human body has about 4,000 tendons. Collagen makes up 60–85% of a normal tendon's dry mass, mostly type I, and its fibers run parallel and are grouped into fascicles. Tendon cells, called tenocytes, synthesize the extracellular matrix that surrounds them. The whole tendon is enclosed by a fascia, and the space between it and the tendon tissue is filled with the paratenon, a fatty loose connective tissue.
