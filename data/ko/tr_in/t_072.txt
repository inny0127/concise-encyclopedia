Practice of extending and maintaining political, social, economic, and cultural domination over a territory and its people by another people, in pursuit of interests defined in an often distant metropole. Colonizers differentiate themselves from the colonized and typically organize the territory into colonies separate from the metropole rather than annexing it. In settler colonialism, settlers seek to partly or completely supplant the existing [[Indigenous peoples]]. The term is often used as a synonym for imperialism, though scholars distinguish them.

Analogous practices occurred outside Europe, including Inca forced resettlements, Arab expansion from the 7th century, and Japan's administration of Taiwan from 1895 and Korea from 1910. The concept, however, was developed mainly to describe the European empires of the modern era, which spread from the 15th century to the mid-20th century, covering 35% of Earth's land by 1800 and peaking at 84% by the beginning of [[World War I]]. Expansion was driven by mercantile interests, strategic rivalry, and missionary zeal. It was in some cases justified by a civilizing mission, often rooted in belief in a Christian mission.

Assessments of its effects differ. Documented consequences include widespread violence, genocides, the enslavement of an estimated 12.5 million Africans in the transatlantic trade, and the disruption of Indigenous societies. Scholars have shown that variations in colonial institutions can account for variations in economic development and state capacity. Some analyses credit colonial railways, ports, and public health measures with improving institutions in some regions, while critics stress persistent inequality and resource dependence.

[[Decolonization]] proceeded in waves, with a large wave between 1945 and 1975. Some academics use the term neocolonialism for the continuation of colonial rule through indirect means.

ENTRY:
Visual perception produced by the activation of the different types of cone cells in the eye by light. Color is not an inherent property of matter, but perception is related to an object's light absorption, emission, reflection, and transmission. Most humans have three types of cone cells (trichromacy); other animals may have a different number of cone types or sensitivity to other wavelengths, as bees do to [[Ultraviolet|ultraviolet]]. The brain processes the signals into sensations of hue, colorfulness, and lightness.

The [[Visible spectrum|visible spectrum]] extends approximately from 390 nm to 700 nm. Most light sources emit many wavelengths, and many more spectral combinations exist than color sensations; spectra that give the same sensation are called metamers. Isaac Newton named the colors of the spectrum in 1671. How the continuous spectrum is divided into named colors is a matter of culture and historical contingency; the ROYGBIV mnemonic is disputed, particularly over indigo and cyan.

The physical color of an object depends on how it absorbs and scatters light. Transparent objects transmit almost all light and are perceived as colorless; opaque objects absorb or reflect it; translucent objects appear colored because they scatter or absorb certain wavelengths internally.

Colors mix additively (light) or subtractively (pigments). Color spaces, abstracted as mathematical color models, assign numbers to colors and are essential to reproduction in print, photography, monitors, and television; well-known models include RGB, CMYK, HSL/HSV, CIE Lab, and YCbCr/YUV. In art, color theory governs the harmonious use of color.

Thomas Young proposed his trichromatic theory in 1801, and Johann Wolfgang von Goethe published his *Theory of Colors* in 1810.

Prolonged state of deep unconsciousness with no response to pain, light or sound. A patient in a coma lacks a sleep–wake cycle, cannot initiate voluntary actions and does not respond normally to stimuli. Clinically, a coma is the consistent inability to follow a one-step command. Consciousness depends on the [[Cerebral cortex|cerebral cortex]] and the reticular activating system in the [[Brainstem|brainstem]]. Causes include trauma, [[Stroke|stroke]], metabolic disorders such as [[Hypoglycemia|hypoglycemia]], infections and drug overdoses; comas can also be medically induced, as during general anesthesia. Complications include [[Pneumonia|pneumonia]] and blood clots, and prolonged coma beyond weeks raises the risk of death, [[Vegetative state|persistent vegetative state]] or long-term disability.

Selection of distinct items from a set, in which order does not matter. Unlike a [[Permutation|permutation]], a combination is a subset of k distinct elements. For example, an apple, an orange and a pear give three combinations of two fruits: an apple and a pear, an apple and an orange, or a pear and an orange. The number of k-combinations of an n-element set is the [[Binomial coefficient|binomial coefficient]], written C(n,k) = n!/(k!(n−k)!). Combinations underpin the [[Binomial theorem|binomial theorem]]. In probability, they count outcomes, such as the 3,744 possible full house hands in five-card poker from a standard deck.

Agricultural machine that harvests grain crops such as [[Wheat|wheat]], [[Rice|rice]] and [[Maize|maize]]. Its name reflects how it combined reaping, threshing and winnowing into one process around the start of the 20th century. It is among the most economically important labor-saving inventions, greatly reducing the share of the population employed in agriculture.
