Common name for plant species in the genus *Dioscorea* that form edible tubers, though some species in the genus are toxic. Yams are perennial herbaceous vines native to Africa, Asia and the Americas, and they are cultivated in many temperate and tropical regions. About 10 species have been domesticated, and the tubers serve as a primary source of carbohydrates in tropical diets. Africa accounts for more than 97 percent of world output, which reached 88 million tonnes in 2022, and [[Nigeria]] produces about 70 percent. Nigeria's New Yam Festival celebrates the harvest.

Late Copper Age to early Bronze Age archaeological culture of the Pontic–Caspian steppe, dating to 3300–2600 BC. River, China; the longest in Asia and third-longest in the world. Also called the Yangzi or Chang Jiang, it rises at Jari Hill in the Tanggula Mountains of the [[Tibetan Plateau]] and flows 6,300 km (3,900 mi) generally eastward to the [[East China Sea]] near [[Shanghai]]. It is the fifth-largest river in the world by discharge volume. Its drainage basin, about 1.8 million sq km, covers one-fifth of China's land area and is home to nearly one-third of the country's population.

The river has long served for water, irrigation, transportation, industry, and war. The Yangtze Delta generates as much as 20% of China's GDP, and the basin includes cities such as [[Wuhan]] and [[Nanjing]]. The [[Three Gorges Dam]] is the largest hydroelectric power station in the world. Supporters credit it with flood control; its construction displaced over 1.3 million people and has been linked to ecological harm.

The Chinese name Cháng Jiāng means Long River. In Old Chinese the river was simply Jiang; by the [[Han dynasty]] that word meant any river, and this one was called the Great River. The English name derives from Yángzǐ Jiāng, the local name of the lower river near Yangzhou, which Europeans extended to the whole river.

The river crosses many ecosystems and shelters endemic and threatened species, including the Chinese alligator and the narrow-ridged finless porpoise. The Yangtze river dolphin (baiji) and Chinese paddlefish are extinct, and the Yangtze sturgeon is extinct in the wild. Pollution, siltation, and loss of wetlands and lakes have degraded the river, and fishing bans have been introduced to restore depleted stocks. A stretch of the upper river in western Yunnan lies within the Three Parallel Rivers of Yunnan Protected Areas, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

River system in [[Siberia]], the fifth-longest in the world and the largest draining into the [[Arctic Ocean]]. Beginning in [[Mongolia]], it follows a northerly course through the Krasnoyarsk Dam before draining into the Yenisei Gulf in the [[Kara Sea]]. Its basin includes [[Lake Baikal]], the world's deepest lake. The river divides the Western Siberian Plain from the Central Siberian Plateau and drains a large part of central Siberia. Gram-negative, non-motile bacterium that causes [[Plague (disease)|plague]]. Its main route to humans is the bite of the oriental rat flea, *Xenopsylla cheopis*, and it also infects rodents and other mammals. Alexandre Yersin identified it in 1894, during a plague epidemic in Hong Kong. ENTRY:
Large, hairy, mythical humanoid said to inhabit the [[Himalayas]]. Also called the Abominable Snowman in Western popular culture, it is often compared with [[Bigfoot]] of North America. The name derives from Tibetan words for rocky place and bear. Folklorists trace the legend to Sherpa folklore and to misidentified animals such as [[Bear|bears]] and [[Yak|yaks]].
