ENTRY:
Algebraic operation on two equal-length sequences of numbers, usually coordinate vectors. The dot product is the sum of the products of corresponding entries; for the vectors [1, 3, −5] and [4, −2, −1] it equals 3. Geometrically, it is the product of the two vectors' lengths and the cosine of the angle between them, and the two definitions agree in [[Cartesian coordinate system|Cartesian coordinates]]. In modern [[Euclidean geometry]], lengths and angles are defined through it: a vector's length is the square root of its dot product with itself. The name derives from the dot operator (⋅) used to write it. The alternative name scalar product stresses that the result is a scalar rather than a vector, as with the [[Cross product|vector product]] in [[Three-dimensional space]].

Dravidian is first attested in the 2nd century BCE in Tamil-Brahmi inscriptions on cave walls in the [[Madurai]] and Tirunelveli districts of [[Tamil Nadu]]. In 1816 Francis Whyte Ellis argued that these languages descended from a common non-[[Indo-European languages|Indo-European]] ancestor. In 1856 Robert Caldwell coined the term Dravidian for the family. The family has not been shown to be related to any other.

Visual art using an instrument to mark [[Paper|paper]] or another two-dimensional surface, or a digital representation of one. Traditional instruments include pencils, crayons, Conté crayons, and ink pens, sometimes combined; modern tools include styluses, mice, and graphics tablets. Cardboard, vellum, wood, plastic, leather, canvas, and board have also served as supports. Drawing concerns chiefly lines and areas of tone, and in Western terminology it is distinct from [[Painting|painting]], although similar media are used in both. A quick freehand drawing not intended as a finished work is called a sketch; other categories include figure drawing, [[Cartoon|cartoon]], caricature, and doodle, and methods include line drawing, stippling, shading, and tracing. Outside art, plans of buildings, machinery, and circuitry are also called drawings, and drafters work in [[Technical drawing|technical drawing]].

Drawing is one of the oldest forms of human expression and is believed to have preceded written communication; [[Cave painting|cave paintings]] of quadrupeds in southern France and Spain date to around 30,000 years ago. Before paper was widely available in Europe, monks used drawings as underdrawings for illuminated manuscripts on vellum or parchment, or as final images. In 1609 [[Galileo Galilei]] explained the phases of Venus and sunspots through his telescopic drawings, and in 1924 [[Alfred Wegener]] used illustrations to show the origin of the continents. After paper became widely available in the 14th century, drawing increased in the arts as a tool for study and preparation. The [[Renaissance]] brought greater sophistication and more realistic representation. In the 17th century the "Quarrel of Color" pitted proponents of color against those of drawing, a distinction between contour lines and colored surface that Leonardo da Vinci had made.

Early 20th-century [[Battleship|battleship]] type. It takes its name from HMS Dreadnought, launched by the [[Royal Navy]] in 1906 with an all-big-gun armament and [[Steam turbine|steam turbine]] propulsion. Its launch renewed the naval arms race between the United Kingdom and Germany, and older battleships became known as pre-dreadnoughts. Most original dreadnoughts were scrapped after [[World War I]] under the Washington Naval Treaty. The only battle between large dreadnought fleets, at [[Battle of Jutland|Jutland]] in 1916, ended without a decisive result.

Succession of images, scenes, ideas, emotions, and sensations that usually occur involuntarily in the mind during certain stages of [[Sleep|sleep]]. Humans generally spend more than two hours dreaming per night, and each dream lasts around 5–20 minutes. Dreams occur mainly in the rapid-eye movement (REM) stage, when brain activity is high and resembles that of waking, but humans also dream during non-REM sleep, and not all REM awakenings produce dream reports. Because a dream must be reduced to a verbal report to be studied, dreaming by non-humans, human fetuses, and pre-verbal infants is currently unprovable. It is not known where in the brain dreams originate or what their purpose is. The scientific study of dreams is called oneirology; most modern study focuses on neurophysiology and on testing hypotheses about function.

Dream interpretation was practiced by the [[Babylonia|Babylonians]] in the third millennium BC and earlier by the [[Sumer|Sumerians]], and it figures in religious texts of several traditions and in [[Psychotherapy|psychotherapy]]. Writings from [[Mesopotamia]] and [[Ancient Egypt]] stress visitation dreams in which a deity or forebear commands the dreamer to act; Gudea of Lagash rebuilt a temple as the result of such a dream. Between the 1940s and 1985, Calvin S. Hall collected more than 50,000 dream reports; the most common emotion was [[Anxiety|anxiety]], negative emotions were much more common than positive ones, and sexual content appeared in no more than 10% of dreams. A study of more than 15,000 dream reports found fear, illness, and death themes two to four times more prevalent after the onset of the [[COVID-19 pandemic]]. People blind from birth do not have visual dreams. In Old English, *drēam* meant "noise", "joy", or "music"; the sense of a sleep experience dates from the 13th century.
