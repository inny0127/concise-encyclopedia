ENTRY:
Forest characterized by a closed, continuous tree canopy, moisture-dependent vegetation, epiphytes and lianas, and the absence of wildfire. Rainforests are generally classified as [[Tropical rainforest|tropical]] or [[Temperate rainforest|temperate]], though other types have been described. Estimates vary from 40% to 75% of all biotic species being indigenous to rainforests. Tropical rainforests have been called the "jewels of the Earth" and the "world's largest pharmacy," because over one quarter of natural medicines have been discovered there. They are typically found within 10 degrees of the [[Equator|equator]] and receive average annual rainfall of no less than 168 cm (66 in). Although they have been called the "Earth's lungs," rainforests contribute little net oxygen to the atmosphere through [[Photosynthesis|photosynthesis]]. Rainforests are disappearing rapidly because of [[Deforestation|deforestation]], habitat loss and pollution of the atmosphere.

Queen regnant of [[Madagascar]] (r. 1828–1861). Ranavalona I took the throne after the death of her husband, Radama I, and pursued isolationism and self-sufficiency. European contemporaries called her a tyrant and the "Mad Monarch of Madagascar", a view that persisted in Western scholarship until the mid-1970s. Later academic research saw her as protecting Malagasy sovereignty against European influence. She remains a controversial figure, largely viewed negatively in Madagascar today.

Measurable function modeling a quantity or object that depends on random events. Despite the name, the mathematical definition refers neither to randomness nor to variability, but to a [[Function (mathematics)|function]]. ENTRY:
Form of [[Experiment|experiment]] that evaluates the efficacy or safety of an intervention by randomly allocating participants to comparison groups. One group receives the intervention under study, while others receive an alternative treatment, a [[Placebo|placebo]] or standard care. Randomized controlled trials are widely considered among the highest-quality sources of evidence in evidence-based medicine, because random allocation reduces selection bias and the influence of confounding factors, though they have been criticized for failing to reduce bias in some cases. A well-blinded trial is considered the gold standard for clinical trials. In *Ortus Medicinae* (1648), Jan Baptist van Helmont proposed a trial of two fever treatments, but probably never conducted it. The first reported clinical trial, by James Lind in 1747, tested treatments for [[Scurvy]]. The modern form emerged with a 1948 British Medical Research Council trial of streptomycin for pulmonary [[Tuberculosis|tuberculosis]], which used sealed envelopes for random allocation.
