Flexible, usually round bar of [[Metal|metal]], commonly formed by drawing the metal through a hole in a die. Wire bears mechanical loads, often as wire rope, and carries electrical signals as [[Electrical cable|electrical cable]]. Rare class of [[Star|star]] with unusual spectra showing broad emission lines of ionized helium and highly ionized nitrogen or carbon. Classic Wolf–Rayet stars are evolved, massive stars that have lost their outer hydrogen and are fusing helium or heavier elements in the core. Their surface temperatures range from 20,000 K to around 210,000 K, hotter than almost all other stars. They are highly luminous, though not especially bright visually because most of their output is [[Ultraviolet|ultraviolet]]. Strong stellar winds drive their mass loss. A separate group are the central stars of [[Planetary nebula|planetary nebulae]], which have shed their atmospheres to reveal a bare carbon-oxygen core.

Salzburg-born composer and musician of the Classical period. He completed more than 800 works, including outstanding examples of most genres of his time: symphonies, [[Concerto|concertos]], [[Chamber music|chamber music]], [[Opera|opera]], and choral music. He is regarded as one of the greatest composers in Western music.

Born in [[Salzburg]], then within the [[Holy Roman Empire]], Mozart was the youngest of seven children, five of whom died in infancy. He emerged as a child prodigy under his father Leopold, an experienced teacher. At five he was competent on keyboard and violin, had begun to compose, and had performed before European royalty. His father took him on a grand tour of Europe and then three trips to Italy. At 17 he was a musician at the Salzburg court. A fruitless search for employment (1777–1779) took him to Paris, Mannheim, and Munich; during this time he wrote his five violin concertos, and the opera *Idomeneo*.

In 1781, while visiting Vienna, his quarrels with his Salzburg employers came to a head and he was dismissed. He remained in Vienna for the rest of his life, achieving fame and some financial success but no long-term security. He married Constanze Weber on August 4, 1782, and was appointed chamber composer to Emperor [[Joseph II]] in 1787.

His early Vienna years produced the opera *Die Entführung aus dem Serail*, the Great Mass in C minor, and he wrote more than a dozen piano concertos there, many considered masterpieces. In his final years he composed his last three symphonies, culminating in the *Jupiter*, the serenade *Eine kleine Nachtmusik*, the operas *Le nozze di Figaro*, [[Don Giovanni]], *Così fan tutte*, and *Die Zauberflöte*, and the Requiem, largely unfinished at his death at 35.

Common accompanying characteristics are larger breasts, a generally wider pelvis, less facial and body hair, higher body fat, shorter stature, and less muscle mass; these facilitate pregnancy, childbirth, and breastfeeding. Natural lignocellulosic material derived primarily from the secondary xylem of trees and other woody plants. In living plants it provides mechanical support and, in the functional sapwood, conducts water and dissolved minerals. It is a natural composite of [[Cellulose|cellulosic]] fibers, strong in tension, embedded in a matrix of lignin and hemicelluloses that resists compression. Dry wood is roughly 40–50% cellulose, 20–35% hemicelluloses, and 15–35% lignin.

A tree's diameter increases by secondary growth: cell division in the [[Vascular cambium|vascular cambium]] adds layers that envelop the whole stem, branches, and roots. Where seasons are distinct, growth appears as rings; the part of a ring formed early in the season is usually lighter, called earlywood, and the later part is latewood. Forests worldwide hold a growing stock of about 630 billion cubic meters, and almost 4 billion cubic meters of wood were harvested in 2023, mostly for furniture and building construction. Wood science emerged as a distinct field in the early 20th century.

ENTRY:
Textile fiber obtained mainly from [[Sheep|sheep]] and also from [[Goat|goats]], [[Rabbit|rabbits]], and camelids. Wool is an animal fiber made mostly of [[Protein|protein]], chiefly keratin, with about 2–3% lipids. This makes it chemically distinct from plant fibers such as cotton, which is mainly cellulose, and akin to silk. Its natural crimp and surface scales help fibers cling together during spinning and felting. Crimp also gives wool fabric bulk, so it holds air that retains heat. A fine wool such as [[Merino]] may have up to 40 crimps per centimeter (100 per inch). Wet wool is generally weaker than dry wool. After collection, wool is cleaned and carded or combed before being made into yarn, felt, insulation, or fertilizer.

ENTRY:
Extinct species of mammoth that lived from the Middle Pleistocene into the Holocene. Its closest living relative is the [[Asian elephant]]. It was covered in fur, with small ears and a short tail that reduced frostbite and heat loss. Its long, curved tusks were used for foraging and fighting. It lived on the mammoth steppe of northern Eurasia and North America and was hunted by early humans. The last mainland populations in [[Siberia]] persisted until about 10,000 years ago, and isolated island populations survived until about 4,000 years ago. Its genome, completed in 2015, prompted discussion of reviving the species.

Fermented [[Condiment|condiment]] invented by the [[Pharmacist|pharmacists]] John Wheeley Lea and William Henry Perrins in Worcester, England, in the first half of the 19th century. The Lea & Perrins brand, the first to bear the Worcestershire name, was commercialized in 1837. A court ruling in 1876 held that the name had become generic. Typical ingredients include vinegar, salt, molasses or sugar, garlic, onions, soy sauce, and anchovies or fish sauce. The company's claim that a "Lord Sandys" brought the recipe from India is not supported by historical records. The sauce flavors steaks and cocktails such as the [[Cocktail|Bloody Mary]] and the [[Caesar salad]].
