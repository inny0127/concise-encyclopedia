ENTRY:
Flat supporting surface tilted at an angle, used as a ramp to raise or lower loads, and one of the six classical [[Simple machine|simple machines]] defined by [[Renaissance]] scientists. Moving a load up an inclined plane requires less force than lifting it, at the cost of a greater distance. Its mechanical advantage equals the ratio of the slope's length to its height. Because of [[Conservation of energy|conservation of energy]], the same work is needed, disregarding [[Friction|friction]]. The angle of repose is the steepest angle at which a load rests without sliding, equal to the arctangent of the coefficient of static friction. The [[Wedge|wedge]] and the screw are often considered derived from it. The Egyptian pyramids were built using inclined planes, and the stones of [[Stonehenge]] are believed to have been moved on them. Wheelchair ramps, loading ramps and roads climbing hills are common examples.

Tax levied on the income or profits of individuals and entities (taxpayers). It is generally computed as a tax rate times taxable income, which is usually total income less income-producing expenses and other deductions. Rates may rise with income in graduated or progressive systems, while companies are commonly taxed at a flat rate. Most jurisdictions require self-assessment and withholding on some payments, and late payers face significant penalties. For most of history, taxes were based on [[Wealth]], social position, and ownership of land and slaves. Emperor Wang Mang of the Xin dynasty established what is recorded as the first income tax in AD 9, a 10% tax on net earnings. Britain introduced a temporary income tax in 1799 to fund military efforts against Napoleon, and it later became a permanent fixture in many nations.

Country in South Asia (pop., 2025: approx. 1,464,000,000), capital [[New Delhi]].

Officially the Republic of India, it is the world's seventh-largest country by area and its most populous. It is bounded by the [[Indian Ocean]], the Arabian Sea, and the Bay of Bengal, and borders Pakistan, China, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, and Myanmar.

Modern humans reached the subcontinent by 55,000 years ago. Settled life emerged in the Indus basin 9,000 years ago and developed into the [[Indus Valley Civilisation]] of the third millennium BCE. By 1200 BCE an archaic form of Sanskrit had spread from the northwest, its earliest hymns containing elements of what became [[Hinduism]]. In 1947 the British Indian Empire was partitioned into a Hindu-majority India and a Muslim-majority Pakistan, with large-scale loss of life and unprecedented migration.

The Green Revolution of the 1970s brought national food security. Economic reforms in the 1990s integrated the economy with the world's, and growth was stably high by the 2010s. Challenges include widening income gaps, gender inequality, and environmental degradation, and some observers describe a political shift toward majoritarianism. India is a nuclear-weapon state, a founding member of the United Nations, and among the world's largest economies. It has 45 UNESCO World Heritage Sites and four biodiversity hotspots.

Third-largest of the world's five oceanic divisions, bounded by Asia to the north, Africa to the west, and Australia to the east.

It covers 70,560,000 sq km, about 20% of Earth's water surface, including the Red Sea and Persian Gulf but excluding the [[Southern Ocean]], or Antarctica, which bounds it to the south depending on the definition in use. Its volume is 264,000,000 cu km, with an average depth of 3,741 m and a maximum depth of 7,290 m. The [[Atlantic Ocean]] boundary is the 20° east meridian, running south from Cape Agulhas, South Africa, and the [[Pacific Ocean]] boundary is the meridian of 146°49′E, running south from South East Cape, Tasmania. Its large marginal seas include the [[Andaman Sea]], the Arabian Sea, the [[Bay of Bengal]], and the Laccadive Sea. It is enclosed by major landmasses on three sides and does not stretch from pole to pole. More than two billion people live in countries bordering it.

Geologically it is the youngest ocean, preceded by the Tethys Ocean, which fragmented into the Mediterranean Sea and the Indian Ocean only 20 million years ago. Its continental shelves are narrow, making up 15% of the ocean. It is the warmest ocean and strongly affects global climate; its waters are shaped by the Indian Ocean Walker circulation, producing distinctive currents and upwelling. The surrounding climate is characterized by [[Monsoon|monsoons]]. Coral reefs, mangroves, and seagrass beds support diverse ecosystems, and the ocean hosts a significant portion of the world's tuna catch and a number of endangered marine species.

Its name comes from India, which projects into it, and has been attested since at least 1515, in the Latin *Oceanus Orientalis Indicus*. Chinese explorers such as [[Zheng He]] in the 15th century called it the Western Oceans, and the ancient Greeks the Erythraean Sea. It has long linked diverse regions through trade, innovation, and religion.

Groups characterized by self-identification, cultural difference from other groups in a state, a special relationship with their traditional territory, and an experience of subjugation and discrimination under a dominant cultural model.

There is no generally accepted definition in the [[United Nations]] or international law, and various bodies have either proposed definitions or declined to offer one. The UN Permanent Forum on Indigenous Issues holds that no formal universal definition is necessary, since any single definition would be either over- or under-inclusive. Estimates of the population range from 250 million to 600 million, in some 5,000 distinct peoples spread across every inhabited climate zone and continent. Many have experienced colonization by settlers from European nations, but Indigenous identity is not determined by Western colonization.

The English word derives from the Latin *indigena*, "sprung from the land, native." Europeans first used it to distinguish the Indigenous peoples of the Americas from enslaved Africans; the first known use is by Sir Thomas Browne in 1646. In the 1970s it came to link the struggles of colonized peoples across borders and to describe a legal category in international and national law.

The 1989 International Labour Organization Convention on Indigenous and Tribal Peoples covers peoples regarded as indigenous because of descent from populations that inhabited a country or region at the time of conquest, colonization, or the establishment of present state boundaries, and makes self-identification a fundamental criterion. In 2007 the UN adopted a Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples, affirming rights to [[Self-determination|self-determination]] and to protect cultures, languages, and ceremonies. Indigenous peoples continue to face threats to their sovereignty, economic well-being, languages, and access to resources. Critics of the open-ended definition argue that reliance on self-identification can enable expansive claims, while supporters regard it as respecting the right of peoples to determine their own identity.

Property of an electrical conductor that opposes changes in the electric current flowing through it. The current generates a magnetic field, and any change in that field through a circuit induces a voltage, a process known as [[Electromagnetic induction]]. This induced voltage opposes the change, as stated by Lenz's law, and is called back EMF. Inductance is defined as the ratio of induced voltage to the rate of change of current, and depends on the geometry of the conductor and the magnetic permeability of nearby materials. A component designed to add inductance to a circuit is an [[Inductor]]. The term was coined by Oliver Heaviside in May 1884. The SI unit is the henry (H), which produces one [[Volt|volt]] of induced voltage when the current changes at one [[Ampere|ampere]] per second. It is named after Joseph Henry, who discovered inductance independently of [[Michael Faraday|Faraday]].
