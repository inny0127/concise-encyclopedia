Ancient kingdom of the Horn of Africa and South Arabia, based in present-day Eritrea and northern [[Ethiopia]]. Emerging from the earlier Dʿmt civilization, it was founded in the 1st century, and its capital was the city of Axum. It became a major power on the trade route between Rome and India and gained a monopoly of Indian Ocean trade. Under Ezana (320s–c. 360) it adopted [[Christianity]] as its state religion, and it conquered [[Meroë]] for a short period. Under Kaleb (514–542) it invaded Himyar in Yemen, and its territory reached around 2,500,000 sq km (970,000 sq mi), though this was later lost in the Aksumite–Persian wars. Its decline began by the 7th century, when it ceased to mint currency, and the kingdom collapsed under uncertain circumstances around 960.

Ancient kingdom of the Near East, existing from 189 BC to 428 AD. Its origins lay in an Achaemenid satrapy formed from [[Urartu]], and the Artaxiad dynasty took the throne in 189 BC. ENTRY:
Web of social relationships based on descent and marriage, central to human societies. In anthropology, kinship refers both to these relationships and to the study of them. Ties through descent are distinguished from [[Affinity (law)|affinity]], the ties created by marriage. Kinship organizes people into social groups, roles and [[Genealogy|genealogies]]. Two equalities describing current and potential difference (commonly known as [[Voltage|voltage]]) in electrical circuits. First described in 1845 by German physicist [[Gustav Kirchhoff]], the laws generalized the work of [[Georg Ohm]] and preceded that of [[James Clerk Maxwell]]. The current law states that the algebraic sum of currents at any node (junction) is zero. The voltage law states that the directed sum of potential differences around any closed loop is zero. Both can be understood as corollaries of [[Maxwell's equations]] in the low-frequency limit. They are accurate for direct-current (DC) circuits and for alternating-current circuits at frequencies whose wavelengths are very large compared with the circuit. A matrix version of the current law is the basis of most circuit simulation software, such as SPICE. The laws do not apply where the lumped-element model fails, as in high-frequency AC circuits.
