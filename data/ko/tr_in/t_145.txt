ENTRY:
[[Keyboard instrument]] whose strings are plucked rather than struck. Pressing a key raises a jack holding a small plectrum, usually of quill or plastic, which plucks a string. Because the sound is fixed, a note sounds equally loud however hard the key is pressed. The harpsichord was widely used in Renaissance and [[Baroque music|Baroque]] music, both as a solo instrument and as part of the continuo. Italian makers evolved a design that survived into later centuries, and the Ruckers family of the Southern Netherlands built the first harpsichords with two keyboards. Around 1700 Bartolomeo Cristofori built the first [[Piano|piano]], and by the late 18th century the piano had largely supplanted the harpsichord. Revived in the 20th century, it is used in historically informed performances of music by [[Johann Sebastian Bach|Bach]] and others.

Large neotropical species of [[Eagle|eagle]], *Harpia harpyja*. It is the largest [[Bird of prey|bird of prey]] throughout its range and usually inhabits tropical lowland [[Rainforest|rainforest]], nesting and hunting in the emergent canopy. It is found from southern Mexico to as far south as Argentina, and preys on [[Sloth|sloths]], [[Opossum|opossums]] and other arboreal mammals. [[Agriculture|Agricultural]] implement used for surface [[Tillage|tillage]]. It is used after [[Plough|ploughing]] to break up clods and smooth the soil into a structure called tilth, suitable for planting [[Seed|seeds]]. Coarser harrowing also removes weeds and covers seed after sowing. Unlike a plow, which cuts the upper 12 to 25 cm (5 to 10 in) of soil and leaves furrows, a harrow disturbs the whole surface. Harrows were once drawn by draft animals but are now almost always attached to a [[Tractor|tractor]].

Collection of states ruled by the Hausa people, between the [[Niger River|Niger River]] and [[Lake Chad|Lake Chad]] in present-day northern [[Nigeria|Nigeria]]. According to the Bayajidda legend, the Hausa states were founded by the sons of a [[Baghdad|Baghdadi]] prince who married Daurama, the last ruler of Daura. The seven "true" states, the Hausa Bakwai, were Biram, Daura, Gobir, Kano, Katsina, Rano and Zazzau, while seven others, the Hausa Banza, lay to their west and south. The kingdoms were built around fortified capitals and exported kola nuts, leather goods and textiles. Internal rivalries and warfare weakened them. In the Fulani jihad of 1804–1808, Usman dan Fodio conquered them and founded the [[Sokoto Caliphate|Sokoto Caliphate]].

ENTRY:
Hypothetical [[Black-body radiation|black-body radiation]] emitted by [[Black hole|black holes]] through quantum effects outside the [[Event horizon|event horizon]]. Predicted by [[Stephen Hawking|Stephen Hawking]] in 1974, it was not foreseen by earlier models that assumed nothing inside the event horizon could escape. It would reduce a black hole's mass and rotational energy, causing evaporation, which for all but the smallest black holes is extremely slow. Its temperature is inversely proportional to mass, so small primordial black holes would emit faster and could end in a burst of high-energy radiation. The radiation is far too faint for current [[Telescope|telescopes]] to detect, and such bursts have not been observed.

Dried grass, legumes or other herbaceous plants used as animal fodder. Cut and dried for storage, hay is fed to livestock such as [[Cattle|cattle]], [[Horse|horses]], [[Goat|goats]] and [[Sheep|sheep]], as well as to rabbits and guinea pigs. It is used when there is not enough pasture, when weather prevents grazing, such as in winter, or when an animal is kept in a [[Stable|stable]] or barn. Common plants include ryegrass, timothy, fescue, and legumes such as alfalfa and clover. Haymaking involves cutting, curing, raking, processing and storing, and it is highly sensitive to weather. Drought stunts seed and leaf production, and wet weather can spoil cut hay in the field or cause rot and toxins after baling.
