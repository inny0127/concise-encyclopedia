English surgeon and pioneer of antiseptic surgery. At the Glasgow Royal Infirmary, Lister introduced carbolic acid (modern-day [[Phenol|phenol]]) to sterilize instruments, skin, sutures, hands and wards. Drawing on [[Louis Pasteur|Louis Pasteur]]'s germ theory of fermentation, he recognized that putrefaction in wounds is caused by germs. His antiseptic system reduced post-operative [[Infection|infection]] and, by his account, cut amputation mortality from 45% to 15%. He was appointed Regius Professor of Surgery at Glasgow in 1860, was elevated to the peerage as Baron Lister in 1897, and served as president of the Royal Society from 1895 to 1900. He is known as the "father of modern surgery".

French chemist and physicist. With [[Alexander von Humboldt|Alexander von Humboldt]], he discovered that water is formed of two parts hydrogen and one part oxygen by volume. He also formulated a law stating that at constant pressure the volume of a gas increases in proportion to its absolute temperature; the law is usually called Charles's law, though some sources use his name. His work on alcohol–water mixtures led to the degrees Gay-Lussac, used to measure alcoholic beverages in many countries. He succeeded Antoine François Fourcroy as professor of chemistry at the École Polytechnique.

Soviet politician and revolutionary who led the [[Soviet Union]] from the late 1920s until 1953.

Born Ioseb Jughashvili into a poor Georgian family in Gori, he left a Tiflis seminary in 1899 and became a Marxist revolutionary. After the [[October Revolution]] of 1917 he joined the Politburo. Following the death of [[Vladimir Lenin]] in 1924, he used his post as General Secretary of the Communist Party (1922–52) to outmaneuver rivals and by the 1930s was the de facto dictator.

His policy of "Socialism in One Country" and five-year plans from 1928 brought rapid collectivization of agriculture and industrialization under a centralized command economy. After his death [[Nikita Khrushchev]] denounced him and began de-Stalinization. Within the international Marxist–Leninist movement he was the subject of a pervasive personality cult. He retains popularity in Russia as a victorious wartime leader, while his regime has been widely condemned for mass repression, ethnic cleansing, and the deaths of millions.

Abrahamic, monotheistic, ethnic religion comprising the spiritual, cultural, and legal traditions of the [[Jews|Jewish people]]. Religious Jews regard it as their means of observing the Mosaic covenant, believed to have been established between God and the Jewish people. It is considered one of the earliest monotheistic religions.

Its core texts are the [[Torah]] ("Teaching"), the Nevi'im ("Prophets"), and the Ketuvim ("Writings"), which together form the [[Hebrew Bible]], also called the Tanakh. The Oral Torah comprises the Mishnah and the [[Talmud]], Midrashim, [[Halakha]] (Jewish law), Aggadah, and later rabbinic commentary. Rabbinic Judaism holds that God revealed commandments to Moses on Mount Sinai in both written and oral form.

The largest movements are [[Orthodox Judaism]], [[Conservative Judaism]], and [[Reform Judaism]]. They differ in their approaches to Halakha, rabbinic authority, and rabbinic literature, and in the significance they give to the State of Israel. Orthodox Judaism maintains that the Torah and Halakha are divine, eternal, and unalterable. Conservative Judaism generally takes a more traditionalist line than Reform, whose typical position treats Halakha as general guidelines. Authority is vested not in any one person or body but in the texts and in the rabbis and scholars who interpret them.

The term derives from Latin *Iudaismus*, from Greek *Ioudaismos*, first attested in 2 Maccabees in the 2nd century BC, and ultimately from the Hebrew *Yehudah* (Judah). Scholars debate the religion's origins: many critical historians describe an evolution from Yahwism, the ancient Israelite worship of Yahweh in the Iron Age, while others argue for an earlier distinct covenant. Jewishness may pass by matrilineal descent or conversion, so secular individuals may still belong to the people. Judaism strongly influenced Christianity and Islam. In 2025 the world Jewish population was estimated at 14.8 million.
