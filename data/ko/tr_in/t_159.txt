Temperate climate type with long, hot summers and cool to mild winters. Normally found on the southeast side of continents, generally between latitudes 25° and 40°, it lies poleward of tropical climates. ENTRY:
Concentration of [[Water vapor|water vapor]] in the air, indicating the likelihood of [[Precipitation|precipitation]], [[Dew|dew]] or [[Fog|fog]]. Humidity depends on temperature and pressure. The same amount of water vapor gives a higher relative humidity in cool air than in warm air, and the [[Dew point|dew point]] is the temperature at which air reaches saturation. Absolute humidity is the mass of water vapor per volume of air, in grams per cubic meter. Relative humidity, often expressed as a percentage, indicates the present amount relative to the maximum at the same temperature. Specific humidity is the ratio of water vapor mass to total moist air mass. High humidity impairs heat exchange for animals that rely on sweating, because it reduces the rate of [[Evaporation|evaporation]] from skin surfaces; a heat index table or a humidex can estimate the effect.

Family of small birds native to the Americas, comprising Trochilidae. The family has around 375 species in 113 genera, from [[Alaska]] to [[Tierra del Fuego]], with most species in Central and South America. Hummingbirds are the smallest mature birds, measuring 7.5–13 cm (3–5 in) long. The bee hummingbird weighs less than 2.0 g, and the giant hummingbird, at 23 cm (9 in), is the largest. Male plumage shows the widest color diversity of any birds. Their wings beat so fast that they produce a humming sound, and they have the highest mass-specific metabolic rate of any homeothermic animal. Hummingbirds feed mainly on flower nectar, and all species also consume small [[Insect|insects]]. Most do not migrate, but the rufous hummingbird travels about 3,900 miles (6,300 km) twice a year between Alaska and [[Mexico]]. The family split from its sister group, the [[Swift (bird)|swifts]], around 42 million years ago.

System of medicine adopted by Ancient Greek and Roman physicians, based on four bodily fluids. Its four humors were blood, phlegm, yellow bile and black bile, and [[Hippocrates]] is usually credited with applying the idea to medicine. ENTRY:
Tendency of experiences to provoke laughter and provide amusement. The word derives from the humoral medicine of the ancient Greeks, which taught that the balance of bodily fluids, or "humors" ([[Latin]] *humor*, "body fluid"), controlled health and emotion. People of all ages and cultures respond to humor, but what a person finds funny depends on geography, culture, maturity, education, intelligence, and context. Young children may favor physical slapstick such as *Tom and Jerry* cartoons, while satire requires an understanding of social meaning and context.

Theories include psychological accounts, most of which regard humor-induced behavior as healthy; spiritual accounts, which may treat humor as a "gift from God"; and views that see it as an unexplainable mystery. The benign-violation theory, endorsed by Peter McGraw, holds that humor occurs only when something seems wrong or threatening but simultaneously seems acceptable or safe. Other theories stress incongruity (pleasure in resolving unexpected patterns), superiority (amusement at others' failings), and relief (discharge of pent-up energy); incongruity is described as the best supported empirically.

Western humor theory begins with [[Plato]], who in the *Philebus* has [[Socrates]] hold that the essence of the ridiculous is an ignorance in the weak, who cannot retaliate when ridiculed. [[Aristotle]], in the *Poetics*, suggested that an ugliness that does not disgust is fundamental to humor. In ancient India, Bharata Muni's *Natya Shastra* defined humor (*hāsyam*) as one of the nine *rasas*, or emotional responses. After the *Poetics* was translated into Arabic, writers including [[Al-Farabi]], [[Avicenna]], and [[Averroes]] linked comedy to Arabic poetic forms such as *hija* (satirical poetry) and called it the "art of reprehension." [[Arthur Schopenhauer]] lamented the use of the German loanword *Humor* for any type of comedy.

Baleen whale species, the only member of the genus Megaptera. Adults range from 14–17 m (46–56 ft) in length and weigh up to 40 metric tons. The humpback is known for breaching. Males produce a complex song that typically lasts from 4 to 33 minutes. Humpbacks migrate between feeding areas toward the poles and breeding areas near the equator, and they feed mostly on [[Krill|krill]] and small fish, often using bubbles to catch prey. [[Orca|Orcas]] are the main natural predators of humpbacks. Like other large whales, the humpback was a target of the [[Whaling|whaling industry]] and was hunted to the brink of extinction, its population falling to around 5,000 by the 1960s. Numbers have partially recovered to some 135,000 animals, but entanglement in fishing gear, collisions with ships and noise pollution continue to affect the species.

Hunting is also done to remove predators dangerous to people or livestock, to eliminate pests, for trade and tourism, and for conservation against overpopulation and [[Invasive species|invasive species]], a practice called culling. Organic compound consisting entirely of hydrogen and carbon. Hydrocarbons are generally colorless and hydrophobic, and occur as gases such as [[Methane|methane]] and propane, liquids such as hexane and benzene, low-melting solids such as paraffin wax, or polymers such as polyethylene. Saturated hydrocarbons contain only single bonds and include [[Alkane|alkanes]], while [[Alkene|alkenes]] and [[Alkyne|alkynes]] contain double or triple bonds, and aromatic hydrocarbons contain at least one aromatic ring. In the fossil fuel industries the term refers to petroleum, natural gas and coal. Combustion of hydrocarbons supplies most of the world's energy, and [[Petroleum]] is the dominant raw material for commodity chemicals such as solvents and polymers. Most human-generated greenhouse gas emissions are carbon dioxide from burning fossil fuels or methane from natural gas handling and agriculture.

ENTRY:
Electricity generated from hydropower, including stations with a [[Dam|dam]] and reservoir. Hydropower supplies 15% of the world's electricity, almost 4,210 TWh in 2023, more than all other renewable sources combined and more than nuclear power. It can provide large amounts of low-carbon electricity on demand, and a station with a [[Reservoir|reservoir]] can raise or lower output within seconds or minutes. Once built, it produces no direct waste and almost always emits considerably less [[Greenhouse gas|greenhouse gas]] than fossil fuel plants, although flooding lowland rainforest can release substantial amounts. Construction can displace populations, destroy arable land and disrupt river ecosystems. China added 24 GW in 2022, nearly three-quarters of global hydropower capacity additions.
