ENTRY:
Life stage of holometabolous insects between larva and adult. Insects with complete metamorphosis pass through four stages: egg, larva, pupa and [[Imago|imago]] (adult). During the pupal stage, adult structures form from imaginal discs while larval structures break down, a process of [[Metamorphosis|metamorphosis]] controlled by hormones such as juvenile hormone and ecdysone. Most pupae are non-feeding and largely immobile, although [[Mosquito|mosquito]] pupae are highly active. Pupae are largely defenseless, so species often use cocoons, camouflage or burial. The stage may last from weeks to years; monarch butterfly pupae last eight to fifteen days. Pupae in temperate climates usually overwinter, while those in the [[Tropics|tropics]] usually wait out the dry season. The adult emerges by splitting the pupal case, a process called eclosion.

Underground water supply system developed in ancient [[Iran]], which carries water to the surface from an [[Aquifer|aquifer]] or well through a buried [[Aqueduct (water supply)|aqueduct]]. Originating about 3,000 years ago, it is known by regional names including kārēz in Afghanistan and Pakistan, foggāra in Algeria and khettāra in the Atlas Mountains. A typical qanat is a gently sloping tunnel reached through well-like vertical shafts. Gravity delivers the water to the surface at a lower point, so no pumping is needed. Qanats still supply water for drinking and irrigation in hot, arid regions, and the largest working examples are in Afghanistan, [[Xinjiang]], [[Oman]] and Pakistan.

Founder of the Qin dynasty and first emperor of China (r. 221–210 BC). Born Ying Zheng, he became king of Qin with the help of the merchant Lü Buwei. By 221 BC he had conquered all the other [[Warring States period|warring states]] and unified China. Rather than keep the title of king, he created the title *huángdì* ("emperor"), combining the names of the mythical Three Sovereigns and legendary Five Emperors; it was used by monarchs in China and other countries for the next two millennia.

His generals added the Yue lands of Hunan and Guangdong in campaigns south of Chu and took the Ordos Plateau from the [[Xiongnu]] in Inner Asia, although the Xiongnu later rallied. With his minister [[Li Si]] he enacted economic and political reforms standardizing the diverse practices of earlier states, including weights, measures, currency, axle widths, and script, and replaced feudal lords with appointed officials. His public works included the incorporation of state walls into a single [[Great Wall of China]], a national road system, and a city-sized mausoleum guarded by the life-sized [[Terracotta Army]].

He is traditionally said to have banned and burned many books and executed scholars. He has often been portrayed as a tyrant and strict Legalist, characterizations stemming partly from the assessments of the succeeding [[Han dynasty]] and of [[Sima Qian]]. Since the mid-20th century scholars have questioned this evaluation and debated the nature of his policies. He died in 210 BC during his fifth tour of eastern China, and the [[Qin dynasty]] collapsed in 206 BC amid revolts. The sinologist Michael Loewe wrote that the achievements of his reign marked "the start of an epoch that closed in 1911."
