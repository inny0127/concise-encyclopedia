ENTRY:
Set of resources and environmental conditions in an area that support a species' survival and reproduction. A species' habitat is the physical manifestation of its [[Ecological niche|ecological niche]], and the term is species-specific, unlike environment or vegetation. Physical factors include soil, moisture, temperature and light; biotic factors include food and predators. Habitat generalists thrive in many conditions, while specialists need a narrow set. A parasite's habitat may be its host's body or a single host cell. Habitat types classify whole environments by vegetation and climate, such as [[Forest|forest]], [[Steppe|steppe]], grassland, desert, marsh and pond. Habitats change through volcanic eruptions, earthquakes and tsunamis, or gradually with climate, and humans alter them through [[Deforestation|deforestation]] and river damming.

Reports of the words, actions and silent approvals of the Islamic prophet [[Muhammad]]. The Arabic word means "report" or "account". === KEY: Hadley_cell_b3994d
HEADWORD: Hadley cell = 해들리 순환
LEVEL: 4
GLOSSARY:

  Monsoon = 계절풍

ENTRY:
Global-scale tropical atmospheric circulation in which air rises near the equator and sinks in the subtropics. Named after George Hadley, who in 1735 proposed hemisphere-spanning cells to explain the [[Trade winds|trade winds]]. Air rises near the equator, flows poleward near the tropopause at 12–15 km, descends around 30 degrees latitude and returns equatorward near the surface. Converging air forms the [[Intertropical Convergence Zone]], where the heaviest rains fall, and shifts in it cause [[Monsoon|monsoons]]. The sinking branches suppress rainfall, so many of Earth's deserts lie in the subtropics. Since at least the 1980s the circulation has expanded poleward as a result of climate change, and models project that it will widen and weaken through the 21st century.

Composite subatomic particle made of two or more quarks, bound by the strong nuclear force. Eel-shaped jawless marine fish of the class Myxini, known for producing slime. Hagfish are the only known living animals with a skull but no vertebral column, though they have rudimentary vertebrae. They are scavengers and predators with simple eyespots and two pairs of horny teeth. When threatened they exude copious slime from glands in the skin; in seawater it expands to 10,000 times its original size in 0.4 seconds. Hagfish and [[Lamprey|lampreys]] form the only living jawless fish groups and are more closely related to each other than to jawed vertebrates. The oldest known hagfish date from the [[Carboniferous|Late Carboniferous]], about 310 million years ago.

ENTRY:
Arabic term for what is permissible under [[Sharia|Islamic law]]. Although the term is often associated with dietary laws, particularly the slaughter of meat, halal also governs business and finance, such as the prohibition of usury (riba), and daily living. In the [[Quran|Quran]], halal is contrasted with [[Haram|haram]], meaning forbidden. Islam generally considers all food halal unless it is specifically prohibited in the Quran or [[Hadith|hadith]], and pork is the only meat that is categorically forbidden. Halal has grown into a global industry, with certification bodies serving the food, pharmaceutical, [[Cosmetics|cosmetic]] and financial sectors. The halal economy has grown especially in countries with large Muslim populations, such as Malaysia, Indonesia and the [[Middle East|Middle East]].

ENTRY:
Time required for a quantity to reduce to half its initial value, symbol t½. Commonly used in nuclear physics to describe how quickly unstable atoms undergo [[Radioactive decay|radioactive decay]], the term also applies to any exponential decay, such as the biological half-life of drugs. [[Ernest Rutherford|Ernest Rutherford]] discovered the principle in 1907 and used it to date rocks by the decay of [[Radium|radium]] to lead-206. For a radioactive atom, half-life is defined in terms of probability: on average, exactly half of the entities decay in that time.

Maritime disaster in [[Halifax, Nova Scotia|Halifax]], Nova Scotia, on December 6, 1917. The French cargo ship SS *Mont-Blanc*, loaded with high explosives, collided at low speed with the Norwegian vessel SS *Imo* in the harbor. Benzol barrels on the French ship's deck leaked and were ignited by sparks, and about 20 minutes later *Mont-Blanc* exploded, devastating the Richmond district.
