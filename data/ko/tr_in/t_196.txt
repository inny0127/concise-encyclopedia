Intermittent one-second adjustment to [[Coordinated Universal Time|Coordinated Universal Time (UTC)]]. Between 1972 and 2016, 27 positive leap seconds were added to keep UTC within ±0.9 seconds of solar time, which varies as Earth's rotation slows. The last was on December 31, 2016, and since January 2017 the difference between UTC and [[Atomic clock|International Atomic Time]] has been 37 seconds. Calendar year containing an added day, or in lunisolar calendars an added month. The solar year lasts slightly less than 365¼ days, so calendars need extra time to stay aligned with the seasons. [[Julius Caesar]] reformed the Roman calendar in 45 BC, adding a leap day every 4 years to create the [[Julian calendar]]. The [[Gregorian calendar]], introduced in 1582 by Pope Gregory XIII, refined the rule: years divisible by 100 are leap years only if also divisible by 400, so 2000 was a leap year but 1900 was not. Lunisolar calendars, traditional across much of Asia, add a leap month instead. The term probably comes from the way a fixed date skips a weekday, advancing two days rather than one, in the year after a leap day. From 1972 to 2016, [[Leap second|leap seconds]] were also added to UTC.

ENTRY:
Process of acquiring new understanding, knowledge, behavior, skills, values, attitudes, and preferences. The ability to learn is possessed by humans, other animals, and some machines, and there is evidence for some kind of learning in certain plants. Some learning is immediate, induced by a single event such as being burned by a hot stove, but much skill and knowledge accumulates from repeated experiences. Its effects often last a lifetime.

Human learning begins at birth, may start before it, and continues until death through interaction with the environment. Habituation has been observed as early as 32 weeks into gestation, indicating that the nervous system is developed enough for learning and [[Memory|memory]] to occur very early. The subject is studied in educational psychology, neuropsychology, cognitive science, and [[Pedagogy|pedagogy]].

Researchers distinguish several kinds. Non-associative learning is a relatively permanent change in the strength of response to a single stimulus due to repeated exposure; it includes habituation, in which an innate response diminishes when a stimulus is repeated, and sensitization, in which a response is progressively amplified. Associative learning includes classical conditioning, studied by [[Ivan Pavlov]], and operant conditioning, which links voluntary behavior to consequences. Behaviorism was pioneered by figures such as [[John B. Watson]] and B. F. Skinner, and observational learning was theorized by Albert Bandura. Learning that an aversive event cannot be avoided may result in learned helplessness.

[[Play (activity)|Play]] has been approached as a form of learning in which children experiment with the world and learn rules; [[Lev Vygotsky]] held it to be the first form of learning language and communication. Evolutionary research suggests that learning is favored over innate behavior in environments that are neither completely stable nor entirely unpredictable.

Method of finding a best-fit model by minimizing the sum of squared residuals, the differences between observed and predicted values. [[Recreation]] differs from leisure in being a purposeful activity that includes the experience of leisure. Economists treat leisure time as valuable to a person, as they do wages. Leisure was historically a privilege of the [[Upper class|upper class]], but opportunities for leisure expanded dramatically in the mid-to-late 19th century, starting in Great Britain. The concept of leisure as a human right appears in article 24 of the [[Universal Declaration of Human Rights]].

ENTRY:
Small rodent of the Arctic [[Tundra|tundra]], related to [[Vole|voles]] and [[Muskrat|muskrats]] in the subfamily *Arvicolinae*. Lemmings measure about 13–18 cm and feed mostly on mosses and grasses, and they are a primary prey source for most Arctic carnivores. They do not hibernate and live in tunnel systems beneath the snow in winter. Their populations boom and then disperse, and the Norway and West Siberian lemmings are among the few vertebrates whose fluctuations are chaotic. Lemmings are conspicuously colored and behave aggressively toward predators, a defense thought to be a warning display. A longstanding myth claims they commit mass suicide by jumping off cliffs.

Yellow fruit of a small evergreen tree, *Citrus × limon*, in the family Rutaceae. A true lemon is a hybrid of the [[Citron|citron]] and the bitter orange. Its origins are uncertain, but it may have originated in northeastern India during the 1st millennium BC. Lemons reached Europe near southern Italy no later than the 2nd century AD. The first substantial cultivation in Europe began in [[Genoa]] in the mid-15th century, and [[Christopher Columbus]] brought seeds to [[Hispaniola]] in 1493. Lemon juice is about 5–6% [[Citric acid|citric acid]], giving it a sour taste, and it is used in drinks such as lemonade. In 1747 the English physician James Lind experimented with adding lemon juice to the diets of seamen suffering from [[Scurvy|scurvy]], before vitamin C was known. World production was 23 million tonnes in 2024, led by India and Mexico with 31% of the total.

ENTRY:
Wet-nosed primate endemic to the island of Madagascar. Lemurs are divided into eight families and about 100 living species. Most are small, with large eyes and long tails, and they usually live in trees and are active at night. They evolved independently from [[Monkey|monkeys]] and [[Ape|apes]]. Living lemurs range from the mouse lemur, at about 30 g, to the indri, at 9 kg. Since humans arrived on the island about 2,000 years ago, more than a dozen species of giant lemurs have become extinct. Lemurs are generally the most social strepsirrhine primates. The IUCN considers them the world's most endangered mammals, noting that as of 2013 up to 90% of species face the threat of extinction within the next 20 to 25 years. Ring-tailed lemurs are an iconic flagship species.

ENTRY:
River in the Russian Far East, the easternmost of Siberia's three great rivers flowing into the Arctic Ocean. The Lena is 4,294 km long and drains 2,490,000 sq km, making it the 11th-longest river in the world and the longest river entirely within Russia. Permafrost underlies its whole catchment and is continuous in over 75 percent of the basin. The river flows north past [[Yakutsk]] and widens into a delta of 30,000 sq km that merges into the Laptev Sea. The Lena is navigable for 3,540 km. Gold is washed from the sands of its tributaries, and mammoth tusks have been dug out of the delta.
