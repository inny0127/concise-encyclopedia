His National Insurance Act 1911 helped establish the modern [[Welfare state|welfare state]]. As minister of munitions from 1915 he expanded shell production, and in December 1916 he replaced H. H. British economist and politician. A leading classical economist, Ricardo worked alongside [[Adam Smith]] and others in shaping the field. ENTRY:
Unit of time equal to one full rotation of the [[Earth]] with respect to the Sun, averaging 24 hours (86,400 seconds). The daily cycle drives [[Circadian rhythm|circadian rhythms]] in many organisms.

Days are organized into [[Calendar|calendars]], grouped into weeks, months, and years. In common usage a day starts at midnight. Because the time of midnight varies between locations, [[Time zone|time zones]] provide a uniform standard time; they began to be adopted in the middle of the 19th century with railroads, and as of 2015, 40 were in use, all defined as offsets from [[Coordinated Universal Time]]. The Jewish religious calendar counts days from sunset, and in astronomy a day begins at noon.

Several definitions serve specific purposes. The SI day is exactly 86,400 seconds. A fictitious mean Sun moving at constant speed defines the mean solar day, removing the yearly variation caused by Earth's orbit and axial tilt. A sidereal day, measured against the celestial background, lasts 23 hours, 56 minutes, and 4 seconds. Tidal interactions with the Moon are lengthening Earth's day by about 2.3 milliseconds per century.

On other bodies a day is a full rotation with respect to the star. The solar day on [[Mercury (planet)|Mercury]] is 176 Earth days, longer than its year of 88 days; on [[Venus]] it is 117 Earth days, shorter than its year of 225 days. [[Jupiter]] has a day of approximately 10 hours, and a solar day on Mars is about 24 hours and 37 minutes.

Practice of advancing clocks by one hour in spring and setting them back in autumn. Daylight saving time (DST) makes better use of summer daylight by moving darkness to a later clock time, hence the mnemonic "spring forward, fall back". The first local implementation was in Port Arthur and Fort William, Ontario, [[Canada]], in 1908, and the first nationwide ones were by the German and Austro-Hungarian Empires, starting April 30, 1916. As of 2023 around 34 percent of the world's countries used DST, mainly in Europe and North America. In a satirical 1784 letter, [[Benjamin Franklin]] suggested that Parisians wake earlier in summer to save candles, but he did not propose changing clocks.

Irreversible cessation of the biological functions that sustain a living organism. After death, organisms stop exhibiting behavior and generally begin to decompose. Death can occur at any stage of development, and entropy, the tendency toward disorder, can contribute to the breakdown of living systems. Clinical death, the stopping of breathing and heartbeat, may in some cases be reversed, sometimes producing a near-death experience.

Defining death is difficult, partly because there is little consensus on how to define life and because life functions do not cease simultaneously across organ systems. Death was originally defined as the end of breathing and heartbeat, but cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) meant that this state was no longer strictly irreversible. For organisms with a brain, death can instead be focused on that organ. Some hold that all brain functions must cease; others hold that a person whose personality and identity are irretrievably lost should be considered dead even if the brainstem is alive. [[Brain death]] is sometimes used as a legal definition. It is diagnosed through clinical examination, apnea testing, and exclusion of confounding factors such as hypothermia or drug effects. Controversies include the rare reports of recovery and the ethics of withdrawing life support.

In the natural world, organisms die through predation, parasitism, competition, starvation, dehydration, natural disasters, disease, or old age. Some organisms, such as the immortal jellyfish, are biologically immortal yet can still die from other causes. Selective pressures cause organisms with unfavorable traits to die off, and [[Natural selection]] drives living things to avoid death. When all members of a clade have died, it is extinct; extinction events are periods of mass death in many species worldwide that cause a global loss of biodiversity.

As of the early 21st century, 56 million people die per year. As of 2022, an estimated almost 110 billion humans had died, roughly 94% of all humans to have ever lived. The most common reason is aging, and the most common cause is [[Cardiovascular disease|cardiovascular disease]]. An autopsy can be done to determine cause of death. Biogerontology seeks ways to eliminate death by natural aging, and humans attempt to reach their maximum lifespan through lifestyle changes such as calorie reduction, diet, and exercise. Humans also die through [[Suicide|suicide]], homicide, punishment for crimes, and war.

Because humans are aware of death, it is a central part of the human condition and universal to human culture. It has motivated religious, spiritual, ethical, and legal responses. Many cultures hold that individuals survive death in an [[Afterlife|afterlife]], as spirits, or through reincarnation, while monist forms of thought such as materialism and physicalism take other views. Customs for honoring the body include the [[Funeral|funeral]], cremation, and sky burial, and family and friends usually go through a process of grieving. The study of death is known as thanatology.

ENTRY:
Plateau forming the bulk of the Deccan Peninsula in western, central and southern India. It covers about 422,000 sq km (163,000 sq mi) and is bounded by the Eastern and [[Western Ghats]], which block rain-bearing winds, leaving the region drier than the coast. Its average elevation is about 600 m, and it slopes gently east toward the [[Bay of Bengal]], so major rivers such as the Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri flow that way. The northwestern [[Deccan Traps]] are layers of basalt from a massive volcanic eruption at the end of the Cretaceous period. In the later Middle Ages the [[Vijayanagara Empire]] held the lower plateau and the [[Deccan sultanates]] the upper. The area was under [[British Raj]] control for nearly two centuries before Indian independence in 1947.

ENTRY:
Large igneous province of west-central India, among the largest volcanic features on Earth. Its many layers of flood basalt are more than 2 km thick, cover about 500,000 sq km and have a volume of about 1 million cubic km. The eruptions occurred over 600,000 to 800,000 years, between about 66.3 and 65.6 million years ago, spanning the [[Cretaceous–Paleogene boundary]]. The eruptions are now generally considered to have played a minor role at most in the Cretaceous–Paleogene mass extinction, with the Chicxulub impact regarded as the primary cause. The province is thought to have been produced largely by the [[Réunion]] hotspot, which created the Mascarene Islands.

ENTRY:
Historiographical term for five medieval Persianate Muslim kingdoms of the [[Deccan Plateau]]: Ahmadnagar, Berar, Bidar, Bijapur and Golconda. They emerged after the Bahmani Sultanate disintegrated; Ahmadnagar declared independence in 1490, followed by Bijapur and Berar that year, Bidar around 1492 and Golconda in 1512. Their founders were of diverse origins: Bidar's by a Georgian slave and Golconda's by a slave of Iranian Turkmen origin. All based their legitimacy on succeeding the Bahmani Sultanate and used its coins. Although often rivals, the sultanates allied against the [[Vijayanagara Empire]] at the Battle of Talikota in 1565, which destroyed the city of Vijayanagara. The [[Mughal Empire]] took Berar in 1596 and Ahmadnagar between 1616 and 1636, and [[Aurangzeb]] conquered Golconda and Bijapur in 1686–87.

Last [[Dacians|Dacian]] king (r. 87–106). He fought three wars against the [[Roman Empire]] under two emperors. After raiding south across the [[Danube]], he defeated a Roman invasion under [[Domitian]], killing its commander Cornelius Fuscus, and secured a period of independence. He was crowned king after the aging Duras abdicated, and the Roman historian Cassius Dio called him a shrewd commander and a worthy antagonist of the Romans for a long time. [[Trajan]] invaded Dacia and defeated Decebalus in 102, after which he remained a client king. A final Roman invasion in 105 was followed by the destruction of the Dacian capital Sarmizegetusa in 106, and Decebalus died by [[Suicide|suicide]] to avoid capture.

Twelfth and final month of the year in the Julian and Gregorian calendars, with 31 days. Its name derives from Latin *decem*, meaning ten, because it was the tenth month of Romulus's calendar, c. 750 BC, which began in March. It is the first month of winter in the Northern Hemisphere, when the [[Winter solstice]] falls, and the first month of summer in the Southern Hemisphere. Its birth flower is the narcissus, and its birthstones are turquoise, zircon and tanzanite.

ENTRY:
Distribution of planning and decision-making away from a central authority, applied to organizations, politics, law, [[Public administration|public administration]], technology, economics and money. The word *centralisation* came into use in France in 1794, and *décentralisation* in the 1820s. In the early 20th century, a decentralist movement in the United States opposed the concentration of economic wealth and political power in large-scale industry. In government, decentralization usually means devolving administrative or fiscal authority to subnational governments. In technology it appears in distributed systems such as [[Blockchain|blockchain]]. The anarchist theorist [[Pierre-Joseph Proudhon]] summed up his political ideas as federation or decentralization. Leopold Kohr's 1957 book *The Breakdown of Nations* influenced E. F. Schumacher's *Small Is Beautiful* (1973).

ENTRY:
Logarithmic unit equal to one tenth of a bel, expressing the ratio of two power or root-power quantities. A tenfold change in power is 10 dB, and a tenfold change in amplitude is 20 dB. Two signals whose levels differ by one decibel have a power ratio of about 1.26. The unit originated in early 20th-century telephone measurement at the Bell System, and the bel is named after [[Alexander Graham Bell]]. The decibel is most widely used for sound in [[Acoustics]], and for [[Amplifier|amplifier]] gains, signal losses and [[Signal-to-noise ratio|signal-to-noise ratios]] in [[Electronics]].
