Set of [[Laboratory|laboratory]] techniques for separating [[Mixture|mixtures]] into their components. A mixture is dissolved in a fluid mobile phase, which carries it through a column, plate, or sheet coated with a stationary phase. Its constituents separate because they have different affinities for the stationary phase and travel at different apparent velocities. Botanist Mikhail Tsvet developed the method in the early 1900s to separate plant pigments such as [[Chlorophyll]], which form colored bands, and he coined the term. Preparative chromatography purifies components for later use at higher cost, while analytical chromatography uses smaller amounts of material to identify analytes or measure their proportions. Archer John Porter Martin and Richard Laurence Millington Synge won the 1952 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for partition chromatography, which encouraged paper, gas, and high-performance liquid chromatography.

The version best known in English was published by [[Charles Perrault]] as *Cendrillon* in 1697, which introduced the fairy godmother, the pumpkin carriage and the glass slipper. The [[Brothers Grimm]] published *Aschenputtel* in 1812. Disney's 1950 animated feature popularized the tale in the 20th century. The word Cinderella has come to mean someone who unexpectedly achieves success after a period of obscurity.

Film industry of mainland China, one of three historical threads of Chinese-language cinema, with those of Hong Kong and Taiwan. Foreign films were screened from 1896, and the first domestic feature, *Dingjun Mountain*, was produced in 1905. After the 1949 communist victory, film production was nationalized and focused on propaganda, nearly ceasing during the [[Cultural Revolution]] (1966–1976). Films remain subject to pre-approval by the National Radio and Television Administration, and [[Censorship|censorship]] shapes content.

Film industry of France and the film productions of French companies abroad. It is the oldest and largest precursor of national cinemas in Europe. The Lumière brothers launched cinematography in 1895 with *L'Arrivée d'un train en gare de La Ciotat*. By the early 1900s French cinema led globally, and [[Georges Méliès|Méliès]] made *A Trip to the Moon* (1902). After World War I, U.S. films flooded Europe and French cinema declined, prompting import quotas. The New Wave of the late 1950s and 1960s, led by [[Jean-Luc Godard]] and [[François Truffaut]], produced *Breathless* (1960) and *The 400 Blows* (1959). In 2013 France was the second-largest exporter of films after the United States. The Centre national du cinéma et de l'image animée (CNC), founded in 1946, regulates and funds the sector through levies on tickets. French films have won 12 Academy Awards for Best International Feature Film, second only to Italy's 14.

State-controlled film industry of the Soviet Union, from the 1920s to 1991. Films were made by the constituent republics under central regulation in Moscow, most prolifically the Russian republic, followed by Armenia, Azerbaijan, Georgia and Ukraine. After the 1917 [[Russian Revolution|Bolshevik Revolution]], [[Vladimir Lenin]] prioritized cinema as a tool of mass propaganda. Output peaked at around 50 features a year.

Motion picture industry of the United States, centered in Hollywood, California. It has influenced the global film industry since the early 20th century. In 1894 the first commercial motion-picture exhibition took place in New York City, using [[Thomas Edison]]'s kinetoscope. The United States produced the first sync-sound musical film, *The Jazz Singer* (1927). By the early 1920s the industry was the world's film capital, dominated by studios such as MGM, Warner Bros. and Paramount.
