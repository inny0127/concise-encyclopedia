Capital city of China (pop., more than 21.8 million), the world's most populous national capital and, after Shanghai, China's second-largest city by urban area. Formerly romanized as Peking, it lies in northern China on the [[North China Plain]] and is a provincial-level municipality of 16 districts, covering 16,410.54 sq km and mostly surrounded by Hebei province. It neighbors Tianjin to the southeast.

Human habitation is ancient: *Homo erectus* fossils at Zhoukoudian date to 230,000–250,000 years ago. The name Beijing, meaning Northern Capital, was applied in 1403 under the [[Ming dynasty]] to distinguish it from Nanjing, the Southern Capital. The city has been the country's political center for most of the past eight centuries, having been established as a capital by the Jin dynasty in 1151 and developed under the Yuan, Ming, and Qing dynasties. It is the seat of the [[Chinese Communist Party]] and central government, whose handling of dissent, notably the 1989 [[Tiananmen Square]] crackdown, has drawn international criticism.

The city has eight [[UNESCO]] World Heritage Sites, among them the [[Forbidden City]], the Temple of Heaven, the Summer Palace, and the Ming Tombs, alongside traditional hutong alleys and siheyuan courtyard houses. It hosts 176 foreign embassies and the headquarters of most of China's largest state-owned companies. [[Tsinghua University]] and [[Peking University]] are among its leading institutions, and the Zhongguancun area is a center of technology and entrepreneurship. Beijing Capital International Airport was Asia's busiest from 2009 to 2019, and Beijing Daxing International Airport has the largest single-structure airport terminal in the world. Beijing hosted the 2008 Summer Olympics and in 2022 became the first city to host both Summer and Winter Olympics.

ENTRY:
Subjective attitude that something is true or that a state of affairs is the case. In [[Epistemology|epistemology]], philosophers use the term for attitudes about the world that can be true or false: to believe that snow is white is comparable to accepting the truth of the [[Proposition|proposition]] "snow is white." Holding a belief does not require active [[Introspection|introspection]]; few people consider whether the sun will rise tomorrow, simply assuming that it will. Beliefs may be dispositional rather than actively thought.

Belief is one necessary condition for [[Knowledge|knowledge]] under the classical analysis of knowledge as justified true belief, a formulation challenged by Edmund Gettier's 1963 cases of justified true beliefs that fall short of knowledge.

Conceptions of belief differ, and there is no consensus. Representationalism, traditionally dominant, treats beliefs as attitudes toward mental representations, typically propositions. Functionalism defines beliefs by their causal role, and dispositionalism, a form of it, identifies them with dispositions to behave in certain ways (Roderick Chisholm). Interpretationism holds that an entity's beliefs depend on someone's interpretation of it (Daniel Dennett, Donald Davidson). Eliminativists such as Paul Churchland argue that nothing in nature corresponds to the folk concept of belief, while formal epistemologists would replace the all-or-nothing notion with credence, a spectrum of degrees of belief.

Directly struck idiophone, usually a hollow cup that vibrates in a single strong tone when struck. Bells are usually cast from [[Bronze|bell metal]], a type of bronze, though small ornamental bells may be made of glass, ceramic, or pressed metal. A bell is struck by an internal clapper, an external hammer, or, in small bells, a loose sphere as in a jingle bell. Eastern [[Indo-Aryan languages|Indo-Aryan]] language of the Indo-European family, also called Bangla, spoken chiefly in the [[Bengal]] region of South Asia. It has over 242 million native speakers and another 43 million second-language speakers as of 2025, making it the sixth most spoken native language and the seventh most spoken language overall.

Bengali is the official, national, and most widely spoken language of [[Bangladesh]], where 98% of the population uses it as a first language. It is the second most widely spoken language in India and the official language of [[West Bengal]], Tripura, and the Barak Valley region of Assam. It has been a second official language of Jharkhand since September 2011. The [[Government of India]] accorded Bengali classical-language status on October 3, 2024.

The language has developed over more than 1,400 years. A Sanskrit-Chinese dictionary compiled in 782 shows the presence of Bengali, and its ancestor was the language of the Pala Empire and the Sena dynasty. Middle Bengali absorbed Arabic and Persian vocabulary after Arab and Turco-Persian settlement in the 13th century, and Bengali gained prominence over Persian at the court of the Sultans of Bengal.

Bengali literature, with a millennium-old history, flourished during the Bengali Renaissance and produced figures such as [[Rabindranath Tagore]], who won the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1913. The Bengali language movement of 1948–56, demanding official status for Bengali in [[Pakistan]], fostered Bengali nationalism in East Bengal and contributed to the emergence of Bangladesh in 1971. In 1999 [[UNESCO]] recognized February 21 as International Mother Language Day in honor of the movement.
