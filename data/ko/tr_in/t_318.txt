Special functions defined on the surface of a [[Sphere|sphere]]. They are often used in solving [[Partial differential equation|partial differential equations]] in many scientific fields. Seed, fruit, root, bark, or other plant substance used primarily for flavoring or coloring food. Spices are distinguished from [[Herb|herbs]], the leaves, flowers, or stems of plants used for flavoring or garnish; both are seasonings. Spices are sometimes used in medicine, religious rituals, cosmetics, and perfume, and plant sweeteners such as sugar are not counted among them. A whole dried spice keeps longest, while a fresh spice such as ginger is usually more flavorful but costlier and shorter-lived. A 2019 review found that most claimed health benefits were not supported by clinical evidence, though it noted that polyphenols in spices may be beneficial. India contributes 75% of global spice production.

Defining spice is difficult. The *Oxford English Dictionary* places spices in the tropics, but caraway shows that they can come from temperate climates, and *The Oxford Companion to Food* notes that ambergris, of animal origin, is often called a spice.

Spices were used in small quantities, leaving few archaeological remains. A spice trade across the Indian subcontinent and the Middle East existed by 2000 BC, in [[Black pepper|black pepper]] and [[Cinnamon|cinnamon]]; [[Clove|cloves]] were used in Mesopotamia by 1700 BC. Discovery of the [[Monsoon|monsoon]] winds enabled sea trade to replace overland caravan routes. Spices appear in the Old Testament, and Pliny the Elder wrote about cloves. In medieval Europe they were among the most demanded and expensive goods, valued for balancing the "humors" under [[Humorism|humorism]] and for their association with "paradise." European demand helped drive exploration in the early modern period. [[Vasco da Gama]] reached India by sea in 1498, and European powers established outposts in spice-producing regions such as the [[Maluku Islands]].

Intrinsic form of [[Angular momentum|angular momentum]] carried by elementary particles, atomic nuclei and atoms. Spin is quantized, so it takes only discrete values, and accurate models of its interaction require relativistic [[Quantum mechanics|quantum mechanics]] or [[Quantum field theory|quantum field theory]]. The existence of electron spin was inferred from the Stern–Gerlach experiment, in which silver atoms showed two possible discrete angular momenta despite having no orbital angular momentum. The relativistic spin–statistics theorem connects spin to the [[Pauli exclusion principle|Pauli exclusion principle]]: particles that obey exclusion have half-integer spin, and half-integer spin implies exclusion. Spin is described mathematically as a vector for particles such as [[Photon|photons]] and as a spinor for particles such as electrons. In practice, spin is given as a dimensionless spin quantum number, found by dividing the spin angular momentum by the reduced [[Planck constant]] ħ.

Group of sporting events involving competitive running, jumping, and throwing. The most common forms are [[Track and field|track and field]], road running, cross-country running, and race walking. Races are decided by finishing position or time, and jumps and throws by the highest or furthest measurement from a series of attempts. Needing little expensive equipment, it is one of the most widely practiced sports in the world.

The word derives from the Ancient Greek *athlētēs*, "combatant in public games". In the 19th century it narrowed in Europe to running, walking, jumping, and throwing. In much of North America it refers to sports in general, and "track and field" is used instead.

Athletic contests are prehistoric in origin. Tombs in [[Ancient Egypt|ancient Egypt]] depict running and high jumping from as early as 2250 BC, and the Tailteann Games in Ireland, founded c. 1800 BC, included running and stone-throwing. The only event at the first [[Ancient Olympic Games|Olympics]] in 776 BC was the *stadion*, a stadium-length race. The first modern clubs were founded at the [[University of Cambridge]] in 1857 and the [[University of Oxford]] in 1860, and the Amateur Athletic Association, established in England in 1880, was the first national governing body.

World Athletics is the global governing body. The foremost international meeting is the World Athletics Championships. The Diamond League is the most prestigious season-long circuit for track and field. Combined events include the [[Decathlon|decathlon]].

Elastic object, typically of metal, bent or molded to return to shape after being compressed, extended or twisted. The spring constant, or rate, is the change in force divided by the change in deflection, and its inverse is compliance. Natural point where groundwater emerges from an [[Aquifer|aquifer]] and flows across the ground surface as surface water. Springs are part of the [[Hydrosphere|hydrosphere]] and the [[Water cycle|water cycle]], and have long been an important source of fresh water, especially in arid regions. They are driven out by natural forces such as gravity and hydrostatic pressure. One of the four temperate seasons, between winter and summer. Spring is also known as springtime and denotes rebirth, renewal and regrowth. When it is spring in the Northern Hemisphere, it is autumn in the Southern Hemisphere. At the [[Equinox|spring equinox]], also called the vernal equinox, days and nights are approximately twelve hours long, and daytime length increases until the [[Summer solstice|summer solstice]].
