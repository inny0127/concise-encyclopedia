Mountain range in West Asia. The Zagros Mountains extend about 1,600 km (990 miles) from southeastern [[Turkey]] through northern [[Iraq]] and western [[Iran]] to the [[Strait of Hormuz]]. The highest point is Mount Dena, at 4,409 m (14,465 ft). The range formed through the collision of the Eurasian and Arabian plates, mainly during the [[Miocene]], about 25 to 5 million years ago, which folded sedimentary rocks deposited from the [[Paleozoic]] onward. The folded rocks trap large petroleum deposits, and the Zagros is an important area for oil production.

Royal dynasty of China that existed for 790 years, from c. 1046 BC until 256 BC, the longest span of any dynasty in Chinese history. During the Western Zhou period (c. 1046–771 BC), the royal house had military control over territories centered on the Wei River valley and North China Plain. Its suzerainty became increasingly ceremonial in the Eastern Zhou period (771–256 BC), which included the [[Spring and Autumn period|Spring and Autumn period]] and the [[Warring States period|Warring States period]], marked by large-scale warfare among former client states. Ancient Chinese text that is one of the two foundational texts of [[Taoism|Taoism]], alongside the *Tao Te Ching*. Written in the late [[Warring States period|Warring States]] period and named for its traditional author, Zhuang Zhou, it consists of stories, maxims, allegories, parables and fables, often irreverent or humorous. Its themes include spontaneity and freedom from human conventions, and it illustrates the arbitrariness of dichotomies such as good and bad, life and death, or human and nature. The only complete version has 33 chapters, prepared around AD 300 by Guo Xiang from an earlier form of 52. The first seven chapters are traditionally attributed to Zhuang Zhou himself, while the rest are generally considered later accretions. Regarded as one of the greatest works of [[Classical Chinese|Classical Chinese]] literature, it has influenced Chinese writers for more than two millennia.
