In three dimensions, a solid rotates around an axis of rotation. A rotation about an internal axis through the body's center of mass is called a spin, and Earth's rotation defines the geographical poles. A rotation around an external axis is a [[Orbit|revolution]], such as [[Earth's orbit]] around the [[Sun]]. Mathematically, a rotation is a rigid body movement that, unlike a translation, keeps at least one point fixed. Rotations around a point or axis form a [[Group (mathematics)|group]], and any spatial rotation can be decomposed into rotations around the x, y and z axes.

[[Casino|Casino]] game of chance, named after the French word for "little wheel." A player may bet on a single number, groups of numbers, red or black, odd or even, or high or low. The croupier spins the wheel one way and a ball the other way around a track, and the ball falls into a numbered and colored pocket. Winning bets are then paid. ENTRY:
Computer networking device that forwards data packets between [[Computer network|computer networks]], including the global [[Internet]]. A router reads the network address in each packet header and uses its routing table or policy to send the packet toward its destination. Home and small-office routers forward IP packets between home computers and the Internet, while enterprise routers connect large networks to core routers on the [[Optical fiber|optical fiber]] backbone. Routers are mostly purpose-built embedded systems, though early models ran software on a general-purpose CPU. Advanced models use application-specific integrated circuits (ASICs) to improve performance and add filtering and firewall functions. The first true IP router was developed by Ginny Travers at BBN from 1975 to 1976 as part of a DARPA-initiated effort that created the TCP/IP architecture.
