ENTRY:
Electric current alternating in direction, unlike [[Direct current|direct current]] (DC). It is the form in which electric power is delivered to homes and businesses, and its usual waveform is a sine wave. Power is transmitted at high voltage because the power lost in a wire equals the square of the current multiplied by the wire's resistance (P = I²R); halving the current by doubling the voltage cuts the loss by a factor of four. [[Transformer|Transformers]] step voltage up for transmission and down for use, typically to 100 V–240 V for domestic supply. Alternating current prevailed over direct current in the late 19th century; Niagara Falls was electrified with AC transmission lines in 1895.

ENTRY:
Concern for the well-being and life of others, independent of personal benefit or reciprocity. The word was popularized, and possibly coined, by French philosopher [[Auguste Comte]] around 1830 as an antonym of egoism. Altruism is an important moral value in many cultures and religions, and it can extend to other sentient beings and future generations. In biology, altruism is an action that costs the actor, in time, energy or survival probability, while benefiting another without reciprocity. Psychological egoism holds that no act is truly altruistic, since the actor may gain an intrinsic reward; whether such rewards count as benefits is disputed. Effective altruism uses evidence and reason to determine the most effective ways to benefit others.

Chemical element (symbol Al, atomic number 13), a post-transition metal in the [[Boron|boron]] group. Silvery, soft, ductile and nonmagnetic, aluminum has a density about one-third that of steel. It forms a protective oxide layer when exposed to air. It is the third-most abundant element in Earth’s crust, after [[Oxygen|oxygen]] and [[Silicon|silicon]], occurring mainly in rock rather than as free metal, and is obtained industrially from [[Bauxite|bauxite]]. Its discovery was announced in 1825 by [[Hans Christian Ørsted]]. In 1954 aluminum became the most produced non-ferrous metal, surpassing [[Copper|copper]]. Most is used in transportation, engineering, construction and packaging. No living thing is known to metabolize aluminum salts, though plants and animals tolerate them well.

ENTRY:
Progressive neurodegenerative disease and the most common cause of [[Dementia|dementia]], accounting for around 60–70% of cases. It usually begins with difficulty remembering recent events. As it advances it can cause problems with language, disorientation, mood swings and withdrawal from family and society. Its progression is characterized by amyloid plaques and neurofibrillary tangles in the [[Cerebral cortex|cerebral cortex]], which lead to irreversible loss of [[Neuron|neurons]]. During the [[American Civil War]], wagons carrying wounded soldiers from the field were called ambulance wagons.

Emperor of Ethiopia (r. 1314–44) of the Solomonic dynasty. He is known mainly from *The Glorious Victories*, a Ge'ez chronicle of his 1332–1333 campaign against Ifat and Adal. Scholars disagree on its date: some regard it as a contemporary account, while Bertrand Hirsch proposes that it is a 15th-century epic that condenses about a century of conflict with the sultans of Ifat into his reign. Most of his wars were against [[Muslims|Muslim]] sultanates to the southeast, which he generally defeated, enlarging the kingdom by incorporating smaller states. His conquests extended Christian territory and power for centuries, and they supported the spread of [[Christianity]] to frontier areas. Edward Ullendorff called him "one of the most outstanding Ethiopian kings of any age."

ENTRY:
Italian scientist known for molecular theory. Avogadro's law states that equal volumes of gases at the same temperature and pressure contain equal numbers of molecules. The hypothesis resolved discrepancies in combining volumes noted by [[Joseph Louis Gay-Lussac]] and helped distinguish atoms from molecules in gases. It gained wide acceptance only after Stanislao Cannizzaro highlighted it at the 1860 Karlsruhe Congress. The [[Avogadro constant]], 6.02214076×10²³ mol⁻¹, is named in his honor and is one of the seven defining constants of the [[International System of Units|SI]].

American breed of zebuine-taurine hybrid beef [[Cattle|cattle]]. The breed has a high tolerance of heat, sunlight and humidity, and good resistance to parasites. It has been exported to many [[Tropics|tropical]] countries, and in Australia it is the most numerous breed of cattle.
