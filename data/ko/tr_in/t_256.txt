Progressive neurodegenerative disease, primarily of the [[Central nervous system|central nervous system]]. The loss of [[Dopamine|dopamine]]-producing neurons in the substantia nigra of the brainstem causes the motor symptoms, known as parkinsonism: tremor, bradykinesia (slowness in initiating movement), rigidity and postural instability. Non-motor symptoms include sleep abnormalities, loss of smell, [[Depression (mood)|depression]], cognitive impairment, psychosis and anxiety. Misfolded alpha-synuclein proteins aggregate into Lewy bodies. No single cause is known; genetic and environmental factors interact. Onset is typically in people over 60, and the condition affects about one percent of that group. No cure is known. Levodopa, MAO-B inhibitors and dopamine agonists improve symptoms initially but become less effective as the disease progresses, and deep brain stimulation is used for severe motor symptoms. Law-making body, or [[Legislature|legislature]], of a state. A parliament generally represents the electorate, makes laws and oversees the executive government through hearings and inquiries. The term is commonly used in current or former monarchies, and a parliament is the institutional form of parliamentary systems. Medieval parliamentary gatherings established that monarchs were subject to law. The Cortes of León in 1188 and an English parliament in 1265 summoned representatives of common people, and the English body developed into the House of Commons. The [[Glorious Revolution]] of 1688 established the primacy of parliamentary sovereignty in Britain. Expansion of [[Suffrage|suffrage]] in the 19th and 20th centuries led many parliaments to become democratically elected.

Unit of length used in astronomy, equal to about 3.26 [[Light-year|light-years]]. The parsec measures distances to objects outside the [[Solar System]]; it is equal to about 206,265 astronomical units (au), or 30.9 trillion kilometers. It is defined as the distance at which 1 au subtends an angle of one arcsecond, a relation derived from [[Parallax|parallax]] and [[Trigonometry|trigonometry]]. The nearest star, [[Proxima Centauri]], is about 1.3 parsecs (4.2 light-years) from the Sun, while the [[Andromeda Galaxy]] lies at over 700,000 parsecs (2,283,000 ly). The word was coined by Herbert Hall Turner in 1913 as a shortened form of "parallax of one arcsecond". Multiples include kiloparsecs (kpc), megaparsecs (Mpc) and gigaparsecs (Gpc). In August 2015 the International Astronomical Union (IAU) passed Resolution B2, which mentions an explicit definition of the parsec as exactly 648000/π au.

Derivative of a function of several variables with respect to one variable, with the others held constant. If all partial derivatives exist and are continuous near a point, the function is termed a C1 function there. Partial derivatives are used in [[Vector calculus]] and [[Differential geometry]]. Branch of physics, also called high-energy physics, that studies the fundamental particles and forces that constitute matter and radiation. The [[Standard Model]], the dominant theory, classifies fundamental particles as fermions (matter particles) and bosons (force-carrying particles). Ordinary matter is made only from the first of three fermion generations: up and down [[Quark|quarks]], which form protons and neutrons, and electrons and electron neutrinos. Quarks cannot exist alone but form hadrons; baryons contain an odd number of quarks and mesons an even number. Every particle has an antiparticle with the same mass and opposite electric charge, such as the positron for the electron, and such particles could form [[Antimatter|antimatter]].

The idea that matter is composed of elementary particles dates from at least the 6th century BC. Nuclear fission was proved in 1939 by [[Lise Meitner]], based on experiments by Otto Hahn, and nuclear fusion by [[Hans Bethe]] the same year. In the 1950s and 1960s collisions at ever higher energies revealed a bewildering "particle zoo," which the Standard Model, formulated in the 1970s, explained as combinations of a small number of more fundamental particles. It also predicted the [[Higgs boson]]; on July 4, 2012, physicists at the [[Large Hadron Collider]] at [[CERN]] announced a new particle that behaves as expected for it.

The Standard Model agrees with almost all experimental tests, but most particle physicists consider it incomplete. It does not incorporate gravity, and measurements of neutrino mass provide the first experimental deviations from it.

Ocean liners emphasized speed and traditional luxury and were often built to cross the [[Atlantic Ocean]] between Europe and the [[United States]]. Cruise ships value amenities such as pools and casinos over speed, and since the 1980s they have grown to carry hundreds to thousands of people, with activity centered in the [[Caribbean Sea]].
