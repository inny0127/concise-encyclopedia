Ring-shaped component surrounding a [[Wheel|wheel's]] rim, spelled tire in Commonwealth English. A tire transfers a vehicle's load from the [[Axle]] to the ground and provides [[Friction|traction]]. Group of similar cells and their extracellular matrix, from the same embryonic origin, that performs a specific function. Tissues lie between cells and [[Organ (biology)|organs]], which are formed by grouping several tissues. The word derives from the French *tissu*, "fabric." The study of tissues is called histology; Xavier Bichat is considered its father. In plants, tissues fall into three systems: the [[Epidermis (botany)|epidermis]], [[Ground tissue]] and [[Vascular tissue]]. Meristematic tissue, found at growing tips, consists of actively dividing cells that increase a plant's length and thickness. Ground tissue makes nutrients by [[Photosynthesis]] and stores reserves. Animal tissues are classified as epithelial, [[Connective tissue]], [[Muscle]] and [[Nervous tissue]].

Small, stocky woodland [[Passerine|passerine]] bird of the family Paridae, found mainly in the [[Northern Hemisphere]] and Africa. Eurasian and African members are called tits, while [[North America|North American]] species are called chickadees or titmice. ENTRY:
Largest moon of [[Saturn]] and second largest in the [[Solar System]], after Jupiter's [[Ganymede (moon)|Ganymede]]. [[Christiaan Huygens]] discovered it in 1655. Titan is the only moon known to have a dense atmosphere, denser than Earth's, and the only known object besides Earth with stable bodies of surface liquid. Its atmosphere is mainly nitrogen and [[Methane]], forming hydrocarbon clouds and haze. The Cassini–Huygens mission, which arrived in 2004, revealed hydrocarbon lakes in the polar regions. At about 94 K (−179 °C), its [[Water cycle|methane cycle]] nearly resembles Earth's water cycle, and it is sometimes called the most Earth-like object in the Solar System.

Tobacco contains the addictive stimulant alkaloid [[Nicotine|nicotine]], making it among the most widely used recreational drugs. Its use causes or raises the risk of many deadly diseases, especially those of the [[Heart|heart]], liver and [[Lung|lungs]], and of many cancers. In 2008 the [[World Health Organization]] named tobacco use the world's single greatest preventable cause of death. The word derives from Spanish *tabaco*, probably from the Taíno language of the Caribbean. Tobacco was used ceremonially in the Americas for thousands of years, with some cultivation in Mexico dating to 1400–1000 BCE. After European contact it spread worldwide, and the English colony of [[Jamestown]] used it as currency and an export crop. Its demand was closely tied to [[Slavery]] in the Caribbean.

City (pop., 2020: 270,871) in Ohio, United States, and county seat of Lucas County. ENTRY:
Colorless, water-insoluble aromatic hydrocarbon, C₆H₅CH₃, also called toluol or methylbenzene. It has a sweet smell and contributes to the aroma of [[Gasoline|gasoline]] and paint fumes. It is mainly used as an industrial feedstock and solvent, including in some paint thinners and glues. Nitration of toluene yields explosives such as trinitrotoluene ([[TNT]]). It was first isolated in 1837 and named *toluène* by Auguste Cahours in 1850. Recreational inhalation can cause severe neurological harm.
