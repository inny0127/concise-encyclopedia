ENTRY:
First imperial dynasty of China, named for the state of Qin, a fief of the [[Zhou dynasty]]. Qin conquered the rival states of the [[Warring States period]] from 230 BC and unified China in 221 BC. Its king, Ying Zheng, took the title [[Qin Shi Huang]], or first emperor of China. Reforms by [[Shang Yang]] strengthened the state, and under Qin the currency, weights, measures and [[Writing system|writing system]] were standardized. The dynasty built the walls that became the [[Great Wall of China]], a national road system and the mausoleum of Qin Shi Huang, guarded by the [[Terracotta Army]]. Lasting only 14 years, with two emperors, it was the shortest of any major Chinese dynasty. It collapsed in revolt after Qin Shi Huang's death, and the [[Han dynasty]] that followed kept many of its administrative practices.

Manchu-led imperial dynasty of China, the last in Chinese history, which ruled from 1644 to 1912. It grew from the Later Jin state founded by [[Nurhaci]] in 1616, and his son [[Hong Taiji]] proclaimed the Great Qing in 1636. Qing forces seized [[Beijing]] from the [[Ming dynasty]] in 1644. At its height the empire stretched from the Sea of Japan to the Pamir Mountains, and under the [[Qianlong Emperor]] it assembled the territorial base of modern China. The [[Taiping Rebellion]] and the Dungan Revolt caused the deaths of over 20 million people. Defeat in the [[First Sino-Japanese War]] (1894–1895) cost China its influence over Korea and led to the cession of Taiwan to the [[Empire of Japan]]. Reforms after 1900 included elections, a new legal code and the abolition of the imperial examination system, but Manchu conservatives later blocked them. The Wuchang Uprising of October 10, 1911 led to the Xinhai Revolution, and the abdication of the Xuantong Emperor on February 12, 1912 ended the dynasty.

ENTRY:
Second-degree polynomial equation of the form ax² + bx + c = 0, where a ≠ 0. The name comes from the Latin *quadratus*, "square". The values of x that satisfy it are called roots or zeros. With [[Real number|real]] coefficients, it has two distinct real roots, one repeated real root, or two complex conjugate roots. Solutions can be found by factoring, by completing the square, or with the [[Quadratic formula]], x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac))/2a. The discriminant, b² − 4ac, determines which case applies. Problems of this kind were solved as early as 2000 BC, and the graph of a quadratic function is a [[Parabola|parabola]] whose x-intercepts are its roots.

ENTRY:
Closed-form expression for the solutions of a quadratic equation in elementary algebra. For coefficients a, b and c, with a ≠ 0, the roots are x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac))/2a, where the ± sign gives two roots. The quantity Δ = b² − 4ac is the discriminant: if it is positive, the roots are two distinct real numbers; if zero, one repeated real root; and if negative, two complex conjugate roots. Geometrically, the roots are the points where the [[Parabola|parabola]] crosses the x-axis. Completing the square yields the same solutions.

Fundamental physical theory describing the behavior of matter and light at and below the scale of atoms. There are limits to how accurately a physical quantity can be predicted before measurement, expressed in the [[Uncertainty principle|uncertainty principle]]: no preparation allows precise prediction of both position and momentum at once.

Current and most recent period of the Cenozoic Era, spanning from 2.58 million years ago to the present. It follows the [[Neogene]] period and comprises two epochs: the [[Pleistocene]], to about 12,000 years ago, and the [[Holocene]]. A proposed third epoch, the [[Anthropocene]], was rejected in 2024 by the International Union of Geological Sciences. The period is defined by Quaternary glaciation, the cyclic growth and decay of continental [[Ice sheet|ice sheets]] related to [[Milankovitch cycles]]. It spans the time during which recognizable humans have existed. The Last Glacial Maximum, about 26,500–19,000 years ago, saw ice sheets cover much of North America and Eurasia. Periodic closings of the [[Bering Strait]] formed a land bridge between Asia and North America.

ENTRY:
Four-dimensional number system that extends the [[Complex number|complex numbers]]. Quaternions have four real-number components, written a + bi + cj + dk, and their multiplication is not commutative. Irish mathematician and physicist [[William Rowan Hamilton]] first described them in 1843. They form a four-dimensional associative normed division algebra over the real numbers, and they represent rotations in [[Three-dimensional space|three-dimensional space]]. Applications include [[Computer graphics|computer graphics]], [[Robotics]] and magnetic resonance imaging. Unit quaternions form a group isomorphic to SU(2), the universal cover of the rotation group SO(3).

ENTRY:
Basic unit of quantum information, the quantum counterpart of the classical [[Bit|bit]]. A qubit can be physically realized with any two-level quantum system, such as the spin of an [[Electron|electron]] or the polarization of a [[Photon|photon]]. Unlike a bit, which is either 0 or 1, a qubit can be in a coherent superposition of both states. Measuring it yields one of two outcomes and disturbs the superposition. The term was coined by Benjamin Schumacher, who wrote in a 1995 paper that it was created in jest during a conversation with William Wootters.

Largest province of [[Canada]] by area and its only Francophone-majority province. Quebec is the second most populous province, after [[Ontario]], with 8,501,833 residents in the 2021 census. Between 1534 and 1763 it was the French colony of Canada, the most developed colony in [[New France]]. After the [[Seven Years' War]] it became British, and in 1867 it was confederated with Ontario, Nova Scotia and New Brunswick. The Catholic Church played a large role in its institutions until the Quiet Revolution of the 1960s to 1980s expanded the provincial government's role. Its economy rests on services and industry, with exports including aeronautics, hydroelectricity, mining, pharmaceuticals, aluminum, wood and paper. Quebec is known for maple syrup, comedy and ice hockey.

Indigenous people of South America who speak the [[Quechuan languages]]. Their languages originated among the Indigenous people of [[Peru]], where most Quechua speakers live, with significant populations in [[Ecuador]], [[Bolivia]], Chile, Colombia and Argentina. The [[Inca Empire]], the largest empire of the pre-Columbian era, was one of many peoples who spoke Quechua, and it promoted the language as its official tongue. Other historical groups include the Chanka and Huanca of Peru and the Cañari of Ecuador, who adopted Quechua from the Incas. Traditional life is tied to local economies, with agriculture at lower altitudes and pastoral farming in the higher Puna. Land is usually owned by the local community, or ayllu. Speakers number about 5.1 million in Peru, 1.8 million in Bolivia and 2.5 million in Ecuador, though some dialects are not mutually intelligible.

ENTRY:
Indigenous language family of the Andes that originated in central Peru. Derived from a common ancestral Proto-Quechua language, it is the most widely spoken pre-Columbian language family of the Americas, with an estimated 8–10 million speakers in 2004 and just under 7 million in the most recent census data available up to 2011. Quechua was the primary language of the [[Inca Empire]], though it had spread across the central Andes for centuries before the Incas. Spanish authorities officially recognized it, and the [[Catholic Church]] used it for evangelization; Domingo de Santo Tomás published a grammar in 1560. After the [[Túpac Amaru II]] rebellion, colonial officials banned it from public use in Peru in the late 18th century. Today it is the majority language in several regions of Peru and, as Kichwa, the second most-spoken language of Ecuador after Spanish.
