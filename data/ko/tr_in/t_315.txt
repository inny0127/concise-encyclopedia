Country in the [[Horn of Africa|Horn of Africa]], the easternmost country in continental Africa, officially the Federal Republic of Somalia. It borders [[Ethiopia|Ethiopia]] to the west, [[Djibouti|Djibouti]] to the northwest and [[Kenya|Kenya]] to the southwest, with the [[Gulf of Aden|Gulf of Aden]] to the north and the [[Indian Ocean|Indian Ocean]] to the east. It has the longest coastline on the African mainland. Its population is estimated at over 19 million, of whom 2.7 million live in the capital and largest city, [[Mogadishu|Mogadishu]]. About 85% of residents are ethnic Somalis, and the overwhelming majority are Sunni Muslims. The Somali sultanates were colonized by Italian and British empires in the late 19th century and merged into an independent republic in 1960. The country is among the least developed in the world, with an informal economy based mainly on [[Livestock|livestock]], remittances and telecommunications. Insurgents still control much of central and southern Somalia.

ENTRY:
Subset of the [[Sensory nervous system|sensory nervous system]] that detects touch, temperature, pain and body position. Its main functions are perceiving external and internal stimuli and regulating body position and balance (proprioception). It has been thought of as having two subdivisions: one for mechanosensory information, such as light touch, vibration, pressure and tension in the [[Skin]], and one for nociception, the detection of pain and temperature deemed harmful. Mechanoreceptors such as tactile corpuscles, which respond to pressure and vibration, are among the cutaneous receptors. Signals travel to the [[Spinal cord]], where they may drive a [[Reflex|reflex]], and to the brain for conscious perception. Neighboring neurons in the somatosensory cortex represent nearby areas of skin, forming a sensory homunculus. As of 2024, debate continued over the underlying mechanisms of the model.

ENTRY:
Method of using sound propagation, usually underwater, to navigate, measure distances and detect objects. It stands for sound navigation and ranging. Passive sonar listens for sounds made by vessels, while active sonar emits pulses of sound and listens for their [[Reflection (physics)|echoes]]. Sonar is also used for robot navigation and, in its upward-looking in-air form called sodar, for atmospheric research. The first recorded use, attributed to [[Leonardo da Vinci]] in 1490, involved a tube inserted into water to detect vessels by ear. Sonar was developed during [[World War I]] to counter the threat of [[Submarine|submarine]] warfare, and an operational passive system was in use by 1918.

Multi-movement instrumental composition, usually of three or four movements, which may be written for various instruments. By the early 19th century the sonata represented a principle of composing large-scale works, and it was regarded, with the [[Fugue]], as one of two fundamental methods of organizing concert music. After the Baroque period most sonatas were written for a solo instrument, most often a keyboard instrument, or for a solo instrument with keyboard accompaniment. Arcangelo Corelli's twelve chamber trio sonatas of Op. 2 (1685) were foundational to the form. A short or technically easy sonata is called a sonatina. Notable examples include [[Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart|Mozart]]'s Piano Sonata No. 11 in A major, K. 331, and [[Ludwig van Beethoven|Beethoven]]'s Piano Sonata No. 14 in C-sharp minor, Op. 27 No. 2, known as the "Moonlight".

Genus of flowering plants in the daisy family ([[Asteraceae]]), commonly known as sow thistles. They are annual, biennial or perennial herbs, and a few are woody, restricted to the [[Canary Islands]] and [[Madeira]]. The plants exude a milky latex when cut, and the common name comes from the belief that feeding them to lactating sows would increase milk production. Bird of the suborder Passeri, one of the two major lineages of [[Bird|perching birds]], with about 4,000 species. The other lineage, the Tyranni, has about 1,000 species. ENTRY:
Sound produced by shock waves from an object traveling faster than the [[Speed of sound|speed of sound]]. A sonic boom sounds similar to an explosion or a [[Thunder|thunderclap]], and the crack of a supersonic bullet is a small example. [[Mach number|Mach 1]] is about 1,192 km/h (741 mph) at sea level and 20 °C (68 °F). Pressure disturbance propagating through an elastic material medium. In physics it is characterized as a mechanical wave of pressure or related quantities such as displacement; in physiological and psychological contexts the term implies a hearing process. The human [[Ear|ear]] is sensitive to frequencies from 20 Hz to 20 kHz, though [[Hearing|hearing]] sensitivity varies among organisms. A sound source, such as the vibrating diaphragm of a loudspeaker, sets the surrounding medium in motion, and the disturbance travels away at the local [[Speed of sound|speed of sound]]. Digital recording samples the signal at equal intervals and stores it as [[Binary number|binary numbers]]. Automatic music reproduction dates to at least the 9th century, when the Banū Mūsā brothers built a water-powered organ that played interchangeable cylinders. Practical recording arrived with [[Thomas Edison|Thomas Edison's]] phonograph in 1877, and digital formats reached the market in the 1970s and 1980s through the [[Compact disc|compact disc]].
