Series of armed conflicts between Greek city-states and the [[Achaemenid Empire]] from 499 BC to 449 BC. Also called the Persian Wars, they grew out of Persian rule over Ionia, which [[Cyrus the Great]] conquered in 547 BC. The Ionian Revolt of 499 BC, incited by Aristagoras of Miletus, prompted the Persian king [[Darius the Great]] to punish Athens and Eretria, which had helped capture Sardis. A Persian force was defeated at the [[Battle of Marathon]] in 490 BC. In 480 BC [[Xerxes I]] invaded with a large army, winning the [[Battle of Thermopylae]] and razing Athens. The Persian fleet was defeated at Salamis that year, and the Persian army at Plataea in 479 BC. The Greek [[Delian League]] then campaigned against Persian forces in Asia Minor and Cyprus. The conflicts ended around 449 BC, and some ancient sources say the purported Peace of Callias formally resolved them.

Country in southeastern Europe on the Balkan peninsula, officially the Hellenic Republic. Its capital, [[Athens]], is the largest city, and the population is around 10 million. With up to 227 inhabited islands, the country has the longest coastline on the Mediterranean basin. The region was home to the [[Ancient Greece|ancient Greek]] city-states, which are regarded as the birthplace of democracy, historiography, political science, and the [[Olympic Games]]. [[Philip II of Macedon]] united most of the area in the 4th century BC. Greece joined the [[European Union]] in 1981 and has a developed, high-income economy in which shipping and tourism are major sectors.

Insatiable desire for material or social gain, often at the expense of others. Also called avarice, greed may target food, money, land, possessions, status, or power. Modern economic thought frequently distinguishes greed from self-interest. In Classical Greek thought, [[Plato]] and [[Aristotle]] discussed an unjust desire to gain at others' expense. [[Christianity]] counts avarice among the [[Seven deadly sins]], and the Sanskrit *Dharmashastras* call greed the root of all immorality. As a psychological concept, greed is an inordinate desire to acquire more than one needs, and it has been found to relate to financial behavior, unethical behavior, and lower well-being.

Script used to write the [[Greek language]] since the late 9th or early 8th century BC. It was derived from the earlier [[Phoenician alphabet]] and is the earliest known alphabetic script to write [[Vowel|vowels]] systematically as well as [[Consonant|consonants]]. The Ionic-based Euclidean alphabet of 24 letters, ordered from alpha to omega, became standard by the end of the 4th century BC and is still used for Greek writing. Throughout antiquity Greek had only one uppercase form of each letter, and Byzantine scribes had begun using lowercase forms by the 9th century. The alphabet is the ancestor of the [[Latin script]], the [[Cyrillic script]], and other scripts, and its letters serve as international symbols in mathematics, science, and other fields. Ancient and Modern Greek use different sound values and diacritics.

[[Indo-European languages|Indo-European language]] forming an independent Hellenic branch of that family. It is native to Greece, Cyprus, Egypt, Turkey, Italy (in Calabria and Salento), southern Albania, and other regions of the Balkans, Caucasus, Black Sea coast, and Eastern Mediterranean. It has the longest documented history of any Indo-European language, with at least 3,400 years of written records. Its writing system, the [[Greek alphabet]], has been used for approximately 2,800 years; earlier, Greek was written in Linear B and the Cypriot syllabary. The earliest written evidence is a Linear B tablet found in Messenia that dates to between 1450 and 1350 BC.

The language is conventionally divided into Proto-Greek, Mycenaean Greek, Ancient Greek, Koine Greek, Medieval or Byzantine Greek, and Modern Greek. Koine, the first common Greek dialect, arose from the fusion of Ionian with Attic and can be traced to the armies and conquered territories of [[Alexander the Great]]. It was spoken from Egypt to the fringes of India and became the first or second language of the Roman Empire. Koine was the original language of the New Testament, and the Hebrew Bible was translated into it as the [[Septuagint]]. Medieval Greek continued Koine until the end of the [[Byzantine Empire]] in the 15th century.

The epic poetry of [[Homer]] begins an ancient Greek literature of lasting importance, and many foundational texts of science and philosophy were composed in Greek. In its modern form Greek is the official language of Greece and Cyprus and one of the 24 official languages of the [[European Union]]. It is spoken by at least 13.5 million people. Greek roots are widely used to coin new words, and Greek and Latin remain the predominant sources of international scientific vocabulary in [[English language|English]] and other languages.

Body of myths and genre of folklore originating among the ancient Greeks, concerning the origin and nature of the world, the lives of deities and heroes, and the significance of cult and ritual practices. Together with [[Roman mythology]] it forms classical mythology. Modern scholars study the myths for what they reveal about the religious and political institutions of [[Ancient Greece]] and about mythmaking itself.

The myths were first transmitted in an oral-poetic tradition, most likely by Minoan and Mycenaean singers beginning in the 18th century BC. The myths of the heroes of the Trojan War and its aftermath became part of the oral tradition behind [[Homer]]'s epic poems, the *Iliad* and the *Odyssey*. [[Hesiod]]'s *Theogony* and *Works and Days* contain accounts of the genesis of the world, the succession of divine rulers, the human ages, and the origin of sacrificial practices. Myths also survive in the Homeric Hymns, the Epic Cycle, lyric poetry, fifth-century BC drama, and Hellenistic and Roman-era writers such as Plutarch and Pausanias.

Pictorial evidence is equally important. Vase paintings, votive gifts, and other artifacts depict gods, heroes, and mythic episodes; geometric pottery of the eighth century BC shows scenes from the Epic Cycle and the adventures of [[Heracles]].

The Olympian gods, who reside on [[Mount Olympus]] under [[Zeus]], include Poseidon, Hades, Hera, Athena, and Apollo. Heroes such as Heracles and Theseus, slayer of the [[Minotaur]], serve as intermediaries between gods and humans. The myths have had an extensive influence on the culture, arts, and literature of Western civilization.

Members of the class Chlorophyceae undergo [[Mitosis|closed mitosis]], while charophytes and land plants undergo open mitosis.
