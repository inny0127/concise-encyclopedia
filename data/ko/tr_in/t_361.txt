Geographic region of modern Romania and one of two historical principalities that laid the foundation for the modern Romanian state. It lies north of the [[Danube|Lower Danube]] and south of the Southern Carpathians. Wallachia began to form as a principality around the 13th century, and by 1330 Basarab I had defeated the [[Kingdom of Hungary]] at the Battle of Posada. ENTRY:
Flat case or pouch used to carry small personal items such as currency, debit and [[Credit card|credit cards]], identification and photographs. Wallets are usually made of fabric or leather and are pocket-sized and foldable. They may include a money clip, coin purse, zipper or strap, and specialized versions hold passports or checkbooks. The word originated in late 14th-century English, meaning "bag" or "knapsack," and the modern meaning of a flat case for paper money is first recorded in 1834 in American English. The ancient Greek *kibisis*, the pouch carried by [[Hermes]] and the sack in which [[Perseus]] carried the severed head of [[Medusa]], is typically translated as "wallet." Wallets developed after paper currency reached the West in the 1600s, and the modern bi-fold wallet with multiple card slots was standardized in the early 1950s with the introduction of the first credit cards.

Edible [[Seed|seed]] of any tree of the genus *Juglans*, most often the Persian or English walnut, *J. regia*. After ripening, the shell is discarded and the kernel eaten. Walnuts ripen between September and November in the northern hemisphere. The antioxidants in the brown seed coat protect the oil-rich kernel from atmospheric oxygen, preventing rancidity. Hulls contain polyphenols that stain hands and can irritate the skin, and juglone reached 2–4% of fresh weight in the husks. The Persian walnut, which originated in Iran, is the species most often grown, and nearly all cultivars are hybrids of it. Poor storage lets molds produce aflatoxin, a potent [[Carcinogen|carcinogen]]; walnuts keep best below 25 °C (77 °F) in low humidity.

Large [[Pinniped|pinniped]] marine mammal with prominent tusks and whiskers, *Odobenus rosmarus*. It is the only living species in the family Odobenidae. Walruses range across the Arctic Ocean and subarctic seas of the Northern Hemisphere, in two subspecies, the Atlantic and the Pacific. Adult Pacific males can weigh more than 2,000 kilograms (4,400 pounds), and among pinnipeds only the two elephant seal species exceed them in size. They live mostly in shallow water over the [[Continental shelf|continental shelves]], feeding on benthic bivalve molluscs and often resting on [[Sea ice|sea ice]]. Considered a keystone species of the Arctic marine region, the walrus figures prominently in the cultures of Indigenous Arctic peoples. In the 19th and early 20th centuries, commercial hunting for blubber, ivory, leather and meat sharply reduced populations. They have partly recovered, but Atlantic and Laptev populations remain fragmented and low. The IUCN lists the species as Vulnerable, citing sea ice loss linked to Arctic warming.

Armed conflict between the armed forces of states, or between governmental forces and armed groups organized under a command structure and able to sustain military operations, or between such organized groups. War is generally characterized by widespread violence, destruction and mortality, fought with regular or irregular forces, and its aims are typically political, economic or territorial. Warfare refers to the activities and characteristics of types of war, or of wars in general. Total war is warfare not restricted to legitimate military targets and can cause massive civilian suffering.

The English word derives from the 11th-century [[Old English]] *wyrre* and *werre*, from Old French *werre*, from Frankish *\*werra*, ultimately from Proto-Germanic *\*werzō* "mixture, confusion."

Anthropologists disagree about whether warfare was common throughout prehistory or arose later, after agriculture or organized states. It is hard to establish for the Paleolithic because few remains survive; evidence of violent conflict appears to increase in the Mesolithic, from about 10,000 years ago. Since 1945, great power wars, territorial conquests and war declarations have declined in frequency, wars have been increasingly regulated by international humanitarian law, and battle deaths have declined, partly owing to military medicine. Scholars debate whether war is in decline: Joshua Goldstein, John Mueller and Steven Pinker argue that it is, while Bear F. Braumoeller challenges the claim. Types of warfare include asymmetric warfare, between belligerents of drastically different capability; [[Biological warfare]], using infectious agents or toxins; [[Chemical weapon|chemical warfare]], in which poison gas in World War I caused over a million estimated casualties, including more than 100,000 civilians; cold warfare, an intense rivalry without direct military conflict; conventional warfare; cyberwarfare; [[Insurgency|insurgency]], a rebellion by irregular forces against authority; information warfare; [[Nuclear weapon|nuclear warfare]]; radiological warfare; and total war. One estimate puts deaths from the 20th century's major conflicts at 100–231 million when direct violence, starvation, disease and related political decisions are combined.
