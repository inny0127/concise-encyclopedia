Any of 22 species of [[Parrot|parrots]] in the family *Cacatuidae*, native to [[Australasia]]. Cockatoos are recognizable by prominent crests and curved bills, and their plumage is generally less colorful than that of other parrots, being mainly white, gray or black. The family ranges from the [[Philippines]] and eastern Indonesian islands to [[New Guinea]], the [[Solomon Islands]] and Australia. Insect of the order Blattodea, which also includes [[Termite|termites]]. About 30 of the 4,600 species are associated with human habitats, and some are well-known pests. Statistical measure, denoted R² or r², of the proportion of variation in a dependent variable that is predictable from the independent variable or variables. Mental processes that deal with knowledge, including the acquisition, storage, retrieval, transformation, and use of information. Cognitive processes are a pervasive part of mental life, and many occur simultaneously. They make individuals aware of their environment and help them plan and carry out appropriate responses.

Processes are typically categorized by function. [[Perception]] organizes and interprets sensory information to construct a coherent experience of objects and events. [[Attention]] prioritizes some aspects of experience while filtering out irrelevant information. [[Memory]] retains, stores, and retrieves information, and includes working memory and long-term memory. Thinking covers reasoning, concept formation, problem solving, and decision-making. Many activities deal with language, including its acquisition, comprehension, and production. Metacognitive processes deal with information about other mental processes. Classifications also distinguish conscious from unconscious processes and controlled from automatic ones. Whether feelings and emotions qualify as cognitions is debated.

Theories differ. Classical computationalism holds that cognitive processes manipulate symbols according to formal rules, much as computers execute [[Algorithm|algorithms]]. Connectionism models the mind as a network of nodes that pass information to one another. Representationalism and anti-representationalism disagree about whether cognitive processes operate on internal representations of the world.

Cognition is studied in [[Psychology|psychology]], [[Neuroscience|neuroscience]], cognitive science, and philosophy. Some researchers study [[Cognitive development]], the growth of mental abilities from infancy through adulthood. The field has roots in antiquity and gained interdisciplinary prominence with the cognitive revolution beginning in the 1950s.

The word derives from the Indo-European root *gnō-*, 'to know', through [[Latin]] *cognōscere*, 'to learn, to understand'. It entered Middle English as *cognicioun* in 1447.

ENTRY:
Technique of art production using an assembly of different forms to create a new whole, usually by gluing flat pieces onto a flat surface. The term derives from the French *coller*, "to glue." Collage may incorporate newspaper clippings, ribbons, paint, [[Paper|paper]] scraps, photographs and other found objects; assemblage is the three-dimensional equivalent. Paper collage is first known from 10th-century Japan. The technique reappeared as an art form in the early 20th century, when [[Pablo Picasso|Pablo Picasso]] and Georges Braque coined the term *papier collé* as collage became a distinctive part of modern art. In the 18th century Mary Delany created 985 botanical collages, which she called flower Mosaicks.

=== KEY: Collatz_conjecture_e4e604

LEVEL: 5

  positive integers = 양의 정수
ENTRY:
Unsolved problem in mathematics concerning repeated arithmetic operations on positive integers. The conjecture holds that repeatedly halving even numbers and replacing odd numbers with three times the number plus one always eventually reaches 1. It has been shown to hold for all positive integers up to 2.36×10²¹, but no general proof has been found. The problem is named after the mathematician Lothar Collatz, who introduced it in 1937. Efforts to solve it have produced new techniques and many partial results.

[[Hobby]] of seeking, acquiring, organizing and displaying items of interest. Collections are most often of manufactured goods such as [[Coin|coins]], stamps and art, but they may also consist of natural objects such as birds' eggs, butterflies, rocks and seashells. Some collectors gather broadly, while completists try to acquire every item in a defined set. Antiques are generally at least 100 years old. Older collectables too young to be antiques, such as [[Art Deco]] and [[Art Nouveau]] pieces, are called vintage. Items that were once everyday objects but are now collectable because almost all examples have been discarded are called ephemera. The value of a collectable is set on the secondary market, and a price guide lists typical selling prices.
