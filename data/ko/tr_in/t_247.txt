Flowering plant of the mallow family, grown for its edible pods. Known as lady's fingers in some English-speaking countries, *Abelmoschus esculentus* is a perennial often grown as an annual, reaching about 2 meters tall. It is related to [[Cotton|cotton]] and [[Hibiscus|hibiscus]]. The fruit is a ribbed capsule up to 18 cm long, and pods must be harvested while immature, usually within a week of pollination, before they become fibrous. Okra originated in Ethiopia, Eritrea and eastern Sudan and spread around the [[Mediterranean Sea|Mediterranean]]. Ships in the [[Atlantic slave trade|Atlantic slave trade]] introduced it to the Americas, and its presence was recorded in Brazil by 1658. It was common throughout the [[Southern United States|Southern United States]] by 1800. It is among the most heat- and drought-tolerant vegetables, and it features in dishes such as [[Gumbo|gumbo]]. Global production exceeds 11 million metric tons a year, led by India.

Range of ages for people nearing and surpassing [[Life expectancy|life expectancy]]. Old people are also called the elderly, elders, senior citizens, seniors, or older adults. Old age is not a definite biological stage: the chronological age denoted as old varies culturally and historically. Disciplines concerned with it include the study of the organic processes of [[Ageing|aging]] (senescence), gerontology, [[Geriatrics|geriatrics]], and gerontechnology.

There is no universal official definition. Most developed Western countries set the [[Retirement|retirement]] age around 65, generally taken to mark the transition from middle to old age. The [[United Nations]] considers old age to begin at 60, while a 2001 joint report by the U.S. National Institute on Aging and the World Health Organization's Regional Office for Africa set it at 50 in Sub-Saharan Africa, where old age is determined by a person's ability to contribute to society and life expectancy is lower.

Gerontologists recognize diversity within old age. In developed countries many people in their later 60s and 70s remain fit and active, while after age 80 they generally become increasingly frail; some, called "superagers," retain vitality much longer. One study distinguishes the young-old (60 to 69), the middle-old (70 to 79), and the very old (80+). Scholars list chronological, biological, psychological, and social dimensions of old age, to which Wattis and Curran add a developmental one.

Older people often have limited regenerative abilities and are more susceptible to illness and injury. They face social problems related to retirement, [[Loneliness|loneliness]], and ageism: a 2023 U.S. poll found that about 37% of adults aged 50 to 80 experienced loneliness. In 2011 the United Nations proposed a human-rights convention to protect old people.

Series of international multi-sport events, held as separate Summer and Winter Games.
