Cutting process producing a hole of circular cross-section in solid materials. A usually multi-point rotary bit is pressed against the workpiece and turned at hundreds to thousands of revolutions per minute, cutting off chips. In [[Rock (geology)|rock]] drilling the hole is usually made by hammering the bit with quickly repeated short movements, either from outside the hole or within it. Liquid intended for human consumption, with water as its common basis. Besides satisfying thirst, drinks play important roles in human culture. Common types include plain [[Drinking water|drinking water]], [[Milk|milk]], juice, smoothies, and soft drinks; traditionally warm beverages include coffee, tea, and hot chocolate. Caffeinated drinks have been consumed for centuries, and alcoholic drinks such as [[Wine|wine]], [[Beer|beer]], and liquor, which contain [[Ethanol|ethanol]], have been part of human culture for more than 8,000 years. Thirst is regulated by the hypothalamus, and complete deprivation of drinks results in death faster than removal of any other substance besides oxygen. Water has also been the carrier of many diseases: the [[World Health Organization]] points out that 94% of deaths from diarrhea, the third biggest cause of infectious death worldwide at 1.8 million annually, could be prevented by improving the victim's environment, particularly safe water.

Many early societies considered alcohol a gift from the gods, leading to gods such as [[Dionysus]]. Coffee was banned in Ottoman Turkey during the 17th century for political reasons. Preparation of drinks can include water purification by filtration and chlorination; pasteurization, used primarily on milk; juicing; infusion; percolation; carbonation; and fermentation, used since the Neolithic age.

Ingestion of water or other liquids into the body, usually through the [[Mouth|mouth]]. Humans drink by swallowing, with peristalsis carrying the liquid through the [[Esophagus|esophagus]] to the [[Stomach|stomach]]. Most animals drink to maintain hydration, though many survive on water gained from their food. [[Cat|Cats]] lap with the tip of the tongue, ruminants draw in water by a plunging action, and [[Elephant|elephants]] draw water into their trunks and squirt it into their mouths. Water safe for ingestion, either drunk directly or consumed through food preparation; also called potable water. For most terrestrial animals, including humans, it is fresh water, surface or underground, whose quality does not cause immediate poisoning or significant short-term harm. In modern societies it is typically supplied to households through a distribution system ending in taps. Sources include [[Spring (hydrology)|springs]], [[Aquifer|aquifers]], rainwater harvesting, surface water from rivers, streams, and [[Glacier|glaciers]], and desalinated seawater; all must receive adequate treatment and meet quality standards. Most bottled water is packaged tap water.

Requirements vary with activity, age, health, and environment; those working in a hot climate may need up to 16 liters a day. In the United States the Adequate Intake for total water is 4.0 liters per day for males older than 18 and 3.0 liters for females over 18, assuming about 80% from drink and 20% from food. The European Food Safety Authority recommends 2.0 liters for women and 2.5 liters for men. The common advice to drink 8 glasses of plain water per day is not scientific; thirst is a better guide. Water intoxication can cause death in minutes or hours.

As many as two billion people lack safe drinking water, with developing countries most affected. Unsafe water, typically due to [[Water pollution|water pollution]] and the lack of water treatment, can carry [[Disease|disease]] and is a major cause of preventable death and illness worldwide. Water scarcity is also a cause of [[Famine|famine]], mass migration, and violent conflict over control of water supplies. Leakage rates of 50% are not uncommon in urban systems, and replacing deteriorating water and sanitation [[Infrastructure|infrastructure]] in industrialized countries may cost as much as $200 billion a year.

ENTRY:
Epic science fiction novel by American author [[Frank Herbert]], published in 1965. It was first serialized in *Analog* and tied with Roger Zelazny's *This Immortal* for the Hugo Award for Best Novel. It won the inaugural Nebula Award for Best Novel in 1966 and is the first installment of the Dune Chronicles. The story follows Paul Atreides, whose family takes stewardship of the desert planet Arrakis, the only source of the life-extending spice melange. As factions of the empire struggle for control of Arrakis, the novel explores politics, religion, ecology and technology. A 1984 film directed by [[David Lynch]] received mostly negative reviews, and a 2021 adaptation by Denis Villeneuve was nominated for ten [[Academy Awards]], winning six.

ENTRY:
Informal group of [[Beetle|beetles]] that feed on feces. They belong to the superfamily Scarabaeoidea but are not a single taxonomic group, so dung feeding has evolved more than once. The subfamily Scarabaeinae alone comprises more than 5,000 species. Many feed on the dung of [[Herbivore|herbivores]] and omnivores, and they occur on every continent except [[Antarctica]]. In 1991 Ilkka Cambefort and Yves Hanski classified dung beetles as rollers, which shape dung into balls for food or brood, tunnelers, which bury dung where they find it, and dwellers, which live within dung. In the late 19th century Jean Henri Fabre showed that apparent helpers of a rolling beetle were waiting to steal its food. [[Ancient Egypt|Ancient Egyptians]] revered dung-rolling as the god Khepri rolling the sun across the sky.

ENTRY:
Fantasy tabletop role-playing game, commonly abbreviated as D&D. Gary Gygax and Dave Arneson created it, and it was first published in 1974 by Tactical Studies Rules (TSR). Players create their own characters, while a Dungeon Master referees the game and voices the non-player characters. It is commonly recognized as the beginning of the modern role-playing game industry and deeply influenced [[Video game|video games]]. Sales of the fifth edition, released in 2014, rose another 52 percent in 2018. A 1980s moral panic attempted to link the game to [[Satanism]] and suicide.

ENTRY:
Evacuation of Allied forces from the beaches and harbor of Dunkirk, northern France, in 1940. Codenamed Operation Dynamo, it took place between 26 May and 4 June 1940, and more than 338,000 Allied soldiers were evacuated. They had been trapped by German forces during the [[Battle of France]]. Royal Navy destroyers and a flotilla of over 800 vessels, including civilian "Little Ships", carried them to Britain. [[Winston Churchill|Churchill]] called the event "a colossal military disaster" but also hailed the "miracle of deliverance", adding that wars are not won by evacuations.

ENTRY:
Extinct genus of large arthrodire ("jointed-neck") fishes. Members lived in the Late [[Devonian|Devonian]] period, about 382–358 million years ago, and were among the first vertebrate apex predators. Fossils have been found in the United States, Canada, Poland, [[Belgium]], [[Morocco]], Russia and Latvia. The type species, *Dunkleosteus terrelli*, is the largest and best known; typical adults are estimated at about 3.4 m long and exceptionally large individuals at 4.1 m. 10 species have been named, although the validity of several is unclear.
