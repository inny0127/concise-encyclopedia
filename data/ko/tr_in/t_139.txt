Form of [[Cooking|cooking]] that applies heat to the surface of food, commonly from above, below or the side. Grilling usually involves significant direct, radiant heat and is used to cook meat and vegetables quickly. Outdoor grilling often uses [[Charcoal|charcoal]], wood or propane gas; in the United States, grilling with the heat source above is called broiling. Direct heat can expose food to temperatures often in excess of 260 °C (500 °F), and the [[Maillard reaction]] that gives grilled meat its roast flavor occurs only above 155 °C (310 °F). Water located beneath Earth's surface in [[Soil|soil]] and [[Rock (geology)|rock]] pore spaces and rock fractures. About 30 percent of all readily available fresh water in the world is groundwater. Groundwater is often cheaper, more convenient and less vulnerable to pollution than surface water, so it supplies many public drinking water systems; over 2 billion people rely on it as their primary source. Set with an associative operation, an identity element and an inverse for every element. For example, the [[Integer|integers]] form a group under addition. The concept was developed to treat numbers, geometric shapes and polynomial roots in a unified way, and some authors regard it as a central organizing principle of contemporary mathematics. In geometry, the symmetries of an object form a group, and Lie groups appear in the [[Standard Model]] of particle physics. Mapping of an abstract group into the invertible linear transformations of a vector space. ENTRY:
Italian radio-frequency engineer and inventor. Marconi developed a practical wireless telegraph system based on [[Radio wave|radio waves]], which led to his being largely credited as the inventor of [[Radio|radio]]. He shared the 1909 Nobel Prize in Physics with Ferdinand Braun for contributions to wireless telegraphy. Building on the 1888 demonstration by [[Heinrich Hertz|Heinrich Hertz]] that [[Electromagnetic radiation|electromagnetic radiation]] could be produced and detected, he worked on wireless telegraphy from the early 1890s. In 1897 he founded the Wireless Telegraph & Signal Company in the [[United Kingdom|United Kingdom]], later the Marconi Company. On December 12, 1901, he received the first transatlantic radio signal, the Morse letter "S", sent from Poldhu, [[Cornwall|Cornwall]], to St. John's, Newfoundland. In 1929 he was made a marquess, and in 1931 he set up Vatican Radio for Pope Pius XI.

ENTRY:
Precambrian geological formation in northeast [[South America|South America]]. It is one of the three cratons of the South American plate and is about 1.7 billion years old. The shield underlies [[Guyana|Guyana]], [[Suriname|Suriname]], [[French Guiana|French Guiana]], much of southern [[Venezuela|Venezuela]], most of [[Brazil|Brazil]] north of the [[Amazon River|Amazon River]], and parts of eastern Colombia. Its higher elevations, the Guiana Highlands, hold flat-topped tepuis and are the source of waterfalls such as [[Angel Falls|Angel Falls]], Kaieteur Falls and Cuquenan Falls. The highest point is Pico da Neblina in Brazil, at 2,995 m (9,826 ft). The shield is one of the world's most biodiverse regions, with over 3,000 vertebrate species, including 1,168 freshwater fish and 269 amphibians. About 13,367 vascular plant species have been found, approximately 40% of them endemic.

ENTRY:
Association of artisans or merchants overseeing the practice of a craft or trade in a particular territory. Guilds were most extensive in medieval Europe, though similar groups existed before and after that period and in other parts of the world. Typically, the key privilege of guild members was control of trade in a given city, and guilds might set minimum or maximum prices, hours of trading and numbers of apprentices. Critics argued that these rules reduced free competition, but defenders maintained that guilds protected professional standards. Craft guilds transmitted skills through apprenticeship, journeymanship and mastery, and in major cities such as Florence, [[Paris]] and [[Barcelona]] they became central to economic and civic life. Guilds also maintained welfare funds for sick or elderly members and supported widows and orphans.

Early Renaissance composer and music theorist, variously described as French or Franco-Flemish. Considered the leading European composer of his time, his music was widely performed and reproduced. He was associated with the Burgundian School, particularly his colleague Gilles Binchois, but was never a regular member of the Burgundian chapel. He has been described as leading the first generation of European musicians who were primarily considered composers by occupation.

French composer and poet, central figure of the *ars nova* style in late medieval music.
