Family of [[Frog|frogs]], sometimes called true frogs, with the widest distribution of any frog family. They occur on all continents except Antarctica, including North America, Europe, Africa (including [[Madagascar]]) and Asia. Typically smooth and moist-skinned, with large, powerful legs and extensively webbed feet, true frogs range from small species such as the wood frog to large ones. ENTRY:
Section of a river with a steep gradient, increased [[Velocity|velocity]] and [[Turbulence|turbulence]]. Rapids lie between a run, a smoothly flowing stretch of stream, and a cascade. The river bed becomes shallower, with some [[Rock (geology)|rocks]] exposed, and splashing water mixes in air bubbles, producing whitewater. Rapids form where the bed material resists [[Erosion|erosion]] better than the bed downstream, so softer rock wears away and leaves an uneven streambed. Four factors are needed: flow, gradient, constriction and obstacles. Constriction narrows channels and speeds the water, while obstacles may come from human activity, landslides, earthquakes or sediment. Rapids aerate the water, improving its quality. They are rated from Class I to Class VI, and the most dangerous class, which poses a threat to life, is often classified as "U" for Unraftable.
