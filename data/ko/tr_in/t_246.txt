Its name comes from [[Latin]] *octō*, "eight," because it was the eighth month of the early calendar of ancient Rome, before January and February were added. Soft-bodied, eight-limbed mollusc of the order Octopoda. Its some 300 species are grouped within the class [[Cephalopod|Cephalopoda]], with squids, cuttlefish and nautiloids. The giant Pacific octopus (*Enteroctopus dofleini*) is often cited as the largest species, with adults usually weighing 10–50 kg and arm spans of up to 4.8 m. Octopuses have a complex nervous system and excellent sight, and are among the most intelligent invertebrates. They live in habitats from [[Coral reef|coral reefs]] to the seabed and abyssal depths. They defend themselves by expelling ink, using [[Camouflage|camouflage]], threat displays and jet propulsion. All octopuses are [[Venom|venomous]], but only the [[Blue-ringed octopus|blue-ringed octopuses]] are known to be deadly to humans.

Type of lyric poem that praises or glorifies an event or individual, or describes nature intellectually and emotionally. Odes originated in [[Ancient Greece]], where they were performed to musical accompaniment, usually on the aulos or the [[Lyre|lyre]]. Three typical forms are recognized. Pindaric odes follow the style of the poet Pindar and have a three-part structure of strophe, antistrophe and epode. Horatian odes follow the conventions of the Roman poet [[Horace]], with stanzas that share one rhyme scheme and meter, and often treat intimate subjects such as love and friendship. Irregular odes use rhyme but neither the three-part form nor fixed stanzas. Order of predatory flying insects that includes [[Dragonfly|dragonflies]] and [[Damselfly|damselflies]]. Odonates have large compound eyes, two pairs of long, transparent wings that move independently, and elongated abdomens. Adults catch prey in flight with specialized legs, and they can land and perch. Almost all have aquatic [[Nymph (biology)|nymphs]] called naiads, which are carnivorous and eat anything they can overpower, including small [[Fish|fish]]. Dragonflies (Anisoptera) have bulkier bodies and hold their wings horizontal at rest, while damselflies (Zygoptera) have slender bodies and close their wings. The order also includes three relict damsel-dragonflies (Anisozygoptera). Johan Christian Fabricius coined the name Odonata in 1793, from the Greek word for tooth. Its lineage traces to the Paleozoic era, and the scientific study of these insects is called odonatology.

Smell caused by volatilized chemical compounds, usually present in low concentrations, that humans and many animals perceive through the [[Olfactory system|olfactory system]]. Commonwealth English spells it odor. The terms scent, aroma and fragrance usually denote pleasant odors. Odorant molecules bind to receptor proteins on the cilia of olfactory receptor neurons in the olfactory epithelium, a small patch of tissue at the back of the nasal cavity. The resulting signals travel along the olfactory nerve to the olfactory bulb, where the smell is interpreted in relation to past experience. The brain identifies a smell from patterns of signals across many receptor types, not from any single compound. Women usually outperform men in odor discrimination, although a 2019 meta-analysis found the difference to be small. Pregnant women have heightened sensitivity to smell.

Ancient Greek epic poem attributed to [[Homer]], one of two great epics with the *Iliad*. It is among the oldest surviving works of literature and is divided into 24 books. It follows [[Odysseus]], king of Ithaca, on his homecoming after the [[Trojan War]]. The journey takes ten years, during which he encounters many perils and all of his crewmates are killed. The first complete English translation appeared in 1616. In 2018, a poll of experts by [[BBC]] Culture found it to be literature's most enduring narrative.

Organic-rich, fine-grained [[Sedimentary rock|sedimentary rock]] containing kerogen. Kerogen is a solid mixture of organic compounds from which liquid [[Hydrocarbon|hydrocarbons]] can be produced. Heating oil shale to a high temperature causes pyrolysis, which yields a vapor; cooling the vapor separates a liquid called shale oil. Shale oil is a substitute for conventional crude oil, but extracting it costs more, both financially and environmentally. Oil shale can also be burned directly as a low-grade fuel for [[Electricity generation|power generation]] and district heating. A 2016 estimate put global resources at 6.05 trillion barrels (962 billion cubic meters) of oil in place. Only [[Estonia]] and China have well-established oil shale industries. Mining and processing raise concerns over land use, waste disposal, water use, [[Greenhouse gas|greenhouse-gas]] emissions and air pollution. Oil shale differs from oil-bearing shales, such as the Bakken Formation, which contain tight oil.

ENTRY:
Drillhole in the Earth designed to bring [[Petroleum|petroleum]] to the surface. Usually some [[Natural gas|natural gas]] is released along with the oil, and a well designed to produce only gas may be called a gas well. Creating a well costs at least hundreds of thousands of dollars, and much more in difficult locations, such as offshore. Wells are often sold between companies as assets, because a low-output well can become valuable when prices rise. Techniques such as hydraulic fracturing have made some wells viable. Neglected or abandoned wells can leak methane and other toxic substances, and a 2020 Reuters estimate counted at least 29 million abandoned wells internationally. The earliest known oil wells were drilled in China in AD 347, using bits attached to [[Bamboo|bamboo]] poles. In the United States the petroleum industry began in 1859, when Edwin Drake found oil near Titusville, [[Pennsylvania]].
