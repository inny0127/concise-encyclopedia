ENTRY:
Class (Collembola) of small, wingless arthropods, the largest of the three lineages of hexapods that are no longer considered insects. Springtails are omnivorous, free-living organisms that prefer moist conditions, and there are more than 9,000 species worldwide. They contribute indirectly to [[Decomposition|decomposition]] by fragmenting organic matter and controlling soil microbial communities. Most species have a forked appendage, the furcula, folded beneath the body, which snaps against the surface to fling the springtail into the air. Members are normally less than 6 mm long, and some can reduce their body size by as much as 30% through [[Moulting|molting]] when temperatures rise. The name Collembola comes from Greek words for glue and peg, after a tube on the abdomen once thought to stick the creature to surfaces.

ENTRY:
Large-scale electrical discharge in the mesosphere, high above [[Thunderstorm|thunderstorm]] clouds. Sprites appear as luminous red-orange flashes, often occurring in clusters at altitudes of 50–90 km. They are usually triggered by positive [[Lightning|lightning]] discharges between an underlying thundercloud and the ground. Although sometimes called upper-atmospheric lightning, they are cold plasma phenomena, more akin to fluorescent tube discharges. The earliest known report dates from 1886, and they were first photographed in 1989 by scientists from the University of Minnesota. Their root cause remains unknown beyond a general association with positive cloud-to-ground lightning.

First artificial Earth satellite, launched by the [[Soviet Union]] on October 4, 1957. It was a polished metal sphere 58 cm (23 in) in diameter with four external radio antennas, and its radio signal was easily detected by [[Amateur radio|amateur radio]] operators. Its 65° orbital inclination meant its flight path covered virtually the entire inhabited Earth. The success was unanticipated by the United States, which precipitated the American Sputnik crisis and triggered the start of the [[Space Race]]. ENTRY:
Regular quadrilateral with four equal sides and four right angles. A square is a special case of a [[Rhombus|rhombus]] and of a rectangle, and its area is the side length multiplied by itself, which gives the term squaring in algebra. Equal squares tile the plane edge to edge, so square tilings appear in floors, graph paper and image [[Pixel|pixels]]. Squares are the only regular polygons whose internal, central and external angles are all equal, each being a right angle. The diagonals of a square are equal, bisect each other and meet at 90 degrees. Attempts to square the circle with compass and straightedge, which the area formula motivated, are now known to be impossible. Squares can be inscribed in any smooth or convex curve, but it remains unsolved whether a square can be inscribed in every simple closed curve.

Number y whose square, y², equals a given number x. Every nonnegative real number has a unique nonnegative square root, called the principal square root and written √x. For example, 4 and −4 are square roots of 16. Every positive number has two square roots, one positive and one negative. Square roots of negative numbers are discussed within [[Complex number|complex numbers]]. A Babylonian clay tablet dated between 1800 and 1600 BC gives a base-60 value corresponding to 1.41421296 for √2. Cephalopod mollusc with an elongated soft body, large eyes, eight arms and two long tentacles. Squid have a small internal skeleton, a rod-like gladius or pen, made of [[Chitin|chitin]]. They diverged from other cephalopods during the [[Jurassic]] and became prominent around 100 million years ago, at the boundary between the Early and Late Cretaceous. Squid occupy a role similar to [[Teleost|teleost]] fish as open-water predators, using their two long tentacles to grab prey and their eight arms to hold it. Many swim rapidly by jet propulsion and locate prey by sight. Groups of Humboldt squid have been observed hunting cooperatively. One person may run a small production, but a modern Broadway show requires carpenters, painters, electricians, stagehands, stitchers and wigmakers. In ancient Greek theater, the mechane, a crane for lifting actors over the skene, supported the endings of plays in which a god suddenly resolved the storyline; it is the literal source of the phrase deus ex machina. By 1300, liturgical dramas in churches spread the message of [[Christianity]] using mansion stages, small wooden platforms representing places such as Heaven or Hell.

Structure of diagonally arranged horizontal steps that bridges a large vertical distance by dividing it into smaller ones. Alternatives include [[Elevator|elevators]], stairlifts, inclined moving walkways, [[Ladder|ladders]] and ramps. Each step is composed of a tread, the part stepped on, and a riser, the near-vertical element between steps. Theory of particle physics describing the electromagnetic, weak, and strong interactions and classifying all known [[Elementary particle|elementary particles]]. It excludes [[Gravity|gravity]], the fourth known fundamental force. The model was developed in stages during the latter half of the 20th century, and its current formulation was finalized in the mid-1970s when the existence of [[Quark|quarks]] was confirmed experimentally. The top quark (1995), the tau neutrino (2000), and the [[Higgs boson]] (2012) were confirmed later.

Key steps included Paul Dirac's 1928 equation, which implied antimatter; the 1954 extension of gauge theory to nonabelian groups by Yang Chen-Ning and Robert Mills; Chien-Shiung Wu's 1957 demonstration that parity is not conserved in the weak interaction; the introduction of quarks by Murray Gell-Mann and George Zweig in 1964; and the incorporation of the Higgs mechanism into Sheldon Glashow's electroweak theory by Steven Weinberg and Abdus Salam in 1967. Neutral weak currents were discovered at [[CERN]] in 1973, and the W and Z bosons in 1983. Glashow, Salam, and Weinberg shared the 1979 Nobel Prize in Physics.

The model includes 12 matter particles of spin 1/2, the [[Fermion|fermions]], divided into six quarks and six [[Lepton|leptons]] in three generations. Forces are carried by bosons: the photon, eight gluons, and the W and Z bosons. The Higgs boson was observed on July 4, 2012, by the ATLAS and CMS experiments at the Large Hadron Collider.

The model remains incomplete. It does not incorporate gravitation as described by general relativity, explain the excess of matter over antimatter, or contain a viable [[Dark matter|dark matter]] particle. Without modification it also lacks neutrino masses and oscillation.

Unit of pressure (symbol atm) defined as 101325 Pa, approximately equal to Earth's average atmospheric pressure at [[Sea level|sea level]]. Originally defined as the pressure exerted by a 760 mm column of mercury at 0 °C under standard gravity, it was adopted for general use by the 10th General Conference on Weights and Measures in 1954, defined independently of any particular substance.
