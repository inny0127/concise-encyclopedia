Conqueror and first ruler of the Kingdom of [[Hawaii]], also known as Kamehameha the Great. By 1795 he had conquered Maui, Lānaʻi, Molokaʻi, and Oʻahu, and full unification of the [[Hawaiian Islands]] came in 1810, when Kauaʻi and Niʻihau submitted peacefully. He used Western firearms and vessels acquired through trade to gain military advantage. As king he centralized governance, promulgated the Law of the Splintered Paddle to protect civilians in wartime, and fostered economic ties with foreign powers. His line, the Kamehameha Dynasty, ruled until 1872. The state of Hawaii gave a statue of him to the National Statuary Hall Collection in [[Washington, D.C.]]

Capital and largest city of [[Uganda]] (pop., 2024: 1,797,722), near the northern shore of [[Lake Victoria]]. The city proper is divided into five political divisions. In 2015 its metropolitan area generated an estimated nominal GDP of $13.8 billion (constant US dollars of 2011), more than half of Uganda's GDP for that year. Estimates of the metropolitan population range from around four million to 6,709,900 in 2019. The name is a local rendering of "Hill of the [[Impala]]," the British name for Old Kampala hill, a royal hunting reserve of the Kabaka of Buganda where colonial officials built Fort Lugard. The consulting firm Mercer has regularly ranked Kampala as East Africa's best city to live in, ahead of [[Nairobi]] and [[Kigali]].

ENTRY:
Japanese judoka, educator, and politician who founded [[Judo|judo]] and established the Kodokan Judo Institute in [[Tokyo]] in 1882. Judo was one of the first Japanese martial arts to gain international recognition, and it has been an official Olympic sport since 1964 for men and 1992 for women. Kanō introduced black and white belts and dan ranking. He was director of primary education in the Ministry of Education from 1898 to 1901 and president of Tokyo Higher Normal School from 1900 to 1920. As the first Asian member of the International Olympic Committee, serving from 1909 to 1938, he represented Japan at most Olympic Games from 1912 to 1936. He also helped introduce and systematize [[Karate|karate]] in Japan. His mottoes included "maximum efficiency minimal effort" and "mutual welfare and benefit."

City in southern [[Afghanistan]] and capital of Kandahar Province, at about 1,010 m (3,310 ft) above sea level between the Arghandab and Tarnak rivers. A fortified city existed on the site by c. 1000–750 BC, and [[Alexander the Great]] laid the foundation of Old Kandahar in the 4th century BC, naming it Alexandria Arachosia. In 1747 [[Ahmad Shah Durrani]] made Kandahar the capital of the Durrani Empire. It has been the traditional seat of the [[Pashtuns|Pashtun]] people for more than 300 years and is a center of trade and fruit production, notably figs, grapes, and pomegranates. Kandahar is the founding city and spiritual center of the [[Taliban]], where the group's supreme leader is based, although the Taliban maintain Kabul as the official capital.
