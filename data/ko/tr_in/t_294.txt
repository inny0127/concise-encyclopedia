The city hosted the 1950 and 2014 FIFA World Cups and hosts the annual Brazilian Grand Prix of [[Formula One]].

Its taste and aroma come from the phytochemicals picrocrocin and safranal, and the [[Carotenoid|carotenoid]] pigment crocin gives dishes and textiles a golden-yellow hue. The crocus is sterile and cannot produce viable seeds, so humans have propagated it across much of [[Eurasia]]. It originated in the Khorasan region of northeastern [[Iran]], and its recorded history begins with a 7th-century BC Assyrian botanical treatise. Harvesting is labor-intensive, and premium saffron regularly exceeds US$5,000 per kilogram. Saffron is an essential ingredient in Persian cuisine and has also been used in [[Traditional medicine]], dyeing, perfumery and religious [[Ritual|rituals]].

Prose story or history composed in [[Iceland]] and, to a lesser extent, elsewhere in [[Scandinavia]]. Sagas originated in the [[Middle Ages]] and were written in the vernacular, Old Norse and its later descendants, primarily Icelandic, rather than in [[Latin]], the dominant language of medieval European history-writing. Their subjects range from pre-Christian Scandinavian legends and saints to Scandinavian kings, contemporary Icelandic politics and chivalric romances. The most famous type is the sagas of Icelanders, which feature Viking voyages, migration to Iceland and feuds between Icelandic families, usually set from the settlement of Iceland in the 870s to a generation or two after its conversion to Christianity in 1000. Kings' sagas, composed in the 12th to 14th centuries, chronicle the lives of Scandinavian kings; Heimskringla, probably compiled by [[Snorri Sturluson]], is a pre-eminent example. Though written in prose, sagas often include [[Epic poetry|verse]] in alliterative form.

[[Supermassive black hole]] at the [[Galactic Center]] of the [[Milky Way]], about 27,000 light-years from Earth. It was discovered in 1974 by Bruce Balick and Robert L. Brown, who identified it as a compact radio source. Observations of stars orbiting it, particularly the star S2, indicate a mass of 4.297±0.012 million [[Solar mass|solar masses]]. Roman Catholic basilica under construction since 1882 in [[Barcelona]], Spain, designed by [[Antoni Gaudí]], who combined Gothic and curvilinear [[Art Nouveau]] forms. Construction began on March 19, 1882, under Francisco de Paula del Villar. In 1883 Gaudí took over as chief architect and devoted the rest of his life to the project; at his death in 1926 less than a quarter of it was complete. Relying solely on private donations, work was interrupted by the [[Spanish Civil War]] in 1936, when anarchists set fire to the crypt and partly destroyed Gaudí's original plans. Largest hot desert in the world, spanning North Africa. With an area of 9,200,000 sq km, it is the third-largest desert overall, smaller only than the deserts of Antarctica and the northern Arctic. It extends from the [[Red Sea]] in the east and the [[Mediterranean Sea]] in the north to the [[Atlantic Ocean]] in the west, and is bounded on the south by the [[Sahel]], a belt of semi-arid tropical savanna. It covers large parts of Algeria, Chad, Egypt, Libya, Mali, Mauritania, Niger, Western Sahara, and Sudan.

The terrain includes rocky plateaus (hamada), sand seas (ergs) with dunes rising over 180 m, gravel plains (reg), dry valleys (wadi), dry lakes, and salt flats. Mountain massifs, many volcanic, include the Aïr, Ahaggar, and Tibesti ranges; the highest peak is Emi Koussi, a shield volcano in northern Chad. Important towns include [[Nouakchott]] and [[Timbuktu]].

The desert lies under the subtropical ridge, a belt of high pressure whose descending dry air prevents cloud formation and produces a hot desert climate ([[Köppen climate classification]] BWh). The Libyan Desert is the sunniest and driest part. For several hundred thousand years the region has alternated between desert and savanna in a 20,000-year cycle, driven by the precession of Earth's axis (about 26,000 years), which shifts the North African [[Monsoon|monsoon]].

Population is sparse, estimated at around 2.5 million, mostly nomadic pastoralists such as the Tuareg and Berbers. The name derives from the Arabic *ṣaḥrā'*, "desert."
