Form of verse, often a narrative set to music. Ballads were characteristic of popular poetry and song in [[England]], Ireland and [[Scotland]] from the Late Middle Ages until the 19th century, and they spread across Europe and later to North America, South America and Australia. Ballads have no prescribed structure, but many use quatrains with ABCB or ABAB rhyme schemes. Originally written to accompany dances, they remained an oral tradition until collectors such as Bishop Thomas Percy published them. Most have no known author or correct version. Scottish ballads such as "Tam Lin" include supernatural elements. From the 18th century, poets including [[Samuel Taylor Coleridge]] wrote lyrical ballads.

Ancient projectile weapon, sometimes called a bolt thrower. It developed from earlier Greek devices, using two levers with twisted-skein torsion springs in place of the bow of a [[Crossbow|crossbow]]. Early versions fired heavy darts or stones for [[Siege|siege warfare]]. The earliest form is thought to have been developed for Dionysius of Syracuse, around 400 BC. The ballista remained in use into the Byzantine era and was supplanted by gunpowder artillery in the 15th century.

ENTRY:
Field of [[Mechanics|mechanics]] concerned with the launching, flight behavior and impact effects of projectiles, especially weapon munitions. The word derives from the Greek *ballein*, "to throw." A ballistic missile is guided only during the brief initial powered phase, after which its trajectory is governed by classical mechanics. Ballistics was placed on a mathematical basis by [[Isaac Newton]] in *Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica* (1687), whose laws of motion and gravity made trajectories predictable. Galileo had derived the parabolic form of the ballistic trajectory in 1638. The field is traditionally divided into internal, transitional, external and terminal branches, covering the weapon, the muzzle, the flight and the impact. Modern applications include weapon design, forensic linking of projectiles to firearms, and safety protocols.

Unpowered aerostat that remains aloft due to its buoyancy. A balloon may use hot air or a gas lighter than air, such as [[Hydrogen]] or [[Helium]]. Unlike an [[Airship]], it cannot propel itself, but its altitude can be adjusted to find favorable winds. The [[Montgolfier brothers]] built the first passenger-carrying balloon, a [[Hot air balloon|hot air balloon]], in Annonay, France, in 1783, and the first passenger flight carried a sheep, a duck and a rooster. Hydrogen was used extensively at first, but after the [[Hindenburg disaster]] it was seldom used because of its flammability. Helium is used for all airships and most manned gas balloons.

Series of prehistoric migrations of Proto-Bantu-speaking peoples from an original nucleus in West-Central Africa across [[Central Africa]], Eastern Africa and [[Southern Africa]].
