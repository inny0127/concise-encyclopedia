Marginal sea of the [[Pacific Ocean|Western Pacific Ocean]], around 3,500,000 sq km (1,400,000 sq mi) in area. It is bounded by South China, the Indochinese Peninsula, Taiwan, Luzon, Borneo and Sumatra, and it connects with the East China Sea through the [[Taiwan Strait]]. A 2016 tribunal under the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea ruled that China's claims based on the "nine-dash line" had no legal basis; China rejected the ruling.

Country in East Asia (pop., about 52 million), occupying the southern half of the Korean Peninsula; officially the Republic of Korea. It borders [[North Korea]] along the Korean Demilitarized Zone, with the Yellow Sea to the west and the Sea of Japan to the east. Half the population lives in the [[Seoul]] metropolitan area.

Silla unified most of it in the late seventh century CE, and the [[Goryeo]] dynasty (918–1392) achieved lasting unification. The Joseon dynasty (1392–1897) was followed by the short-lived Korean Empire (1897–1910), which was annexed in 1910 by the [[Empire of Japan]]. South Korea endured a series of dictatorships punctuated by coups, revolutions, and violent uprisings. Meanwhile the economy rose rapidly, making the country one of the Four Asian Tigers. It now has the 14th-largest economy by nominal GDP, and belongs to the OECD's Development Assistance Committee and the [[G20]]. Its population is aging rapidly because of one of the world's lowest fertility rates.

Southernmost point on Earth, at 90° South [[Latitude|latitude]], where the planet's axis of rotation meets the surface. It is also called the Geographic or True South Pole, to distinguish it from the magnetic south pole. All lines of [[Longitude|longitude]] converge there, so every direction points north, and no time zone applies. The pole lies on [[Antarctica]] at an elevation of 2,835 m (9,301 ft). The [[Ice sheet|ice sheet]] moves roughly 10 m (33 ft) a year, so the marker is repositioned each New Year's Day. A Norwegian expedition led by [[Roald Amundsen]] reached the pole on December 14, 1911. The Amundsen–Scott South Pole Station, established in 1956, has been permanently staffed since then. A ceremonial pole nearby is surrounded by [[Flag|flags]] of the original Antarctic Treaty signatories.

Southeastern subregion of Asia (pop., about 676 million), lying south of China, east of the [[Indian subcontinent]], and northwest of mainland Australia. It is bordered on the north by East Asia, on the west by South Asia, and on the east by Oceania. It covers about 4,500,000 sq km, 9.80% of Asia and 3% of Earth's total land area. Mainland Southeast Asia lies entirely in the Northern Hemisphere; [[Timor-Leste]] and southern Indonesia lie south of the [[Equator]].

The region has eleven countries: Brunei, Cambodia, [[Indonesia]], Laos, Malaysia, Myanmar, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, Timor-Leste, and Vietnam. All are members of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations ([[ASEAN]]); Timor-Leste joined in 2025. Geographically it divides into Mainland Southeast Asia, or the Indochinese Peninsula, and Maritime Southeast Asia. Sovereignty issues exist over some islands in the [[South China Sea]].

It lies near the intersection of geological plates. The Alpide belt and the Pacific [[Ring of Fire]] meet in Indonesia, giving the region relatively high rates of earthquakes and volcanic eruptions, particularly in the Philippines and Indonesia.

Southeast Asia is one of the most culturally diverse regions of the world, with hundreds of languages spoken. Indian, Chinese, Muslim, and colonial cultures shaped its institutions. Most modern countries were colonized by European powers, and several were briefly occupied by the [[Empire of Japan]] during World War II; most of the region was decolonized afterward.

The term "Southeast Asia" was first used in 1839 by the American pastor Howard Malcolm, who included only the mainland. The Allies popularized it through the South East Asia Command, formed in 1943, and a roughly standard usage emerged by the late 1970s.

State having [[Sovereignty|sovereignty]], the highest authority over a defined territory. A sovereign state is generally considered independent and must have a permanent population, a defined territory, a government not under the control of another, and the capacity to interact with other sovereign states. Recognition by other states plays an important role in determining its status, and unrecognized states often have difficulty conducting diplomatic relations. Three-dimensional continuum containing positions and directions. In classical physics, physical space is often conceived in three linear dimensions; modern physicists usually consider it, with time, part of a boundless four-dimensional continuum known as [[Spacetime|spacetime]]. Philosophers continue to disagree over whether space is an entity, a relationship between entities, or part of a conceptual framework.

Debates about its nature date to antiquity: the *Timaeus* of [[Plato]], and the definition of *topos* (place) in the *Physics* of [[Aristotle]]. Galilean and Cartesian theories of space underlie the Scientific Revolution, understood to have culminated in Newton's *Principia Mathematica* in 1687. [[René Descartes|Descartes]] held that space was Euclidean in structure, infinite, uniform, and flat, and defined it as that which contains matter.

[[Isaac Newton]] viewed space as absolute, existing permanently and independently of whether any matter was in it. [[Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz|Gottfried Leibniz]] argued instead that space is a collection of relations between objects, given by their distance and direction from one another. This dispute underlies the continuing debate between substantivalism and relationalism. In the 19th and 20th centuries mathematicians began to examine non-Euclidean geometries, in which space is curved rather than flat. According to [[Albert Einstein]]'s general relativity, space around gravitational fields deviates from Euclidean space, and experimental tests have confirmed that non-Euclidean geometries model its shape better. Reconciling quantum and gravitational descriptions of space remains unresolved.
