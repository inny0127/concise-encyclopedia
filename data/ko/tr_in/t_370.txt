ENTRY:
Chinese empress (r. 690–705), the only undisputed female sovereign in Chinese history. Wu was a concubine of Emperor Taizong, then empress consort of Emperor Gaozong from 655. After Gaozong suffered a stroke in 660, she effectively ruled until his death in 683, then governed for her sons. In 690 she proclaimed the [[Zhou dynasty]] in place of the [[Tang dynasty]] and crowned herself emperor. Over a 45-year de facto rule she used informants and strict legal measures, purged members of the Tang royal house, reformed the civil service to promote merit, and patronized [[Buddhism]], literature and the arts. Her rule formed part of the High Tang period, though her later governance became increasingly autocratic. She was removed in the Shenlong Coup, which restored the Tang, and died a few months later.

64-bit extension of the [[X86|x86]] instruction set, also known as AMD64. Announced in 1999 and first available in the AMD Opteron family in 2003, it adds a 64-bit mode with larger memory addressing and increases the general-purpose registers from 8 to 16. A compatibility mode lets 16-bit and 32-bit applications run unmodified alongside 64-bit programs. ENTRY:
Form of high-energy electromagnetic radiation with a wavelength shorter than ultraviolet and longer than gamma rays. X-rays have wavelengths from 10 nanometers to 10 picometers and photon energies from 100 eV to 100 keV. Wilhelm Conrad Röntgen discovered them in 1895 and named them X-radiation for an unknown type of radiation. They penetrate many solid substances, so [[Radiography|X-ray radiography]] is widely used in medical diagnostics, such as checking for broken bones, and in materials science. X-rays are ionizing radiation, and exposure can cause DNA damage, cancer and, at higher intensities, burns and radiation sickness. Their generation and use are strictly controlled by public health authorities.

Long-haired domesticated Asian [[Cattle|cattle]] (*Bos grunniens*) found throughout the Himalayan region, the [[Tibetan Plateau]], [[Tajikistan]], the Pamir Mountains, and north to [[Mongolia]] and [[Siberia]]. It descends from the wild yak (*Bos mutus*). Domesticated about 5,000–10,000 years ago on the eastern Qinghai-Tibetan Plateau, the yak is adapted to altitudes of 3,000–5,500 meters, with thick [[Wool|wool]], large lungs and heart, and a low heart rate. Around 14 million domestic yaks exist, more than 90 percent of them in China, Mongolia and Russia. They provide milk, meat, wool, hides, dung for fuel and fertilizer, and transport. The wild yak, estimated at about 15,000 individuals, is classified as vulnerable.

Capital and largest city of the [[Sakha Republic]], Russia, about 450 km (280 mi) south of the [[Arctic Circle]]. Fueled by mining, it had a population of 355,443 at the 2021 census. Its average annual temperature is −8.0 °C (17.6 °F), and a record low of −64.4 °C (−83.9 °F) has been recorded. It is the coldest city of more than 200,000 people in the world, though some smaller towns in the region are colder. Yakutsk is the largest city built on continuous [[Permafrost|permafrost]], and it is a major port on the [[Lena River]].

ENTRY:
Members of transnational organized crime syndicates originating in [[Japan]], also called *gokudō* or the Japanese mafia. Their strict codes of conduct and unconventional rituals include *yubitsume*, the amputation of the left little finger. Membership peaked at 184,100 members and quasi-members in 1963. By 2025 there were only 9,400 members and 8,200 quasi-members, as young people stopped joining and legal and social changes discouraged membership. The yakuza still regularly engage in criminal activities, and no strict prohibition on membership exists, although many laws target their revenue and liability.

Private Ivy League research university in [[New Haven, Connecticut|New Haven]], [[Connecticut]]. Founded in 1701 as the Collegiate School by Congregationalist clergy, it is the fourth-oldest institution of higher education in the United States and one of nine colonial colleges chartered before the [[American Revolution]]. It awarded the first PhD in the United States in 1864 and organized as a university in 1887. Yale comprises fifteen constituent schools, including Yale Law School and the Yale Graduate School of Arts and Sciences. Its endowment was valued at $54.5 billion in 2025, the second-largest among educational institutions, and its library holds more than 15 million volumes. Alumni include five U.S. presidents, and the university's athletic teams compete as the Yale [[Bulldog|Bulldogs]].

ENTRY:
Wartime meeting of the heads of government of the United States, the United Kingdom and the Soviet Union, held February 4–11, 1945, near Yalta in [[Crimea]]. [[Franklin D. Roosevelt]], Winston Churchill and [[Joseph Stalin]] met to plan the postwar reorganization of Germany and Europe, with the aim of a peace that gave self-determination to the liberated peoples of Europe. It was the second of three major wartime meetings of the Big Three, after the Tehran Conference in November 1943 and before the Potsdam Conference in July 1945. Its decisions included four zones of occupation for Germany and a framework for the United Nations. Because the [[Cold War]] divided Europe within a few years, the conference became a subject of intense controversy.
