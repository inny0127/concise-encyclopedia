Group of [[Antibiotic|antibiotics]] originally obtained from Penicillium molds. Most penicillins in clinical use are produced by fermenting *Penicillium chrysogenum* in deep tanks. Penicillin G, given intramuscularly or intravenously, and penicillin V, taken by mouth, are the two purified forms in clinical use. Penicillins were among the first drugs effective against many staphylococcal and streptococcal infections, and they remain widely used, although many [[Bacteria|bacteria]] have developed resistance. Fleming, Florey and Chain shared the 1945 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine. Semisynthetic penicillins later broadened the range of bacteria they can treat.

Landform that extends from a mainland, is connected to it on one side and is mostly surrounded by water. Peninsulas exist on every continent, and the largest is the [[Arabian Peninsula|Arabian Peninsula]], at approximately 3.2 million sq km. The connection to the mainland may be an [[Isthmus|isthmus]], such as the Isthmus of Corinth linking the Peloponnese to central Greece. Peninsulas have sheltered prehistoric humans and Neanderthals, who could hunt both land and sea animals, and they have been important for trade through their access to water.

Short chain of [[Amino acid|amino acids]], typically 2–50 residues long, linked by peptide bonds. Nonribosomal peptides are assembled by enzymes rather than by the [[Ribosome|ribosome]]; glutathione, a component of antioxidant defenses, is a common example.

Identification, interpretation, and organization of sensory information in order to represent and understand the environment. The term comes from Latin *perceptio* ('gathering, receiving'). Theater acts out stories before an audience through speech, gesture, music, dance, and spectacle, and is linked to ritual in ancient cultures. ENTRY:
Ordered arrangement of the [[Chemical element|chemical elements]] into rows ("periods") and columns ("groups"). Elements in the same group tend to show similar chemical characteristics, and the table is also divided into four roughly rectangular blocks. It depicts the periodic law, which states that when elements are arranged by their [[Atomic number|atomic numbers]], an approximate recurrence of their properties is evident.

The first generally accepted table was that of the Russian chemist [[Dmitri Mendeleev]] in 1869, who formulated the periodic law in terms of atomic mass. He left gaps for elements not yet known and successfully used the law to predict properties of some of them. The law was explained early in the 20th century by the discovery of atomic numbers and by pioneering work in [[Quantum mechanics|quantum mechanics]]. A recognizably modern form was reached in 1945 with Glenn T. Seaborg's discovery that the actinides are f-block rather than d-block elements.

Each element has a unique atomic number, the number of protons in its nucleus. Atoms with the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons are [[Isotope|isotopes]] of the same element and are grouped together in the table. A new row begins when a new electron shell receives its first electron, and columns are determined by [[Electron configuration|electron configuration]]; oxygen, sulfur, and selenium share a column because each has four electrons in the outermost p-subshell. Of the 118 known elements, the first 94 occur naturally, 83 of them primordial and 11 only in decay chains; the remaining 24, americium to oganesson (95–118), are [[Synthetic element|synthetic]]. By 2010 all 118 were known, completing the first seven rows.

Part of the nervous system outside the brain and spinal cord, made of nerves and ganglia. The peripheral nervous system (PNS) is one of two components of the nervous system of bilateral animals, the other being the [[Central nervous system|central nervous system]] (CNS). Its main function is to connect the CNS to the limbs and organs, relaying signals between the brain and spinal cord and the rest of the body. Unlike the CNS, it is not protected by the skull and vertebral column or by the blood-brain barrier, leaving it more vulnerable to injury. The PNS has somatic and autonomic divisions, each with sensory and motor sectors. The somatic division is under voluntary control, and humans have 31 pairs of spinal nerves. The autonomic division exerts involuntary control over smooth muscle and glands, such as those governing heart rate and [[Digestion|digestion]].

Mass extinction at the boundary of the [[Permian]] and [[Triassic]] periods, about 251.902 million years ago. It is Earth's most severe known extinction event, with 57% of biological families, 62% of genera, 81% of marine species and 70% of terrestrial vertebrate species lost. It is also the greatest known mass extinction of insects, and is called the Great Dying. The scientific consensus is that the main cause was the flood basalt eruptions that formed the [[Siberian Traps]], which released sulfur dioxide and [[Carbon dioxide|carbon dioxide]]. The result was euxinia (oxygen-starved, sulfurous oceans), elevated temperatures and acidified oceans. Atmospheric carbon dioxide rose from around 400 ppm to 2,500 ppm. Other proposed factors include [[Methane|methane]] released from methane clathrates and an extraterrestrial impact, though the chronology and mechanisms remain debated.

Geologic period and last division of the Paleozoic Era, spanning 47 million years. It ran from the end of the [[Carboniferous]] Period, 298.9 million years ago, to the beginning of the [[Triassic|Triassic]] Period, 251.902 million years ago. Sir Roderick Murchison introduced the name in 1841, after the Russian region of Perm. The period saw the diversification of synapsids and sauropsids (reptiles). The world was dominated by the supercontinent [[Pangaea]]. Desert expanded in continental interiors, and amniotes, better suited to drier conditions, rose to dominance over amphibians. At least three and possibly four major extinctions occurred, including the Capitanian mass extinction, associated with the Emeishan Traps. The period ended with the [[Permian–Triassic extinction event]], the largest mass extinction in Earth's history, associated with the Siberian Traps. On land, ecosystems took about 30 million years to recover.

Bijective function of a finite set onto itself, or a rearrangement of its members. When the set is ordered, a permutation changes the place of each member; the six permutations of {1, 2, 3} are the six arrangements of those numbers. The number of permutations of n distinct objects is n [[Factorial|factorial]] (n!), the product of the first n positive integers. The [[Function composition|composition]] of two permutations is a permutation, and all permutations of a set form a [[Group (mathematics)|group]] under this operation, the symmetric group of the set. The study of permutations is a starting point of [[Combinatorics|combinatorics]]. In computer science, a [[Sorting algorithm|sorting algorithm]] computes a permutation that puts data in natural order. Permutation-like hexagrams appeared in the *I Ching* in China.
