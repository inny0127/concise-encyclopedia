Port city (pop., 2024: 5,700,000) on the Mediterranean coast of northern [[Egypt]], at the western edge of the [[Nile]] Delta. Founded by [[Alexander the Great]] in 331 BC on the site of an earlier Egyptian settlement, it became the capital of the [[Ptolemaic Kingdom]] and a leading center of learning in the Hellenistic age and late antiquity, when it was at one time the most populous city in the ancient world. Branch of mathematics that studies abstract systems, known as [[Algebraic structure|algebraic structures]], and the manipulation of expressions within those systems. Algebra generalizes [[Arithmetic|arithmetic]] by introducing variables and algebraic operations other than the standard arithmetic operations, such as addition and multiplication.

An algebraic structure is a non-empty set of mathematical objects, such as the integers, together with operations defined on that set. Arithmetic studies operations in a particular domain of numbers, such as the real numbers. Elementary algebra is the first level of abstraction above it; abstract algebra is a higher level, not limited to a particular domain; and universal algebra is more abstract still, investigating the characteristics of algebraic structures in general.

[[Elementary algebra]], also called school algebra, is the oldest and most basic form. It uses [[Variable (mathematics)|variables]], symbols for unspecified or unknown quantities, to express general laws, such as the commutative property of multiplication, a × b = b × a. Algebraic expressions combine variables and numbers, as in 5x + 3. An equation states that two expressions are equal. Identity equations, such as 2x + 5x = 7x, are true for all values of the variables; conditional equations are true only for some values, as x² = 4 is true if x is either 2 or −2. Elementary algebra seeks the values for which a statement is true by transforming equations to isolate variables. [[Linear algebra]] is a closely related field that investigates linear equations and systems of them, and supports applications in data analysis and quantum mechanics.

[[Abstract algebra]] studies algebraic structures, which consist of a set of objects with one or several operations. It allows objects other than numbers and non-arithmetic operations, and distinguishes types of structures, such as groups, rings, and fields, by the number of operations they use and the laws, called axioms, they follow. Universal algebra and category theory provide general frameworks for studying classes of structures.

Algebraic methods were first studied in the ancient period to solve specific problems in fields like geometry; Babylonian clay tablets from around 1800 BC solved quadratic equations by geometric methods. [[Brahmagupta]] established rules for zero, negative quantities, and operations on variables. In the 9th century the Persian mathematician Muhammad ibn Musa [[Al-Khwarizmi|al-Khwarizmi]] systematized algebra as an independent discipline, distinct from geometry. The word comes from the Arabic *al-jabr*, which originally referred to bonesetting; he used it in the title of his treatise *The Compendious Book on Calculation by Completion and Balancing*. The word entered English in the 16th century. Equations were described in words and abbreviations until the 16th and 17th centuries, when a rigorous symbolic formalism developed. In the mid-19th century algebra broadened beyond a theory of equations to cover diverse operations and structures.

Finite sequence of rigorous instructions, used in mathematics and [[Computer science|computer science]] to solve a class of problems or perform a computation.

An algorithm can be expressed in a finite amount of space and time and in a well-defined formal language. The instructions proceed from an initial state through a finite number of well-defined states, eventually producing output and terminating; randomized algorithms incorporate random input. By contrast, a [[Heuristic|heuristic]] is a problem-solving approach that may not guarantee correct or optimal results. A program is an algorithm only if it stops eventually. In *The Art of Computer Programming*, [[Donald Knuth]] specified five properties: finiteness, definiteness, input, output, and effectiveness.

Step-by-step procedures are recorded in Babylonian mathematics (around 2500 BC), Egyptian mathematics (around 1550 BC), and Indian, Greek, Chinese, and Arabic mathematics. A Sumerian tablet from Shuruppak, c. 2500 BC, describes the earliest division algorithm. The [[Euclidean algorithm]] was first described in Euclid's *Elements* (c. 300 BC), and the [[Sieve of Eratosthenes]] in Nicomachus's *Introduction to Arithmetic*. In the 9th century [[Al-Kindi]] gave the first description of cryptanalysis by frequency analysis, the earliest codebreaking algorithm.

The word derives from the Latinized name of the Persian scholar [[Al-Khwarizmi|al-Khwarizmi]], who around 825 AD wrote a book on Indian computation; its 12th-century Latin translations began *Dixit Algoritmi*, "Thus spoke Al-Khwarizmi." In English, *algorism* meant place-value calculation and occurs c. 1225; [[Geoffrey Chaucer|Chaucer]] used a variant in the late 14th century. Under the influence of Greek *arithmos*, "number," the Latin became *algorithmus* in the 15th century, and Thomas Hood used *algorithm* in English by 1596.

Its population was 34,783 as of June 2025. The Arrernte people have lived in the area for at least 30,000 years.

ENTRY:
American rock band formed in [[Seattle|Seattle]] in 1987. Rooted in heavy metal and associated with grunge, the band is known for harmonized vocals between Layne Staley and Jerry Cantrell. It rose to fame in the early 1990s alongside [[Nirvana (band)|Nirvana]] and [[Pearl Jam|Pearl Jam]]. Its albums *Jar of Flies* (1994) and *Alice in Chains* (1995) both topped the Billboard 200. After Staley's death the band went on hiatus, then reunited with William DuVall as a vocalist. It has sold over 30 million RIAA-certified records in the United States.
