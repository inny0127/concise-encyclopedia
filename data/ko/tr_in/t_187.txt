Group of African languages known for click consonants, once classified as one family. Joseph Greenberg proposed the grouping, but linguists now reject its genetic unity and treat Khoisan as a *sprachbund*, a set of languages that share features through contact. It is now held to comprise three distinct families and two language isolates, spoken mainly in the [[Kalahari Desert]] of [[Namibia]] and [[Botswana]] and in central [[Tanzania]]. In 2002 the [[Library of Congress|Library of Congress]] added it to the National Recording Registry, and [[Rolling Stone|Rolling Stone]] ranked it number 12 in 2003 and number 31 in 2020. The Recording Industry Association of America certified it quintuple platinum in 2019, for five million units in the United States.

Branch of physics and geometry describing motion without regard to the [[Force|forces]] that cause it. ENTRY:
Energy of a moving physical body, equal to one-half its [[Mass|mass]] times the square of its speed. In classical mechanics, a non-rotating object of mass *m* moving at speed *v* has kinetic energy ½*mv*². This equals the [[Work (physics)|work]] needed to accelerate the object from rest to that speed, and the object does the same amount of work when it decelerates to rest. The term was coined in the mid-19th century by [[Lord Kelvin|William Thomson, later Lord Kelvin]], and the word derives from the Greek *kinesis*, meaning motion.
