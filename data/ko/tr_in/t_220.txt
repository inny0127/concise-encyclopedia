ENTRY:
Celestial event of meteors seemingly radiating from one point in the night sky, caused by streams of cosmic debris. Meteor showers occur when [[Meteoroid|meteoroids]] enter Earth's atmosphere at high speed on parallel trajectories. Most meteors are smaller than a grain of sand and disintegrate before reaching the ground. The Meteor Data Centre lists over 900 suspected showers, about 100 of them well established. Very intense showers are called meteor storms, producing at least 1,000 meteors an hour, most notably the Leonids. In 1794, Ernst Chladni proposed that meteors originated in [[Outer space|outer space]]. In 1866, Giovanni Schiaparelli showed that the Leonids shared an orbit with Comet Tempel, linking meteor showers to [[Comet|comets]].

ENTRY:
Rock from outer space, fallen to the surface of a planet or moon. Meteorites are traditionally divided into stony meteorites, mainly of silicate minerals; iron meteorites, largely of ferronickel; and stony-iron meteorites, which contain both metallic and rocky material. Meteorites recovered after being observed during their fall are called falls, and all others are finds. Most originate from a handful of asteroid breakup events in the [[Asteroid belt|asteroid belt]]. Iron meteoroids transit the atmosphere most easily and cause most of the hypervelocity impact craters on Earth. The [[Tunguska event]] probably resulted from a stony or icy body that disintegrated in the atmosphere without leaving a crater. Meteorites have also been found on the [[Moon]] and [[Mars]].

ENTRY:
Small body in outer space, ranging from grains up to one meter wide and far smaller than an asteroid. Most meteoroids come from the [[Asteroid belt|asteroid belt]], perturbed by planetary gravity, while others are particles from [[Comet|comets]] that give rise to [[Meteor shower|meteor showers]]. Some are impact debris ejected from the [[Moon]] or [[Mars]]. An estimated 25 million meteoroids, micrometeoroids, and other space debris enter Earth's atmosphere each day, which results in an estimated 15,000 metric tons (16,535 short tons) entering each year. Stony meteoroids that contain grain-like inclusions are called chondrites. In 2017 the International Astronomical Union revised the definition to objects between 30 μm and one meter in diameter.

ENTRY:
Scientific study of the Earth's atmosphere and short-term weather, focusing on forecasting. Its applications include the military, aviation, energy production, transport, agriculture, construction, and disaster management. Together with [[Climatology|climatology]], atmospheric physics, and atmospheric chemistry, it forms the broader atmospheric sciences. Meteorologists use instruments such as thermometers, barometers, weather satellites, balloons, buoys, and radars to forecast weather at local, regional, and global scales. Ancient civilizations relied on folklore and astrology, and [[Aristotle]]'s treatise *Meteorology* summarized early observations. Numerical weather prediction in the 20th century, coupled with computers, transformed forecasting. Because the atmosphere is chaotic, uncertainty remains, a limit described by the [[Butterfly effect|butterfly effect]].

Study of research methods, and the philosophical assumptions behind them. A method is a structured procedure for reaching a goal, such as acquiring knowledge or verifying knowledge claims. Methodologies are traditionally divided into [[Quantitative research|quantitative]] and [[Qualitative research|qualitative]] research. Quantitative research is the main methodology of the natural sciences, using precise numerical measurements to find universal laws. The dominant approach there is the [[Scientific method]], which includes observation, formulating a [[Hypothesis|hypothesis]], testing it by experiment, and publishing the findings. In recent decades, many social scientists have adopted mixed-methods research, which combines both approaches. A few theorists reject methodology as a discipline, while methodologists argue that a good methodology helps researchers reach reliable theories efficiently.

Metric spaces provide a general setting for studying concepts of [[Mathematical analysis|mathematical analysis]] and [[Geometry|geometry]], such as balls and completeness. Capital and largest city of [[Mexico]] (pop., 2020: 9,209,944), the most populous city in North America. It lies in the Valley of Mexico on the high Mexican Central Plateau at an altitude of 2,240 m and is divided into 16 boroughs, or *alcaldías*. The city proper covers 1,495 sq km and is the second-largest Spanish-speaking city proper in the world after Lima. Greater Mexico City, with 21,804,515 people, is the world's 15th-largest metropolitan area. In 2011 it had a GDP of $411 billion; the city accounted for 15.8% of Mexico's GDP and the metropolitan area for about 22%.

It is the oldest capital city in the Americas and one of only two founded by Indigenous peoples. It began as [[Tenochtitlan]], a Mexica city built around 1325 on islands in Lake Texcoco. After the 1521 siege it was nearly destroyed and rebuilt to Spanish urban standards, and during the colonial period it was a major political, administrative, and financial center, the capital of [[New Spain]]. The surrounding territory was organized in 1824 as the country's only federal district, the Distrito Federal.

Residents won the right to elect the head of government and the unicameral Legislative Assembly in 1997; since then left-wing parties, first the Party of the Democratic Revolution and later Morena, have controlled both offices. The city has adopted policies including legal abortion, no-fault divorce, [[Same-sex marriage|same-sex marriage]], and legal gender change. On January 29, 2016, the Federal District was officially renamed Ciudad de México, or CDMX. A constitutional clause prevents it from becoming a state while it remains the capital. Baron [[Alexander von Humboldt]] is credited with the nickname City of Palaces. Its informal demonym is *chilango*.

ENTRY:
Group of 37 phyllosilicate [[Mineral|minerals]] whose crystals split easily into fragile, elastic plates along perfect basal cleavage. Mica has a layered structure in which two silica sheets bond to an octahedral sheet, with interlayer [[Potassium|potassium]], [[Sodium|sodium]], or [[Calcium|calcium]] ions. It is common in [[Granite|granite]], pegmatite, and [[Schist|schist]], and occasionally found as flakes in [[Sedimentary rock|sedimentary rock]]. The most economically important members are muscovite, a potassium-aluminum silicate, and phlogopite, a potassium-magnesium silicate. Mica's high dielectric strength and heat resistance make it useful as an electrical insulator in capacitors, and it is also used in paints, drywall, and cosmetics. Muscovite withstands temperatures up to about 700°C.
