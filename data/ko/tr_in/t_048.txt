Rhythmic process of inhaling [[Oxygen|oxygen]]-rich air into the [[Lung|lungs]] and exhaling carbon dioxide, enabling gas exchange. All aerobic organisms require oxygen for [[Cellular respiration|cellular respiration]], which produces carbon dioxide as a waste product. In [[Vertebrate|vertebrates]] with lungs, breathing consists of repeated cycles of inhalation and exhalation through a branched system of airways that lead to the alveoli, where gases move by [[Diffusion|diffusion]]. Clastic rock of angular, coarse fragments bound by a finer-grained matrix or cement. Unlike [[Conglomerate (geology)|conglomerate]], whose clasts are rounded, breccia contains at least 30 percent [[Gravel|gravel]]-sized particles over 2 mm with sharp edges that have not been worn down, indicating deposition close to the source. Sedimentary breccias form by sedimentary processes, such as scree cemented at the base of a cliff. Fault or tectonic breccias result from the grinding of two fault blocks as they slide past each other. Volcanic breccias form by explosive eruption of lava and other rock. Megabreccias, with fragments from at least 1 meter to more than 400 meters, can form through [[Landslide|landslides]], [[Impact event|impact]] events or [[Caldera|caldera]] collapse.

Group of domestic animals within a [[Species|species]] that share homogeneous appearance, behavior or other characteristics distinguishing them from other members of the species. Breeds form through genetic isolation and either natural adaptation or [[Selective breeding|selective breeding]], or both. No single scientifically accepted definition of the term exists; a breed is a term used among breeders for a nameable subset of a species. Individuals of a breed pass predictable traits to their offspring, a property called breeding true. Plant breeds are more commonly called [[Cultivar|cultivars]]. Offspring of animals from different breeds are crossbreeds or mixed breeds, and crosses above the breed level, such as between species, are [[Hybrid (biology)|hybrids]]. Breeders select foundation stock and mate the most desirable animals, and a breed standard describes desirable and undesirable traits. Populations must be large enough to maintain a breed without forced [[Inbreeding|inbreeding]].

[[Electromagnetic radiation|Electromagnetic radiation]] produced when a charged particle, typically an [[Electron|electron]], is deflected and decelerated by the field of another charged particle, usually an [[Atomic nucleus|atomic nucleus]]. The particle loses [[Kinetic energy|kinetic energy]], which is converted into [[Photon|photons]], in accordance with [[Conservation of energy|conservation of energy]]. The spectrum is continuous, becoming more intense and shifting toward higher frequencies as the energy change of the decelerated particles increases. In the narrower sense used most often, the term refers to radiation produced when electrons decelerate in matter, as in [[X-ray|X-ray]] tubes.

Structure designed to span an obstacle, such as a river or railway, and carry vehicles, pedestrians, and other loads across it. Most bridges consist of a flat deck supported by beams, arches, or cables, resting on a [[Foundation (engineering)|foundation]] designed to transfer the bridge's weight to the subsoil without settling.

The earliest bridges were wooden boardwalks or logs across wetlands and creeks. Corbel arch bridges were built c. 13th century BC in Mycenaean Greece, and the Arkadiko Bridge still exists. The Romans and ancient Chinese built major arch bridges of timber, stone, and brick; the Romans also used cement, mixed with small rocks to form [[Concrete|concrete]]. Trajan's Bridge (c. 105 AD) crossed the [[Danube]] and was over 900 m long. The Anji Bridge, built from 595 to 605 AD during the [[Sui dynasty]], is the oldest surviving stone bridge in China. The Inca built rope suspension bridges in the [[Andes]]. In Renaissance Europe, scientific and engineering advances produced wider spans, truss bridges, and stone segmental arches.

The [[Industrial Revolution]] brought mass-produced [[Steel|steel]], which enabled truss and cantilever bridges able to cross wide rivers and deep valleys. The longest spans use suspension or cable-stayed designs, both relying on high-strength steel cables, and the maximum span reached 2 km in 2022. Other forms include viaducts, trestles, and movable bridges such as drawbridges and swing bridges.

A bridge must support its own weight and that of its traffic, and tolerate stresses from winds, floods, and earthquakes; engineers typically use limit state design and the finite element method. Bridges can shorten transport times and raise gross domestic product, but can also increase pollution. Some serve as landmarks, such as the [[Golden Gate Bridge]].
