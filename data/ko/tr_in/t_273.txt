ENTRY:
Numerical description of how likely an event is to occur, expressed as a number between 0 and 1.

The larger the probability, the more likely the event. It is often given as a percentage from 0% to 100%. For a fair coin, "heads" and "tails" are equally probable, so each has probability 1/2, or 0.5, or 50%. These ideas are formalized axiomatically in [[Probability theory]], used in [[Statistics|statistics]], mathematics, science, finance, gambling, artificial intelligence, machine learning, and game theory to draw inferences about the expected frequency of events and to describe regularities of complex systems.

Two main categories of interpretation compete. Objectivists describe an objective or physical state of affairs. The most popular version is frequentist probability, the relative frequency of an outcome when an experiment is repeated indefinitely. Subjectivists treat probability as a degree of belief. The most popular version is Bayesian probability, which combines expert knowledge, represented by a prior probability distribution, with experimental data to produce a posterior distribution.

The word derives from Latin *probabilitas*, which could also mean the authority of a witness in a legal case. In the 16th century the Italian polymath [[Gerolamo Cardano]] defined odds as the ratio of favorable to unfavorable outcomes. The doctrine of probabilities dates to the 1654 correspondence of [[Pierre de Fermat]] and [[Blaise Pascal]]. [[Christiaan Huygens]] (1657) gave the earliest known scientific treatment. Jakob Bernoulli's *Ars Conjectandi* (1713) and Abraham de Moivre's *Doctrine of Chances* (1718) treated the subject as a branch of mathematics. In 1933 [[Andrey Kolmogorov]] established its rigorous foundation, axiomatizing it using measure theory.

Function describing the relative likelihood that a continuous [[Random variable|random variable]] takes a given value. Probability density is the probability per unit length. Because any single exact value of a continuous variable has probability zero, the density shows how much more likely one value is than another. The probability of falling within a range is the [[Integral|integral]] of the density over that range, and the density is nonnegative with total area equal to one. In one example, the probability that a bacterium dies between 5 and 5.01 hours is 0.02, giving a density of 2 hour⁻¹ at 5 hours. The term density function is also used for the probability mass function of discrete variables, which causes confusion.

Difference between revenue from outputs and the total costs of inputs, also called surplus value. Economic profit is smaller than accounting profit, which counts only the explicit costs that appear on financial statements. Meaning of a declarative sentence, and bearer of truth value. Propositions are the meanings shared by sentences such as *Snow is white* and its German equivalent, and they are the objects of beliefs. True propositions describe the world as it is, while false ones do not. Researchers distinguish affirmative and negative, universal and existential, and categorical and conditional propositions. Realists hold that propositions form part of reality, a view rejected by anti-realists. Some theories define propositions as sets of possible worlds, others as structured entities built from individuals and concepts. The study of propositions began in ancient philosophy with [[Aristotle]] and the Stoics, and later involved [[William of Ockham]], [[Gottlob Frege]] and [[Bertrand Russell]]. Logicians examine their inferential patterns, while psychologists study attitudes such as belief and desire.

ENTRY:
Branch of classical logic dealing with propositions and their relations. Also called sentential calculus or statement logic, it studies compound propositions formed with connectives for conjunction, disjunction, implication, biconditional and negation. Propositions are represented by letters called propositional variables, and the subject deals with neither quantifiers nor non-logical objects. All of its machinery is included in [[First-order logic]] and higher-order logics, which makes it their foundation. In its classical truth-functional form, each formula has exactly one truth value, and the principle of bivalence and the law of excluded middle are upheld. Chrysippus is often credited with developing a deductive system for propositional logic in the 3rd century BC. [[Gottlob Frege]]'s predicate logic built upon it, and natural deduction was invented by Gerhard Gentzen and Stanisław Jaśkowski.

ENTRY:
Nonselective beta blocker medication. Propranolol is used to treat high blood pressure, some irregular heart rhythms, angina, essential tremor, thyrotoxicosis and infantile hemangiomas, and to prevent migraine headaches. It works by blocking β-adrenergic receptors. Common side effects include nausea, abdominal pain and constipation, and it may worsen [[Asthma]]. Patented in 1962 and approved for medical use in 1964, it is on the [[World Health Organization]]'s List of Essential Medicines. In 2023 it was the 69th most commonly prescribed medication in the United States, with more than 9 million prescriptions.

Also called propene, the second-simplest [[Alkene|alkene]] (CH₃CH=CH₂). Propylene is a colorless gas with a faint petroleum-like odor. It was discovered in 1850 by John Williams Reynolds, a student of A. W. von Hofmann, as the only gaseous product of the thermal decomposition of amyl alcohol to react with chlorine and bromine. Global production reached about 130 million metric tons in 2024, and most of it is used to make [[Polypropylene]]. The dominant production route is steam cracking of [[Naphtha]], although [[Propane]] is a common feedstock in North America. Propylene is also made by dehydrogenation of propane. Its derivatives include propylene oxide, acrylonitrile, and cumene, which is used to make [[Phenol]] and [[Acetone]].

ENTRY:
Ordinary form of language, following conversational speech without regular rhythm. Prose differs from poetry, which is organized by rhythmic meter, rhyme or other artistic structure. Prose can range from informal everyday speech to formal academic writing, and in writing it follows typical conventions and formatting. The word entered English in the 14th century, from Old French and the Latin *prosa oratio*, meaning straightforward speech. Latin was the dominant medium for learned prose in Europe until recent times, and works by [[Francis Bacon]] and [[Baruch Spinoza]] were first published in Latin. Dante created vernacular prose in the non-metrical parts of *Vita Nuova*, written about 1293. Academic writing, journalism and fiction are usually written in prose. The critic [[T. S. Eliot]] observed that the distinction between verse and prose is clear, but that between poetry and prose is obscure.
