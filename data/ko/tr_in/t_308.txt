ENTRY:
Decapod [[Crustacean|crustacean]] with an elongated body that moves mainly by swimming. Shrimp usually belong to the decapod groups Caridea or Dendrobranchiata. They swim by paddling swimmerets under the abdomen and escape by flicking the tail, which drives them backward in a movement called "lobstering". Unlike [[Crab|crabs]] and [[Lobster|lobsters]], shrimp have thin, fragile legs used mainly for perching. Thousands of species live in freshwater and marine habitats, and they are an important food for fish and [[Whale|whales]]. Most live from one to seven years. Their muscular tails are widely eaten, with about 440 billion farmed and about 25 trillion wild shrimp killed annually. Excessive bycatch and overfishing are significant concerns, and intensive farming has raised concerns over [[Mangrove|mangrove]] deforestation and pollution.

Length of [[Linen|linen]] cloth bearing a faint image of a naked man, venerated by some Christians as the burial shroud of Jesus. The shroud is documented from 1354, when it was exhibited at Lirey, France, and the bishop of Troyes denounced it as a forgery in 1389. Small- to medium-sized perennial woody plant with several stems rising from or near the ground. Shrubs are distinguished from [[Tree|trees]] by their multiple stems and by a height of less than 6–10 m (20–33 ft). Small shrubs under 2 m (6.6 ft) tall are called subshrubs. Some species survive only about five years in good conditions, while larger woody species can live beyond 70 years. Shrubs may be deciduous or evergreen, providing wildlife habitat and food while helping to stabilize soil on slopes. Many garden cultivars are bred for flowers, such as [[Rhododendron|rhododendrons]] and [[Rose|roses]]. Apart from berry-bearing species, few shrubs are eaten directly, but [[Tea|tea]] and coffee are normally harvested from shrub-sized plants, and perfumed species such as lavender are used. A landscape dominated by shrubs is called [[Shrubland|shrubland]].

ENTRY:
Psychological trait marked by apprehension and inhibition in new social settings or when others may judge. Shyness, also called diffidence, is frequently associated with social avoidance and is considered a normal personality variant. It is distinct from social anxiety disorder, which impairs daily functioning. Although shyness can be an attribute of people with [[Self-esteem|low self-esteem]], it is often defined by concern about the negative evaluation of others, such as mocking or rejection. Genetic data suggest that shyness is heritable, though environmental factors such as child abuse or ridicule contribute to its development. Research indicates a positive correlation between childhood fearfulness and shyness, and higher cortisol levels in shy children are associated with increased susceptibility to illness. Shyness is distinct from introversion, which involves a preference for low-stimulation environments rather than fear of social judgment.

Single-handed agricultural tool with a variously curved blade, typically used to reap grain or cut forage for [[Livestock|livestock]]. Harvesting sickles, first developed around 15,000 years ago, had wooden handles and flint blades. Sickles helped drive the transition to [[Agriculture|agriculture]] in the Neolithic era, and their use is accepted as having led directly to the domestication of Near Eastern wild grasses. Group of inherited blood disorders involving abnormal [[Hemoglobin|hemoglobin]]. He remains a controversial figure in Poland: his patronage of the arts and architecture and his military victories are weighed against intrigues, religious conflicts and the cession of Ducal Prussia to Brandenburg. Sigismund's Column in Warsaw, the first secular column monument of modern history, was commissioned by his son, Władysław IV Vasa.

Austrian neurologist, founder of [[Psychoanalysis]].

His early research on nervous tissue, in Ernst Brücke's physiology laboratory, proved seminal for the discovery of the [[Neuron|neuron]] in the 1890s.

Psychoanalysis is a clinical method for treating pathologies arising from conflicts in the psyche through dialogue between patient and analyst. His redefinition of sexuality to include infantile stages led him to formulate the [[Oedipus complex]] as a central tenet. Natural language that conveys meaning through the visual-manual modality rather than spoken words. Process of varying one or more properties of a periodic waveform to transmit information. ENTRY:
Measure of the level of a desired signal relative to background [[Noise|noise]], usually expressed in [[Decibel|decibels]]. It is defined as the ratio of signal power to noise power, and a ratio above 1:1 (greater than 0 dB) indicates more signal than noise. SNR is a key parameter for communication systems, audio equipment, [[Radar|radar]], imaging and data acquisition systems. A high SNR means a signal is clear and easy to detect, while a low SNR means the signal is obscured or corrupted. It can be improved by increasing signal strength, reducing noise, filtering or using error-correction techniques. The Shannon–Hartley theorem, a fundamental law of information theory, shows that the maximum data rate of a channel depends on its bandwidth and SNR. In decibels, SNR (dB) = 10 log₁₀(signal power ÷ noise power).
