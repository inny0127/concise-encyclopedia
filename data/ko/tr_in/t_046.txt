State of matter formed by cooling a dilute gas of [[Boson|bosons]] to temperatures close to [[Absolute zero|absolute zero]]. Most bosons then occupy the lowest quantum state, so quantum effects such as wave interference become visible on a macroscopic scale. [[Albert Einstein]] predicted the condensate in 1924–25, building on a paper by [[Satyendra Nath Bose]] on quantum statistics. On June 5, 1995, Eric Cornell and Carl Wieman of the University of Colorado Boulder produced the first gaseous condensate, using rubidium atoms cooled to 170 nanokelvins. Later that year Wolfgang Ketterle at [[Massachusetts Institute of Technology|MIT]] produced one with sodium atoms. Cornell, Wieman and Ketterle shared the 2001 Nobel Prize in Physics. In 1938 Fritz London proposed the condensate as a mechanism for [[Superfluidity|superfluidity]] in helium-4.

Class of subatomic particles with integer spin. Some bosons are elementary force carriers: the [[Photon|photon]] mediates the electromagnetic force, gluons mediate the strong force, and the W and Z bosons mediate the weak force. Relaxed style of [[Samba|samba]] developed in [[Rio de Janeiro]], Brazil, in the late 1950s and early 1960s. ENTRY:
Branch of biology that studies the growth, reproduction, and evolution of plants; also called plant science.

Plants can be studied at levels from biochemistry, molecular biology, and genetics to ecology, pathology, anatomy, morphology, and taxonomy. They include approximately 410,000 species of land plants and some 391,000 species of vascular plants, of which approximately 369,000 are flowering plants and approximately 20,000 are bryophytes. The term comes from the Ancient Greek *botanē*, meaning pasture, herbs, or fodder.

Botany originated in prehistoric herbalism, the identification of edible, poisonous, and medicinal plants. Theophrastus (c. 371–287 BC), a student of [[Aristotle]] and called the Father of Botany, wrote *Enquiry into Plants* and *On the Causes of Plants*, the most important contributions to the subject until the Middle Ages. Medieval physic gardens were forerunners of university botanical gardens; the Padua garden of 1545 is usually considered the first still in its original location. Cataloguing garden collections led in 1753 to the binomial nomenclature of [[Carl Linnaeus]], still used to name all biological species.

In the last two decades of the 20th century, botanists used molecular genetic analysis, including DNA sequences, to classify plants more accurately. Dominant 21st-century themes are molecular genetics and epigenetics, which study the control of gene expression during differentiation of plant cells and tissues.

Botanical research supplies staple foods, timber, oil, rubber, fiber, and drugs, such as aspirin derived from willow bark, and serves horticulture, agriculture, forestry, plant breeding, and the maintenance of biodiversity. An estimated 45% of known plant species face extinction risks (as of 2023).

Member of the highest rank of the feudal nobility in many Eastern European states. Comparable to dukes, boyars were second only to ruling princes, grand princes or tsars from the 10th to the 17th centuries. The rank existed in Bulgaria, [[Kievan Rus']] (later Russia), Moldavia, [[Wallachia]] (later [[Romania]]), [[Lithuania]] and among Baltic Germans. ENTRY:
Empirical gas law describing the relationship between [[Pressure|pressure]] and [[Volume|volume]] of a confined gas. At constant temperature and for a fixed amount of gas, the pressure of an ideal gas is inversely proportional to the volume it occupies, so their product is constant. [[Robert Boyle]] published the law in 1662 after experiments with air in a closed J-shaped tube. The French physicist Edme Mariotte discovered the same relationship independently, so the law is also called Mariotte's law.

ENTRY:
Infraorder of decapod crustaceans comprising the [[Crab|true crabs]]. They have a very short abdomen usually hidden under the thorax, a thick exoskeleton and five pairs of legs, the frontmost ending in pincer-like chelae. True crabs live in all the world's oceans, in fresh water and on land. They are omnivores, eating a significant proportion of algae as well as detritus and other invertebrates, and over 1.5 million tonnes are caught annually for food. True crabs first appeared in the fossil record during the Jurassic period, around 200 million years ago, and diversified in the Cretaceous. Around 7,000 living species in 96 families are known. Females brood fertilized eggs on their abdomen until the zoea larvae hatch into the plankton.
