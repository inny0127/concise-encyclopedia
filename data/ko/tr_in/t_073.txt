Icy small Solar System body that warms and releases gas when passing close to the Sun. This outgassing produces an extended atmosphere, the coma, around the nucleus, and sometimes a tail of gas and dust blown out by solar radiation and the solar wind. Nuclei range from a few hundred meters to tens of kilometers across and are loose collections of ice, dust, and small rocky particles. The coma may be up to 15 times Earth's diameter, and the tail may stretch beyond one [[Astronomical unit|astronomical unit]].

Nuclei contain water ice, frozen carbon dioxide, carbon monoxide, methane, and ammonia, along with organic compounds such as methanol and formaldehyde. The amino acid glycine was confirmed in comet dust recovered by NASA's Stardust mission in 2009. Nuclear surfaces have very low albedo: the Giotto probe found that the nucleus of [[Halley's Comet]] reflects about four percent of incident light, compared with seven percent for asphalt.

Comets usually have highly eccentric orbits, with periods from several years to potentially several million years. Short-period comets originate in the [[Kuiper belt]] or its scattered disc beyond Neptune; long-period comets are thought to come from the [[Oort cloud]].

Comets differ from [[Asteroid|asteroids]], which form no tail and are thought to have formed inside the orbit of Jupiter, although main-belt comets have blurred the distinction. As of November 2021, 4,584 comets were known. NASA's Deep Impact blasted a crater on Comet Tempel 1, and the [[European Space Agency]]'s Rosetta was the first to land a robotic spacecraft on a comet.

The word derives from [[Latin]] *comēta*, from Greek *κομήτης*, 'wearing long hair'.

Medium that expresses ideas with images, often combined with text, typically in a sequence of panels. Speech balloons, captions, and [[Onomatopoeia|onomatopoeia]] indicate dialogue, narration, and sound effects. Theorists and historians have no consensus on a single definition; some emphasize the combination of image and text, others sequentiality, mass reproduction, or recurring characters. Common forms include comic strips, gag cartoons, and comic books, and since the late 20th century graphic novels and webcomics.

The European, American, and Japanese traditions followed different paths. Europeans often trace their tradition to the Swiss Rodolphe Töpffer, whose cartoon strips date from as early as 1827. American comics emerged as a mass medium in the early 20th century through newspaper strips, with Richard F. Graphic novels such as [[Will Eisner]]'s *A Contract with God* (1978) later broadened the medium. Comics had a lowbrow reputation for much of their history, but toward the end of the 20th century they gained respect as a serious art form among the public and academics.

Viral infectious disease of the upper respiratory tract that primarily affects the mucosa of the nose, throat, sinuses, and larynx. Symptoms may appear as little as two days after exposure and include cough, runny nose, sneezing, nasal congestion, sore throat, headache, fatigue, and sometimes fever, which is uncommon in adults but common in infants and young children. People usually recover in seven to ten days, though some symptoms may last up to three weeks. Occasionally, those with other health problems develop [[Pneumonia|pneumonia]]. Influenza symptoms are similar but usually more severe.

Well over 200 virus strains are implicated. Rhinoviruses cause up to 40% of cases and have over 100 known serotypes; [[Coronavirus|coronaviruses]], adenoviruses and adenoviruses are also common. Transmission is by airborne droplets or by contact with nasal secretions or contaminated objects. Risk factors include attending child care facilities, poor sleep, and psychological stress. Symptoms are mostly due to the body's immune response rather than tissue destruction by the viruses.

There is no [[Vaccine|vaccine]], because of the rapid mutation and wide variation of the viruses, and no cure. Prevention rests on hand washing, not touching the eyes, nose, or mouth with unwashed hands, and avoiding sick people. Symptoms can be treated: [[Zinc|zinc]] may reduce their duration and severity if started shortly after onset, and nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs such as [[Ibuprofen|ibuprofen]] may help with pain. [[Antibiotic|Antibiotics]] should not be used, since colds are caused by viruses, and there is no good evidence that cough medicines shorten a cold.

The common cold is the most frequent infectious disease in humans. The average adult has two to three colds a year and the average child six to eight, most often in winter.

Transmission of information, in which a source conveys a message through a channel to a receiver. The precise definition is disputed. Scholars disagree about whether unintentional or failed transmissions count, whether deliberate deception counts, and whether communication only transmits meaning or also creates it. The term can also refer to the message itself or to the field of inquiry, communication studies. It derives from the [[Latin]] verb *communicare*, 'to share' or 'to make common'.

Definitions range from broad to narrow. Broad definitions include unconscious and non-human behavior, so that animals communicate within their species and flowers signal the location of nectar to bees through color and shape. Narrower ones restrict communication to conscious interaction among humans, focus on symbols and signs, or treat the communicator's intent as central. Philosopher Paul Grice identifies communication with actions that aim to make the recipient aware of the communicator's intention. Researchers of animal and plant communication tend to define it by observable responses and by benefit to the participants.

Models of communication are simplified, usually diagrammatic overviews of its main components and their interaction. Many include a source that uses a coding system to express a message, which is sent through a channel to a receiver who must decode it. [[Claude Shannon]] and Warren Weaver characterized communication as transmission involving a source, message, encoder, channel, decoder, and receiver. Models are commonly classed as linear transmission models, in which information flows in one direction; interaction models, which add a feedback loop; and transaction models. Transactional and constitutive views reject the pure transmission view, holding that communication also shapes participants' experience by creating meaning. [[Noise]] and other barriers can disrupt the process.

Human communication is commonly classified as verbal or non-verbal. Verbal communication uses messages in linguistic form, including spoken and written messages and [[Sign language|sign language]]. Non-verbal communication occurs without a linguistic system, through [[Body language|body language]], touch, and facial expressions. Communication may also be interpersonal, between distinct persons, or intrapersonal, with oneself. Communicative competence is the ability to formulate and understand messages.

Non-human communication includes animal and plant communication. Animals communicate in courtship and mating, parent–offspring relations, navigation, and self-defense. Chemical communication is especially important for relatively immobile plants; maple trees, for example, release volatile organic compounds into the air to warn other plants of a herbivore attack. Most communication occurs within a species because its purpose is usually cooperation. Interspecies communication occurs mainly in symbiotic relationships, and humans engage in it when interacting with pets and working animals.

How people exchange information has changed with communication technologies, among them writing systems, mass printing, radio and television, and the internet. These advances also created new forms, such as the exchange of data between computers.

Political and economic ideology whose goal is a communist society: a classless order based on common ownership of the means of production, distribution, and exchange, with products allocated according to need. Such a society would have no private property or social classes and, ultimately, no money or state. The word derives from French *communisme*, from [[Latin]] *communis*, 'common'.

Communists often seek voluntary self-governance but disagree on the means. A libertarian socialist approach stresses revolutionary spontaneity and workers' self-management; an authoritarian socialist, party-driven approach aims to establish a socialist state expected to wither away. These share the analysis that under capitalism the proletariat, who sell their labor power, and the bourgeoisie, who own the means of production, stand in an exploitative relationship that only social revolution can resolve.

Modern communism grew out of the 19th-century European socialist movement. In 1848 [[Karl Marx]] and Friedrich Engels offered a new definition in *The Communist Manifesto*. Communist governments espousing Marxism–Leninism came to power first in the Soviet Union with the 1917 Russian Revolution, and after World War II in Eastern Europe, Asia, and elsewhere.

For much of the 20th century more than one-third of the world's population lived under Communist governments, characterized by one-party rule, state control of the economy, and suppression of opposition. Supporters and critics assess the record differently: some regimes achieved rapid industrialization or literacy gains, while critics point to repression, forced labor, and famines. Mass killings under communist regimes and their mortality figures remain controversial and debated.

After the [[Dissolution of the Soviet Union]] in 1991, many governments abolished Communist rule. Remaining Communist governments include China, Cuba, Laos, North Korea, and Vietnam.

Type of [[Topological space|topological space]] central to general topology and analysis. A space is compact if every open cover has a finite subcover, which lets it behave in many ways like a finite set. Every [[Continuous function|continuous]] real-valued function on a compact space attains a maximum and a minimum. For subsets of [[Euclidean space]], compactness is equivalent to being closed and bounded, by the Heine–Borel theorem. Arbitrary products of compact spaces are compact, by Tychonoff's theorem.
