Phenomenon in which an oscillating system absorbs energy at its [[Frequency|resonant frequency]] and responds with maximum amplitude. It occurs when an external force or vibration matches that frequency, and the system then vibrates with greater [[Amplitude|amplitude]] than at other frequencies. This can be useful, as in musical instruments or radio receivers, but it can also cause excessive vibration or structural failure. Biological process of transporting oxygen from the environment to body tissues and removing carbon dioxide, using a respiratory system. Gas exchange in the lungs occurs through [[Breathing|ventilation]] (commonly called breathing), the movement of air in and out of the lungs, and perfusion, the pulmonary circulation of [[Blood|blood]] through the [[Capillary|pulmonary capillaries]]. Inhalation is usually active, while exhalation is usually passive, though speaking, singing, coughing and swimming involve active exhalation. Physiological respiration differs from [[Cellular respiration|cellular respiration]], the metabolic process by which cells obtain energy from nutrients in the form of ATP and NADPH. Condition in which inadequate gas exchange leaves arterial oxygen, [[Carbon dioxide|carbon dioxide]], or both outside normal levels. A drop in blood oxygen is called hypoxemia, and a rise in arterial carbon dioxide is called hypercapnia. Respiratory failure is classified as Type 1 or Type 2, depending on whether carbon dioxide is high, and it can be acute or chronic. Biological system of organs and structures used for gas exchange in animals and plants. In land animals the respiratory surface is internalized as the lining of the [[Lung|lungs]], where gas exchange occurs in millions of small air sacs with a rich blood supply: alveoli in [[Mammal|mammals]] and reptiles, atria in [[Bird|birds]]. Airways link these sacs to the environment. The trachea branches in the middle of the chest into two main bronchi, which divide into progressively narrower secondary and tertiary bronchi and then into bronchioles (parabronchi in birds). Air is pumped in by breathing, which involves the muscles of respiration; inhalation at rest results mainly from contraction of the diaphragm.

In humans and other mammals the tract is divided into an upper part (nose, nasal cavities, sinuses, pharynx, and the part of the larynx above the vocal folds) and a lower part (the lower larynx, trachea, bronchi, bronchioles, and alveoli). The branching lower airways, called the respiratory tree, have about 23 generations in the adult human and about 13 in the mouse. Generations 0–16 serve only as conduits; gas exchange takes place in approximately generations 17–23. Because alveoli are dead ends, about 150 ml of dead space stays filled with air after exhalation.

In most [[Fish|fish]] and many other aquatic animals the system consists of [[Gill|gills]], whose thin filaments and lamellae expose a very large surface of vascularized tissue to water. [[Insect|Insects]] have very simple respiratory anatomy, and in [[Amphibian|amphibians]] the skin also plays a vital role in gas exchange. Plants also have respiratory systems, built around [[Stoma|stomata]], but the direction of gas exchange can be opposite to that in animals.

Characteristic number in [[Fluid dynamics|fluid dynamics]] that measures the ratio of inertial to viscous forces. The state was established on August 30, 1946, by the French military administration from parts of Prussia, [[Hesse|Hesse]] and [[Bavaria|Bavaria]]. It became part of the Federal Republic of Germany in 1949. Its heritage includes the Palatinate winegrowing region, picturesque landscapes and many castles and palaces.

Family of large odd-toed ungulates (perissodactyls), *Rhinocerotidae*, with five living species. The black and white rhinoceroses are native to [[Africa|Africa]], while the Javan, Indian and Sumatran rhinoceroses are native to South and Southeast Asia. All living species weigh more than half a tonne as adults. They have one or two horns made of keratin, the material of hair and fingernails, Poachers kill rhinos for their horns, which are sold on the [[Black market|black market]] at high prices, and most living species are endangered. Demand is driven mainly by China and Vietnam, where horn is used in [[Traditional Chinese medicine|traditional Chinese medicine]], although there is no good evidence of health benefits. The southern white rhinoceros recovered from near extinction in the early 20th century to over 18,000 individuals by the 2010s.

Diverse, species-rich [[Clade|clade]] of mostly unicellular [[Eukaryote|eukaryotes]]. Except for chlorarachniophytes and three species of the genus *Paulinella*, they are not photosynthetic, but many [[Foraminifera|Foraminifera]] and [[Radiolaria|Radiolaria]] have symbiotic relationships with unicellular algae. Most are amoeboid, with filose, reticulose or microtubule-supported pseudopods, and they lack clear morphological characters that define the group. Some possess mineral skeletons made of opal, celestite or calcite. Certain species form colonies about 1 cm in diameter and more than 1 m long. The three main groups are Cercozoa, Foraminifera and Radiolaria. Cavalier-Smith introduced the expanded group in 2002, and molecular phylogenetic studies have supported Rhizaria as a clade. Symbiotic forms contribute significantly to the total primary production of the ocean.

Smallest U.S. state by area, in the [[New England|New England]] region of the Northeastern United States. It borders [[Connecticut|Connecticut]] and [[Massachusetts|Massachusetts]]. Its population of slightly more than 1.11 million as of 2025 is the seventh-least of any state. Providence is the capital and largest city. Roger Williams founded it in 1636 as a haven for religious liberty, the first North American settlement with an explicitly secular government. The colony was the first to call for a Continental Congress, in 1774, and it was the last of the original 13 states to ratify the Constitution, on May 29, 1790.

Largest of Greece's Dodecanese islands and their historical capital. It is the ninth largest island in the Mediterranean Sea, located northeast of [[Crete|Crete]] and southeast of [[Athens|Athens]]. The island had 125,113 inhabitants by 2022. In antiquity the island was famous for the [[Colossus of Rhodes|Colossus of Rhodes]], one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World. The Knights of Saint John of Jerusalem ruled it from 1310 to 1522, and it is nicknamed the Island of the Knights. The medieval Old Town of the city is a [[World Heritage Site|World Heritage Site]]. Tourism routinely overwhelms the island's small population and limited infrastructure.

Unrecognized state in southern Africa, 1965–1979. It was the de facto successor to the self-governing colony of Southern Rhodesia after its unilateral declaration of independence from the [[United Kingdom|United Kingdom]] on November 11, 1965. The white-minority government conceded majority rule in 1978, but the [[Rhodesian Bush War|war]] continued. Under the Lancaster House Agreement of December 1979, elections were held and the territory became independent as Zimbabwe on April 18, 1980, under [[Robert Mugabe|Robert Mugabe]]. Critics note that Rhodesia's gains were shared unequally, as the black majority was excluded from political power. Supporters of the former government contrast its economy with later decline in Zimbabwe.
