[[Species]] of [[Fly|fly]] (*Musca domestica*) in the family Muscidae, spread worldwide as a commensal of humans. The species possibly originated in the Middle East. Their short life cycles have made them useful in laboratory research on aging and sex determination.

Amphibious craft, also called an air-cushion vehicle (ACV), that travels over land, water, mud, ice and other surfaces. Blowers force a large volume of air below the hull, creating a cushion slightly above [[Atmospheric pressure|atmospheric pressure]] that lifts the craft. Air is typically blown through slots or holes around a disk- or oval-shaped platform, giving most hovercraft a rounded-rectangle shape. Unlike ground-effect vehicles and hydrofoils, hovercraft can lift while stationary. Very large versions carried hundreds of people and vehicles across the [[English Channel]], while military models transport tanks and soldiers. ENTRY:
French Protestants of the Reformed (Calvinist) tradition. The term was in use by about 1550 and may derive from Besançon Hugues, a burgomaster who defended [[Geneva]] from Catholic Savoy. Huguenots made up as much as 10% of the French population on the eve of the St. Bartholomew's Day massacre in 1572. Catholic hostility grew, and the [[French Wars of Religion]] ended with the Edict of Nantes in 1598, which granted substantial religious and military autonomy. [[Louis XIV]] revoked Protestant rights in the Edict of Fontainebleau in 1685, and Dragonnades forced conversion or flight. Persecution officially ended with the Edict of Versailles in 1787, and the revolutionary [[Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen]] of 1789 gave Protestants equal rights. In 2013 the Reformed Church of France merged with the Lutheran church to form the United Protestant Church of France.
