Group of common endocrine diseases characterized by sustained high blood sugar levels (diabetes mellitus). It results from reduced production of the hormone [[Insulin|insulin]] by the [[Pancreas|pancreas]] or from unresponsiveness of bodily cells to its effects, and tends to progress in severity. Classic symptoms are excessive thirst, urination, and hunger, with weight loss and blurred vision. Untreated, it can damage the cardiovascular system, eyes, kidneys, and nerves.

The major types are type 1 and type 2. Type 1 is an autoimmune condition in which the immune system attacks the beta cells of the pancreas, preventing insulin production; it typically begins early in life and is treated with insulin replacement. Type 2, the most common type, occurs when the body becomes resistant to insulin, so [[Glucose|glucose]] remains in the bloodstream; it is managed with medications such as [[Metformin|metformin]], semaglutide, or tirzepatide and with lifestyle changes, and is strongly linked to [[Obesity|obesity]], physical inactivity, and dietary excess. Gestational diabetes sometimes arises during pregnancy and normally resolves shortly after delivery.

[[Hypoglycemia]] is a recognized complication of insulin treatment, with sweating, trembling, confusion, seizures, and rarely death.

Long-term complications arise from damage to blood vessels. Diabetes doubles the risk of [[Cardiovascular disease|cardiovascular disease]], and about 75% of deaths in people with diabetes are due to coronary artery disease. Diabetic retinopathy is the most common cause of blindness in people of working age. Diabetic nephropathy accounts for over 50% of patients on dialysis in the United States, and diabetic neuropathy can cause sensory loss, pain, and foot ulceration, the most common cause of non-traumatic lower-limb [[Amputation|amputation]].

Written or audiovisual record of events, with discrete entries arranged by date. Diaries were traditionally handwritten but are now often digital. A personal diary may record a writer's experiences, thoughts and feelings, and someone who keeps one is a diarist. Form of government in which a leader or small group holds absolute or near-absolute political power. A [[Dictator|dictator]] rules through an inner circle of elites, including advisers, generals, and other high officials, whom the dictator appeases while repressing opposition such as rival parties, armed resistance, or disloyal insiders. Dictatorships may arise from a military coup or from a self-coup in which elected leaders make their rule permanent. They are authoritarian or totalitarian and are classified as military, one-party, or personalist.

The word *dictator* originated in the early [[Roman Republic]] for a constitutional office granting temporary absolute power to handle an emergency. Modern dictatorships first developed in the 19th century, including Bonapartism in Europe and caudillos in [[Latin America]]. In the 20th century fascist and communist dictatorships rose in Europe; [[Fascism|fascism]] was largely eradicated after World War II in 1945, while communism spread to other continents and remained prominent until the end of the [[Cold War]] in 1991. Personalist dictatorships in Africa and military dictatorships in Latin America became prominent in the 1960s and 1970s.

Dictatorships suppress freedom of thought and speech and use [[Propaganda|propaganda]] against alternative systems. They often present a democratic facade, holding elections that are not competitive. Stability rests on coercion and political repression: restricting information, tracking the opposition, and committing acts of violence. Those that fail to repress opposition are vulnerable to coups or revolutions, whereas those that came to power through revolutions tend to be exceptionally durable.

Some dictatorships have sustained economic development through decisive policy, but evidence on economic outcomes compared with democracies is mixed.

Electrical insulator that can be polarized by an applied electric field. Unlike a conductor, a dielectric has no loosely bound, free electrons to carry current. In an electric field its charges shift slightly from their equilibrium positions, producing an internal field that reduces the overall field. Dielectric properties govern the storage and dissipation of electric and magnetic energy, and they are important in electronics, optics and solid-state physics. A dielectric is the insulating material between the plates of a [[Capacitor|capacitor]], where polarization increases the capacitor's surface charge. The term was coined by William Whewell at the request of [[Michael Faraday]].

ENTRY:
Internal combustion engine that ignites its fuel through the heat of compression. Diesel engines compress only air, or air mixed with recirculated exhaust gas, and injected fuel ignites in the hot air. Power is controlled by the amount of fuel injected rather than by throttling the air, so the air-fuel ratio is usually high. Diesel engines have the highest thermal efficiency of any practical internal or external combustion engine, and low-speed marine engines can reach effective efficiencies of up to 55 percent. Named after German engineer [[Rudolf Diesel]], the engine was first used as a more efficient replacement for [[Steam engine|steam engines]]. Since the 1910s it has powered [[Submarine|submarines]] and ships, and it is used in [[Locomotive|locomotives]], [[Bus|buses]] and [[Truck|trucks]]. Controlling its air pollution is harder than for gasoline engines, so in the United States its use is now largely limited to larger vehicles.

ENTRY:
Liquid fuel specifically designed for [[Diesel engine|diesel engines]]. The most common type is a fractional distillate of [[Petroleum|petroleum]], although biodiesel and other alternatives are increasingly developed and adopted. Diesel is a high-volume product of oil refineries. As of 2016, almost all petroleum-based diesel in the United Kingdom, mainland Europe and North America was ultra-low-sulfur diesel (ULSD). Diesel fuel originated in experiments by [[Rudolf Diesel]] for his compression-ignition engine, invented around 1892. It is often used in heavy trucks, but diesel exhaust, especially from older engines, can damage health.

ENTRY:
Sum of food consumed by a person or other organism. The word often implies a specific intake of nutrition for health or weight-management reasons. Complete nutrition requires vitamins, minerals, essential amino acids and fatty acids, along with energy from carbohydrate, [[Protein (nutrient)|protein]] and fat. A healthy diet can improve and maintain mental and physical health, and specific diets such as the DASH diet are used to manage chronic conditions. Exclusionary diets avoid certain foods; vegetarian and [[Veganism|vegan]] diets can provide adequate nutrition if they focus on protein, iron, calcium, zinc and [[Vitamin B12|vitamin B12]]. Changing dietary intake can promote weight loss or gain. Obesity rates have increased by almost 10% over the past 10 years.

Manufactured product intended to supplement a person's diet, in the form of a pill, capsule, tablet, powder or liquid. Supplements may provide vitamins, minerals, fiber, fatty acids or [[Amino acid|amino acids]], and multivitamins are the most commonly used type. Some contain substances not confirmed as essential, such as plant [[Pigment|pigments]] or polyphenols. In the United States the supplement industry was valued at $151.9 billion in 2021, and about 60% of American adults use dietary supplements. Under the Dietary Supplement Health and Education Act of 1994, supplements are regulated as foods rather than drugs. Manufacturers may not claim that a product prevents or treats disease, but may use "structure/function" wording with a disclaimer. Systematic reviews have found limited high-quality evidence of broad preventive benefits, and critics argue that regulation is inconsistent.
