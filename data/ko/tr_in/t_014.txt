Colonial rebellion and political movement (1765–1789) in the [[Thirteen Colonies]] of Great Britain, ending in the creation of the United States.

Discontent with British rule began shortly after the end of the [[French and Indian War]] in 1763, when Parliament imposed new taxes, ostensibly to compensate for wartime costs. In 1765 the Stamp Act Congress in New York City issued the Declaration of Rights and Grievances on the principle of no taxation without representation. The Stamp Act was repealed in 1767, but the Townshend Acts renewed tensions, and the deployment of British troops to Massachusetts led to the Boston Massacre in 1770. In December 1773 the Sons of Liberty staged the [[Boston Tea Party]]; Parliament's punitive Intolerable Acts increased support for revolution.

Twelve colonies sent delegates to the First Continental Congress in 1774. Fighting began with the [[Battles of Lexington and Concord]]. The Second Continental Congress created the Continental Army and appointed [[George Washington]] commander-in-chief in 1775. On July 4, 1776, Congress unanimously adopted the Declaration of Independence. On September 28, 1781, combined French and Continental forces under Washington captured a British army at the [[Siege of Yorktown]]. On September 3, 1783, the [[Treaty of Paris (1783)|Treaty of Paris]] recognized American independence and ceded nearly all territory east of the Mississippi River and south of the Great Lakes.

The Articles of Confederation preceded the [[Constitution of the United States]] took effect in 1789. The new republic had significant democratic limitations; it preserved slavery and displaced Native American populations through expanded settlement. Roughly one-third of colonists were Loyalists, who faced property confiscation and exile.

Bison species endemic to [[North America]], also called the American buffalo. The species, *Bison bison*, is one of two living bison species, along with the European bison. The historical range, the great bison belt, spanned from Alaska to the Gulf of Mexico. An estimated population of 60 million in the late 18th century fell to 541 animals by 1889 through commercial hunting and bovine diseases from [[Cattle|domestic cattle]]. Reintroductions have restored free-roaming herds in the United States, Canada and Mexico. Indigenous peoples of the [[Great Plains]] relied on the bison for food, hides and tools, and the species is the national mammal of the United States.

Inorganic chemical compound of nitrogen and hydrogen, with the formula NH₃. A colorless gas with a distinctive pungent smell, it is widely used in [[Fertilizer|fertilizers]], refrigerants, explosives and cleaning agents, and as a precursor for numerous chemicals. About 70% of industrially produced ammonia is used to make fertilizers such as [[Urea|urea]] and diammonium phosphate. Ammonia is produced biologically by nitrogen fixation, but most is made by the [[Haber process]], which provided cheap fertilizers and helped revolutionize agriculture. Conventional production accounts for about 500 million tons a year of CO₂ emissions. Global industrial production was 235 million metric tons in 2021. Ammonia is toxic, and most animals excrete it as dissolved urea. It boils at −33.34 °C at one atmosphere. Household ammonia is a solution of ammonia in water.

Unicellular organism or cell type that changes shape, mainly by extending and retracting pseudopods. Amoebae do not form a single taxonomic group; they occur in every major lineage of eukaryotic organisms, and amoeboid cells also occur in fungi, algae and animals. Amoebae have no cell walls and feed by phagocytosis, engulfing food particles with pseudopods. Free-living forms may be naked or testate, enclosed in a shell. Most freshwater species have a contractile [[Vacuole|vacuole]] that expels excess water. The best-known species, *Amoeba proteus* and *Chaos carolinense*, are widely studied in laboratories. *Naegleria fowleri* is the so-called 'brain-eating amoeba', and *Entamoeba histolytica* is an intestinal parasite that causes amoebic dysentery. The multicellular 'social amoeba' *Dictyostelium discoideum* is a [[Slime mold|slime mold]].

Major taxonomic group of about 2,400 described species of amoeboid [[Protist|protists]]. Its members often have blunt, fingerlike, lobose pseudopods and tubular mitochondrial cristae. Traditionally ranked as a phylum within the kingdom Protista or Protozoa, it is retained by the International Society of Protistologists as an unranked 'supergroup' within [[Eukaryote|Eukaryota]].
