City (pop., 2026: 249,385), capital of [[Veneto|Veneto]] in northeastern Italy. Built on 126 islands in the shallow Venetian Lagoon and linked by 472 bridges, it was the capital of the [[Republic of Venice|Republic of Venice]] from 810 to 1797. A major financial and maritime power in the Middle Ages and Renaissance, it was a staging area for the [[Crusades|Crusades]] and the Battle of Lepanto, and a center of commerce in silk, grain and spice. The lagoon and city were inscribed as a [[UNESCO|UNESCO]] World Heritage Site in 1987, but overtourism, pollution, tide peaks and cruise ships threaten the city, and its listing has been under continuing review.

Diagram showing all possible logical relations between a collection of sets. In a Venn diagram, sets are shown as regions inside overlapping closed curves, usually [[Circle|circles]], and each point inside a curve represents an element of that set. The area where two curves overlap shows the elements common to both sets, their [[Intersection (set theory)|intersection]], and the combined area is their [[Union (set theory)|union]]. Earlier proposals include Christian Weise's of 1712 and Leonhard Euler's of 1768, but John Venn introduced the form in 1880 and popularized it in *Symbolic Logic* (1881). Because every possible overlap is drawn, Venn diagrams are a special case of Euler diagrams, which need not show all relations. They are used to teach [[Set theory|elementary set theory]] and to illustrate relationships in [[Probability|probability]], [[Logic|logic]], [[Statistics|statistics]] and [[Computer science|computer science]].

ENTRY:
Toxin produced by an animal and actively delivered to another animal through a [[Wound|wound]], usually by a bite, sting or other penetrative action. Venoms are injected through specialized structures such as fangs or stingers, a process called envenomation, and differ from [[Poison|poisons]], which are passively ingested, inhaled or absorbed through the skin. Venom has evolved independently at least 104 times across eight phyla, among predators and prey and in both [[Vertebrate|vertebrates]] and [[Invertebrate|invertebrates]], on land and in water. Venoms kill through at least four major classes of toxin: necrotoxins and cytotoxins, which kill cells; neurotoxins, which affect nervous systems; myotoxins, which damage muscles; and hemotoxins, which disrupt blood clotting. Venomous animals cause tens of thousands of human deaths each year. Toxins from venom are used to treat conditions including thrombosis, [[Arthritis|arthritis]] and some [[Cancer|cancers]].

Second planet from the [[Sun]], similar in size and mass to Earth. Its diameter is 12,103.6 km and its mass is 81.5% of Earth's. Venus has no liquid water, and its atmosphere is far thicker and denser than that of any other rocky body in the Solar System. It is 96.5% [[Carbon dioxide|carbon dioxide]], with most of the remaining 3.5% nitrogen, and a cloud layer of [[Sulfuric acid|sulfuric acid]] spans the whole planet. The resulting intense [[Greenhouse effect|greenhouse effect]] gives a mean surface temperature of 737 K (464 °C; 867 °F), the hottest of any planet in the Solar System, and a surface pressure 92 times that at Earth's sea level, which turns the lowest layer of the atmosphere into a supercritical fluid.

From Earth, Venus is visible as a star-like point of light brighter than any other natural point of light in the sky, as the "morning star" or "evening star". It has no moons and rotates very slowly in a retrograde direction; a Venusian day lasts 116.75 Earth days, and its year 224.7. Venus has a weak magnetosphere induced by the [[Solar wind|solar wind]] interacting with the atmosphere. Internal heat escapes through active [[Volcano|volcanism]] and periodic resurfacing; about 80% of the surface is covered by smooth volcanic plains. Potential biomarkers found in 2020 at cloud-layer altitudes, where conditions are the most Earth-like in the Solar System, spurred new research.

By 1613, the phases of Venus seen through telescopes were presented as decisive evidence for the heliocentric model. Venera 1 flew past the planet in 1961, achieving the first interplanetary spaceflight, and Mariner 2 returned the first data in 1962. Mariner 10 captured the first images during its 1974 [[Gravity assist|gravity assist]] en route to [[Mercury (planet)|Mercury]], and Magellan mapped the surface in 1990–91.

Port city and municipal seat on the [[Gulf of Mexico|Gulf of Mexico]] (pop., 2020: 537,952), in Veracruz state, [[Mexico|Mexico]]. In 1523 it became the first Spanish settlement on the American mainland to receive a coat of arms. Animal with a vertebral column and a cranium; also called a craniate. The vertebral column surrounds and protects the [[Spinal cord|spinal cord]], and the cranium protects the [[Brain|brain]]. Vertebrates form the subphylum Vertebrata, by far the largest ranked grouping in the phylum [[Chordate|Chordata]], yet they make up less than five percent of all described animal species. The two major living subgroups are the jawless Cyclostomi, which include [[Hagfish|hagfish]] and [[Lamprey|lampreys]], and the jawed Gnathostomata. Bony fish include the lobe-finned fish, which gave rise to the [[Tetrapod|tetrapods]], the four-limbed animals.

Vertebrates are distinguished from other animals, including other chordates, by a vertebral column and a skull made of cartilage or bone; a brain divided into 3 or more sections; a muscular heart with 2 or more chambers; an inner ear with semicircular canals; kidneys with glomeruli; a thyroid; and a liver. Most vertebrates are aquatic and breathe with gills; in jawed vertebrates, the first pair of gill arches evolved into the jaws. Tetrapods lost the gills and adapted the swim bladder into lungs. Size ranges from the frog *Brachycephalus pulex*, with a minimum adult snout–vent length of 6.45 millimeters, to the [[Blue whale|blue whale]], at up to 33 m and some 150 tonnes.

The first vertebrates appeared in the [[Cambrian explosion]] some 518 million years ago and were jawless. Jawed vertebrates evolved in the Ordovician or Silurian. The first tetrapods appeared towards the end of the Devonian, and the first land amphibians in the Carboniferous. Mammals and dinosaurs appeared in the Triassic, and birds arose from dinosaurs in the Jurassic. Populations of many species have declined steeply since 1970 because of land-use change, overexploitation of natural resources, climate change, pollution, and invasive species.

Period of the [[Middle Ages]], about 800–1050, of large-scale raiding, colonizing, conquest, and trading by Norsemen, known as [[Vikings]]. Traditional accounts place the beginning at the attack of June 8, 793, that destroyed the abbey on Lindisfarne, off Northumberland, and the end at the defeat of the Norwegian king Harald Hardrada at the [[Battle of Stamford Bridge]] in 1066.

Voyaging from Denmark, Norway, and Sweden, the Norse settled in the British Isles, Ireland, the Faroe Islands, [[Iceland]], Greenland, [[Normandy]], and along the Dnieper and Volga trade routes. In the 11th century the Norse homelands were unified into larger kingdoms, and the short-lived North Sea Empire of [[Cnut]] included large parts of Scandinavia and Britain.

Proposed causes of expansion include wealthy towns and monasteries overseas, weak kingdoms, overpopulation, lack of good farmland, political strife arising from the unification of Norway, and innovations in sailing.

Knowledge comes largely from texts written by those the Vikings encountered, from archaeology, and from the Icelandic [[Saga|sagas]]. Regarded as barbaric by the late 18th century, the age was reassessed by scholars outside Scandinavia from the 1890s, who recognized the Vikings' artistry, technological skills, and seamanship.
