Naval battle of the First World War, May 31–June 1, 1916, off the [[North Sea]] coast of Denmark's Jutland Peninsula. Britain's [[Royal Navy]] Grand Fleet under Admiral Sir John Jellicoe fought Germany's High Seas Fleet under Vice-Admiral Reinhard Scheer. It was the largest naval battle and the only full-scale clash of battleships in the war. Fourteen British and eleven German ships sank, with 9,823 casualties. Both sides claimed victory. Greek victory over a Persian invasion force in 490 BC, on the plain of Marathon in Attica. Naval battle of the [[Pacific War]], June 4–7, 1942, near [[Midway Atoll]] in the central Pacific. Northeastern part of the [[Indian Ocean]], between the [[Indian subcontinent]] and the Indochinese peninsula. It covers 2,600,000 sq km (1,000,000 sq mi), making it the world's largest bay. It is bounded by India to the west and northwest, Bangladesh to the north, Myanmar to the northeast, and the Andaman and Nicobar Islands of India to the east. Major rivers, including the [[Ganges]], Brahmaputra, Irrawaddy and Godavari, flow into the bay, which receives more than one billion tons of sediment annually. Its coast includes the [[Sundarbans]], the largest mangrove forest in the world. The bay generates about five to six [[Tropical cyclone|tropical cyclones]] a year, and its fisheries yield around six million tons of catch annually.

Gulf of the northeast [[Atlantic Ocean]] located south of the Celtic Sea. It lies along the northern coast of Spain and the western coast of France, where it is known as the Gulf of Gascony. ENTRY:
Mathematical rule for inverting conditional probabilities, allowing the probability of a cause to be found from its effect. For example, it gives the probability that a patient has a disease given a positive test, from the probability of a positive test when the disease is present. In symbols, P(A∣B) = P(B∣A)P(A)/P(B). Named after Thomas Bayes, whose essay was published posthumously in 1763, the theorem was developed in the 18th century by Bayes and independently by [[Pierre-Simon Laplace]]. It underlies [[Bayesian inference]], which uses it to update the probability of a hypothesis as new evidence arrives. Harold Jeffreys wrote in 1973 that it "is to the theory of probability what the [[Pythagorean theorem]] is to geometry."

ENTRY:
Method of statistical inference that uses [[Bayes' theorem]] to update the probability of a hypothesis as more information becomes available. It combines a prior distribution with the likelihood function of a statistical model for observed data to produce a posterior probability. Bayesian updating is particularly important in the analysis of a sequence of data. The method has been applied in science, engineering, philosophy, [[Medicine|medicine]], sport, [[Psychology|psychology]] and [[Law|law]]. In decision theory it is closely related to subjective probability. Unlike [[Confidence interval|confidence intervals]] in frequentist methods, Bayesian approaches yield credible intervals that express the probability of parameter values.

ENTRY:
Theory of statistics based on treating probability as a degree of belief. It codifies prior knowledge as a [[Probability distribution|prior distribution]] and uses [[Bayes' theorem]] to update probabilities after new data are obtained. This contrasts with the frequentist view of probability as the limit of a relative frequency after many trials. Many statisticians viewed Bayesian methods unfavorably for much of the 20th century, but computing advances such as Markov chain Monte Carlo methods gave them prominence in the 21st century.

Embroidered linen cloth nearly 70 meters (230 feet) long, depicting the events leading to the [[Norman Conquest]] of England. Despite its name, it is an embroidery rather than a true [[Tapestry|tapestry]], with images stitched in wool thread on a coarse [[Linen|linen]] ground. Its 58 scenes, with Latin inscriptions, run from 1064 to the [[Battle of Hastings]] in 1066, when [[William the Conqueror|William, Duke of Normandy]] defeated Harold Godwinson. It is generally thought to have been commissioned by Bishop Odo of Bayeux and made in England in the years after the Conquest. Although it presents the Conquest favorably to William, some historians read it as propaganda, and it remains a major source on 11th-century life. It has been inscribed on [[UNESCO]]'s Memory of the World Register since 2007. It is on loan to the [[British Museum]] in London from September 2026, its first time outside France, and is due to return by July 2027.
