American cytogeneticist and Nobel laureate whose research on [[Maize|maize]] chromosomes led to the discovery of transposons. She received her PhD in botany from [[Cornell University]] in 1927 and developed techniques for visualizing maize chromosomes. Optical, machine-readable representation of data.

Linear barcodes encode data in parallel lines whose widths, spacings and sizes vary, and are read by optical scanners. Later two-dimensional (2D) matrix codes use rectangles, dots or hexagons, and can be read by a digital camera running software, such as on a [[Smartphone|smartphone]]. [[QR code|QR codes]], a 2D type, rose in popularity in the second decade of the 2000s with growing smartphone ownership.

Bark refers to all tissues outside the [[Vascular cambium|vascular cambium]] and consists of inner bark and outer bark. His influence peaked during the reign of King Bijjala II in Karnataka, where as chief minister he used the state treasury to fund social reforms. He spread social awareness through vachanas, poems in [[Kannada]], and rejected gender and social discrimination, superstitions and rituals. He introduced the ishtalinga necklace, to be worn by every person regardless of birth as a reminder of devotion to [[Shiva]]. He also launched the Anubhava Mantapa, a public assembly that drew men and women from many walks of life to discuss spiritual and social issues. Traditional legends credit him as the founder of the Lingayats, but modern scholarship holds that he revived, refined and energized an existing tradition. Scholars note that poems and legends about him were written down long after his death, which raises questions about their accuracy.

Chemical substance that reacts with acids. Three definitions are in common use, all agreeing that bases react with [[Acid|acids]].

In 1884 [[Svante Arrhenius]] proposed that a base dissociates in aqueous solution to form hydroxide ions (OH⁻), which combine with hydrogen ions from acids to form water. A base was therefore a metal hydroxide such as NaOH or Ca(OH)₂. In the more general Brønsted–Lowry theory (1923), a base is a substance that accepts hydrogen cations (H⁺), that is, [[Proton|protons]]. This includes aqueous hydroxides and other bases such as [[Ammonia]] (NH₃) and its organic derivatives, the [[Amine|amines]]. In Lewis theory, a base is an electron-pair donor; this is more general still, since the Lewis acid need not be a proton and can be a molecule such as boron trifluoride (BF₃).

In water, bases yield solutions with a pH above 7.0 at standard conditions. A soluble base that releases OH⁻ ions quantitatively is called an alkali. Basic solutions feel slippery, taste bitter, turn red litmus paper blue, and conduct electricity. Concentrated or strong bases are caustic to organic matter and react violently with acids. Strong bases such as [[Sodium hydroxide]] ionize completely in water, while weak bases ionize only partially. A reaction between aqueous solutions of an acid and a base is called neutralization and produces water and a [[Salt (chemistry)|salt]].

The term base was first used in 1717 by Louis Lémery as a synonym for the Paracelsian term matrix. Its modern meaning is usually attributed to G.-F. Rouelle, who in 1754 defined a neutral salt as the product of an acid with any substance capable of serving as a base for it.

ENTRY:
Bat-and-ball game between two teams of nine players each, who take turns batting and fielding. Batters advance around four bases to score runs, and the team with the most runs after a defined number of innings wins. Baseball evolved from older bat-and-ball games popular in [[England]] in the mid-18th century and developed into organized clubs in [[North America]] by the mid-19th century. It has been unofficially recognized as the national sport of the [[United States]] since the late 19th century, although it is less popular there than some other sports. Playoffs in [[Major League Baseball|Major League Baseball (MLB)]] culminate in the [[World Series]]. Baseball was played at the Olympic Games from 1992 to 2008 and in 2020, and will return in 2028.

City in northwestern [[Switzerland]] on the [[Rhine]] River. Basel has 177,595 inhabitants within its municipal limits and is Switzerland's third most populous city. It is commonly considered the cultural capital of Switzerland, and its Kunstmuseum (1661) was the first collection of art accessible to the public in the world. The University of Basel, founded in 1460, is the country's oldest university. The city is home to Art Basel and to the headquarters of the Bank for International Settlements.
