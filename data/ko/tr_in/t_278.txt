Divide-and-conquer [[Sorting algorithm|sorting algorithm]] developed by British computer scientist Tony Hoare in 1959 and published in 1961. It selects a pivot element, partitions the other elements into sub-arrays of lesser and greater values, and sorts those recursively. On average, it takes O(n log n) comparisons to sort n items, but O(n²) in the worst case. Polymeric molecule, ribonucleic acid, essential for most biological functions, either by acting itself (non-coding RNA) or by forming a template for protein production (messenger RNA). RNA and [[DNA]] are [[Nucleic acid|nucleic acids]], one of the four major macromolecule classes essential to all known life. RNA is a chain of nucleotides, each with a ribose sugar, a phosphate group, and a base: adenine, cytosine, guanine, or uracil. Adenine pairs with uracil and cytosine with guanine through hydrogen bonds. Unlike DNA, it is typically single-stranded and forms short helices packed into structures akin to those of proteins. Viral disease that causes inflammation of the brain in humans and other [[Mammal|mammals]]. It is caused by lyssaviruses, including the rabies virus, and is usually spread when an infected animal bites or scratches another animal or a person; saliva that contacts the eyes, mouth or nose can also transmit it. Symptoms usually appear one to three months after infection, beginning with fever and abnormal sensations at the site of exposure, and then progress to violent movements, fear of water and confusion. The rabies vaccine, sometimes with rabies immunoglobulin, prevents the disease if given before symptoms begin, and vaccination programs have reduced the risk from dogs in many regions.

Bengali polymath, poet, playwright, composer, philosopher, social reformer, and painter, one of the foremost figures of the Bengal Renaissance. Youngest of 13 surviving children in a Calcutta family at the forefront of that renaissance, he wrote poetry at age eight. At sixteen he released his first substantial poems under the pseudonym Bhānusiṃha ("Sun Lion"), which literary authorities seized upon as long-lost classics; by 1877 he was publishing short stories and dramas under his own name. He largely avoided classroom schooling, and his studies at Presidency College lasted a single day.

His canon comprises paintings, sketches, hundreds of texts, and some 2,000 songs known as Rabindra Sangeet. Spurning rigid classical forms, he introduced new prose and verse forms and colloquial language into Bengali literature. His best-known works are [[Gitanjali|*Gitanjali*]] (*Song Offerings*), *Gora* (*Fair-Faced*), and *Ghare-Baire* (*The Home and the World*). In 1913 he became the first Indian and first non-European to win a Nobel Prize in any category, and the first lyricist to win the Nobel Prize in Literature. His compositions became the national anthems of [[India]] ("Jana Gana Mana") and [[Bangladesh]] ("Amar Sonar Bangla"); his song "Banglar Mati Banglar Jol" is the state anthem of [[West Bengal]], and the Sri Lankan anthem was inspired by his work.

A humanist, universalist, and internationalist, he was an ardent critic of [[Nationalism|nationalism]]. He denounced the [[British Raj]] and advocated independence, contributing songs and essays during the Swadeshi movement, and renounced his knighthood after the 1919 [[Jallianwala Bagh massacre]]. He founded Santiniketan in 1901 as an open-air school; it became Visva-Bharati University in 1921. Called "the Bard of Bengal," he was also known as Gurudeb, Kobiguru, and Bishwokobi.
