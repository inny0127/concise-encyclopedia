Largest and deepest artesian basin in the world, underlying 22% of the Australian continent. It covers about 1,700,000 square kilometers and is estimated to hold 64,900 cubic kilometers of [[Groundwater|groundwater]]. Most recharge enters along the eastern edge, in [[Queensland]] and [[New South Wales]], and flows slowly toward the south and west through sandstone laid down during the Triassic, Jurassic and early Cretaceous periods. Before European settlement, its water discharged through mound springs. Boreholes now tap the pressurized water for stock, [[Irrigation|irrigation]] and domestic use, often without pumps. Because recharge is much less than extraction, water use from the basin is effectively a mining operation. The Great Artesian Basin Coordinating Committee coordinates activity among federal, state, territory and local governments.

Region of enhanced gravitational attraction in the local universe, approximately 150–250 million [[Light-year|light-years]] from the [[Milky Way]]. Its presence was inferred from peculiar motions of galaxies that deviate from the motion expected solely from the expansion of the universe. It is not a single galaxy or cluster but a broad concentration of matter. Early models attributed to it a mass of about 10¹⁶ [[Solar mass|solar masses]]. It was incorporated into the definition of the [[Laniakea Supercluster]] in 2014.

Large oceanic bight (open gulf) off the southern coast of mainland [[Australia]]. Famine that struck [[Bengal]] and [[Bihar]] between 1769 and 1770, affecting some 30 million people, about one third of the area's population. It occurred under dual governance: the [[East India Company]] held the right to collect revenue, while civil administration remained with the Mughal governor, the Nawab of Bengal. The famine hastened the end of dual governance, with the Company becoming sole administrator soon after.

ENTRY:
Severe global economic downturn lasting from 1929 to 1939. It was marked by high unemployment and poverty, drastic reductions in industrial production and international trade, and widespread bank and business failures. It began in the United States with the [[Wall Street crash of 1929]], often considered its beginning. The U.S., the U.K., and Germany were among the countries with the most unemployed.

The Depression was preceded by the "Roaring Twenties," a boom in which much profit went into speculation and banks faced minimal regulation. After the October 1929 crash, share values slid until July 1932. By 1933 U.S. unemployment had risen to 25%, about one-third of farmers had lost their land, and 9,000 of its 25,000 banks had failed. President [[Herbert Hoover]] was unwilling to intervene heavily in the economy, and in 1930 he signed the Smoot–Hawley Tariff Act, which inadvertently worsened the Depression. In 1932 he was defeated by [[Franklin D. Roosevelt]], who from 1933 pursued expansive [[New Deal]] programs of relief and job creation. A severe drought also struck the agricultural heartland, as in the [[Dust Bowl]].

Between 1929 and 1932, worldwide gross domestic product fell by an estimated 15%, and U.S. GDP contracted by 30%. International trade fell by more than 50%, and unemployment in some countries rose as high as 33%. In Germany, which depended heavily on U.S. loans, unemployment rose to nearly 30% and political extremism grew, paving the way for [[Adolf Hitler]]'s Nazi Party to take power in 1933. The outbreak of [[World War II]] in 1939 ended the Depression by stimulating factory production.

The causes remain disputed. Some historians emphasize non-monetary economic causes, regarding the crash either as the main cause or as a symptom of broader trends. Others hold that failures of monetary policy, including the [[Federal Reserve]]'s inadequate response to liquidity crises and bank failures, explain the downturn's severity and length.

Period of mass starvation and disease in [[Ireland]] from 1845 to 1852. Its proximate cause was blight that infected [[Potato|potato]] crops across Europe, but longer-term factors included absentee landlordism and dependence on a single crop. The population fell by 20–25% between 1841 and 1871, and the famine became part of Irish folk memory.

They drain to the [[Atlantic Ocean]] through the Saint Lawrence River, and the Great Lakes Waterway enables shipping among them. Shipping, trade and fishing have long depended on the lakes, and major cities on their shores include [[Chicago]], [[Detroit]] and [[Toronto]].

Supreme life force in the traditional beliefs of many Indigenous cultures in [[Canada]] and the [[United States]]. Ruined medieval city in southeastern [[Zimbabwe]]. The city stands near Masvingo and served as the capital of the Kingdom of Great Zimbabwe from the 13th century. It was settled around 1000 AD, and major construction took place from the 11th to the 15th century before the city was abandoned in the 16th or 17th century. It is the largest stone structure in precolonial [[Southern Africa]], covering 7.22 sq km (2.79 sq mi). Large [[Shark|shark]] species, *Carcharodon carcharias*, of the mackerel shark family Lamnidae. It has a grayish upperside and a white underside. Adults commonly measure 4 to 5 m, and the largest reliably measured specimen was 6 m long. The species is an [[Apex predator|apex predator]] and is partially warm-blooded, an adaptation that allows it to remain active in colder waters. It has about 300 triangular, serrated teeth that are continuously replaced, and its fatty liver can exceed a quarter of its body weight, providing buoyancy.
