Philosophical thought, traditions, and works of the Western world, beginning with the ancient Greek philosophy of the pre-Socratics. Ancient Western philosophy covered today's philosophical problems and also pure mathematics and natural sciences such as physics, astronomy, and biology; [[Aristotle]] wrote on all of these. The pre-Socratics were interested in cosmology and sought the *arche*, the first principle of the world. [[Thales of Miletus]] identified water as the arche, and his use of observation and reason leads many to call him the first philosopher. [[Pythagoras]] and his followers held that "all is number." Semi-aquatic ecosystem whose ground is flooded or saturated with water, permanently or seasonally. Flooding makes the soil oxygen-poor, and wetland plants have roots adapted to these waterlogged conditions. Wetlands form a transitional zone between water bodies and dry land and exist on every continent except Antarctica, with freshwater, brackish or saltwater. [[Marsh]]es are dominated by herbaceous plants such as reeds and cattails, [[Swamp]]s by trees and shrubs, and [[Mangrove]] forests by salt-tolerant woody plants. The Millennium Ecosystem Assessment of 2005 found them more threatened than any other ecosystem on Earth. Since 1971, an international treaty has worked to protect "wetlands of international importance."
