Writing material made from the pith of the wetland sedge *Cyperus papyrus*. It was first manufactured in [[Ancient Egypt|Egypt]] in the 3rd millennium BCE, and Egyptians also used the reeds for boats, mats, rope, sandals, and baskets. Papyrus rolls excavated in 2012 and 2013 date from c. 2560–2550 BCE and describe the final years of building the [[Great Pyramid of Giza]]. ENTRY:
Mirror-symmetrical, approximately U-shaped plane curve and a [[Conic section|conic section]]. It is the set of points equidistant from a fixed point, the focus, and a fixed line, the directrix. A parabola is also the graph of a quadratic function, y = ax² + bx + c. Light traveling parallel to its axis of symmetry is reflected to the focus, a property used in parabolic antennas, microphones, telescopes, and headlight reflectors. [[Apollonius of Perga]] gave the curve its name, and [[Archimedes]] calculated the area of a parabolic segment in the 3rd century BC. When [[Isaac Newton]] built the first reflecting telescope in 1668, he used a spherical mirror because a parabolic one was difficult to make.

Swiss physician, alchemist, and philosopher of the German [[Renaissance]]. He was a pioneer of the medical revolution of the Renaissance, emphasizing observation alongside received wisdom, and is credited as the father of [[Toxicology|toxicology]]. He introduced mineral and chemical remedies, such as mercury for treating [[Syphilis]], and rejected the humoral theories of [[Galen]] and [[Avicenna]]. He wrote that "solely the dose determines that a thing is not a poison." In [[Alchemy|alchemy]], he proposed that all matter consists of three principles: sulfur, mercury, and salt. Briefly a professor at [[Basel]] in 1527–1528, he was ousted after conflicts with established physicians. His writings often blended medicine, alchemy, and theology.

Distinct set of concepts or thought patterns, including theories, research methods, postulates, and standards for legitimate contributions to a field. Apparent displacement of an object viewed from two different lines of sight. The displacement is measured by the angle between those sight lines, and nearby objects show a larger parallax than distant ones, so parallax can be used to find distances. In astronomy, stellar parallax, observed when Earth is on opposite sides of the [[Sun]] in its orbit, forms the lowest rung of the [[Cosmic distance ladder|cosmic distance ladder]]. German astronomer Friedrich Bessel measured the parallax of 61 Cygni as 0.314 arcseconds in 1838. Parallax also affects optical instruments such as [[Binoculars]] and [[Microscope|microscopes]]. Many animals, including humans, use it for depth perception through stereopsis, in which the brain combines the slightly different views from the two eyes. Parallax scrolling creates an illusion of depth in some 2D video games.

Computation in which many calculations or processes are carried out simultaneously. Large problems are divided into smaller ones that are solved at the same time, using bit-level, instruction-level, data or task parallelism. Parallelism has long been used in high-performance computing. Physical limits on processor frequency scaling, along with concern over power consumption and heat, have made parallelism the dominant paradigm in [[Computer architecture]], chiefly as multi-core processors. In [[Computer science]], parallelism differs from concurrency: parallel programs use several CPU cores at once, while concurrency lets a single core switch between tasks. Clusters and [[Supercomputer|supercomputers]] use multiple computers on one task, and [[Graphics processing unit|GPUs]] accelerate specialized work. Explicitly parallel programs are harder to write, because concurrency introduces bugs such as race conditions. Amdahl's law sets an upper bound on speed-up, limited by the fraction of a program that can be parallelized.

Property of an [[Integer|integer]] as even or odd. An integer is even if it is divisible by 2 and odd if it is not; for example, −4, 0 and 82 are even, while −7, 5 and 23 are odd. Parity applies only to integers, not to fractions or decimals such as 1⁄2 or 7.2742. Zero is even, and any two consecutive integers have opposite parity. In the decimal system an integer is odd if its last digit is 1, 3, 5, 7 or 9, and in the binary system it is odd if its last digit is 1. Parity is a special case of [[Modular arithmetic]]: modulo 2, the even and odd numbers form a field with two elements. When the quotient of two integers is itself an integer, it is even if and only if the dividend has more factors of two than the divisor.
