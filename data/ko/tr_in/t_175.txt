ENTRY:
City (pop., 2024: 1,050,153) on a plateau in the Judaean Mountains, in the Southern Levant between the Mediterranean and the Dead Sea. One of the oldest cities in the world, it is holy to the three [[Abrahamic religions]]: Judaism, Christianity, and Islam.

The City of David shows first signs of settlement in the 4th millennium BC, in the form of encampments of nomadic shepherds. Egyptian tablets of the 14th century BC name the city Urusalim, probably meaning "foundation of [the god] Shalem." Radiocarbon dating of 103 samples shows Iron Age settlement in the 12th–10th centuries BC and growth until the Babylonian destruction in 586 BC. According to the Hebrew Bible, King David made it the capital of the United Kingdom of Israel and his son Solomon commissioned the First Temple. Jerusalem has been destroyed at least twice, besieged 23 times, captured and recaptured 44 times, and attacked 52 times. The walls that define the Old City were rebuilt in 1538 under [[Suleiman the Magnificent]]. The Old City became a [[World Heritage Site]] in 1981.

Despite covering only 0.9 km², the Old City contains the [[Temple Mount]], with the Western Wall, Dome of the Rock, and Al-Aqsa Mosque, and the [[Church of the Holy Sepulchre]]. Christians associate the city with the crucifixion and resurrection of Jesus. In Islam it is the third-holiest city, after Mecca and Medina, and was the first direction of Muslim prayer.

Israel occupied East Jerusalem from Jordan in 1967 following the [[Six-Day War]] and annexed it. The 1980 Jerusalem Law calls the city Israel's undivided capital, and the Knesset and Supreme Court sit there. Neither Israel's nor Palestine's claim to the city as capital is widely recognized internationally, and its status remains a core issue in the [[Israeli–Palestinian conflict]].
