Small fruit-bearing [[Citrus|citrus]] tree, or its edible fruit, native to southern [[China|China]]. The fruit resembles an orange in color and texture but is about the size of a large olive. The English name comes from [[Cantonese|Cantonese]] words meaning "golden" and "mandarin orange." Unlike most citrus, kumquats are usually eaten whole, peel included, and the fruit is a rich source of [[Vitamin C|vitamin C]], with 44 mg per 100 g of raw fruit. Mountain range in western [[China|China]], one of the longest mountain chains in Asia, extending more than 3,000 km. The range runs east from the Pamir region of [[Tajikistan|Tajikistan]] through [[Xinjiang|Xinjiang]] to Qinghai province, forming the northern edge of the [[Tibetan Plateau|Tibetan Plateau]] and the southern edge of the Tarim Basin. In Chinese mythology, Kunlun was a semi-mythical mountain believed to be a [[Taoism|Taoist]] paradise.

Kurdish languages belong to the Western Iranic branch of the Iranic language family.
