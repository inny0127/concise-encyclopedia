ENTRY:
Acceleration of a [[Chemical reaction|chemical reaction]] by an added substance, the catalyst, that is not consumed and remains unchanged afterward. It works by opening an alternative mechanism with a lower activation energy, which may be much faster than the uncatalyzed route. Because the uncatalyzed route remains possible, a catalyst can only increase the total rate, never decrease it. A catalyst has no effect on the [[Chemical equilibrium|chemical equilibrium]] of a reaction: one that shifted an equilibrium would amount to a perpetual motion machine, contradicting the [[Second law of thermodynamics]].

Catalysis is classed as homogeneous, when the catalyst is in the same phase as the reactants (usually gaseous or liquid), or heterogeneous, when it is not, as with a solid surface acting on gases or liquids. [[Enzyme|Enzymes]] and other biocatalysts are often considered a third category. Manganese dioxide speeds the decomposition of [[Hydrogen peroxide|hydrogen peroxide]] into water and oxygen (2 H₂O₂ → 2 H₂O + O₂); in living organisms the enzyme catalase does the same work. [[Nitric oxide]] catalyzes the oxidation of sulfur dioxide (2 SO₂ + O₂ → 2 SO₃). Platinum in [[Catalytic converter|catalytic converters]] helps purify exhaust gas.

The SI derived unit of catalytic activity is the katal, measured in moles per second. Estimates hold that 90% of all commercially produced chemical products involve catalysts at some stage of manufacture. The term derives from the Greek *kataluein*, 'loosen' or 'untie'; Jöns Jacob Berzelius is credited with coining it in 1835.

In 252 it acknowledged the suzerainty of the [[Sasanian Empire]], and it remained part of that empire until its fall. Its kings were replaced by an Arsacid family by the end of the 3rd century, and by the Mihranids in the 5th century. Its population spoke Northeast Caucasian languages, unrelated to Indo-European Albanian. The kingdom adopted Christianity as state religion in the early 4th century.

Mathematical inequality bounding the inner product of two vectors. In an [[Inner product space|inner product space]], the absolute value of the inner product is at most the product of the vectors' [[Norm (mathematics)|norms]], with equality if and only if the vectors are linearly dependent. Theorem in [[Complex analysis|complex analysis]] giving integral formulas for all derivatives of a [[Holomorphic function|holomorphic function]]. It states that a holomorphic function defined on a disk is completely determined by its values on the disk's boundary. Physics of the cause–effect relation. Causality requires that the cause of an event lie in the past light cone of its result and be ultimately reducible to [[Fundamental interaction|fundamental interactions]]; a cause cannot have an effect outside its future light cone. The weak causality principle operates at the microscopic level and need not lead to information transfer, whereas the strong principle forbids information transfer faster than the [[Speed of light|speed of light]]. In [[Special relativity]], the cause must precede its effect for all inertial observers, since a faster-than-light signal would travel backward in time for some of them. [[Bell's theorem]] shows that local causality, tested in experiments on [[Quantum entanglement|quantum entanglement]], produces non-classical correlations predicted by [[Quantum mechanics]].

Natural void under a planetary surface. Caves form mainly by the weathering of rock and can extend deep underground; they have also been observed on other rocky worlds, such as Mars. Exogene caves are short openings such as rock shelters, while endogene caves extend farther underground than they are wide. The scientific study of caves is speleology. Most caves are solutional, or karst, caves formed when acidic [[Groundwater|groundwater]] dissolves soluble rock, most often [[Limestone|limestone]], along bedding planes and faults.
