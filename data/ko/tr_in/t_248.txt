Scientific work by the English naturalist [[Charles Darwin]], published November 24, 1859. Considered the foundation of evolutionary biology, it argued that populations evolve over generations through [[Natural selection|natural selection]] and that the diversity of life arose by [[Evolution|common descent]] through a branching pattern. Darwin drew on evidence collected on the Beagle expedition in the 1830s and on later research, correspondence and experiments. Written for non-specialist readers, it sparked widespread debate over its implications for the fixity of [[Species|species]] and for religion, at a time when the English scientific establishment was closely tied to the [[Church of England]]. Within two decades most scientists accepted that evolution had occurred, although natural selection was slow to gain prominence. After the modern evolutionary synthesis of the 1930s and 1940s, natural selection became the unifying concept of the life sciences.

Branch of medicine dealing with the study, diagnosis, treatment and prevention of [[Cancer|cancer]]. Practitioners are called oncologists, a term derived from the Greek *onkos*, meaning tumor, which the Greek physician Galen first used to describe tumors. Cancer was first documented in humans in Egypt around 3000 BC, when the Edwin Smith papyrus described a form of cancer as "a bulging tumor of the breast." Oncology covers diagnosis, therapy, monitoring after treatment, palliative care, screening and clinical research into cancer treatments. Oncologists often specialize by organ or modality, such as breast, brain or radiation oncology.

Population control policy in [[China]], in force from 1979 to 2015. A near-universal one-child limit was imposed in 1980 and written into the constitution in 1982. Exceptions included rural families whose first child was a daughter and some ethnic minorities; by 1984 only about 35.4% of the population was subject to the original restriction. Families who violated the limit faced large fines, and enforcement sometimes included forced [[Abortion|abortion]] and [[Sterilization (medicine)|sterilization]]. The limit was raised to two children in 2015 and to three in 2021, and all limits were removed in July 2021. The [[Chinese Communist Party]] credits the policy with preventing 400 million births, a figure some scholars dispute, while the policy has been widely criticized in the West for [[Human rights|human rights]] violations.

Branch of philosophy that studies being, traditionally understood as the subdiscipline of [[Metaphysics|metaphysics]] concerned with the most general features of reality. Ontology examines the characteristics shared by all things and their classification into basic types, such as particulars and universals. Particulars are unique, non-repeatable entities, such as [[Socrates]], whereas universals are general, repeatable entities, such as the color green. Concrete objects exist in space and time; abstract objects, such as the number 5, exist outside them. Systems of categories employ notions such as substance, property, relation, state of affairs, and event.

Ontologists disagree about which entities exist at the most basic level. Platonic realism holds that universals exist objectively, conceptualism that they exist only in the mind, and nominalism denies their existence. [[Materialism]] posits that fundamentally only matter exists, whereas dualism holds that mind and matter are independent principles. [[George Berkeley]] proposed that all existence is mental, in the slogan "to be is to be perceived." Methods include the analysis of concepts and experience, the use of intuitions and [[Thought experiment|thought experiments]], and the integration of findings from natural science.

Formal ontology investigates the most abstract features of objects, while applied ontology studies entities in specific domains; social ontology, for example, examines basic concepts of the social sciences. In information and computer science, an ontology is a formal, explicit specification of a shared conceptualization of a domain, a usage popularized in the 1990s, and it allows the structured storage of information in a [[Database|database]].

The relation between ontology and metaphysics is disputed, and some philosophers use the terms as synonyms. The ancient Greeks did not use the word, which was coined by philosophers in the 17th century.

Eisenhower]], it was a three-pronged attack on [[Casablanca]], [[Oran]], and [[Algiers]], followed by a move on [[Tunis]] in conjunction with a British advance from Egypt. Order of [[Arachnid|arachnids]] commonly called harvestmen or daddy longlegs, with over 6,650 species discovered worldwide. Many have exceptionally long legs, and the connection between the cephalothorax and abdomen is broad, so the body appears to be a single oval. ENTRY:
Judgment, viewpoint, or statement that is not conclusive, as opposed to [[Fact|facts]], which are verifiable true statements. Facts can be agreed on by a consensus of experts, whereas an opinion may concern subjective matters. An opinion supported by facts and principles becomes an [[Argument|argument]]. For example, that the United States was involved in the [[Vietnam War]] is a fact, while that it was right to get involved is an opinion. People can reach opposing conclusions from the same facts, and opinions rarely change without new arguments. Public opinion, the aggregate of individual attitudes in a population, is usually estimated through [[Sampling (statistics)|survey sampling]]. In [[Political science|political science]] and [[Psychology|psychology]], group opinion concerns the views of members of a [[Jury|jury]], a legislature, or another decision-making body, including conformity and group polarization. Scientific opinion may be partial, uncertain, or conflicting, and is distinct from scientific consensus.

ENTRY:
Survey of public opinion drawn from a sample, designed to represent a population by asking questions and extrapolating within [[Confidence interval|confidence intervals]]. The first known example was a tally of voter preferences in the 1824 presidential race, which showed [[Andrew Jackson]] leading [[John Quincy Adams]] by 335 votes to 169. In 1936 the Literary Digest's survey of 2.3 million voters suggested that Alf Landon would win, but [[Franklin D. Roosevelt]] was re-elected in a landslide. Smaller, scientifically based surveys by George Gallup, Archibald Crossley, and Elmo Roper correctly predicted the result. A Gallup subsidiary in the [[United Kingdom]] was almost alone in correctly predicting Labour's victory in the 1945 general election. By the 1950s, polling had spread to most democracies.
