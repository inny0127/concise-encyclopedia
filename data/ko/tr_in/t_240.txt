Period of darkness when the [[Sun]] is below the [[Horizon|horizon]], the opposite of daytime. Night lasts about 58 Earth days on [[Venus]], and nights on [[Mercury (planet)|Mercury]] are also much longer than on Earth. ENTRY:
Major north-flowing river of northeast Africa, emptying into the [[Mediterranean Sea]]. At 7,088 km it is the longest river in the world, although it carries much less water than the Amazon or the Congo. Its length was measured in 2009 by satellite imagery, from a source in Nyungwe National Park, Rwanda.

The Nile has two major tributaries. The [[White Nile]] is longer and is considered the headwaters; it begins near [[Lake Victoria]] and flows through [[Uganda]] and [[South Sudan]]. The [[Blue Nile]] begins near Lake Tana in [[Ethiopia]] and contributes over twice the water volume of the White Nile. They meet at [[Khartoum]], from which the river flows north through the Nubian Desert to Cairo and empties near Alexandria, forming a large river delta. The Nile has followed its present course for about 15,000 years. Its drainage basin spans eleven countries. Egypt and Sudan, largely desert, withdraw its water for irrigation.

The river was the foundation of Ancient Egyptian civilization. Annual floods deposited nutrient-rich silt that supported crops, and the Nile facilitated trade, communication, transportation, and governance. Upstream of the second cataract lay Nubia, home of the Kerma culture and the Kushite Empire. European explorations around Lake Victoria in the late 19th century located the source of the river.

Today the Nile is critical to the economies of Egypt and Sudan. Over a dozen dams have been built in the basin since the late 20th century for irrigation and electricity. They have altered the flood cycle and restricted downstream silt, causing the delta to shrink. Dams such as the Aswan High Dam and the Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam have been the subject of international disputes over water scarcity, safety, food security, and displacement.

Large crocodilian of Africa's freshwater habitats. The Nile crocodile (*Crocodylus niloticus*) is present in 26 countries and is widely distributed in sub-Saharan Africa. Adult males are typically 3.5 to 5 m long, and specimens over 6.1 m have been recorded. It is Africa's largest predator and the second-largest living reptile, after the [[Saltwater crocodile]]. An ambush predator, it eats mainly fish, reptiles, birds and mammals. It is one of the most dangerous crocodiles, responsible for hundreds of human deaths each year, yet it is common and not endangered.

Proposed family of about 210 Native African languages. The languages are spoken by tens of millions of people in 17 countries across northern Africa, from Algeria to Benin in the west and from Egypt to Tanzania in the east. Joseph Greenberg named the family in *The Languages of Africa* (1963), grouping the non-click languages that did not belong to the Niger–Congo, Afroasiatic or Khoisan families. Its genetic unity is not accepted by all linguists. Glottolog (2013) does not recognize it, and Güldemann (2018) wrote that "the current state of research is not sufficient to prove the Nilo-Saharan hypothesis." Eastern Sudanic, one of its constituent groups, is estimated to date to at least the 5th millennium BC. The earliest written language associated with the family is Old [[Nubia|Nubian]], attested from the 8th to the 15th century AD.

Propositions for an academic disputation on indulgences, written by [[Martin Luther]] in 1517. Ancient Assyrian city in [[Mesopotamia|Upper Mesopotamia]], on the eastern bank of the [[Tigris]] River in northern [[Iraq]]. Nineveh was the capital and largest city of the Neo-Assyrian Empire and was potentially the wealthiest city of the ancient world. For about fifty years it was the largest city in the world. In 612 BC, after civil war in Assyria, it was sacked by a coalition including the Babylonians, Medes and Scythians. It was never again a political or administrative center. Under [[Ashurbanipal]], its library held over 30,000 cuneiform tablets. The name is also used for the eastern half of [[Mosul]]. The ruins include a shrine to Jonah, a prophet who preached to Nineveh in the Hebrew Bible and the Quran. The city declined in the Middle Ages and was mostly abandoned by the 14th century AD after massacres and dispersal of Assyrian Christians by [[Timur]].
