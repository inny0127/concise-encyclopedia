Bacterial infection, also called lockjaw, caused by *Clostridium tetani* and characterized by muscle spasms. The spasms usually begin in the jaw and then spread through the body, lasting a few minutes each and recurring for three to four weeks; some are severe enough to fracture bones. Symptoms typically begin 3 to 21 days after infection, and recovery may take months. About 10% of cases prove fatal. The disease does not spread between people and can be prevented by [[Vaccination|vaccination]]. Globally there were about 59,000 deaths in 2015, down from 356,000 in 1990.

Any of various fiber-based materials, including fibers, yarns, filaments, threads, and fabrics. The word comes from Latin *textilis* ('woven'). Textiles were originally woven, but other methods developed later, notably [[Knitting|knitting]] and non-woven processes such as felting. Fibers, which may be natural, synthetic, or both, are twisted or laid out into yarn, and yarns are made into fabric by [[Weaving|weaving]], knitting, crocheting, knotting, tatting, or braiding. Natural fibers include [[Cotton|cotton]], [[Wool|wool]], and [[Silk|silk]]; [[Polyester|polyester]] is synthetic. Consumer textiles, such as clothing and furnishings, prioritize aesthetics and comfort; technical textiles, such as geotextiles and medical textiles, prioritize function. Cotton or blend garments can last twenty years or more with regular use and care.

Textiles rarely survive across millennia, so prehistoric evidence consists mostly of spinning and weaving tools. The earliest spinning tool was the spindle, to which a whorl was eventually added; the spinning wheel came later, though historians are unsure whether in China or India. The first clothes, probably animal skins, were worn at least 70,000 years ago. Dyed flax fibers found in a cave in the Republic of Georgia, dated to 34,000 BC, suggest that textile-like materials were made in the Paleolithic.

The textile industry grew out of craft and was sustained by craft guilds. During the [[Industrial Revolution]] it became increasingly mechanized; with the spinning jenny, invented in the United Kingdom in 1765, textile production became the first economic activity to be industrialized. For plain weave, twill, and satin weave, ancient and modern methods differ little. Textile manufacture is the oldest industrial art.

American rock band formed in Hawthorne, California, in 1961 by brothers [[Brian Wilson]], Dennis and Carl Wilson, their cousin Mike Love and Al Jardine. Known for vocal harmonies and songs about [[Surfing|surfing]] and youth culture in [[Southern California]], the group had 16 consecutive U.S. top 40 hit singles in a five-year streak. English rock band formed in [[Liverpool]] in 1960, comprising [[John Lennon]], [[Paul McCartney]], [[George Harrison]], and [[Ringo Starr]]. Rooted in skiffle, beat, and 1950s rock 'n' roll, their sound incorporated classical music and traditional pop and later ranged from folk and Indian music to psychedelia and hard rock.

The band evolved from Lennon's earlier group, the Quarrymen, and built its reputation playing clubs in Liverpool and Hamburg. Lennon, McCartney, and Harrison, together since 1958, went through a succession of drummers before Starr joined in 1962. Manager [[Brian Epstein]] shaped them into a professional act, and producer George Martin developed their recordings. After signing with EMI, they had their first hit, "Love Me Do", in late 1962. "Beatlemania" followed, and by early 1964 they were international stars leading the British Invasion of the U.S. pop market.

They retired from live performance in 1966 to refine their studio work. Their later albums include *Rubber Soul* (1965), *Revolver* (1966), *Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band* (1967), *The Beatles* ("the White Album", 1968), and *Abbey Road* (1969). In 1968 they founded Apple Corps, which oversees projects related to their legacy. After the break-up in 1970, all four enjoyed solo success; Lennon was murdered in 1980 and Harrison died of lung cancer in 2001.

The Beatles are the best-selling music act of all time, with estimated sales of over 600 million units worldwide, and hold the records for most number-one singles (20) and albums (19) on the U.S. Billboard charts. Their accolades include nine [[Grammy Awards]], and they entered the [[Rock and Roll Hall of Fame]] in 1988, their first year of eligibility.
