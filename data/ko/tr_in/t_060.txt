Subregion of North America, an isthmus bordering Mexico to the north, Colombia to the southeast, the Caribbean to the east, and the Pacific Ocean to the southwest. It is usually defined as seven countries: Belize, [[Costa Rica]], [[El Salvador]], [[Guatemala]], [[Honduras]], [[Nicaragua]], and [[Panama]]; in specialized contexts Mexico is also included. The Mesoamerican biodiversity hotspot extends from southern Mexico to southeastern Panama. Active faults and the Central America Volcanic Arc make seismic activity high.

The region includes parts of two cultural areas: the Mesoamerican, covering Guatemala, Belize, El Salvador, and western Honduras and Nicaragua, and the Isthmo-Colombian, covering Panama, Costa Rica, and eastern Honduras and Nicaragua. Hundreds of indigenous groups lived there before the voyages of [[Christopher Columbus]], notably the Maya in the north. Spain began colonization from 1502 and began conquering the Maya kingdoms in 1523. From 1609 to 1821 most territories, except what became Belize and Panama, were governed from Mexico City as the Captaincy General of Guatemala within [[New Spain]].

Independence from Spain was proclaimed on September 15, 1821. Some provinces were annexed to the First Mexican Empire, but in 1823 they seceded to form the Federal Republic of Central America. Costa Rica, Guatemala, Honduras, and Nicaragua became independent countries in 1838, El Salvador in 1841, Panama in 1903, and Belize in 1981.

Economies depend heavily on agriculture, particularly coffee, bananas, and sugar, as well as tourism, manufacturing, and remittances from migrants in the United States. The Panama Canal opened in 1914. The region continues to face poverty, violence, corruption, and natural disasters.

Heating system that supplies warmth to many spaces in a building from one main source of heat. In 1793 British engineer William Strutt developed a hot-air system that used metal ducts to distribute furnace-heated air.

Theorem in [[Probability theory|probability theory]] stating that, under appropriate conditions, the distribution of a normalized sample mean converges to a [[Normal distribution|normal distribution]]. This holds even when the original variables are not normally distributed, so methods developed for normal distributions apply to many other problems. Earlier versions date back to 1811, but in its modern form it was only precisely stated in the 1920s. In statistics it justifies the use of z-tests, t-tests and confidence intervals without assuming population normality.

Device that uses [[Centrifugal force|centrifugal force]] to subject a specimen to a specified constant force, most often to separate components of a fluid. Spinning fluid at high speed in a container separates components by density, driving denser substances and particles outward, or separates liquids from solids. In a laboratory sample tube, denser particles settle to the bottom while lighter substances rise to the top. Industrial centrifuges sediment suspended solids or separate immiscible liquids, and the cream separator in a [[Dairy|dairy]] is an example. They take part in forming cilia during interphase and in organizing the aster and mitotic spindle during cell division, although cells whose centrioles were removed by laser ablation can still progress through G1. Sperm centrioles are needed for sperm motility and for embryonic development. In humans the oocyte is acentriolar, so the sperm donates two centrioles that form the first centrosome in the zygote.

Influenced by [[Mahatma Gandhi]], he led nonviolent strikes and boycotts, most notably the Delano grape strike of 1965–1970. By 1973 the UFW had lost most of the contracts and membership it had won in the late 1960s. Critics within the union cited his autocratic control and purges of perceived opponents. In 2026, allegations of sexual abuse surfaced, and honorary tributes to him were removed, renamed or canceled.

ENTRY:
Serial assembly of connected links, typically of metal. Like a [[Rope|rope]], a chain is flexible and curved in compression but linear, rigid and load-bearing in tension. Chains designed for lifting, pulling or securing have torus-shaped links that bend in two dimensions. Chains for transferring power in machines have links that mesh with sprocket teeth and bend in only one dimension; these are known as roller chains. Bicycle chains transfer power from the pedals to the drive wheel, and timing chains transfer rotation from the crankshaft to an engine's valves and ignition system. Uses range from jewelry and [[Chain mail]] to [[Chainsaw|chainsaws]]. Two chains may be joined with a quick link, carabiner or shackle.
