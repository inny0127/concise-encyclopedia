Chronic, neurodegenerative autoimmune disease of the [[Central nervous system|central nervous system]], in which the immune system attacks myelin, the insulating covering of nerve cells, impairing the transmission of signals. Arithmetic operation that combines two factors to produce a product. It is one of the four elementary operations of arithmetic, along with addition, subtraction and [[Division (mathematics)|division]], and is the inverse of division. For whole numbers it can be understood as repeated addition, so 3 × 4 equals 4 + 4 + 4 or 3 + 3 + 3 + 3. Multiplication is [[Commutative property|commutative]]: the order of the factors does not change the product. It extends to [[Integer]]s, [[Rational number]]s and [[Real number]]s, and the product of two measurements carries a derived unit, so multiplying the sides of a rectangle gives its area in square meters. Some extensions, such as [[Matrix (mathematics)|matrix]] multiplication, are not commutative.

Extension of [[Calculus|calculus]] to functions of several variables, covering their differentiation and integration rather than one variable alone. A special case in three-dimensional space is often called [[Vector calculus]]. Partial [[Derivative|derivatives]] measure how a function changes with respect to one variable while the others are held constant. Generalizing to several variables introduces difficulties: a limit can depend on the path along which a point is approached, and continuity in each variable separately does not guarantee continuity overall. The subject is used in physics, [[Engineering]] and [[Economics]].

"Murasaki Shikibu" is a descriptive name; her personal name is unknown, though she may have been Fujiwara no Kaoruko, named in a 1007 court diary as a lady-in-waiting.

Heian women were traditionally excluded from learning Chinese, the written language of government, but Murasaki raised in her scholar father's household, showed a precocious aptitude for the Chinese classics and became fluent. She married in her mid-to-late twenties and had a daughter, Daini no Sanmi; her husband died after two years. In about 1005 Fujiwara no Michinaga invited her to serve as lady-in-waiting to Empress Shōshi, probably because of her reputation as a writer, and she continued writing at court. After five or six years she left court and retired with Shōshi to the Lake Biwa region. Scholars differ on the year of her death: most agree on 1014, while others suggest she was alive in 1025.

She wrote *The Diary of Lady Murasaki*, *The Tale of Genji*, and a volume of poetry. Within a decade of its completion, *Genji* was distributed throughout the provinces; within a century it was recognized as a classic of [[Japanese literature]]. It was published in English between 1925 and 1933. Her works reflect Heian court society at its peak, and since the 13th century they have been illustrated by Japanese artists, including well-known [[Ukiyo-e|ukiyo-e]] woodblock masters.

Specialized soft tissue, one of the four basic types of animal tissue, whose cells contract to produce force and movement. Skeletal muscle is striated and made of elongated, multinucleate cells called muscle fibers, 10 to 100 micrometers wide, containing contractile units called [[Sarcomere|sarcomeres]]. In the latter half of the 20th century, reduced government funding pushed museums to rely on private support and to attract visitors with interactive exhibits. In the 21st century they emphasize accessibility and inclusion, use digital tools, and host discussion of social issues.

The International Council of Museums' definition, adopted in 2022, describes a museum as a not-for-profit, permanent institution in the service of society that researches, collects, conserves, interprets, and exhibits tangible and intangible heritage. Approximately 95,000 museums operate worldwide, up from 22,000 in 1975. The American Alliance of Museums reports that U.S. museums contribute over 18 million instructional hours annually and $50 billion to GDP. The 2015 Recommendation of [[UNESCO]] stresses combating illicit trafficking. Debates persist over the ethical acquisition of objects in colonial eras and demands for their repatriation.

ENTRY:
Art of arranging sound to create some combination of form, harmony, melody, rhythm, or other expressive content. Music is generally agreed to be a cultural universal, present in all human societies, though definitions vary widely and scholars have reached no consensus on which elements are necessary. Its making is often divided into composition, improvisation, and performance. It may be performed on a wide variety of [[Musical instrument|musical instruments]], including the human voice, or produced mechanically or electronically.

Music plays a key role in social events and religious ceremonies, and its techniques are often transmitted as part of a cultural tradition. It is performed at festivals and concerts and used in the soundtracks of films, television shows, operas, and video games. Listening is a common means of entertainment, and the culture around music extends to academic study, journalism, philosophy, psychology, and therapy. The [[Music industry|music industry]] includes songwriters, composers, performers, producers, and publishers of sheet music and recordings. Recording technology has included microphones, phonographs, and tape machines, with digital playback common on smartphones.

The word entered English in the 1630s, from Middle English *musike*, Old French *musique*, and Latin *mūsica*, ultimately from the Greek *mousiké technē*, "art of the [[Muses]]". Concepts of music vary across cultures. Before Western contact, neither Japan nor China had a single word covering music in a broad sense; the Chinese *yue* shares a character with *le*, "joy". The Songye of the Democratic Republic of the Congo and the Tiv of Nigeria have a broad conception of music but no corresponding word.

Origins are debated. Many scholars see a relationship with the [[Origin of language|origin of language]], and disagree on whether music arose before, after, or alongside it, and on whether it was a direct result of natural selection or a byproduct of evolution. [[Charles Darwin]] proposed in 1871 that music arose through sexual selection, perhaps via mating calls; the theory has been heavily criticized, though some 21st-century scholars have promoted it. Other theories hold that music arose to organize labor, aid communication, build community cohesion, or scare off predators. The disputed Divje Babe flute, a perforated cave bear femur, is at least 40,000 years old. The earliest widely accepted instruments are bone flutes from the Swabian Jura, Germany, with three from Geissenklösterle dated oldest, c. 43,150–39,370 BP.

In [[Ancient Egypt]], harps, flutes, and double clarinets are securely attested in the Old Kingdom, and percussion instruments, lyres, and lutes joined orchestras by the Middle Kingdom. The "Hurrian Hymn to Nikkal", found on clay tablets in the Syrian city of [[Ugarit]] and dating to approximately 1400 BC, is the oldest surviving notated work of music. In ancient Greece music was one of the main subjects taught to children, with boys beginning at age six, and musical education was considered important for the development of the soul, as in [[Plato]]'s *Republic*.

Conventional category that groups pieces of [[Music|music]] sharing a tradition or set of conventions. Genre is distinguished from musical form and style, though the terms are sometimes used interchangeably. Music is divided into genres in many ways, such as popular versus art music, or religious versus secular. Classification is often subjective and controversial, since genres evolve and novel music is sometimes lumped into existing categories. A subgenre adopts the basic characteristics of its genre while distinguishing itself within it; the proliferation of popular music in the 20th century has produced over 1,200 definable subgenres. In 1982, Franco Fabbri proposed a definition now considered normative: "musical genre is a set of musical events (real or possible) whose course is governed by a definite set of socially accepted rules."

Traditions of music in China, including classical court forms, folk music and modern popular music. Archaeological finds show a developed musical culture as early as the [[Zhou dynasty]] (1122–257 BC), when a formal system of court and ceremonial music later termed yayue was established. Study of the theoretical frameworks for understanding the practices and possibilities of music. The term has three related uses: the rudiments needed to read notation, such as key signatures, time signatures and rhythmic notation; the study of scholars' views on music from antiquity to the present; and a subtopic of [[Musicology|musicology]] that seeks to define processes and general principles in music. Teaching as a tenure-track music theorist at a university in the United States or Canada typically requires graduate study to the MA or PhD level.
