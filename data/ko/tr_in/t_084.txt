ENTRY:
Standard unit of mass for atomic-scale entities, symbol Da, defined as 1/12 of the mass of an unbound neutral [[Carbon|carbon-12]] [[Atom|atom]] at rest in its ground state. Also called the unified atomic mass unit (symbol u), it is used in physics and chemistry to express the masses of atoms, [[Molecule|molecules]] and particles. A [[Proton|proton]] has a mass of about one dalton. The name was proposed by IUPAC in 1993 and honors English chemist and physicist [[John Dalton]]. The molar mass of a substance in grams per mole is numerically equal to the average mass of one of its molecules in daltons: one mole of [[Water|water]] weighs about 18.0153 grams, and one water molecule weighs about 18.0153 Da. Proteins are measured in kilodaltons (kDa) and megadaltons (MDa).

ENTRY:
Structure built across a watercourse to impound water or restrict its flow, usually creating a [[Reservoir|reservoir]]. Dams are classified into four basic types: gravity dams, of concrete or masonry, which resist the water by their weight; embankment dams, large earthworks of rock, clay, sand, soil, or gravel; buttress dams, with a sloped concrete face supported by triangular buttresses; and arch dams, whose curved concrete wall transfers the water's weight to the valley walls. Natural dams include rockslide, volcanic, and beaver dams.

Dams serve [[Irrigation|irrigation]], [[Hydropower|hydropower]], water supply, flood management, recreation, inland navigation, and fish farming. About 20% of the world's arable land is irrigated with water from reservoirs behind dams. Many include locks, fish ladders, spillways, and outlets. The embankment dam is the most common type; it is cheaper than concrete and can sit on softer soils, but it is inherently permeable, and seepage can cause failure by "piping."

An early dam was Jawa Dam in modern Jordan, built around 3000 BC. The Hittite Empire built several dams in modern Turkey between the 17th and 13th centuries BC, and in the 1st century AD the [[Roman Empire]] began building masonry gravity dams. From the late 18th century, dam design became an engineering discipline rooted in science, and in the 20th century concrete and heavy machinery enabled many large projects. Over 58,000 large dams now operate worldwide.

Failures cause flooding and loss of life, as in the 1975 collapse of the Banqiao Dam in China. Dams can also disrupt ecosystems, trap sediment, and alter aquatic habitats. A dam may be removed if it is unsafe, no longer serves its purpose, or blocks fish migration or sediment flow.

Insect of the suborder Zygoptera in the order [[Odonata]], closely related to the [[Dragonfly|dragonfly]]. Damselflies are usually smaller and slimmer than dragonflies, and most species fold their wings along the body at rest. About 3,300 species are known, and the group has existed since the Late Jurassic and is found on every continent except [[Antarctica]]. All damselflies are predators. ENTRY:
Art form consisting of sequences of body movements with aesthetic and often symbolic value, either improvised or purposefully selected. Dance can be described by its [[Choreography|choreography]], its repertoire of movements, its historical period, or its place of origin. It is typically performed with [[Music|musical]] accompaniment.

Two common types of group dance are theatrical and participatory. Theatrical dance is intended primarily as a spectacle, usually performed on a stage by virtuoso dancers. It often tells a story, using mime, costume, and scenery. Examples include Western [[Ballet|ballet]] and [[Modern dance|modern dance]], classical Indian dance such as [[Bharatanatyam]], and Chinese and Japanese song and dance dramas such as the dragon dance. Either type may serve a social, ceremonial, competitive, erotic, martial, sacred, or liturgical function. Dance is also used as [[Exercise|exercise]] and as training for other sports, and it may be done alone for self-expression.

Archaeological evidence, including rock art at Bhimbetka in India, indicates that dance-like activities date back at least 10,000 years. Researchers have proposed that dance developed into a mechanism for social signaling, group coordination, and mate attraction. It was long presumed universal across human cultures, though some ethnographic studies have identified isolated groups with minimal formalized dance. Studies associate dancing with improved cardiovascular fitness, motor coordination, memory, and spatial awareness.

Proposed form of energy driving the accelerated expansion of the [[Universe|universe]]. It also slows the rate of structure formation. In the lambda-CDM model, dark energy makes up about 68% of the total mass-energy of the present-day observable universe, compared with 27% for [[Dark matter|dark matter]] and 5% for ordinary matter. Its density is very low, about 7×10⁻³⁰ g/cm³, but because it is uniform across space it dominates the universe's mass-energy. Evidence first came from measurements of [[Supernova|Type Ia supernovae]], whose constant luminosity allows accurate distance measurement; their redshift showed that expansion is accelerating. The simplest explanation is the [[Cosmological constant]], first proposed by Einstein, although scalar fields such as quintessence are also considered. Its nature remains unknown.

ENTRY:
Invisible, hypothetical form of matter that does not interact with electromagnetic radiation, including light. Its existence is inferred from gravitational effects that general relativity cannot explain unless more matter is present than is observed, such as in the motion of [[Galaxy|galaxies]] and in [[Cosmic microwave background|cosmic microwave background]] anisotropies. In the standard lambda-CDM model, dark matter makes up 26.8% of the mass-energy of the universe, against 5% for ordinary matter and 68.2% for [[Dark energy|dark energy]]. It is thought to provide gravitational scaffolding for cosmic structures. The leading candidates are unknown particles such as weakly interacting massive particles (WIMPs) or axions; the main alternative is [[Black hole|primordial black holes]]. Most astrophysicists accept its existence, though a minority argue for modified gravity theories such as MOND, and none of these explains all observations.

Family of mainly tropical waterbirds (Anhingidae), also called anhingas or snakebirds. The family contains a single genus, *Anhinga*, with four living species in the Americas, Africa, Asia and Australasia. The name snakebird refers to the long, thin neck, which looks like a snake when the bird swims with its body submerged. Competitive sport and pub game in which players throw small, sharp-pointed darts at a round target called a dartboard. Points are scored by hitting marked areas spread across the board, unlike in [[Archery|archery]], where points rise toward the center. The game is commonly played in the [[British Isles]] and enjoyed around the world. In 1908 darts was declared a game of skill, allowing it in pubs, after a Leeds landlord was prosecuted for playing it on licensed premises. Before [[World War I]], dartboards were made of elm, which was easily damaged. From 1935 they were made of compressed sisal fibers bound with a metal ring, a more durable design.
