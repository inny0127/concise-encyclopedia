Principle of international law that a people has the right to form its own political entity. The principle does not specify how the decision is made or what the outcome should be, whether independence, federation or autonomy, and no right to secession is recognized under international law. The concept emerged with 19th-century nationalism. [[Woodrow Wilson]] and others proclaimed a general principle of self-determination during and after [[World War I]], and it was included in the 1941 Atlantic Charter. It was recognized as an international legal right when it was listed in the [[United Nations]] Charter. Implementing it is difficult because there are multiple interpretations of what constitutes a people. As World Court judge Ivor Jennings put it, "the people cannot decide until somebody decides who are the people."

Confidence in one's own worth, abilities or morals. It encompasses beliefs about oneself, such as "I am worthy," as well as emotional states such as triumph, pride and [[Shame|shame]]. Psychologists usually regard it as an enduring personality trait, although short-term variations also exist. High self-esteem is associated with academic achievement, relationship satisfaction, happiness and lower rates of criminal behavior, while low self-esteem has been linked to anxiety, [[Loneliness|loneliness]] and vulnerability to substance abuse. In the mid-1960s Morris Rosenberg defined self-esteem as a feeling of self-worth and developed the Rosenberg self-esteem scale, the most widely used such scale in the social sciences. Self-esteem typically declines from ages 4–8 and peaks around ages 60–70.

Intentional injury to one's own body, usually without suicidal intent. Common methods include cutting, scratching, hitting or burning. Study of meaning in language. Semantics examines what linguistic meaning is and how words and complex expressions acquire it. It distinguishes sense, the ideas and concepts associated with an expression, from reference, the object to which it points. It contrasts with [[Syntax]], which studies the rules for grammatical sentences, and with [[Pragmatics]], which investigates how people use language. Lexical semantics studies word meaning, phrasal semantics studies how sentences build meaning from their parts, and formal semantics relies on [[Logic|logic]] and mathematics. Together with syntactics and pragmatics, semantics is part of [[Semiotics]]. Its study began in antiquity, but it was not recognized as an independent field until the 19th century.

ENTRY:
Material with electrical conductivity between that of a conductor and an insulator. Its conductivity can be modified by adding impurities, a process called doping, and by electric fields or light. Raising the temperature increases conductivity, contrary to the behavior of a metal. Examples include [[Silicon|silicon]], [[Germanium|germanium]], gallium arsenide, and elements near the "metalloid staircase" of the periodic table. After silicon, gallium arsenide is the second most common semiconductor and is used in laser diodes, solar cells, and microwave-frequency integrated circuits.

For most semiconductors the band gap, the energy needed to lift an electron from the valence band to the conduction band, ranges from about 0.5 to 3 eV; silicon's is 1.1 eV, germanium's 0.67 eV, gallium arsenide's 1.43 eV, and silicon carbide's 3.2 eV.

Adding a small amount, of the order of 1 in 10⁸, of pentavalent atoms (antimony, phosphorus, arsenic) or trivalent atoms (boron, gallium, indium) to silicon produces doped, or extrinsic, semiconductors. Group V dopants act as donors, creating free electrons ("n-type"); Group III dopants act as acceptors, creating free holes ("p-type"). Where regions of different doping meet in one crystal they form a p–n junction. The behavior of charge carriers at such junctions is the basis of the [[Diode|diode]], the [[Transistor|transistor]], and most modern [[Electronics|electronics]]. Devices are used for amplification, switching, and energy conversion. In certain semiconductors excited electrons relax by emitting light, as in the [[Light-emitting diode|light-emitting diode]].

The first practical electronic application was the 1904 cat's-whisker detector, a primitive diode used in early radio receivers. Quantum physics led to the transistor in 1947 and the [[Integrated circuit|integrated circuit]] in 1958.

Study of signs and of how meaning is created and communicated through them. Signs can be sounds, images, written marks or gestures, and they include icons, which resemble their referents; indexical signs, which have a direct physical link such as smoke signaling fire; and conventional symbols, such as most words. Biological system used by an organism to gather information about its surroundings by detecting stimuli, a process called sensation. Sense organs collect stimuli such as sound or smell and transduce them into a form the [[Brain|brain]] can interpret. Sensation and [[Perception|perception]] are fundamental to nearly every aspect of an organism's cognition, behavior, and thought. Some cultures traditionally identified five human senses (sight, smell, touch, taste, and hearing), but many more are now recognized, and non-human organisms use still more.

Receptor cells, such as mechanoreceptors, photoreceptors, chemoreceptors, and thermoreceptors, convert stimuli into action potentials. Signals travel through cranial and spinal nerves of the [[Central nervous system|central]] and [[Peripheral nervous system|peripheral nervous systems]] to the sensory cortices of the brain, where they are processed and perceived.

Senses are divided into external (exteroception) and internal (interoception) systems. Human external senses rely on the eyes, ears, skin, nose, and mouth. Internal senses include proprioception (body position), equilibrioception, and nociception ([[Pain|pain]]), and further signals produce hunger, thirst, suffocation, and nausea.

Sensory modality refers to how information is encoded. Listing modalities, which can number as many as 17, involves dividing the major senses into submodalities. Touch can be separated into light pressure, deep pressure, vibration, itch, pain, temperature, and hair movement; taste into sweet, salty, sour, bitter, spicy, and [[Umami|umami]]. Multimodality integrates senses into one perceptual experience, so that one sense can influence how another is perceived.

Other mammals generally have a stronger sense of smell than humans. Some animals detect electrical and magnetic fields, air moisture, or polarized light, or perceive through echolocation. Psychophysics, neurobiology, [[Cognitive psychology|cognitive psychology]], and cognitive science study the senses.

The name comes from Latin *septem*, "seven", because September was the seventh month of the oldest known Roman calendar before January and February were added. The September equinox falls in this month. In the United States, September is the third most common birth month.

Ordered list of elements, finite or infinite, in which the same element may recur at different positions. Unlike a [[Set (mathematics)|set]], order matters: (1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8) is a valid sequence. Cherokee polymath and creator of the Cherokee syllabary. Also known as George Gist or George Guess, Sequoyah was a neographer, a creator of new writing systems, of the Cherokee Nation.
