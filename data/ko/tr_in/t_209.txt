[[Mood disorder|Mood disorder]], also called [[Depression (mood)|clinical depression]], marked by at least two weeks of pervasive low mood, low self-esteem and loss of interest in normally enjoyable activities. Diagnosis rests on clinical evaluation, including reports from family or friends and a mental status examination, because no laboratory test exists for it. About 40% of the risk is genetic, and risk factors include family history, major life changes, childhood trauma and substance use disorders. It causes the second-most years lived with disability, after lower back pain. Symptoms can include hopelessness, emotional blunting, poor concentration, insomnia or hypersomnia, and changes in appetite. Treatment usually combines [[Psychotherapy|psychotherapy]] with antidepressant [[Medication|medication]], such as [[Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor|SSRIs]], although the clinical efficacy of antidepressants is disputed. Untreated, it increases the risk of [[Suicide|suicide]], the 11th leading cause of death in the U.S.

Opaque, green-banded copper carbonate hydroxide [[Mineral|mineral]], Cu₂(CO₃)(OH)₂. It forms in weathered copper ore deposits, often with azurite, and usually as botryoidal, fibrous or stalactitic masses in fractures and underground spaces. Its name derives from the Greek for mallow-green stone, reflecting its resemblance to mallow leaves, and [[Copper|copper]] gives it its green color. Largest class of crustaceans, with about 40,000 living species in 16 orders. Members include crabs, lobsters, crayfish, shrimp, [[Krill|krill]], isopods and [[Mantis shrimp|mantis shrimp]], and they inhabit marine, freshwater and terrestrial environments. Their bodies have 20 segments (rarely 21), divided into a head, thorax and abdomen. Except for fairy shrimps, malacostracans are the only living arthropods with compound eyes on movable stalks. The name, from the Greek for soft shell, is a misnomer because the shell is usually hard. French zoologist Pierre André Latreille applied it in 1802.

Mosquito-borne infectious disease caused by single-celled parasites of the genus *Plasmodium*. It is transmitted by the bite of infected female *Anopheles* mosquitoes. The parasites first reproduce and mature in the [[Liver|liver]] without causing symptoms, then spread into the bloodstream, where they infect and destroy [[Red blood cell|red blood cells]]. Five species commonly infect humans. *P. falciparum*, responsible for the vast majority of deaths, *P. vivax*, and *P. knowlesi* are associated with more severe cases, while *P. ovale* and *P. malariae* generally cause milder illness.

Symptoms, which usually begin 10 to 15 days after the bite, include fever, chills, fatigue, vomiting, and headaches, and in severe cases jaundice, seizures, coma, or death. Cerebral malaria, if untreated, can lead to death within forty-eight hours of the first symptoms. Survivors may develop partial immunity, which fades over months to years without continuing exposure. Diagnosis is by microscopic examination of blood films or rapid diagnostic tests; methods using the [[Polymerase chain reaction]] are considered the gold standard but are costly and not widely available where malaria is common.

Prevention relies on mosquito nets, repellents, insecticide spraying, draining standing water, and prophylactic medications. As of 2023, two vaccines have been endorsed by the [[World Health Organization]]. Parasites have evolved resistance to several drugs, including chloroquine and, in parts of [[Southeast Asia]], artemisinin.

The disease is widespread in tropical and subtropical regions around the [[Equator|equator]]. In 2024, some 282 million cases resulted in an estimated 610,000 deaths. The WHO African Region accounted for approximately 94% of cases and 95% of deaths. Malaria is commonly associated with poverty; in 2005 Africa was estimated to lose US$12 billion a year to it.

Peninsula in [[Mainland Southeast Asia]], ending at Tanjung Piai, the southernmost point of the Asian continental mainland. It contains [[Malaysia|Peninsular Malaysia]], southern [[Thailand]] and the southern tip of [[Myanmar]]. The [[Strait of Malacca]] separates it from [[Sumatra]], and the Straits of Johor separate its south coast from [[Singapore]]. The Titiwangsa Mountains form its backbone, and the Kra Isthmus is its narrowest point. The term *Tanah Melayu* ("Malay land") appears in texts from the early 17th century, including the *Hikayat Hang Tuah*, where it refers to the area under the [[Malacca Sultanate]]. In the early 20th century, Malay nationalists used the term to call for uniting the peninsula's Malay states, an ambition largely realized in the Federation of Malaya in 1948. The peninsula is covered with tropical moist broadleaf forests, with dipterocarp trees in the lowlands.

Vertebrate animal of the class Mammalia, characterized by milk-producing mammary glands for feeding the young, a broad neocortex region of the brain, fur or hair, and three middle ear bones. About 6,640 extant species have been described and divided into 27 orders.

The largest orders by number of species are the [[Rodent|rodents]], [[Bat|bats]], and eulipotyphlans (hedgehogs, moles, and shrews), followed by the [[Primate|primates]], the even-toed ungulates (including pigs, bovids, and whales), and the [[Carnivora]].
