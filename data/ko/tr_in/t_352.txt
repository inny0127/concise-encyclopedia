Process of 1866–71 that built the first German nation-state, excluding Austria. It began on August 18, 1866, with the North German Confederation Treaty, a military alliance dominated by the [[Kingdom of Prussia|Kingdom of Prussia]]. It symbolically concluded on January 18, 1871, when the southern German states joined and the [[German Empire|German Empire]], with 25 member states, was proclaimed under Prussian leadership. Legally, the decisive steps were January 1, 1871, when the southern states acceded and the name German Empire was adopted; May 4, 1871, when the permanent constitution took effect; and May 10, 1871, with the Treaty of Frankfurt. Historians debate whether [[Otto von Bismarck|Otto von Bismarck]] had a master plan or simply sought to expand Prussian power. Prussian victories in three regional wars generated national pride that politicians could harness. The Peace of Prague settled the question of a "small Germany" without Austria against a "greater Germany" with it in favor of the former.

Units of measurement used in the [[United States]], derived from English units. The customary system was standardized in 1832 on the basis of the system then in use in the United Kingdom before the imperial system took effect on January 1, 1826. Many units are similar to their [[Imperial units|imperial]] counterparts, though definitions differ. The Mendenhall Order of 1893 redefined most units in terms of the meter and kilogram. Federal republic primarily in North America, consisting of 50 states and a federal capital district, [[Washington, D.C.]] The 48 contiguous states border Canada and Mexico, with [[Alaska]] in the northwest and the archipelago of [[Hawaii]] in the Pacific. It has the world's fourth-largest total area, 9,833,517 sq km, and third-largest population, exceeding 341 million.

Paleo-Indians migrated from North Asia to North America at least 15,000 years ago. British colonization beginning in 1607 established the [[Thirteen Colonies]]. Disputes over taxation without parliamentary representation sparked the [[American Revolution]]; independence was formally declared on July 2, 1776, in the Lee Resolution, and the 1775–1783 war confirmed international recognition. The Constitution dates from 1787. The country expanded westward through purchase, settlement, and conquest. A sectional division over slavery led 11 Southern states to secede and form the [[Confederate States of America]], fighting the Union in the Civil War of 1861–1865; slavery was abolished with Union victory. By 1900 the U.S. had emerged as a great power. It entered World War II after Japan's attack on Pearl Harbor in 1941, then rivaled the Soviet Union in the [[Cold War]]; the Soviet collapse in 1991 left it the sole superpower.

The Constitution established a separation of powers among legislative, executive, and judicial branches. Executive authority is vested in a president; Congress is bicameral, with a House of Representatives and a Senate. The Supreme Court exercises judicial review, and the 50 states have substantial autonomy.

The economy accounts for over a quarter of nominal global GDP and has been the world's largest since about 1890; wealth inequality is highly pronounced. The U.S. makes up nearly a third of global military spending. "America" derives from the first name of Amerigo Vespucci and was first used as a place name in 1507.
