Intense emotional state involving a strong, uncomfortable, and non-cooperative response to a perceived provocation, hurt, or threat.

Anger is also called rage; wrath is its extreme counterpart. It is often accompanied by increased heart rate, elevated [[Blood pressure|blood pressure]], and raised levels of the stress hormones [[Adrenaline|adrenaline]] and noradrenaline, and some view it as triggering part of the fight-or-flight response. Management approaches aim to reduce emotional intensity and physiological arousal through cognitive reappraisal, relaxation techniques, and problem-solving.

The Church of England declared independence from the [[Holy See]] through the Act of Supremacy in 1534, a separation later consolidated by the Elizabethan Religious Settlement. It retained apostolic succession, and its mid-16th-century formularies correspond closely to those of historical [[Protestantism]]. The Book of Common Prayer, unique to Anglicanism, is acknowledged as a principal tie binding the communion. After the American Revolution, Anglican congregations in the United States and British North America became autonomous churches, a model that spread with the [[British Empire]]. The archbishop of Canterbury is recognized as the communion's primus inter pares. In 2020 there were 63,556,000 adherents in Africa, the center of the communion having shifted to the Global South.

Fishing technique using a hook attached to a line, usually handled with a rod. The name derives from [[Old English]] angol, meaning “hook.” The angler casts a baited hook, or a lure with several hooks, into the water and waits for a fish to bite; a float, bell or quiver tip signals the strike. The angler then sets the hook, tires the fish and lands it, often with a net. Angling is the principal method of recreational fishing, though commercial fisheries use related methods such as longlining and trolling. Many regions require a fishing license and impose size limits, and catch-and-release is increasingly practiced to conserve stocks. Popular game fish include [[Tuna]] and grouper in saltwater, and [[Trout]] and [[Salmon]] in freshwater. Angling differs from snagging, which uses large grappling hooks that pierce the fish externally.

Series of four conflicts between the Dutch Republic and the states of England and Great Britain, 1652–1784. England won the first war, while the Dutch were successful in the second and third. During the [[Napoleonic Wars|French Revolutionary and Napoleonic Wars]], intermittent conflict continued, and Britain captured most of the Dutch colonial empire and destroyed much of the Dutch navy.

ENTRY:
Early medieval cultural group speaking Old English and inhabiting much of what is now England and south-eastern Scotland. Its members traced their origins to Germanic settlers who became one of the most important cultural groups in Britain by the 5th century. The Anglo-Saxon period is usually dated from about 450 to the [[Norman Conquest]] in 1066. By the 8th century an Anglo-Saxon identity called *Englisc* had developed from the interaction of the settlers with the existing Romano-British culture. By 1066 most inhabitants of what is now England spoke [[Old English]] and were considered English. Late Anglo-Saxon structures and language are the direct predecessors of the high medieval [[Kingdom of England]] and [[Middle English]]. Although less than 26% of modern English words derive from Old English, these include over 80% of everyday words. The historian [[Bede]] gave the earliest detailed account of their origins.

British colonial war against the Zulu Kingdom in present-day [[South Africa]], fought from January to early July 1879. The Zulu Kingdom had grown into a regional power under [[Shaka]] in the 1810s, and the war was part of British plans to consolidate control over southern Africa. Sir Bartle Frere, the British High Commissioner for Southern Africa, sent an ultimatum to King Cetshwayo on 11 December 1878, and after its rejection Lord Chelmsford invaded Zululand. The Zulu won the Battle of Isandlwana, but a small British garrison held Rorke's Drift against a much larger attack. The British gained the upper hand at Kambula and took the Zulu capital of Ulundi. The Zulu Kingdom was made a protectorate of the [[British Empire]] and was annexed in 1887.

Country on the western coast of Southern Africa (pop., 2024: 36.6 million). It is the second-largest Portuguese-speaking country after [[Brazil]] in both area and population, and its capital is [[Luanda]]. The [[Kingdom of Kongo]] was the dominant state in the region from the 14th century, and Portuguese colonization of the coast began in the 16th century. Independence from [[Portugal]] in 1975 was followed by a civil war that lasted until 2002. Since then Angola has been a relatively stable constitutional republic with one of the world's fastest-growing economies, though wealth is concentrated among a small part of the population and life expectancy is among the lowest in the world. Petroleum drives approximately 50% of GDP and more than 90% of exports. Angola is a member of the [[United Nations]] and the [[African Union]].

Civil war in [[Angola]] from 1975 to 2002, fought between rival former anti-colonial movements. The communist People's Movement for the Liberation of Angola (MPLA) fought the anti-communist National Union for the Total Independence of Angola (UNITA). The National Front for the Liberation of Angola (FNLA) played almost no role after the initial phase. Backed by [[Cuba]] and the [[Soviet Union]], the MPLA won the initial conventional fighting, ousted the FNLA from [[Luanda]] and became the de facto government. ENTRY:
Unit of length equal to 10⁻¹⁰ meter, or 0.1 nanometre. It is named after the Swedish physicist Anders Jonas Ångström. The angstrom is used to express the sizes of [[Atom|atoms]] and [[Molecule|molecules]], the lengths of chemical bonds and the wavelengths of electromagnetic radiation; visible light has wavelengths in the range of 4000–7000 Å. Spectroscopists adopted it in the late 19th century, and in 1960 the meter was redefined so that the angstrom again equaled 10⁻¹⁰ meter. Never part of the [[International System of Units]], it has increasingly been replaced by the nanometre.
