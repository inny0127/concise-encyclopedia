ENTRY:
[[Composite material]] of aggregate bound together with a fluid cement that cures to a solid. It is the most widely used building material and the most-manufactured material in the world; only water is used more. Cement-bound concrete differs from the less rigid, less durable asphaltic concrete, which has a bituminous binder.

When aggregate is mixed with dry Portland [[Cement|cement]] and water, the mixture forms a fluid slurry that can be poured and molded. The cement reacts with the water in an exothermic process called hydration, which hardens it over several hours into a durable, stone-like matrix. Most structural concrete is poured with embedded [[Steel|steel]] rebar to provide tensile strength, yielding reinforced concrete. Non-cementitious types include asphalt concrete, with a [[Bitumen|bitumen]] binder frequently used for road surfaces, and polymer concretes.

The word comes from the Latin *concretus*, 'compact' or 'condensed'. Concrete floors were found in the royal palace of Tiryns, Greece, dated roughly to 1400–1200 BC, and the Assyrian Jerwan Aqueduct (688 BC) used waterproof concrete. The Romans used concrete extensively from 300 BC to AD 476, making it from quicklime, pozzolana, and an aggregate of pumice. It freed Roman builders from the limits of stone and brick, and the [[Colosseum]] was built largely of it; the Pantheon has the world's largest unreinforced concrete dome. Modern tests show Roman concrete had a compressive strength similar to modern Portland-cement concrete, c. 20 MPa, but a far lower tensile strength. Its durability is attributed to volcanic rock and ash in the mix, and it resists seawater erosion better than modern concrete.

Mild traumatic brain injury that temporarily affects brain function. A concussion results from a direct blow to the head or from forces transmitted to the head from elsewhere on the body. Symptoms may include headache, dizziness, difficulty concentrating, sleep disturbances, brief memory loss, blurred vision and mood changes, usually appearing within 72 hours. They can last two weeks in adults and four weeks in children. Common causes include motor vehicle collisions, falls, sports injuries and bicycle accidents. Treatment involves relative rest for no more than one or two days, followed by gradual return to activity, and aerobic exercise may improve recovery. Worldwide, concussions affect more than 3.5 per 1,000 people a year. System of thought and behavior originating in ancient China, variously described as a tradition, philosophy, religion, theory of government, or way of life. Also called Ruism, it was founded by [[Confucius]] in the Hundred Schools of Thought era (c. 500 BCE) and integrates philosophy, ethics, and social governance, with a core focus on virtue, social harmony, and familial responsibility.

Confucianism emphasizes virtue through self-cultivation and communal effort. Key virtues include *rén* (benevolence), *yì* (righteousness), *lǐ* (propriety), *zhì* (wisdom), and *xìn* (sincerity). These are tied to *tiān* (Heaven), which, though not an omnipotent deity, is upheld as a transcendent moral order. Tradition holds that he authored or edited the Five Classics, though scholars debate how far they took shape in his hands. His sayings are preserved in the [[Analects]].

Suppressed under the Legalist [[Qin dynasty]] (c. 200 BCE), Confucianism flourished under the [[Han dynasty]] (c. 130 BCE), becoming the dominant ideological framework. Under the Tang dynasty (c. 600 CE), in response to the rising influence of [[Buddhism]] and [[Taoism]], Neo-Confucianism developed; it became central to the imperial examination system and the scholar-official class of the [[Song dynasty]]. The abolition of the examination system in 1905 marked the decline of state-endorsed Confucianism. In the early 20th century Chinese reformers associated it with China's Century of Humiliation and embraced other ideologies, including Sun Yat-sen's Three Principles of the People and later Maoism. Critics argue that it can entrench hierarchy and authority; others credit it with fostering social cohesion and East Asian economic development. It remains influential in China, Korea, Japan, Vietnam, and Chinese diaspora communities.

Born Kong Qiu, he is known in Mandarin as Kongzi, 'Master Kong'; the Latinized name was coined in the late 16th century by Jesuit missionaries. His teachings, called [[Confucianism]], emphasized personal and governmental morality, harmonious social relationships, righteousness, kindness, sincerity, and a ruler's responsibility to lead by virtue.

According to tradition he was born in Zou, in modern Qufu, [[Shandong]], in the state of Lu. His father, an elderly commandant of the local garrison, died when Confucius was three, and his mother raised him in poverty. He married at 19. In 501 BCE he was appointed governor of a town and eventually rose to Minister of Crime.

Confucius considered himself a transmitter of the values of earlier periods, which he claimed had been abandoned in his time. He advocated filial piety, endorsing strong family loyalty, ancestor veneration, and respect for elders. He championed the Silver Rule, a negative form of the [[Golden Rule]]: "Do not do unto others what you do not want done to yourself."

His ideas gained prominence during the [[Warring States period]] but suffered a setback after the Qin conquest. Under [[Emperor Wu of Han]] they received official sanction, and affiliated works became mandatory reading for careers in officialdom. Tradition credits him with authoring or editing the Five Classics, but modern scholars are cautious about attributing specific assertions to him, since some of the texts and philosophy were of older origin. Aphorisms of his teaching were compiled in the [[Analects]] many years after his death.

Connective tissue proper includes loose and dense varieties, while special connective tissue includes supportive [[Bone|bone]] and [[Cartilage|cartilage]]. [[Tendon|Tendons]] and [[Ligament|ligaments]] are examples of dense regular connective tissue, in which collagen fibers run parallel for strength in one direction. Type I collagen makes up about 25% of the total protein content of the mammalian body. The term was introduced in 1830 by Johannes Peter Müller.

Cognitive process of moral judgment based on an individual's values. It produces emotion and rational associations, often felt as remorse when a person acts against their [[Morality|moral values]]. ENTRY:
Awareness of something internal to oneself or of states or objects in one's external environment. It has been the topic of debate among philosophers, scientists, and theologians for millennia. There is no consensus on what exactly needs to be studied, or whether consciousness can be considered a scientific concept. In some accounts it is synonymous with [[Mind|mind]]; in others it is an aspect of it. Medicine also uses a definition that distinguishes [[Coma|coma]] from other states.

The English words date to the 17th century and derive from the Latin *conscius*, 'knowing with', especially in sharing a secret. [[Thomas Hobbes]] wrote in 1651 that when two or more people know the same fact, they are said to be conscious of it to one another. [[René Descartes]] is generally taken as the first philosopher to use the Latin *conscientia* in a sense closer to modern 'conscience', and the modern concept of consciousness is often attributed to [[John Locke]], who in 1690 defined it as 'the perception of what passes in a man's own mind'.

Philosophers frame the central question as the hard problem: why and how physical processes in the brain give rise to subjective experience. Distinctions are drawn between phenomenal experience, the 'what it is like' quality of feeling, and access consciousness, the availability of information for reporting and decision-making. In [[Neuroscience|neuroscience]], research seeks the neural correlates of consciousness. Prominent theories include higher-order theories, global neuronal workspace theory, predictive processing theories, and integrated information theory, which quantifies consciousness as irreducible causal integration. Whether it can arise in artificial systems remains debated.

ENTRY:
Law of physics and chemistry on the conservation of total energy in isolated systems. Energy can be neither created nor destroyed, only transformed or transferred from one form to another. In a closed system, total energy changes only when energy enters or leaves it. When dynamite explodes, chemical energy becomes the [[Kinetic energy|kinetic]] and potential energy of the fragments, heat and sound, and their sum equals the decrease in chemical energy. Classically, energy and mass were treated as separate, but [[Special relativity|special relativity]] links them through [[Mass–energy equivalence|mass–energy equivalence]] (E = mc²), so mass-energy as a whole is conserved. Mass can be converted to energy, as observed in the nuclear binding energy of atomic nuclei. [[Noether's theorem|Noether's theorem]] proves conservation from the continuous time-translation symmetry of physical laws. It follows that a perpetual motion machine of the first kind cannot exist. Depending on how energy is defined, [[General relativity|general relativity]] arguably violates conservation on the cosmological scale.
