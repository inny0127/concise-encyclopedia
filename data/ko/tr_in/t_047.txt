ENTRY:
Water with more salinity than freshwater but less than seawater. It results from mixing seawater and fresh water, as in [[Estuary|estuaries]], or occurs in brackish fossil aquifers. Technically it contains 0.5 to 30 grams of salt per liter, and water above 30 parts per thousand is considered saline. Estuaries are the most extensive brackish habitats worldwide. Tactile writing system used by blind and visually impaired people. Braille characters are formed from six raised dots in a 3 × 2 cell, giving 64 possible combinations, including none for a word space. ENTRY:
Organ forming the center of the nervous system in all vertebrate and most invertebrate animals, consisting of nervous tissue and typically located in the head.

The brain receives information from the sensory nervous system, processes it (thought, [[Cognition|cognition]], and intelligence), and coordinates motor control and the endocrine system. It acts on the body by generating patterns of muscle activity and by driving the secretion of hormones, allowing rapid and coordinated responses to the environment. Simple responses such as reflexes can be mediated by the spinal cord, but sophisticated control of behavior requires a centralized brain.

Vertebrate brains develop from the dorsal nerve cord as an enlargement at the end of the neural tube and can be embryonically divided into forebrain, midbrain, and hindbrain. Together, the brain and [[Spinal cord|spinal cord]] constitute the [[Central nervous system|central nervous system]]. In humans, the main regions are the cerebrum, cerebellum, and brainstem; the cerebrum has two hemispheres and four lobes: frontal, parietal, temporal, and occipital.

Brains are composed primarily of [[Neuron|neurons]] and glial cells. Glial cells provide structural and metabolic support, insulation, and guidance of development. Neurons send signals to specific target cells, sometimes over long distances, by means of axons, which are usually myelinated and carry rapid electrical pulses called action potentials. The human cerebral cortex contains approximately 14–16 billion neurons and the cerebellum an estimated 55–70 billion; another account gives about 86 billion neurons in total. The human brain weighs about 1.4 kg (3 lb) and consumes roughly 20% of the body's oxygen and energy while making up about 2% of body mass. Its neuroplasticity enables learning and recovery from injury.

Destruction or degeneration of brain cells, also called brain damage or neurotrauma. Baked food made from water, flour, and often yeast. A staple food across the world, particularly in Europe and the Middle East, bread is one of the oldest human-made foods. The oldest evidence of bread-making comes from a 14,500-year-old Natufian site in Jordan's northeastern desert, and grains became its mainstay around 10,000 BC with the dawn of the [[Neolithic]] age and the spread of agriculture.

Bread may be leavened by naturally occurring microbes, as in [[Sourdough|sourdough]], by chemicals, by industrially produced [[Yeast|yeast]], or by high-pressure aeration; it may also be unleavened. In yeast fermentation, carbon dioxide and ethanol vapors produce the air pockets of the crumb. An early leavened bread was baked as early as 6000 BC in southern Mesopotamia, home of the [[Sumer|Sumerian]] civilization, which may have passed the knowledge to the Egyptians around 3000 BC. [[Pliny the Elder]] reported that the Gauls and Iberians used the foam skimmed from beer, called barm, for a lighter bread, and that the most common leavening was a piece of dough kept from the previous day. The Chorleywood process, developed in 1961, uses intense mechanical working to shorten fermentation and is widely used in large factories.

Bread is the staple of the Middle East, Central Asia, North Africa, Europe, and European-derived cultures, in contrast to parts of South and East Asia, where rice or noodles are the staple. Most is made from [[Wheat|wheat]], whose gluten gives dough sponginess and elasticity. Other cereals, including [[Rye|rye]], barley, maize, oats, sorghum, millet, and rice, have been used, usually mixed with wheat flour because they contain less gluten. Gluten-free breads use flours such as almond, rice, sorghum, corn, legumes, and cassava.

Ending of an intimate relationship, romantic or platonic. Slang calls a breakup started by one partner "dumping." For a married couple the ending is typically called a separation or [[Divorce|divorce]]. Psychotherapist Susie Orbach (1992) has argued that the end of dating and cohabiting relationships can be as painful as divorce, because such relationships are less socially recognized. Platonic breakups are usually caused by failure to maintain a [[Friendship|friendship]].
