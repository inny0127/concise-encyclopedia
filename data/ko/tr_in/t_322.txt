ENTRY:
Medical procedure that intentionally and permanently leaves a person unable to reproduce. Sterilization comprises several methods of permanent [[Birth control|birth control]] available to both sexes, and reversal is generally difficult. The most common are tubal ligation (salpingectomy) for women and vasectomy for men. Tubal sterilization is highly effective, and surgical complications are low in the [[United States]], but it remains [[Surgery|surgery]] with risks, and women have a higher risk of serious side effects than men who have a vasectomy. Some studies estimate that 3% of women become [[Pregnancy|pregnant]] after tubal ligation; if this happens, the risk of ectopic gestation is high. Sterilization may be voluntary or promoted by governments, and some governments have historically or presently run compulsory or incentivized programs, while certain nations restrict or prohibit the practice.

ENTRY:
[[Organic compound|Organic compound]] with four fused rings, designated A, B, C and D. Steroids have two principal biological functions: as components of [[Cell membrane|cell membranes]], where they alter membrane fluidity, and as signaling molecules. Examples include the [[Lipid|lipid]] [[Cholesterol|cholesterol]], the sex hormones estradiol and [[Testosterone|testosterone]], anabolic steroids, and the anti-inflammatory corticosteroid dexamethasone. Hundreds occur in fungi, plants and animals, and all are manufactured in cells from sterol precursors. The name derives from cholesterol, a sterol first described in gallstones, and the steroid nucleus typically has seventeen carbon atoms in four fused rings. Cutting ring B produces secosteroids, one of which is vitamin D3. Widely used trivial names of natural steroids with significant biologic activity include progesterone, testosterone and cortisol.

ENTRY:
Medical device for listening to sounds inside the body, used in auscultation. A stethoscope has a small disc-shaped resonator placed against the skin, with one or two tubes leading to earpieces. It is used to listen to the heart, lungs and intestines and to blood flow in arteries and veins, and it is commonly used with a sphygmomanometer when measuring [[Blood pressure|blood pressure]]. René Laennec invented the first stethoscope in [[France]] in 1816, a wooden monaural tube that spared him from placing his ear directly on a woman's chest. Arthur Leared invented a binaural version in 1851, and George Philip Cammann perfected a binaural design for commercial production the following year. A design by Rappaport and Sprague in the 1940s became the standard by which other stethoscopes are measured.

Resistance of an object to deformation in response to an applied [[Force|force]]; its inverse is flexibility or compliance. For a body with a single degree of freedom, stiffness is the force divided by the displacement it produces along the same direction, k = F/δ, usually defined under quasi-static conditions. Shear and torsional stiffness are further measures.

Stoics believed the universe operates according to reason, or *logos*, and held that [[Virtue|virtue]] is the highest good and sufficient for a well-reasoned life. The philosophy is traditionally divided into logic, physics and ethics. Stoic ethics stressed self-discipline, emotional self-control and living in accordance with nature, and it distinguished what lies within one's control from what does not. Chrysippus, the third head of the school, shaped its system, and Stoic propositional logic was one of the two great systems of logic in the classical world. Stoicism was widely followed by educated elites in the Roman Empire, including [[Marcus Aurelius]], and it declined after Christianity became the state religion in the 4th century CE. It has since been revived in Renaissance Neostoicism and has inspired modern cognitive therapies.

ENTRY:
Pore in the epidermis of plant leaves and stems, bordered by a pair of guard cells that regulate its opening. Carbon dioxide used in [[Photosynthesis]] and oxygen used in respiration pass through stomata by diffusion, while water vapor leaves through them in [[Transpiration]]. Plants therefore cannot take in carbon dioxide without losing water. Stomata occur in the sporophyte of most land plants, though not in [[Liverwort|liverworts]]. Most tree species have them only on the lower leaf surface, whereas [[Monocotyledon|monocotyledons]] such as onion, oat and maize often have about the same number on both surfaces. Many desert plants that use crassulacean acid metabolism open their stomata at night, when water evaporates more slowly, to reduce water loss. Size varies by species, but lengths range from 10 to 80 μm end to end.

ENTRY:
Broad prehistoric period during which stone was widely used to make tools with an edge, a point, or a percussion surface. It lasted roughly 3.4 million years and ended between 4000 BC and 2000 BC with the advent of metalworking, and it spans over 99% of human history. The smelting of copper marks the end of the period; in Western Asia this occurred by about 3000 BC, when bronze became widespread, ushering in the [[Bronze Age]].

Tools were made by modern humans, earlier *Homo* species, and possibly [[Australopithecus]] and *Paranthropus*. The Stone Age is the first period of the three-age system used in archaeology, followed by the Bronze Age and Iron Age. It is commonly divided into the [[Paleolithic]], a transitional [[Mesolithic]] with finer tools, and the [[Neolithic]], when people first moved from hunter-gatherer life to settled towns and villages as agriculture spread. The Neolithic usually overlaps with the [[Chalcolithic]] ("Copper") era.

The Stone Age coincides with the evolution of the genus *Homo*. The cradle of the genus appears to be the East African Rift System, especially in [[Ethiopia]]. [[Homo erectus]] found an ecological niche as a tool-maker on the grasslands of the rift.

The oldest indirect evidence of stone tool use is animal bones with tool marks, 3.4 million years old, from the Lower Awash Valley in Ethiopia. The oldest stone tools, from Lomekwi 3 in West Turkana, Kenya, are 3.3 million years old. In July 2018 scientists reported what may be, if confirmed, the oldest known stone tools outside Africa, at 2.12 million years old, in China.
