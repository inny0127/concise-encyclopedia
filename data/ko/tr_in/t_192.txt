Disaccharide of galactose and glucose, found in [[Milk|milk]]. It makes up around 2–8% of milk by mass and is the predominant sugar in the milk of most mammals. Its name comes from *lac*, the Latin word for milk. Bovine milk contains about 4.8% lactose. Industrially, lactose is extracted from whey, the liquid left after milk is curdled for [[Cheese|cheese]] production, and it is used in pharmaceuticals, food additives, and infant formula. More than 70% of western Europeans digest lactose as adults, compared with less than 30% of people from areas of Africa, eastern and southeastern Asia, and Oceania.

Method in [[Mathematical optimization|mathematical optimization]] for finding the local maxima and minima of a function subject to equation constraints. Named after [[Joseph-Louis Lagrange]], it converts a constrained problem into an unconstrained one through a Lagrangian function, which combines the objective with each constraint weighted by a multiplier λ. Equilibrium point for a small object under the gravitational influence of two massive orbiting bodies. Five such points exist for any pair of bodies, labeled L1 through L5 and lying in their orbital plane. At these points the gravitational forces of the two bodies balance the centrifugal pseudo-force, so satellites there need minimal fuel to hold position. Freshwater lake in [[Nicaragua]], the largest in Central America. Of tectonic origin, it covers 8,264 km², making it the 19th-largest lake in the world by area and the tenth largest in the Americas. Its outflow is the San Juan River, which flows east to the [[Caribbean Sea]]. Bull sharks migrate into the lake from the Caribbean, and the volcanic island of Ometepe, the largest island in the lake, rises within it. Before the Panama Canal was built, a stagecoach line owned by [[Cornelius Vanderbilt]]'s company linked the lake with the Pacific, and a Nicaragua Canal was proposed. The United States secured canal rights in the Bryan–Chamorro Treaty of 1916, but the treaty was rescinded in 1970. In 2014 Nicaragua granted a 50-year concession for a canal at a cost of US$40 billion. Protests over its ecological and social effects, and doubts about financing, meant construction never began.

Freshwater lake in northwestern [[Russia]], the second-largest lake in Europe after [[Lake Ladoga]]. It covers about 9,700 km² and belongs to the [[Baltic Sea]] drainage basin. The lake is fed by about 50 rivers and drained by the Svir River, and it contains about 1,650 islands. Kizhi, one of them, hosts a historic complex of 89 Orthodox churches and other wooden structures dating from the 15th to 20th centuries. The complex, Kizhi Pogost, is a [[UNESCO]] [[World Heritage Site]]. The major cities on the lake are Petrozavodsk, Kondopoga and Medvezhyegorsk.

Easternmost and smallest by surface area of the five [[Great Lakes]] of North America. Northernmost, westernmost and highest of the [[Great Lakes]] of North America, and the largest freshwater lake in the world by surface area. It straddles the Canada–United States border, with [[Ontario]] to the north and east and [[Minnesota]], [[Michigan]] and [[Wisconsin]] to the west and south. Large lake in the [[Andes]] on the border of [[Peru]] and [[Bolivia]]. It is the largest lake in South America by surface area and volume. At 3,812 m above sea level, it is often called the highest navigable lake in the world. Although often listed as freshwater, its waters are slightly brackish. Saline alkaline lake in the Kenyan Rift Valley, extending into Ethiopia at its far northern end. It is the world's largest permanent desert lake and largest alkaline lake. By volume it is the fourth-largest salt lake, after the [[Caspian Sea]], [[Issyk-Kul]] and [[Lake Van]]. Three rivers, the Omo, Turkwel and Kerio, flow into it, but with no outlet its only water loss is [[Evaporation|evaporation]]. The level fell 10 m between 1975 and 1993. The Omo supplies most of its water, and the Gilgel Gibe III Dam in Ethiopia threatens the lake. Its water is too saline and fluoride-rich for drinking. Lake Turkana National Parks are a [[World Heritage Site]], and the surrounding area has yielded abundant hominid fossils, including remains of [[Homo habilis]] and [[Homo erectus]].
