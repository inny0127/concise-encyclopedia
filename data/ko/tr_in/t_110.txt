Large family of [[Flowering plant|flowering plants]], commonly called spurges or euphorbias. Ancient scholars attributed 95 plays to him, though the Suda says 92 at most. Nineteen attributed plays survive more or less complete, one of which, *Rhesus*, is often considered not genuinely his. Continent lying entirely in the Northern Hemisphere and mostly in the Eastern Hemisphere, forming the western part of Eurasia. It is bordered by the Arctic Ocean to the north, the Atlantic Ocean to the west, the Mediterranean Sea to the south, and Asia to the east. Europe is commonly considered to be separated from Asia by the watershed of the [[Ural Mountains]], the Ural River, the Caspian Sea, the Greater Caucasus, the Black Sea, and the Turkish straits. Islands are generally grouped with the nearest continental landmass, so Iceland is part of Europe while Greenland is usually assigned to North America.

Europe covers approximately 10,186,000 sq km, or 2% of Earth's surface (6.8% of its land area), making it the second-smallest continent in the seven-continent model. It is politically divided into about fifty sovereign states, of which Russia is the largest and most populous, spanning 39% of the continent and comprising 15% of its population. Europe had a total population of about 745 million, about 10% of the world population, in 2021, the third-largest after Asia and Africa. Warm Atlantic currents such as the [[Gulf Stream]] produce a temperate climate across much of the continent.

The name derives from Europa, a Phoenician princess of Greek mythology. One view derives it from Greek elements meaning "wide" and "eye" or "face"; Robert Beekes argued for a pre-Indo-European origin.

European culture consists of national and regional cultures that form central roots of Western civilization and commonly reference ancient Greece and ancient Rome. The fall of the [[Western Roman Empire]] in 476 CE and the Christian consolidation that followed the Migration Period marked the Middle Ages. The Italian [[Renaissance]] spread across many Western European countries. Since the [[Age of Discovery]], led by Spain and Portugal, Europe played a predominant role in global affairs, and between the 16th and 20th centuries European powers colonized the Americas, almost all of Africa and Oceania, and the majority of Asia. The [[Industrial Revolution]], which began in Great Britain at the end of the 18th century, brought radical economic, cultural, and social change.

Both world wars began and were fought largely in Europe, contributing to a decline in Western European dominance by the mid-20th century as the Soviet Union and the United States took prominence. The [[Cold War]] divided the continent along the Iron Curtain, with NATO in the West and the Warsaw Pact in the East. The divide ended with the Revolutions of 1989, the fall of the Berlin Wall, and the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991, which allowed European integration to advance. Integration has proceeded institutionally since the founding of the Council of Europe in 1949, and above all through the [[European Union]], which represents the majority of the continent. A majority of its members use the euro, and the Schengen Area has abolished internal border controls.

Large-scale colonization of the Americas by European powers, primarily between the late 15th century and early 19th century, during the [[Age of Discovery]]. The Norse had earlier colonized Greenland and created a short-lived settlement near the northern tip of Newfoundland c. 1000 AD.

Systematic colonization began in 1492, when a Spanish expedition led by [[Christopher Columbus]], seeking a new trade route to the Orient, landed in The Bahamas. Under the [[Treaty of Tordesillas]] of 1494, Portugal and Spain agreed to divide the world between them; Spanish claims covered essentially all the Americas, while Portugal received the eastern tip of South America, where it established Brazil in the early 1500s. Conflict between European empires and Indigenous nations remained a leading dynamic into the 1800s, and the United States pursued a settler colonial policy of [[Manifest destiny]] and Indian removal. By the 19th century, independence movements had reshaped much of the Americas into nation-states.

Because the most energetic [[Molecule|molecules]] leave, the remaining liquid cools, a process called evaporative cooling; this is why evaporating sweat cools the human body.
