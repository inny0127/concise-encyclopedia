Instrument that uses visible light and a system of [[Lens|lenses]] to produce magnified images of small objects, also called a light microscope. Microscopes of this kind are the oldest type, and the compound form appeared in the 17th century. [[Galileo Galilei]] developed an improved compound microscope in 1609. In a compound microscope, an objective lens forms a real image that an eyepiece magnifies, and most modern research microscopes are of this type. Specimens may be lit from below, or by light passing through (bright field) or around (dark field) the objective. Phase-contrast imaging increases contrast by highlighting small details of differing refractive index. Resolving power is typically limited to around 200 nanometers by the [[Diffraction|diffraction]] limit of visible light, so larger magnification reveals no more detail. Alternatives such as electron microscopes, which do not use visible light, can achieve much greater magnifications.

ENTRY:
Branch of physics that studies the behavior, manipulation, and detection of [[Electromagnetic radiation|electromagnetic radiation]], including its interactions with matter and with instruments that use or detect it. Optics usually describes visible, ultraviolet, and infrared light, and extends to radio waves, microwaves, and X-rays.

Geometric optics treats light as rays that travel in straight lines and bend when they pass through or reflect from surfaces. Physical optics adds wave effects such as [[Diffraction|diffraction]] and interference. The ray model was developed first, followed by the wave model; 19th-century progress in electromagnetic theory, notably by [[James Clerk Maxwell]], showed that light waves are electromagnetic radiation. Phenomena that depend on light's wave-like and particle-like properties require quantum mechanics, which models light as particles called [[Photon|photons]]. Optics is relevant to astronomy, engineering, photography, and medicine, and underlies mirrors, lenses, telescopes, microscopes, lasers, and fiber optics.

The earliest known lenses, of polished crystal, date from as early as 2000 BC from Crete. Greek philosophers split between the intromission theory of vision, held by Democritus, Epicurus, and Aristotle, and the emission theory articulated by Plato. [[Euclid]] linked vision to geometry in a treatise on optics. In 984 the Persian mathematician Ibn Sahl described a law of refraction equivalent to [[Snell's law]]. In the early 11th century [[Ibn al-Haytham]] wrote the *Book of Optics*, rejecting emission theories and proposing that light reflects in straight lines from every point of a viewed object into the eye. In the 17th century Isaac Newton proposed a particle model of light and [[Christiaan Huygens]] a wave explanation. Lasers were developed in 1960.

Form of human communication in which knowledge, art, beliefs, ideas, and culture are received, preserved, and transmitted orally from one generation to the next. Transmission is through speech or song and may include folktales, ballads, chants, prose, or poetry. The information is held by oral repositories, sometimes called "walking libraries," who are usually also performers, such as griots in West Africa. Societies use oral tradition to pass on oral history, oral literature, oral law, and other knowledge, either without a writing system or alongside one. It is the most widespread medium of human communication.

[[Folklore]] is one type of oral tradition. [[Buddhism]], [[Hinduism]], Catholicism, Yazidism, and [[Jainism]] have used it, in parallel to writing, to transmit canonical scriptures, rituals, hymns, and mythologies. African societies have broadly been labeled oral civilizations. In Africa the principal political, legal, social, and religious texts were transmitted orally, and when the Bamums in [[Cameroon]] invented a script, the first texts written down were the royal chronicle and the code of customary law. Most African courts had archivists who learned the royal genealogy by heart. Performance context shapes content: in Burundi traditions were short because they were told at informal gatherings where everyone took a turn, whereas in neighboring [[Rwanda]] a professional performer had to entertain a patron for a whole evening.

The fidelity of oral traditions varies. The Kyrgyz *Manas* cycle, exceeding 500,000 lines, has been recited across generations, yet traditions remain susceptible to telescoped timelines and conflations, so they serve as historical sources most reliably when corroborated by independent evidence.

The English word assassin derives from *hashshashin*, which is linked to hashish, although the association with hashish is contested.

=== KEY: Order_of_operations_303978
HEADWORD: Order of operations = 연산 순서
LEVEL: 4

  Exponentiation = 거듭제곱

  programming languages = 프로그래밍 언어
ENTRY:
Set of conventions determining which arithmetic operations to perform first. The rank of an operation is its precedence, and operations with higher precedence are performed first. The conventional order is parentheses, [[Exponentiation|exponentiation]], multiplication and division, then addition and subtraction. Operations of equal precedence are evaluated from left to right. Thus 1 + 2 × 3 equals 7, not 9. Exponents were introduced in the 16th and 17th centuries and given precedence over addition and multiplication, so 3 + 5² = 28 and 3 × 5² = 75. Parentheses override the convention, as in (2 + 3) × 4 = 20. Some calculators and [[Computer programming|programming languages]] adopt different conventions.

Method for estimating the unknown parameters in a [[Linear regression|linear regression]] model. OLS chooses parameters by minimizing the sum of the squared differences between observed values of the dependent variable and the values predicted by the linear function of the independent variables. ENTRY:
Specialized subunit within a [[Cell (biology)|cell]] that has a specific function. The name, from the diminutive suffix -elle, reflects the idea that organelles are parts of cells as [[Organ (biology)|organs]] are to the body. Membrane-bound organelles are enclosed by their own lipid bilayers, while non-membrane-bound ones are spatially distinct functional units. Eukaryotic cells contain many types, including the [[Endoplasmic reticulum|endoplasmic reticulum]], the [[Golgi apparatus|Golgi apparatus]], the nuclear envelope, [[Mitochondria|mitochondria]] and [[Plastid|plastids]]. [[Prokaryote|Prokaryotes]] lack eukaryotic organelles, although some have protein-shelled bacterial microcompartments that may be primitive organelles. Organelles are identified by microscopy and can be purified by cell fractionation.
