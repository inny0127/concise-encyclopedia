ENTRY:
Northernmost circle of latitude at which the Sun can be directly overhead. It lies at 23°26′08.9″ north of the [[Equator]], a position set by Earth's [[Axial tilt|axial tilt]] that currently drifts south by about 15 meters per year. The Sun reaches overhead here at the June solstice, when the Northern Hemisphere is tilted toward it to its maximum extent. At this latitude, at solar midnight on the December solstice, the Sun is 90 degrees below the horizon in all directions. The name, from the Latin for crab, dates to the last centuries BC, when the Sun was in the zodiacal constellation Cancer at the June solstice; precession has since moved that alignment. The line marks the southern limit of the mutual defense obligation of [[NATO]], since member states are not obligated to defend territory south of it. Much land on or near the line is part of the [[Sahara]], where summer temperatures often reach 45 °C (113 °F).

Southernmost circle of latitude at which the Sun can be directly overhead. It is the southern of the two [[Tropics|tropics]], lying at 23°26′08.9″ south of the [[Equator]] and marking the dividing line between the Southern Temperate Zone and the tropics. The Sun is directly overhead here at the December solstice. The line is drifting northward by about 15 meters per year as [[Axial tilt|Earth's axial tilt]] changes. It passes through [[Chile]], Argentina, Paraguay, [[Brazil]], Namibia, Botswana, [[South Africa]], Mozambique, [[Madagascar]] and Australia. Daylight lasts about 13 hours and 35 minutes at the December solstice and about 10 hours and 41 minutes at the June solstice. The name recalls the zodiacal constellation Capricorn, where the Sun stood at the northern solstice when the line was named; precession has since moved that alignment.

ENTRY:
Major climate group in the [[Köppen climate classification|Köppen]] system, marked by the letter A. Tropical climates have a monthly average temperature of 18 °C (64 °F) or higher in the coolest month, with hot temperatures and high [[Humidity|humidity]] all year. [[Precipitation|Annual precipitation]] is often abundant, and there are normally only two seasons, a wet (rainy or [[Monsoon|monsoon]]) season and a dry season. The annual temperature range is very small, and sunlight is intense. The three subtypes are tropical rainforest (Af), tropical monsoon (Am) and tropical savanna (Aw or As), distinguished by the precipitation of the driest month. Tropical rainforest climates usually extend only 5–10 degrees north and south of the [[Equator]], with yearly average temperatures between 21 and 30 °C (70 and 86 °F).

ENTRY:
Rapidly rotating storm system with a low-pressure center, strong winds and a spiral arrangement of thunderstorms. Depending on location, it is called a hurricane in the [[Atlantic Ocean]] or northeastern Pacific, a [[Typhoon|typhoon]] in the northwestern Pacific, or a tropical cyclone in the Indian Ocean and South Pacific. About 80 to 90 named tropical cyclones form each year, and over half develop winds of 65 kn (120 km/h; 75 mph) or more. They form over large bodies of warm water and draw energy from evaporation, unlike mid-latitude storms powered by temperature contrasts. They rarely form within 5° of the [[Equator]]. Coastal damage comes from strong winds, high waves, [[Storm|storm surges]] and tornadoes. Extremely heavy rain can reach 40 km (25 mi) from the coastline, causing [[Flood|flooding]]. [[Climate change]] can increase the duration, occurrence and intensity of these storms.

ENTRY:
Dense, warm forest with high rainfall, typically found within 10° north and south of the [[Equator]]. It is a type of tropical moist broadleaf forest, and true rainforests have no dry season, with at least 60 mm (2.4 in) of average precipitation in every month. Temperatures exceed 18 °C (64 °F) every month. Rainforests are home to 40–75% of all species globally, including half of the world's animal and plant species and two-thirds of flowering plant species. Described as the "world's largest pharmacy," they hold over a quarter of natural medicines discovered to date. Soils are typically nutrient-poor, and the forest is stratified into emergent, canopy, understory and forest-floor layers. Logging and agricultural expansion threaten rainforests through [[Deforestation|deforestation]] and habitat fragmentation, and REDD and REDD+ programs aim to curb forest loss.

ENTRY:
Region of Earth surrounding the [[Equator]], where the Sun may shine directly overhead. The tropics receive the most solar energy over the year and have the highest temperatures on the planet, but the lowest seasonal temperature variation, so seasons are more often divided by precipitation than by temperature. Their width in latitude is twice Earth's [[Axial tilt|axial tilt]], bounded by the [[Tropic of Cancer]] and the [[Tropic of Capricorn]]. The tropics cover 39.8% of Earth's surface and contain 36% of its landmass. As of 2014 they were home to 40% of the world's population, a share projected to reach 50% by 2050. Climates within them include [[Rainforest|rain forests]], monsoons, [[Savanna|savannahs]], deserts and high-altitude mountains. Global warming is expanding tropical weather conditions into subtropical areas, with more heatwaves and intense storms.

Conformity to reality or fact. Its precise definition is contested. The correspondence theory holds that a statement is true if it corresponds to facts. According to the coherence theory, a belief is true if it is consistent with, and supported by, other beliefs. Most Romance languages name the day after Mars, from Latin *dies Martis*. In Japanese it is *kayōbi*, from the planet Mars. In the Eastern Orthodox Church, Tuesdays are dedicated to [[John the Baptist|Saint John the Baptist]].

ENTRY:
Rock consolidated from volcanic ash ejected during an eruption. Rock containing more than 75% ash is classified as tuff, while rock with 25–75% ash is called tuffaceous. The ash lithifies into solid rock after deposition. Despite its softness, tuff has been used for construction since ancient times; the Romans used it often because it is common in Italy. The Rapa Nui people used it to make most of the [[Moai|moai]] statues on [[Easter Island]]. Tuff can be classified as either [[Igneous rock|igneous]] or [[Sedimentary rock|sedimentary]] rock, and it is usually studied in igneous petrology. Tuff is often erroneously called tufa in guidebooks and television programs, but tufa is a form of travertine.

Two-day massacre by white mobs of Black residents of the Greenwood district of [[Tulsa, Oklahoma|Tulsa]], on May 31 and June 1, 1921. The mobs burned and destroyed more than 35 square blocks of Greenwood, then one of the wealthiest Black communities in the United States. The Oklahoma Bureau of Vital Statistics recorded 36 deaths, while a 2001 commission reported estimates ranging from 39 to around 300. About 10,000 people were left homeless. A state commission, authorized in 1997, recommended reparations in its 2001 report, and a memorial park was dedicated in 2010. Oklahoma schools have been required to teach about the massacre since 2002.
