ENTRY:
Self-written account of one's own life, narrated from the author's perspective. The term was first used in 1797, but the practice dates to antiquity. Unlike [[Biography|biographies]], which are written by someone else, autobiographies rest on the author's memory and interpretation of events, so they may contain inaccuracies or embellishments. Early examples include Saint Augustine's *Confessions* (c. 400), often considered one of the first Western autobiographies. *The Book of Margery Kempe* (c. 1430s) is considered the first autobiography in English. Forms include memoirs, which focus on specific periods or themes, spiritual autobiographies and fictional first-person autobiographies.

Disease resulting from an anomalous response of the adaptive [[Immune system|immune system]], which mistakenly attacks healthy, functioning parts of the body. Unlike autoinflammatory diseases, which involve the innate immune system, they stem from a malfunction of the adaptive system. Symptoms vary but typically include low-grade fever, fatigue and general malaise. One of the four temperate [[Season|seasons]] on [[Earth]], known as fall in North American English. Outside the tropics, autumn marks the transition from summer to winter, beginning in September in the Northern Hemisphere and in March in the Southern Hemisphere. Day length decreases and temperatures cool until the [[Winter solstice]] in December (Northern Hemisphere) and June (Southern Hemisphere). In temperate climates its main feature is the change in color of deciduous trees’ leaves as they prepare to shed. Definitions vary: meteorologists use calendar months, September to November in the Northern Hemisphere, while traditional English-speaking usage ran from Lammas Day to around [[Halloween]]. ENTRY:
Rapid flow of [[Snow|snow]] down a slope, such as a hill or mountain. Avalanches may be triggered spontaneously by increased precipitation or weakening of the snowpack, or by people, animals or [[Earthquake|earthquakes]]. Large avalanches can capture and move ice, rocks and [[Tree|trees]]. Slab avalanches, in which tightly packed snow breaks along a weak layer, are the most hazardous type, while loose-snow avalanches start at a point and gather more snow as they descend. Fast-moving avalanches can mix with air to form powder snow avalanches. Asphyxia from burial causes most fatalities, followed by trauma and [[Hypothermia]]. Mitigation relies on snowpack assessment, forecasting and equipment such as transceivers, probes and shovels, which have improved survival rates when used effectively. Contrary to popular belief, loud sound does not trigger avalanches.

Persian polymath, physician, and philosopher of the Islamic world, known in Arabic as Ibn Sina. Avicenna was born in the village of Afshana, near the Samanid capital of Bukhara, to a Persian family; his father, a Samanid official, came from Balkh in [[Bactria]]. A non-logical axiom in mathematics makes a substantive assertion about a domain, such as a + 0 = a in [[Integer|integer]] arithmetic, and is also called a postulate or assumption. Long-fingered [[Lemur|lemur]] native to [[Madagascar]]. It is the world's largest nocturnal primate and the only living member of the genus *Daubentonia*. [[Alternative medicine]] system with historical roots in the [[Indian subcontinent]]. Ayurveda is heavily practiced in India and Nepal, where as much as 80% of the population report using it. Its name comes from [[Sanskrit]] and means knowledge of longevity. Therapies include herbal medicines, special diets, [[Meditation|meditation]], [[Yoga|yoga]], massage, laxatives, enemas and medical oils. Classical texts, dating in their present form to the first centuries CE, describe three doshas (vāta, pitta and kapha) whose balance is held to produce health, and they also describe surgical techniques such as cataract surgery and rhinoplasty. The theory and practice of Ayurveda are considered pseudoscientific. Many preparations, particularly in the rasashastra tradition, contain toxic levels of lead, mercury and [[Arsenic]]. There is no evidence that cancer can be treated or cured through Ayurveda.
