English [[Mountaineering|mountaineer]] who took part in the first three British expeditions to [[Mount Everest|Everest]] in the early 1920s. English civil and mechanical engineer, known as the "Father of Railways." Stephenson devised a miner's safety lamp by trial and error, working on the same problem as the scientist [[Humphry Davy]]. His chosen rail gauge, sometimes called the "Stephenson gauge," was the basis for the 4-foot-8+1⁄2-inch (1.435 m) standard gauge used by most of the world's railways. The *Locomotion No. 1*, built by the company of Stephenson and his son Robert, was the first [[Steam locomotive|steam locomotive]] to carry passengers on a public rail line, the Stockton and Darlington Railway, in 1825. He also built the Liverpool and Manchester Railway, opened in 1830, the first public inter-city railway in the world to use locomotives. Rail transport, which he pioneered, was a key component of the [[Industrial Revolution]]. He also advised on roadway construction, bridge design and locomotive manufacture.

Appointed commander-in-chief in 1775, he directed a poorly organized and equipped force against disciplined British troops. He won at Trenton in late 1776 and Princeton in early 1777, but lost at Brandywine and Germantown. In 1781 he led a combined French and American force to a decisive victory at [[Siege of Yorktown|Yorktown]], and in the [[Treaty of Paris (1783)|Treaty of Paris]] of 1783 Britain acknowledged American independence. He presided over the Constitutional Convention in 1787, which drafted the [[Constitution of the United States]].

Washington was unanimously elected president by the Electoral College in 1789 and 1792. He established a strong, well-financed national government and remained impartial in the cabinet rivalry between [[Thomas Jefferson]] and [[Alexander Hamilton]]. During the [[French Revolution]] he adopted a policy of neutrality. He set precedents for the presidency, including a peaceful transfer of power, the title "Mr. President", and the two-term tradition, and his 1796 farewell address warned of the dangers that regionalism, partisanship, and foreign influence pose to national unity.

A planter of tobacco and wheat at Mount Vernon, he owned slaves. He began opposing slavery near the end of his life, and his will provided for the eventual manumission of his slaves. His namesakes include the national capital and the State of Washington, and in popular and scholarly polls he is consistently ranked among the greatest American presidents.

After brief independence following the Russian Revolution, the Red Army annexed Georgia in 1921, and it became a republic of the [[Soviet Union]] until seceding in April 1991. After the 2003 Rose Revolution, Georgia pursued integration with the [[European Union]] and [[NATO]], worsening relations with Russia and culminating in the [[Russo-Georgian War]] of 2008. It is a unitary parliamentary republic and became the second country to legalize cannabis in 2018.

Circular orbit above Earth's equator, following the direction of Earth's rotation. A satellite in it has an orbital period equal to Earth's rotational period, one sidereal day, so it appears motionless to ground observers. The orbit lies at an altitude of 35,786 km (22,236 mi) and is a special case of [[Geosynchronous orbit|geosynchronous orbit]]. The concept was popularized by [[Arthur C. Clarke]] in a 1945 paper as a way to revolutionize telecommunications. [[Communications satellite|Communications satellites]] use it so that ground antennas can point permanently at them without moving, and weather and navigation satellites also operate there. Satellites are launched into a temporary orbit and placed in a "slot" above a fixed point on Earth's surface, then require periodic station-keeping. Retired satellites are moved to a higher graveyard orbit to avoid collisions.

ENTRY:
Earth-centered orbit with a period matching Earth's rotation. Its period is one sidereal day, 23 hours, 56 minutes and 4 seconds, so an observer on the surface sees the object return to the same position in the sky each day. Its path may stay still or trace a figure-eight, depending on inclination and eccentricity. A circular geosynchronous orbit has a constant altitude of 35,786 km (22,236 mi). The [[Geostationary orbit|geostationary orbit]] is a special case, circular and in the equatorial plane, with both inclination and eccentricity equal to 0. Communications satellites are often placed in geostationary or near-geostationary orbits so that their antennas need not move. In a 1945 paper, [[Arthur C. Clarke]] popularized the concept as a means of relaying communications; the collection of satellites in this orbit is called the Clarke Belt.

Medical specialty focused on the health needs of older adults. Geriatric medicine aims to promote health by preventing, diagnosing and treating disease in older people, who are more likely to have chronic conditions and need more medical care. The term derives from the Greek words "old man" and "healer". Common diseases may present atypically in older patients: [[Pneumonia|pneumonia]] may cause low-grade fever and confusion rather than high fever and cough. Geriatric care is interdisciplinary, drawing on [[Medicine|medicine]], [[Nursing|nursing]], pharmacy, social work, and physical and occupational therapy, and covers medication management, memory care, rehabilitation and long-term care. Care is guided by patient goals and preferences rather than a set age. Geriatrics is sometimes called medical gerontology, but it is distinct from gerontology, the multidisciplinary study of [[Ageing|aging]].
