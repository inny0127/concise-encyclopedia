Artificial process converting salt water, generally seawater, to fresh water for human consumption or irrigation. Along with recycled wastewater, desalination is one of the few water resources independent of rainfall, but it is generally more costly than surface water, groundwater or water recycling because of its energy use. Processes are thermal, as in [[Distillation|distillation]], or membrane-based, as in [[Reverse osmosis|reverse osmosis]], and both produce [[Brine|brine]] as a by-product. In 2018 the energy intensity of desalination was about 3 kWh/m³, improved by a factor of 10 from 20–30 kWh/m³ in 1970.

ENTRY:
Landscape receiving little precipitation, and therefore supporting distinctive biomes and ecosystems. About one-third of Earth's land surface is arid or semi-arid. Deserts generally receive less than 250 mm (10 in) of precipitation a year. The category includes polar regions, sometimes called "cold deserts"; [[Antarctica]], about 98% thick continental ice sheet and 2% barren rock, is the world's largest.

[[Weathering]] forms deserts as large day–night temperature swings strain rocks until they break. Occasional downpours cause [[Flash flood|flash floods]], and wind carries sand and dust, sometimes forming sand or dust storms, and abrades surfaces. Sand ends up as level sheets or high [[Dune|dunes]]; other deserts are stony plains whose surfaces form a mosaic of smooth stones known as desert pavement. Temporary lakes and salt pans form where waters evaporate, and oases occur where springs or [[Aquifer|aquifers]] supply water. Deserts reflect more incoming light than forests or the sea, which moderates Earth's temperature.

Plants are tough and wiry, with small or no leaves, water-resistant cuticles, and often spines; some annuals germinate, bloom, and die within weeks of rain, while long-lived plants have deep roots. Many animals are nocturnal, shelter in shade or underground, conserve water, and may lie dormant for long periods before breeding rapidly after rain.

Nomads move flocks to available grazing, and oases support settled life. Cultivating semi-arid regions encourages erosion and desertification, but [[Irrigation|irrigation]] allows farming, as in California's Imperial Valley. Camel caravans carried salt, gold, ivory, and other goods along trade routes across the [[Sahara]], and large numbers of slaves were also taken north.solar energy]].|solar energy]].

The word comes from ecclesiastical Latin *dēsertum*, "an abandoned place."

ENTRY:
Concept or proposal for an object, process, or system, intentionally created by a thinking agent. A design is expected to have a purpose within a specific context, typically satisfying goals and constraints while accounting for aesthetic, functional, and experiential considerations. Traditional examples are architectural and engineering drawings, circuit diagrams, sewing patterns, and less tangible artifacts such as business process models.

People who produce designs are designers, a term usually qualified by field, such as fashion, product, web, or interior designer. A designer's sequence of activities is the design process, which can be a quick sketch or a lengthy effort involving research, negotiation, modeling, and re-design. Designing is not confined to professionals: Herbert A. Simon wrote that "Everyone designs who devises courses of action aimed at changing existing situations into preferred ones." Design researcher Nigel Cross likewise held that design ability is embedded in the brain as a natural cognitive function.

Historians differ on where design history begins. John Heskett and others look to the [[Industrial Revolution]] and mass production, while others begin with prehistoric artifacts. The discipline of design history coalesced in the 1970s. The [[Bauhaus]], founded in 1919, greatly influenced modern design education.

Researchers disagree about how designers work. The rational model, developed by Simon and by Gerhard Pahl and Wolfgang Beitz, treats design as plan-driven optimization for known constraints through discrete stages. The alternative view, called reflection-in-action or coevolution, treats design as an interactive process; the two views have been linked to [[Positivism|positivism]] and constructionism respectively.

ENTRY:
Scalar value computed from the entries of a square [[Matrix (mathematics)|matrix]]. A square matrix is invertible if and only if its determinant is nonzero, and the determinant of a product of matrices is the product of their determinants. For real linear transformations, the determinant gives the factor by which volume changes, and its sign shows whether orientation is preserved. Determinants can be computed efficiently by [[Gaussian elimination]], and Cramer's rule expresses the solutions of linear systems in terms of determinants. The concept emerged in the late 17th century with [[Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz]], who studied values associated with arrays of numbers for solving equations. The characteristic polynomial, whose roots are the eigenvalues of a matrix, is defined as a determinant.

ENTRY:
Metaphysical view that all events in the [[Universe|universe]] can occur only in one possible way. Determinism is often contrasted with [[Free will|free will]], although some philosophers argue that the two are compatible. Causal determinism holds that every event results from preceding events and the laws of nature. The antonym is indeterminism, the view that events are not deterministically caused. The idea appears in ancient Western thought and in Eastern philosophy, including the doctrine of [[Karma|karma]]. In 1814 [[Pierre-Simon Laplace]] described a hypothetical intellect that could compute the whole course of the cosmos from a complete snapshot of its particles and forces. Quantum mechanics has been interpreted both as introducing fundamental indeterminism and in deterministic ways, while [[Chaos theory|chaos theory]] complicates prediction without denying causality.

Stable [[Hydrogen|hydrogen]] isotope with one neutron, also called heavy hydrogen. Its name comes from the Greek *deuteros*, "second." American chemist [[Harold Urey]] discovered it in 1931, and the discovery won him a Nobel Prize in 1934. Nearly all deuterium found in nature was synthesized in the [[Big Bang]] 13.8 billion years ago. ENTRY:
Droplets of water that form on exposed objects in the morning or evening. As an exposed surface cools by radiating its heat, atmospheric moisture condenses faster than it can evaporate, forming droplets through [[Condensation|condensation]]. Dew forms most easily on surfaces not warmed by heat from deep ground, such as grass, leaves, railings and car roofs. Cooling is strongest on clear nights, when a surface loses more energy by [[Infrared|infrared]] radiation than it receives from the [[Sun]]. Calm nights favor dew, because wind carries warmer air to cold surfaces. Dew is related to the [[Dew point|dew point]], and when temperatures are low enough it freezes into [[Frost|frost]]. Measured dew rarely exceeds 0.5 mm per night. In regions with long dry seasons, plants such as lichen and pine seedlings benefit from it.

Capital and largest city of [[Bangladesh]], on the Ganges Delta, with an estimated population of 36.6 million. Dhaka is the second largest city in the world by population and is widely considered the most densely populated built-up urban area. It was the capital of Mughal Bengal for 75 years (1608–39 and 1660–1704) and a hub of the muslin trade in the [[Mughal Empire|Mughal]] period. Under [[British Raj|British rule]] it gained electricity, railways and a modern water supply. It became the capital of an independent Bangladesh in 1971. The city accounts for 35% of Bangladesh's economy, and the Dhaka Stock Exchange has over 750 listed companies. Its Jamdani sari, rickshaw art and Bengali New Year parade have been recognized by [[UNESCO]] as intangible cultural heritage.

Collection of sayings of [[The Buddha|the Buddha]] in verse, one of the most widely read and best known Buddhist scriptures. The original version is in the Khuddaka Nikāya of the Pāli Canon of [[Theravada|Theravāda]] Buddhism. Its title combines [[Dharma|dhamma]], meaning teaching or doctrine, and pada, meaning path or verse. According to tradition, each saying was spoken on a separate occasion, and a 4th or 5th century commentary attributed to Buddhaghosa links the verses to 305 stories. The Pāli text has 423 verses in 26 chapters. Versions in [[Sanskrit]], Gāndhārī and Tibetan, and several Chinese translations, also survive. Brough (2001) found that the Pāli, Gāndhārī and Udānavarga texts have 330 to 340 verses in common.

ENTRY:
Key concept in Indian-origin religions, with multiple meanings. From the [[Sanskrit]] root dhr-, meaning to hold or to support, it denotes the law that sustains things, from one's life to society and the universe. In [[Hinduism]], it covers duties, laws and conduct in accord with ṛta, the order that makes life and the universe possible. In [[Buddhism]], it refers to the teachings of [[The Buddha|the Buddha]] and to the true nature of reality. In [[Jainism]], it refers to the teachings of the Tirthankara. In [[Sikhism]], it indicates the path of righteousness. In the 3rd century BCE, the Mauryan emperor [[Ashoka]] translated the word into Greek and Aramaic.
