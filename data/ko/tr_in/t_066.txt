ENTRY:
Class of jawed fish with skeletons primarily composed of [[Cartilage|cartilage]] rather than bone, distinguishing them from the [[Osteichthyes|bony fishes]]. Chondrichthyes are aquatic vertebrates with paired fins, placoid scales and a conus arteriosus in the heart. They breathe through five to seven pairs of [[Gill|gills]], and because they lack bone marrow, they produce red blood cells in the [[Spleen|spleen]]. The class has two subclasses: Elasmobranchii, including [[Shark|sharks]], rays, skates and sawfish, and Holocephali, the chimaeras. Living members range from the 10 cm finless sleeper ray to the [[Whale shark|whale shark]], over 10 m long. Elasmobranchs alone account for over 1,200 species.

Simultaneous sounding of two or more musical notes. The most common chord has three notes and is called a triad. An arpeggio sounds the notes of a chord separately, and a series of chords is called a chord progression. The word derives from accord, which became cord in [[Middle English|Middle English]] and was respelled chord in the 17th century. Until the [[Middle Ages|Middle Ages]], harmony meant any combination of two notes; in the [[Renaissance|Renaissance]], three simultaneous notes came to define it. By the 13th century, popular carols in vernacular languages had developed in France, Germany, and Italy, particularly under the influence of [[Francis of Assisi]]. Carols were originally communal festival songs and were adopted for church use later. [[Martin Luther]] promoted congregational singing after the Protestant Reformation, and carols were sung privately during the Puritan ban on Christmas in England. The 19th-century revival included "God Rest Ye Merry, Gentlemen" and "Hark! The Herald Angels Sing," which appear in William Sandys' 1833 collection.

Columbus went to sea young, traveled widely, and was based for several years in [[Lisbon]]. He planned a western sea passage to the East Indies, hoping to profit from the spice trade. After the Granada War and his persistent lobbying, Queen [[Isabella I of Castile|Isabella I]] and King [[Ferdinand II of Aragon|Ferdinand II]] agreed to sponsor him. He left Palos in August 1492 with three ships and made landfall on 12 October on an island in [[The Bahamas]]. As colonial governor, Columbus was accused by some contemporaries of significant brutality; he was arrested and removed from Hispaniola in 1500 and later fought litigation over the privileges he claimed from the Crown. His voyages began the transfer of plants, animals, diseases, people, and ideas between the Old and New Worlds known as the [[Columbian exchange]]. Diseases, warfare, enslavement, and other mistreatment contributed to the decline of Hispaniola's American Indians. Growing awareness of these actions has brought increased scrutiny of a figure long idealized as a heroic discoverer.
