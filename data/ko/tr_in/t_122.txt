German-language Jewish writer from [[Prague]]. His works fuse realism and the fantastic and typically feature isolated protagonists facing bizarre or incomprehensible bureaucratic powers. Intentional deception to deprive a victim of a legal right or to gain from a victim unlawfully or unfairly. Fraud can be a civil wrong, in which a victim may sue to stop the fraud or recover money, or a [[Crime|criminal]] offense, for which the perpetrator may be prosecuted and imprisoned. In common law jurisdictions, civil fraud is a [[Tort|tort]] whose elements generally include an intentional misrepresentation or concealment of an important fact on which the victim relies to their detriment. Capacity of agents to choose between possible courses of action, to exercise the control over their actions that moral responsibility requires, or to be the ultimate source of those actions. The central debate concerns whether and how such freedom can coexist with physical determinism, divine foreknowledge, and other constraints. Free will is closely linked to moral responsibility, praise, blame, and deliberation, since traditionally only freely willed actions are seen as deserving credit or blame.

The problem was identified in ancient Greek literature. A compatibilist notion of free will has been attributed to [[Aristotle]] and to Epictetus; according to Susanne Bobzien, an incompatibilist notion appears first in the works of Alexander of Aphrodisias in the 3rd century AD. The term *liberum arbitrium* was introduced by Christian philosophy in the 4th century AD and meant a lack of necessity in the human will.

[[Determinism]] holds that the natural world is governed by cause and effect, so that only one course of events is possible. Incompatibilists regard free will as inconsistent with it. Metaphysical libertarians argue that determinism is false and free will therefore possible; hard determinists argue that determinism is true and free will therefore impossible. Hard incompatibilists, or illusionists, hold that indeterminism is equally incompatible with free will.

Compatibilists hold that free will can coexist with determinism, though they define it differently. Classical compatibilists treated it as freedom of action: a person is free if, had they wanted to do otherwise, they could have done so without physical impediment. Many contemporary compatibilists treat it as a psychological capacity to direct one's behavior in a way that is responsive to reason.

Romance language of the Indo-European family, spoken natively in France and in parts of Canada, Belgium, Switzerland, Luxembourg, and Monaco. French and its closest relatives, the langues d'oïl, historically spoken in northern France and southern Belgium, descended from the Vulgar Latin of the [[Roman Empire]]. Celtic languages of northern Roman Gaul and the Germanic Frankish language of the post-Roman invaders influenced it, and it largely supplanted regional [[Romance languages|Romance]] varieties across France, Belgium, and Switzerland.

Latin was gradually adopted in Gaul under Roman rule, and the local variety evolved into the Gallo-Romance tongues. Gaulish likely survived into the 6th century and left loanwords and calques, including *oui*, "yes"; the *Petit Robert* attributes 154 French words to it. The Ordinance of Villers-Cotterêts of 1539 mandated French in legal documents, and the Académie Française was established in 1635.

French is estimated to have about 310 million speakers, about 74 million of them native. It is the 22nd most natively spoken language, the sixth most spoken overall, and among the five most studied. It is an official language in 26 countries, and 54 states belong to the Organisation internationale de la Francophonie. In 2025 approximately 50% of the Francophone population lived in sub-Saharan Africa and the Indian Ocean, 30% in Europe, 15% in North Africa, and 7% in the Americas. Colonialism carried it to the Americas, Africa, and Asia, giving rise to creoles such as Haitian Creole.

French has a long history as an international language of literature, diplomacy, and science. It is a primary or second language of many organizations, including the [[United Nations]], the [[European Union]], NATO, and the International Olympic Committee, and it is the second most widely spoken mother tongue in the European Union.
