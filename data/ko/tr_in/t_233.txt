ENTRY:
Genre of [[Folklore|folklore]] consisting primarily of narratives that play a fundamental role in a society. For scholars this differs from the ordinary sense of the term, a belief that is not true: the veracity of a piece of folklore is irrelevant to whether it constitutes a myth.

Myths are often endorsed by religious and secular authorities and may be natural or supernatural in character. [[Creation myth|Creation myths]] take place in a primordial age before the world achieved its later form, origin myths explain how a society's customs, institutions, and taboos were established and sanctified, and national myths symbolize a nation's values. The relationship between the recital of myths and the enactment of [[Ritual|rituals]] is complex.

Main characters are usually gods, demigods, and other supernatural figures; stories of ordinary humans are usually classed as [[Legend|legends]]. Myths are sometimes distinguished from legends in that they deal with gods, usually have no historical basis, and are set in a remote past very different from the present. As faiths change, myths may come to be considered folktales. Definitions vary among scholars. The Finnish folklorist Lauri Honko describes a myth as a religious account of the world's beginning and the exemplary deeds of the gods, which expresses a society's religious values and provides patterns of behavior to imitate.

The word comes from [[Ancient Greek]] *mythos*, meaning "speech", "narrative", or "fiction". [[Plato]] used *mythología* as a general term for fiction or story-telling of any kind. English adopted *mythology* in the 15th century, first attested in John Lydgate's *Troy Book* (c. 1425), and *myth* itself appeared only in 1830.

Narratives, beliefs and sacred oral traditions of the [[Indigenous peoples of the Americas]], which differ widely by region. North American myths commonly feature a close relation to nature and animals, a belief in a Great Spirit, and [[Creation myth|creation stories]]. Animals often shape-shift, and marriages between humans and bears are common. Tricksters such as [[Coyote (mythology)|Coyote]] and the Plains figure Old Man provide humor and moral lessons. In the Andes, the Inca creator god Viracocha shaped humanity from stone at Lake Titicaca.
