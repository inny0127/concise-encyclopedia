ENTRY:
Voltage produced across a conductor, transverse to a current flowing in it and to a magnetic field applied perpendicular to the current. Edwin Hall discovered the effect in 1879 while working on his doctoral degree at [[Johns Hopkins University|Johns Hopkins University]] in [[Baltimore|Baltimore]], [[Maryland|Maryland]]. Eighteen years before the electron was discovered, his measurements confirmed [[James Clerk Maxwell|James Clerk Maxwell's]] electromagnetic theory. Charge carriers moving in a magnetic field experience the [[Lorentz force|Lorentz force]], which pushes them to one face of the conductor. The resulting electric field opposes further migration of charge, so a steady voltage is established.

ENTRY:
Short-period comet visible to the naked eye from Earth, officially designated 1P/Halley. It returns every 74 to 79 years, last appearing in 1986 and next due in mid-2061. Its returns were recorded from at least 240 BC, but in 1705 the English astronomer [[Edmond Halley|Edmond Halley]] used [[Isaac Newton|Isaac Newton]]'s laws of gravity to show that several sightings were of the same comet. In 1986 the [[European Space Agency|European Space Agency]]'s [[Giotto|Giotto]] mission became the first spacecraft to observe a comet in detail. Its results supported Fred Whipple's "dirty snowball" model, which predicted that the comet would be a mixture of volatile ices and dust. They also showed that the surface is largely dusty, non-volatile material, with only a small portion of it icy.

Sensory perception in the absence of an external stimulus, with a compelling sense of reality. Hallucinations can occur in any sense, and auditory hallucinations are the most common type; they are very common in [[Schizophrenia|schizophrenia]]. They differ from illusions, which distort a real stimulus, and from mental imagery, which is under voluntary control. Hypnagogic hallucinations, which occur as one falls asleep, and hypnopompic hallucinations, which occur on waking, are considered normal. The English word was introduced in 1646 by the physician Sir Thomas Browne, from the Latin *alucinari*, "to wander in the mind."

Optical phenomenon of rings, arcs and spots of light in the sky, produced by ice crystals. Halos form when light, typically from the [[Sun|Sun]] or [[Moon|Moon]], is refracted and reflected by ice crystals in cirrus or cirrostratus clouds, or in diamond dust near the ground. Organic compound derived from an alkane in which one or more hydrogen atoms are replaced by halogens. Haloalkanes, also called alkyl halides, have the general formula RX, where R is an alkyl group and X is [[Fluorine|fluorine]], [[Chlorine|chlorine]], [[Bromine|bromine]] or [[Iodine|iodine]]. They serve as flame retardants, refrigerants, propellants, solvents and pharmaceuticals. Chlorofluorocarbons, a widely used class, were shown to lead to [[Ozone depletion|ozone depletion]], and haloalkanes containing chlorine, bromine or iodine threaten the ozone layer. Chloro-, bromo- and iodoalkanes have higher melting and boiling points than their parent alkanes. Haloalkanes were known for centuries; chloroethane was produced in the 15th century, and systematic synthesis developed in the 19th century. Substantial amounts are biogenic, produced by living organisms.

Group of chemically related elements in group 17 of the periodic table. ENTRY:
Decision problem in computability theory. It asks whether a given computer program, run on a given input, will eventually halt or will continue to run forever. [[Alan Turing|Alan Turing]] proved in 1937 that no general [[Algorithm|algorithm]] can solve the problem for all program–input pairs, making it undecidable. The proof constructs a "pathological" program that does the opposite of whatever a supposed halting decider predicts about it. Specific programs can often be shown to halt, and tools such as the T2 Temporal Prover have established that certain device-driver routines always return.

Handled bag used to carry personal items. Also called a purse in North American English and a pocketbook in parts of the U.S., it was carried in antiquity: Egyptian burial sites (c. 2686–2160 BC) have yielded [[Leather|leather]] bags with two straps. The [[Ancient Greece|ancient Greeks]] used leather, papyrus and linen purses called *byrsa*, the origin of the English word "purse". The modern handbag took shape in England during the [[Industrial Revolution]], partly through rail travel.

Indoor team sport played by two teams of seven players each, six outfield players and a goalkeeper. Item production entirely by hand or with simple tools. Handicraft covers creative work with textiles, clay, wood, metal, paper and plant fibers, usually in traditional techniques that produce items both practical and aesthetic. Handicraft industries make goods for local needs without machines, and the individual artisanship of each item is the key criterion, so mass-produced goods do not qualify. Many crafts have been practiced for centuries. One of the oldest is Dhokra, a metal casting practiced in [[India]] for over 5,000 years. Handicrafts often carry cultural or religious meaning and may carry political messages, as in craftivism. The [[Arts and Crafts movement]], a late-19th-century design reform and social movement, drew on the belief that pre-industrial people found fulfillment through handicrafts. The [[Industrial Revolution]] and increasing [[Mechanization|mechanization]] reduced the roles of professional craftspeople, and many handicrafts are now practiced as hobbies or folk art.

ENTRY:
Alphabetic writing system of the Korean language. Called Chosŏn'gŭl in North Korea and Hangeul in South Korea, the script was created under [[Sejong the Great]] of the [[Joseon]] dynasty and published in 1446 in the text *Hunminjeongeum*. Korea had used [[Chinese characters|Hanja]] since antiquity, but Hanja was poorly suited to Korean, and its difficulty contributed to widespread illiteracy. The elite looked down on Hangul for centuries, and it gained wide acceptance only in the late 19th century. It is now the predominant script for Korean in both Koreas and among the Korean diaspora. Modern orthographies use 24 basic letters called jamo, 14 consonants and 10 vowels. Combinations yield 27 more letters, for 51 in total, arranged in syllable blocks. Linguists and historians have praised the script.
