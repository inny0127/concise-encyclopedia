Salt-cured [[Pork|pork]] made from the belly or less fatty parts of the back. ENTRY:
Domain of mostly single-celled prokaryotic microorganisms, typically a few micrometres long. Bacteria were among the first life forms on Earth, appearing about 4 billion years ago, and for about 3 billion years they and [[Archaea]] were the dominant forms of life. They inhabit nearly every habitat, including air, [[Soil]], water, acidic [[Hot spring|hot springs]], radioactive waste, and the deep biosphere of [[Earth's crust]].

Bacterial cells have no nucleus and rarely contain membrane-bound organelles; their DNA usually forms a circular chromosome. Most reproduce by binary fission. Shapes include spheres, rods, and spirals, and cell-wall structure divides them into Gram-positive and Gram-negative types. The word comes from the Greek for staff or cane, because the first ones discovered were rod-shaped. Once classed as plants, bacteria were recognized in the 1990s as one of two very different prokaryotic groups, the other being archaea. Although an estimated 43,000 species have been named, most have never been studied; 90% of research focuses on less than 1% of species.

Bacteria are essential to the nutrient cycle, recycling nutrients, fixing atmospheric nitrogen, and driving decomposition. Near [[Hydrothermal vent|hydrothermal vents]], extremophile bacteria convert compounds such as hydrogen sulphide and [[Methane]] into energy. Eukaryotic cells arose when ancient bacteria entered endosymbiotic associations with their ancestors, giving rise to mitochondria and, later, chloroplasts. Humans carry approximately 10¹³ to 10¹⁴ bacteria, mostly in the gut; most are harmless or beneficial. Pathogenic species cause [[Cholera]], syphilis, anthrax, leprosy, [[Tuberculosis]], tetanus, and bubonic plague. Antibiotics treat bacterial infections, but their use in farming has made resistance a growing problem. Bacteria also serve in sewage treatment, cheese and yogurt fermentation, mining of gold, palladium, and copper, and biotechnology.

Domestic Bactrians do not descend from the wild Bactrian camel (*C. ferus*), the only truly wild camelid in the Old World; the two species split around 1 million years ago.

Racquet sport in which a shuttlecock is hit across a net. Played as singles (one player per side) or doubles (two per side), it is often a casual outdoor activity, while professional games use a rectangular indoor court. Shuttlecocks have much higher [[Drag (physics)|drag]] than other balls and decelerate rapidly, yet their high top speed makes badminton the fastest racquet sport in the world. The modern game developed in the mid-19th century among British expatriate officers in [[India]] as a variant of battledore and shuttlecock. Badminton debuted as a Summer Olympic sport in 1992, and mixed doubles was added four years later. Denmark dominated European play, while the game has become very popular in Asia.

The second half protected the civil and religious rights of the Palestinian Arabs, who formed the vast majority of the local population. The declaration became a core component of the British Mandate for [[Mandatory Palestine]] and indirectly led to the State of Israel. It is considered a principal cause of the [[Israeli–Palestinian conflict]], and controversy remains over whether it contradicted British promises to the Sharif of Mecca.

Round object, usually spherical but sometimes ovoid. Balls are used in ball games, where play follows the ball as it is hit, kicked or thrown, and in simpler activities such as catching and juggling. Balls made of hard-wearing materials form ball bearings, which provide very low [[Friction|friction]] bearings in engineering. Black-powder weapons used stone and metal balls as projectiles. Rubber balls were unknown outside the [[Americas]] until after the voyages of Columbus. The first known use of the English word *ball* for a globular object played with is from 1205, in Layamon's *Brut*.
