Any of several commercially important ray-finned fishes of the family [[Salmonidae]]. Salmon of the genera *Salmo* and *Oncorhynchus* are native to tributaries of the North Atlantic and North Pacific basins, respectively. Salmon are typically anadromous: they hatch in gravel beds of freshwater streams, migrate to the ocean as adults and return to their birthplace to spawn. Some populations are landlocked. Folklore holds that fish return to the exact stream of their hatching, and tracking studies have shown this to be mostly true. Norway is the world's largest producer of farmed salmon, followed by [[Chile]]. Salmon are also prized game fish, and several species have been introduced to the [[Great Lakes]], [[Patagonia]] and the [[South Island]] of New Zealand. Their migrations carry ocean-derived nutrients inland, feeding bears and other animals.

Genus of rod-shaped, gram-negative bacteria in the family Enterobacteriaceae, named after the American veterinary surgeon Daniel Elmer Salmon. Family of ray-finned fishes, the sole extant member of the suborder Salmonoidei, including [[Salmon|salmon]], [[Trout|trout]], char, graylings and whitefishes. It comprises 11 extant genera and over 200 species of coldwater predatory fish native to the subarctic and cool temperate Northern Hemisphere. Most are migratory, spawning in gravel beds of freshwater headstreams and spending their juvenile years in rivers and lakes. Mineral composed primarily of [[Sodium chloride|sodium chloride]] (NaCl), used as a seasoning and preservative. Granulated for food it is called table salt; as a natural crystalline mineral it is rock salt or halite. It supplies the essential dietary minerals sodium and chlorine, and saltiness is one of the basic human tastes. Salting, brining, and pickling are ancient methods of [[Food preservation|food preservation]].

Some of the earliest evidence of salt processing, around 6000 BC, comes from present-day Romania, where people boiled spring water to extract salts; the harvest of salt from Xiechi Lake, China, dates to at least 6000 BC. Salt was prized by the ancient Hebrews, Greeks, Romans, Byzantines, Hittites, Egyptians, and Indians. It was carried across the [[Mediterranean Sea]] by boat and across the [[Sahara]] by camel caravan; [[Herodotus]] described salt routes across Libya in the 5th century BC. Scarcity and universal need led nations to fight over salt and tax it, as in the El Paso Salt War of the late 1860s.

Salt is obtained from salt mines and by evaporating seawater and spring water. Of an annual global production of around three hundred million tonnes, only a small percentage is eaten; the greatest use is as a feedstock for chemicals such as caustic soda and chlorine, and for products such as polyvinyl chloride, plastics, and paper pulp. Table salt usually contains an anti-caking agent and may be [[Iodine|iodized]] to prevent iodine deficiency.

Sodium is essential to health as an electrolyte, but excessive salt consumption increases the risk of cardiovascular diseases such as [[Hypertension|hypertension]]. The [[World Health Organization]] recommends that adults consume less than 2,000 mg of sodium, equivalent to 5 grams of salt, per day.

ENTRY:
Chemical compound consisting of positively charged [[Ion|ions]] (cations) and negatively charged ions (anions), giving no net electric charge. The ions are held together by electrostatic forces termed [[Ionic bonding|ionic bonds]]. Also called an ionic compound, a salt may contain inorganic ions, such as chloride (Cl⁻), or organic ones, such as acetate (CH₃COO⁻), and ions that are monatomic, such as Na⁺, or polyatomic, such as ammonium (NH₄⁺) and carbonate (CO₃²⁻).

Individual ions usually have several near neighbors, so they form a continuous three-dimensional network rather than molecules, and salts usually form [[Crystal|crystalline]] structures when solid. Salts of small ions typically have high melting and boiling points and are hard and brittle. As solids they are almost always electrical insulators, but when melted or dissolved they conduct well because the ions become mobile. Historically, the term referred only to the compound formed by neutralization of an acid and a base; as the definitions broadened, it became synonymous with ionic compound, though the older distinction persists in some literature.

In 1913 William Henry Bragg and his son William Lawrence Bragg determined the structure of [[Sodium chloride|sodium chloride]], showing six equidistant nearest neighbors for each atom and long-range crystalline order. Proof that such compounds are made of ions came in the mid-1920s from X-ray reflection experiments. [[Max Born]] predicted crystal energies from the assumption of ionic constituents, and they matched thermochemical measurements.

Salts form when metals react with halogen gases, when solvent evaporates from a solution, or by precipitation on mixing two solutions. An [[Acid–base reaction|acid–base reaction]] gives a salt and water, as in NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H₂O.

ENTRY:
Selection of a subset of individuals from a [[Statistical population|statistical population]], used to estimate characteristics of the whole. Sampling is central to statistics, [[Survey methodology|survey methodology]] and quality assurance. It costs less and yields data faster than a [[Census]] of the entire population, which is often impossible. Samples should be representative, and statisticians aim for an unbiased group; survey weights can adjust for the design, particularly in stratified sampling. Random sampling by lots is mentioned several times in the [[Bible]]. In 1786 Pierre Simon Laplace estimated the population of France from a sample. A famous failure came in 1936, when the Literary Digest predicted a Republican win in the U.S. presidential election, because its subscription and telephone lists were heavily [[Bias|biased]] toward Republicans. Probability methods include simple random, systematic, stratified and cluster sampling.

Last of the judges of the ancient [[Israelites]] in the Book of Judges, famed for superhuman strength. As a Nazirite he slew a lion bare-handed and killed a Philistine army with a donkey's jawbone. Betrayed by his lover Delilah, who had his hair cut while he slept, he was blinded and made to grind grain for the [[Philistines]]. In the temple of Dagon he prayed for strength, pushed down its pillars and died with his enemies. Some Christians have seen him as a type of [[Jesus]]. Many scholars regard the stories as folk legend rather than reliable history.

South Korean multinational [[Conglomerate (company)|conglomerate]] and the largest *chaebol*, a family-controlled business group, headquartered in [[Seoul]]. Founded in 1938 by Lee Byung-chul as a trading company, it diversified into food processing, textiles, [[Insurance|insurance]], securities and retail. It entered electronics in the late 1960s, starting with black-and-white television sets, and construction and [[Shipbuilding|shipbuilding]] in the mid-1970s. After Lee died in 1987 the group was divided into five business groups. Its key affiliate Samsung Electronics was the world's largest information technology company by 2017 revenues, and Samsung Heavy Industries was the world's second-largest shipbuilder by 2010 revenues. Samsung ranked fifth in the world for brand value as of 2024. Its leaders have been convicted of corruption, and critics argue that state-favored industrial policies distort competition.

ENTRY:
Second emperor of the [[Gupta Empire]] of ancient India. The son of Chandragupta I and the Licchavi princess Kumaradevi, he transformed the kingdom into a vast empire through military campaigns. The Allahabad Pillar inscription, a eulogy composed by his courtier Harisena, credits him with defeating nine kings of northern India and annexing their territories, and with a southern campaign against twelve kingdoms whose rulers became tributaries. At his height his direct rule extended from the Ravi River in the west to the [[Brahmaputra River]] in the east, and from the Himalayan foothills to central India. He performed the Ashvamedha sacrifice to assert his sovereignty and remained undefeated in battle. His gold coins and inscriptions suggest he was a poet and played the veena. His son [[Chandragupta II]] continued his expansionist policy. Scholars variously date his reign from c. 319 to c. 350 CE.

ENTRY:
American statesman and Founding Father, second cousin of [[John Adams]]. A politician in colonial Massachusetts, he founded the Sons of Liberty and opposed British taxation of the colonies. His 1768 Massachusetts Circular Letter urged non-cooperation, and in 1772 he devised a committee of correspondence system that linked patriots throughout the [[Thirteen Colonies]]. He helped guide the Continental Congress in [[Philadelphia]] toward the Continental Association in 1774 and the Declaration of Independence in 1776. He was later elected governor of Massachusetts. Historians disagree on his methods: 19th-century accounts praised him, while early 20th-century accounts called him a propagandist.
