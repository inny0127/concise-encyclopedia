[[International System of Units|SI]] base unit of time, symbol s. It was originally defined as 1/86,400 of a day, since the day divides into 24 hours, 60 minutes and 60 seconds. ENTRY:
Physical law based on empirical observation that heat always flows spontaneously from hotter to colder regions. It establishes [[Entropy|entropy]] as a physical property of a thermodynamic system, and the entropy of an isolated system left to spontaneous evolution cannot decrease. The law forbids what the [[First law of thermodynamics|first law]] would allow, such as a broken cup reassembling and jumping back onto a table. Sadi Carnot showed in 1824 that the efficiency of a heat engine has an upper limit. Rudolf Clausius gave the first rigorous entropy-based definition in the 1850s. [[Statistical mechanics]] explains the law through probability distributions of atoms or molecules.

ENTRY:
Process of choosing which animals or plants reproduce in order to develop particular traits. Also called artificial selection, it is often combined with [[Inbreeding|inbreeding]], linebreeding and outcrossing. Domesticated animals are known as breeds, and domesticated plants as varieties or cultivars. [[Charles Darwin]] used artificial selection as an analogy for [[Natural selection|natural selection]] in *On the Origin of Species* (1859). Selective breeding has been practiced since prehistory: wheat, rice and [[Dog|dogs]] differed from their wild ancestors for millennia, and [[Maize|maize]] was bred from teosinte in [[Mesoamerica]]. Robert Bakewell established it as a scientific practice during Britain's Agricultural Revolution in the 18th century. It is now common in agriculture and experimental biology.

ENTRY:
Chemical element with symbol Se and atomic number 34. Discovered in 1817 by [[Jöns Jacob Berzelius]], who noted its similarity to the previously discovered [[Tellurium|tellurium]], selenium takes its name from the Greek word for "moon." It is found in metal sulfide ores, where it substitutes for [[Sulfur|sulfur]], and is produced commercially as a byproduct of refining them. Its chief uses are in glassmaking and pigments, and it is a [[Semiconductor|semiconductor]] used in photocells. Trace amounts are necessary for cellular function in many animals, including humans, but both elemental selenium and its salts are toxic in small doses, causing selenosis. Selenium is a component of the antioxidant enzymes glutathione peroxidase and thioredoxin reductase.

Hellenistic Greek state in West Asia (312–63 BC). It was founded by the Macedonian general [[Seleucus I Nicator|Seleucus I]] after the division of the empire of [[Alexander the Great]]. At its height it covered Anatolia, Persia, the Levant, Mesopotamia and parts of Central Asia. It was a center of Hellenistic culture, and an urban Greek elite formed its dominant political class. The Seleucid rump state in Syria was conquered by [[Tigranes the Great]] of Armenia in 83 BC and overthrown by the Roman general [[Pompey]] in 63 BC.
