Large phylum of mainly parasitic [[Alveolate|alveolates]]. Apicomplexa are unicellular, spore-forming [[Protozoa|protozoans]], and most are obligate endoparasites of animals. The exception, *Nephromyces*, is a symbiont in marine animals. The name comes from Latin *apex* (top) and *complexus* (infolds), for the set of [[Organelle|organelles]] in the infective sporozoite that allow penetration of host cells. Most members possess a non-photosynthetic [[Plastid|plastid]] called an apicoplast. Life cycles involve asexual and sexual replication, some members use two hosts, and most use gliding motility. Diseases caused by members include [[Malaria]] (*Plasmodium*), [[Toxoplasmosis]] (*Toxoplasma gondii*) and cryptosporidiosis (*Cryptosporidium parvum*). The group includes coccidia, gregarines, piroplasms and plasmodia.

Umayyad forces twice placed [[Constantinople]] under siege, in 674 to 678 and 717 to 718, without taking it, and the border then stabilized at the Taurus Mountains. Abbasid vassals conquered [[Crete]] in 827 and took [[Sicily]] between 831 and 878. From c. 920 to 976 the Byzantines pushed Arab forces back in Anatolia, and Crete was reconquered in 961. The [[Fatimid Caliphate]] later replaced the Abbasids as the main Arab power in the Levant. Byzantine defenses used [[Greek fire]], a naphtha-based incendiary weapon, which proved devastating in naval engagements.

Conflict between Israel and surrounding Arab states, rooted in the [[Israeli–Palestinian conflict]]. Zionists viewed Palestine as the Jewish ancestral homeland and sought to establish a Jewish state or homeland there through colonization, while Arabs generally opposed [[Zionism|Zionism]] and regarded Palestine as Arab Palestinian land. The [[League of Nations]] gave Britain the Mandate for [[Mandatory Palestine]] in 1920, following agreements including the [[Balfour Declaration|Balfour Declaration]]. Intercommunal violence became civil war in 1947, after the United Nations adopted the Partition Plan for Palestine, and an international war began when Israel was declared on May 14, 1948. The 1948 war ended with the 1949 Armistice Agreements, which established the Green Line. Further wars followed in 1956, 1967, 1973 and 1982. Peace treaties were later signed with Egypt and Jordan, and the Abraham Accords of 2020 further calmed relations. Historians differ on the causes: some Israeli historians, such as Anita Shapira, locate them in Arab rejection of Jewish self-determination, while Palestinian narratives emphasize the displacement of Palestinians in 1948.

Regional organization of Arab states, founded in [[Cairo]] on March 22, 1945. It began with seven members, Egypt, Iraq, Transjordan, Lebanon, Saudi Arabia, Syria and North Yemen, and now has 22, including three non-Arab countries: [[Comoros]], [[Djibouti]] and [[Somalia]]. Its stated aim is to draw closer relations between member states, coordinate their collaboration and safeguard their independence and sovereignty. Series of pro-democracy protests, uprisings and armed rebellions across much of the Arab world in the early 2010s. It began in [[Tunisia]] after the self-immolation of Mohamed Bouazizi and spread to Libya, Egypt, Yemen, Syria and Bahrain. Vast desert wilderness in West Asia, occupying almost the entire [[Arabian Peninsula]]. At 2,330,000 sq km (900,000 sq mi), it is the fourth largest desert in the world and the largest in Asia. Its southern end is Ar-Rub' al-Khali (the Empty Quarter), the largest continuous body of sand in the world. It is an extension of the [[Sahara]] Desert. Most of the area receives around 100 mm (3.9 in) of rain a year, although some places receive as little as 50 mm, and temperatures swing between intense heat and seasonal night-time freezes. Most of the desert lies in [[Saudi Arabia]].

Sea of the northern Indian Ocean, lying between the [[Arabian Peninsula]] and the Indian subcontinent. It has an area of 3,862,000 sq km (1,491,000 sq mi) and a maximum depth of 5,395 meters (17,700 feet). The [[Gulf of Aden]] links it to the [[Red Sea]] through the Bab-el-Mandeb strait, and the Gulf of Oman connects it to the [[Persian Gulf]]. Trade was historically restricted by the [[Monsoon|monsoon]] winds and their currents. The sea is highly productive, but overfishing, pollution and warming have put many of its species at risk. The largest river flowing into it is the Indus, and its largest islands include [[Socotra]], Masirah and Astola.
