Southernmost of the five major circles of latitude that mark maps of [[Earth]], lying 66°33′51.1″ south of the [[Equator]] by current measurement. Its position shifts with changes in Earth’s [[Axial tilt|axial tilt]], and the circle is currently drifting south at about 14.5 m (48 ft) per year. South of it the Sun is above the horizon for 24 continuous hours at least once a year, a [[Midnight sun|midnight sun]] event, and below the horizon for 24 hours at least once a year. Continental glacier covering 98% of [[Antarctica]], the largest of Earth’s two current ice sheets. It covers 14 million sq km, averages over 2 km in thickness, and holds 26.5 million cubic km of ice, equivalent to 61% of all fresh water on Earth. It is often divided into the [[Antarctic Peninsula]], the East Antarctic Ice Sheet and the West Antarctic Ice Sheet. The East sheet is over 10 times larger and at higher elevation, so it is less vulnerable to climate change. Ice loss from West Antarctic glaciers such as Thwaites and Pine Island is far greater than the East sheet’s net mass gain in the early 2020s. Net ice loss from Antarctica alone would add about 11 cm (5 in) to global sea level by 2100. Earth's southernmost and least-populated continent, lying almost entirely south of the [[Antarctic Circle]] and surrounded by the Southern Ocean.

It contains the geographic [[South Pole]] and is the fifth-largest continent, with an area of 14,200,000 sq km (5,500,000 sq mi), about 40% larger than Europe. Most of it is covered by the [[Antarctic ice sheet]], with an average thickness of 1.9 km (1.2 mi). About 70% of the world's freshwater reserves are frozen there; if melted, they would raise global sea levels by almost 60 m (200 ft).

It is the coldest, driest, and windiest continent on average, with the highest average elevation. It is mainly a polar desert, with annual precipitation of over 200 mm (8 in) along the coast and far less inland. The lowest temperature recorded on Earth, −89.2°C (−128.6°F), was measured there; coastal regions can exceed 10°C (50°F) in summer. Native animals include mites, nematodes, [[Penguin|penguins]], and seals; vegetation is mostly lichen or moss.

The ice shelves were probably first seen in 1820 during a Russian expedition led by Fabian Gottlieb von Bellingshausen and Mikhail Lazarev. The first confirmed landing was by a Norwegian team in 1895, and [[Roald Amundsen]] first reached the geographic South Pole in 1911.

The continent is governed under the 1959 Antarctic Treaty System, which prohibits military activity, mining, nuclear explosions, and nuclear waste disposal. About 5,000 people live at research stations in summer and around 1,000 in winter. Human activity affects the continent through pollution, [[Ozone depletion|ozone depletion]], and climate change.

The name derives from Greek *antarktikos*, "opposite to the Arctic"; it was adopted in the 1890s, with first use attributed to the cartographer John George Bartholomew.

All have elongated snouts, toothless mouths and long, sticky tongues, and they use large foreclaws to break into termite mounds and ant nests. Anteaters are more closely related to [[Sloth|sloths]] than to any other mammal group, and together with sloths they form the order Pilosa. The name is also applied to unrelated animals such as the [[Aardvark|aardvark]], [[Pangolin|pangolins]] and [[Echidna|echidnas]], which share similar features through convergent evolution.

Scientific study of humanity, concerned with human behavior, biology, cultures, societies, and language, in both the present and the past.

The discipline crosses biology and sociology. It is traditionally divided into four subfields. [[Social anthropology]] studies patterns of behavior, and [[Cultural anthropology|cultural anthropology]] studies cultural meaning, including norms and values; the term sociocultural anthropology is commonly used today. [[Linguistic anthropology]] studies how language influences social life. [[Biological anthropology]] studies the biology and evolution of humans and their close primate relatives. Debate continues over the relative weight given to cultural and biological explanations of human behavior.

In [[Camera|cameras]], [[Binoculars|binoculars]], [[Telescope|telescopes]] and microscopes the coating improves contrast by eliminating stray light, which is especially important in planetary astronomy. On eyeglass lenses the benefit is mainly cosmetic, though it also lets slightly more light through. The simplest form was discovered by Lord Rayleigh in 1886, when he found that old, tarnished glass transmitted more light than new glass. A bare glass-air interface reflects 4–5% of visible light per surface, and effective coatings can reduce this to below 1%.

Antimicrobial substance active against [[Bacteria|bacteria]].

Antibiotics are the most important type of antibacterial agent against bacterial [[Infection|infections]] and are widely used in their treatment and prevention. They may kill bacteria or inhibit their growth. A limited number also have antiprotozoal activity, but they are not effective against viruses or fungi.

In normal medical usage, antibiotics such as [[Penicillin|penicillin]] are produced naturally by one microorganism fighting another, whereas non-antibiotic antibacterials such as sulfonamides and antiseptics are fully synthetic; both are included in antimicrobial chemotherapy. In current usage it applies to any medication that kills bacteria or inhibits their growth, whether or not a microorganism produced it.

Chemical analyses of Nubian skeletons from northern Sudan, dated AD 350–550, show high levels of tetracycline, probably from beer brewed with *Streptomyces*. Later evidence from Egypt, China, Serbia, Greece, and Rome shows moldy bread applied to infections. Synthetic antibacterial chemotherapy began in Germany with Paul Ehrlich in the late 1880s. [[Alexander Fleming]] discovered modern penicillin in 1928, and Howard Florey and Ernst Chain developed it therapeutically in the early 1940s, enabling widespread use in [[World War II]]. Prontosil, the first sulfonamide and first systemically active antibacterial drug, was developed by a team led by Gerhard Domagk in 1932 or 1933 at Bayer Laboratories.

Antibiotics sharply reduced mortality from [[Pneumonia|pneumonia]], sepsis, and wound infections. Their overuse in healthcare, agriculture, and veterinary medicine has driven bacterial resistance through mutation and [[Plasmid|plasmid]] transfer. The [[World Health Organization]] calls antimicrobial resistance a "serious threat" that is "happening right now in every region of the world." Nearly 5 million deaths a year are associated with it; deaths directly attributable to it numbered 1.27 million in 2019.

Large [[Protein|protein]], also called an immunoglobulin, used by the [[Immune system|immune system]] to identify and neutralize antigens such as those on [[Bacteria|bacteria]] and viruses. Each antibody recognizes one or more specific antigens. The paratope at the tip of each branch of the Y-shaped molecule binds a particular epitope, allowing antibodies to tag an antigen for attack by immune cells or to neutralize it directly, for example by blocking a part of a [[Virus|virus]] needed to invade a host cell. In humans there are five classes: IgA, IgD, IgE, IgG and IgM, which differ in their effector functions and where they are released in the body. Antibodies are generally considered part of the adaptive immune system, and they are a manifestation of immunological memory.

Ancient Greek hand-powered orrery, the oldest known analog computer. It could predict astronomical positions and [[Eclipse|eclipses]] decades in advance and track the four-year cycle of the [[Ancient Olympic Games]]. Its fragments were recovered in 1901 from a shipwreck off the Greek island of Antikythera and are held by the National Archaeological Museum in Athens. Estimates of its construction date range from about 87 BC to 205 BC, and the shipwreck is dated to approximately 70–60 BC. Its surviving bronze gears enabled it to model the Moon's irregular orbit, a motion a motion studied by the astronomer [[Hipparchus]], who may have been consulted in its construction.
