Painting by [[Sandro Botticelli]], probably executed in the mid-1480s, showing the goddess [[Venus (mythology)|Venus]] arriving on the shore after her birth from the sea. She stands nude on a giant [[Scallop]] shell as the wind god Zephyrus blows her toward land, and a Hora holds a cloak to cover her. The painting is in the [[Uffizi]] Gallery in [[Florence]]. Its appeal is sensory and accessible, and its main meaning is a straightforward treatment of a Greek mythological scene.

ENTRY:
Wandering religious teacher and founder of [[Buddhism]], of the eastern [[Indo-Gangetic Plain|Indo-Gangetic Plains]], 6th or 5th century BC. Born Siddhartha Gautama, he is known as the Buddha, "the awakened one"; the word is a title for those who have attained bodhi (awakening), not a personal name.

According to Buddhist tradition, he was born in Lumbini, in what is now [[Nepal]], to royal parents of the Shakya clan, but renounced home life to become a wandering ascetic. After a life of mendicancy, asceticism, and meditation, he attained nirvana at Bodh Gaya, in what is now [[Bihar]], India. He then wandered through the lower Indo-Gangetic Plain, teaching and building a monastic order (sangha). Tradition holds that he died at Kushinagar, in modern [[Uttar Pradesh]], and reached parinirvana, "final release from conditioned existence".

He taught a Middle Way between sensual indulgence and severe asceticism, leading to freedom from ignorance, craving, rebirth, and suffering. His core teachings are summarized in the [[Four Noble Truths]] and the Noble Eightfold Path. Other key concepts are the five skandhas and dependent origination. The earliest attestation of the title Buddha is from the 3rd century BC, when edicts of [[Ashoka]] mention the Buddha and Buddhism.

His teachings were compiled into the Vinaya Pitaka, on monastic discipline, and the Sutra Pitaka, discourses attributed to him, and passed down through an [[Oral tradition|oral tradition]]. Later generations added treatises, biographies, Jataka tales of his past lives, and the Mahayana sutras. Buddhism diversified into Theravada, Mahayana, and Vajrayana traditions, largely disappeared from India after the 8th century, and grew more prominent in Southeast and East Asia.

Medical encyclopedia in five books, compiled by Avicenna and completed in 1025. It presents the medical knowledge of the Islamic world, which had been influenced by [[Galen]] and by earlier Greco-Roman, Persian, Chinese and Indian medicine. ENTRY:
Set of four violin concertos by [[Antonio Vivaldi]], each evoking a season of the year. Composed around 1718–1723, when Vivaldi was court chapel master in Mantua, they were published in 1725 in [[Amsterdam]] as part of *Il cimento dell'armonia e dell'inventione*. Vivaldi depicted flowing creeks, singing birds, buzzing flies, storms and frozen landscapes, a revolution in musical conception. He published the concertos with [[Sonnet|sonnets]], possibly written by Vivaldi himself, that describe each season. The concertos are among the earliest and most detailed examples of program music. Each concerto has three movements, fast–slow–fast, and each sonnet has three matching sections. A performance of all four takes about 40–43 minutes. The first proper electrical recording was made in 1942 by Bernardino Molinari. It is the best known of Vivaldi's works.

Country in [[West Africa]] (pop., 2024: 2,422,712), the smallest country in continental Africa. It is surrounded by [[Senegal]] except on the west, where it meets the [[Atlantic Ocean]]; its territory lies along both banks of the lower Gambia River. Banjul, formerly Bathurst, is the capital. ENTRY:
Five-panel triptych by [[Hieronymus Bosch]], painted between 1490 and 1510. Closed, its outer panels show the Third Day of Creation in green-gray grisaille. Open, its inner panels show a paradise, a central panel of lustful humanity, and [[Hell]]. Interpretations usually treat the work as a warning against temptation, reflecting Bosch's religious beliefs, but twentieth-century art historians disagree over whether the central panel is a moral warning or a panorama of paradise lost. It is not known whether the painting was intended as an altarpiece, although its extreme subject matter makes a church or monastery an unlikely setting. The triptych has been housed in the [[Museo del Prado]] in Madrid since 1939.
