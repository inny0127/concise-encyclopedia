ENTRY:
Scientific law stating that the mass of a closed system remains constant. Mass can be neither created nor destroyed, though it may be rearranged in space or changed in form. In chemical reactions the mass of the reactants equals the mass of the products. Jean Rey demonstrated this in 1630, and [[Antoine Lavoisier]] confirmed it in the late 18th century, a formulation central to the progress from [[Alchemy|alchemy]] to modern [[Chemistry|chemistry]]. The law is widely used in chemistry, mechanics and [[Fluid dynamics|fluid dynamics]], where the continuity equation expresses it. It holds only in the classical limit. In [[General relativity|general relativity]], mass–energy conservation is subject to different definitions, and mass is not generally conserved in open systems.

Cultural, social, and political philosophy and ideology that seeks to promote and preserve traditional institutions, customs, and values. Its central tenets vary with the culture and civilization in which it appears. In Western culture, depending on nation and period, conservatives have sought to preserve institutions such as the nuclear family, organized religion, the military, the nation-state, constitutions, property rights, the rule of law, aristocracy, and [[Monarchy|monarchy]].

[[Edmund Burke]], an 18th-century Anglo-Irish statesman who opposed the [[French Revolution]], is credited as one of the forefathers of Western conservative thought in the 1790s, along with Joseph de Maistre. The first established political use of the term came in 1818 with François-René de Chateaubriand, during the Bourbon Restoration. Conservatism is counted among the three major ideologies with [[Liberalism|liberalism]] and [[Socialism|socialism]], and it is the dominant ideology in many countries, including Hungary, India, Iran, Israel, Italy, Japan, Poland, and Russia. Historically associated with [[Right-wing politics|right-wing politics]], it has been libertarian or authoritarian, populist or elitist, progressive or reactionary, moderate or extreme, and conservatives in different countries may disagree on a wide range of issues.

Scholars have tried to define it by principles. Andrew Heywood identifies five central beliefs: tradition, human imperfection, organic society, authority and hierarchy, and property. Russell Kirk, in *The Conservative Mind* (1953), set out five canons, including belief in a transcendent order, faith in custom and prescription, and respect for prudence. Samuel P. Huntington described conservatism as a situational ideology that defends the established institutions of its time. Left-wing theorists such as Corey Robin instead define it mainly as a defense of social and economic inequality, while supporters stress its caution toward utopian schemes.

ENTRY:
Body of fundamental principles or established precedents that form the legal basis of a state, organization, or other entity and commonly determine how it is governed. A constitution written down in one or more documents is a written constitution; one contained in a single comprehensive document is codified. The [[United Kingdom]] is a notable example of an uncodified constitution, set out instead in numerous acts of a legislature, court cases, and [[Treaty|treaties]]. A treaty establishing an international organization is also its constitution.

Within states, a constitution defines the principles on which the state is based and the procedure by which laws are made, and by whom. Some constitutions, especially codified ones, also limit state power by establishing lines rulers cannot cross, such as fundamental rights. Changes frequently require consensus or a supermajority. In most modern states the constitution has supremacy over ordinary statutory law, and an official act beyond the powers it grants is null and void from inception.

The word comes through French from Latin *constitutio*, used for imperial enactments, and was later used in canon law for a decree issued by the [[Pope]]. [[William Blackstone]] used it for violations of public trust serious enough to justify a revolutionary response.

The [[Constitution of India]] is the longest written constitution of any country, with 146,385 words in English; Monaco's is the shortest, with 3,814. The constitution of [[San Marino]] might be the oldest active written one, with core documents in operation since 1600, while the [[Constitution of the United States]] is the oldest active codified one. The historical life expectancy of a written constitution since 1789 is approximately 19 years.

Fire provided warmth and light, protection from predators, a means of making hunting tools and a method of cooking food, enabling dispersal and changes in diet and behavior. Human use of fire is documented 1.79 million years ago in Wonderwerk Cave, [[South Africa|South Africa]]. Microscopic wood ash is accepted as evidence of fire use by [[Homo erectus|Homo erectus]] from about 1 million years ago. The oldest likely evidence of cooking, fish teeth heated deep in a cave at the Daughters of Jacob Bridge in the Golan Heights, dates to about 780,000 years ago. The oldest definitive evidence of fire-making, from a [[Neanderthal|Neanderthal]] site in eastern England, dates to about 400,000 years ago. Controlled fire becomes far more widespread 400–300,000 years ago, and nearly universal among [[Early modern human|anatomically modern humans]] around 125–120,000 years ago.

Branch of engineering and applied mathematics dealing with the control of [[Dynamical system|dynamical systems]]. It seeks a model or algorithm that applies system inputs to drive a system to a desired state, minimizing delay, overshoot and steady-state error while keeping the system stable. A controller compares the controlled process variable with a set point, and the resulting error is fed back to generate a corrective action. Control theory underlies the automation that revolutionized manufacturing, aircraft and communications and created [[Robotics|robotics]]. Formal analysis began in 1868, when [[James Clerk Maxwell|James Clerk Maxwell]] analyzed the centrifugal governor. Stability criteria were contributed by Edward Routh and Adolf Hurwitz, and PID control was developed from 1922 by Nicolas Minorsky. As the general theory of feedback systems, control theory is useful in [[Economics|economics]], [[Operations research|operations research]] and the life sciences.

Movement of a fluid driven by differences in its properties, especially [[Density|density]], together with body forces. In thermodynamics the term often refers to [[Heat transfer|heat transfer]] by convection. In natural convection, [[Buoyancy|buoyancy]] makes less dense fluid rise and denser fluid sink, and the density contrasts usually arise from differences in temperature or composition. Informal, interactive [[Communication|communication]] between two or more people. Conversational skills and [[Etiquette|etiquette]] are an important part of socialization, and conversational ability in a new language is a frequent focus of language teaching. No generally accepted definition exists beyond the involvement of at least two people, so the term is often defined by what it is not. Conversation analysis, a branch of sociology, studies the structure and organization of human interaction.

Zero-player cellular automaton devised by British mathematician John Horton Conway in 1970.
