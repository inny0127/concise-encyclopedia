Haploid stage in the life cycle of plants and algae. The gametophyte is one of the two alternating multicellular phases in plant life cycles, developing from a haploid spore and bearing sex organs that produce gametes. Gametes fuse in fertilization to form a diploid zygote, which develops into the [[Sporophyte|sporophyte]] phase; the sporophyte produces haploid spores by [[Meiosis|meiosis]] that germinate into new gametophytes. In [[Bryophyte|bryophytes]] such as mosses, liverworts and hornworts, the gametophyte is the most visible and longest-lived stage and is nutritionally independent, while the sporophyte depends on it. In most [[Fern|ferns]], the gametophyte is a photosynthetic, free-living structure called a prothallus, which maintains the sporophyte during its early development. As land plants evolved reproduction by seeds, the female gametophyte became smaller and more dependent on the sporophyte.

Most common extension of the factorial function to [[Complex number|complex numbers]]. Denoted by Γ, it is defined for all complex numbers except non-positive integers, and satisfies Γ(n) = (n−1)! for every positive integer n. [[Daniel Bernoulli]] first studied it. Penetrating form of [[Electromagnetic radiation|electromagnetic radiation]]. Trans-boundary river of South Asia, 2,525 km long, flowing through India and Bangladesh. Known as the Ganga in India and the Padma in Bangladesh, it rises in the western [[Himalayas]] in the Indian state of [[Uttarakhand]] and flows south and east through the Gangetic plain. Its basin covers approximately 1,114,000 sq km of fertile alluvial plains that support intensive agriculture.

The main stem begins at Devprayag, where the Alaknanda and the Bhagirathi meet. The Alaknanda is longer and thus the source stream in hydrology, while the Bhagirathi, which rises at Gomukh at 4,356 m, is considered the source in Hindu mythology. At [[Prayagraj]] the river joins the 1,444 km-long [[Yamuna]]. The Ghaghara is its largest tributary by discharge. In West Bengal, a feeder canal diverts half of its flow south to the Hooghly River. In Bangladesh the river, renamed the Padma, is joined by the Jamuna and the Meghna, forming the Ganges Delta and emptying into the [[Bay of Bengal]]. The Ganges–Brahmaputra–Meghna system is the second-largest river system on earth by discharge.

The river is a lifeline to hundreds of millions of people, and many former imperial capitals lie on its banks or those of its tributaries. It is home to approximately 140 species of fish and 90 species of amphibians, including the critically endangered [[Gharial|gharial]]. It is the most sacred river to followers of [[Hinduism]], who worship it as the goddess Ganga.

The Ganges is severely polluted: fecal coliform bacteria near [[Varanasi]] exceed the Indian government's official limit by more than 100 times. The Ganga Action Plan, a cleanup initiative, has been considered a failure, with causes variously attributed to corruption, government inattention, poor technical expertise and planning, and a lack of support from religious authorities.

ENTRY:
State of matter with neither fixed volume nor fixed shape, a compressible form of fluid. A pure gas consists of individual atoms, as in a [[Noble gas|noble gas]] such as neon, or of molecules, such as oxygen (O₂) or carbon dioxide; pure gases can also be mixed, as in air. What distinguishes gases from liquids and solids is the vast separation of the individual particles, which makes some gases invisible. The gaseous state lies between the liquid and [[Plasma (physics)|plasma]] states.

The only elements that are stable diatomic molecular gases at standard temperature and pressure are hydrogen (H₂), nitrogen (N₂), oxygen (O₂), fluorine (F₂), and chlorine (Cl₂). In the atmosphere, nitrogen makes up about 78% and oxygen about 21%.

The word was first used in the early 17th century by the Brabantian chemist Jan Baptist van Helmont, who identified carbon dioxide, the first known gas other than air. It appears to be a phonetic transcription of the Greek *chaos*, following alchemical usage first attested in the works of [[Paracelsus]].

Gases are described by four macroscopic properties: pressure, volume, number of particles, and temperature. [[Robert Boyle]] trapped gas in the closed end of a J-shaped tube with a column of mercury and found that as pressure increased the volume decreased, the product of the two remaining constant. Such observations by Boyle, Jacques Charles, John Dalton, Joseph Gay-Lussac, and Amedeo Avogadro led to the [[Ideal gas law|ideal gas law]]. Because the particles are widely separated, gases have weaker [[Intermolecular force|intermolecular forces]] than liquids or solids, low [[Density|density]] and [[Viscosity|viscosity]], and diffuse to distribute themselves evenly throughout any container. According to kinetic molecular theory, the particles move in continuous random motion and exert pressure on their container through elastic collisions.

Giant planet composed mainly of hydrogen and helium. The [[Solar System]] has two gas giants, [[Jupiter]] and [[Saturn]]. The term was originally synonymous with giant planet, but from the 1970s [[Uranus]] and [[Neptune]] were increasingly classified separately as ice giants, which are composed mainly of heavier volatile substances. Jupiter and Saturn contain heavier elements making up between 3 and 13 percent of their mass. ENTRY:
Continuous-flow [[Internal combustion engine|internal combustion engine]] in which compressed air is burned with fuel and the hot gas expands through a turbine. The turbine shaft drives the compressor and may also produce thrust, shaft power or both. Gas turbines with constant-pressure combustion operate on the Brayton cycle and power [[Aircraft|aircraft]], electric generators, ships and pumps. In aircraft the same core may drive turbojets, [[Turbofan|turbofans]], turboprops or turboshafts. A turbocharger contains a turbine but is not a gas-turbine engine, because it lacks a combustor. The first complete gas-turbine design was patented in 1791 by the Englishman John Barber. In 1903 the Norwegian Ægidius Elling built the first gas turbine to produce more power than it needed to run its own components. Sanford Alexander Moss, who joined [[General Electric]] that year, later applied gas-turbine concepts to the turbocharger.
