1962 book by the philosopher Thomas S. Kuhn, published by the University of Chicago Press. Kuhn challenged the view that science progresses through the steady accumulation of accepted facts and theories. He argued instead that periods of "normal science" are interrupted by revolutions, in which accumulating anomalies lead to new [[Paradigm|paradigms]] that ask new questions and change the rules of research. He illustrated this with the Copernican revolution, which at first did not predict planetary positions more accurately than the Ptolemaic system. The book launched the word "paradigm" into widespread use, and it is a landmark in the history, philosophy and sociology of science.

Treatise on political economy by Scottish economist [[Adam Smith]], published on March 9, 1776. Smith argued that national wealth arises from productive labor and the division of labor rather than from accumulated gold, and he criticized the [[Feudalism|feudal]] and mercantilist policies of European states that restricted free exchange. His metaphor of an "invisible hand" described how self-interested individuals in a free market unintentionally promote the community's welfare. He also criticized slavery, primogeniture and imperial military expenditure. Although Smith did not use the term [[Capitalism]], the book became a foundational work of [[Liberalism|economic liberalism]]. [[Karl Marx]] called it "the true Bible of the classical political economy of the 18th century."
