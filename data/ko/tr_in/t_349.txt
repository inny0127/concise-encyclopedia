Biome in which tree growth is hindered by frigid temperatures and short growing seasons. Arctic, alpine and Antarctic tundras exist. Vegetation is composed of dwarf shrubs, sedges, grasses, [[Moss|mosses]] and [[Lichen|lichens]]. In the Arctic, the soil is frozen 25 to 90 centimeters (10 to 35 in) down, and winter temperatures average about −28 °C (−18 °F). Arctic tundra is also a polar desert, receiving only 150–250 mm (6–10 in) of precipitation per year. The soil is rich in nitrogen and phosphorus and holds large amounts of carbon as [[Methane|methane]] and [[Carbon dioxide|carbon dioxide]] in [[Permafrost|permafrost]]. As warming thaws the soil, these gases are released into the atmosphere, creating a feedback cycle that contributes to [[Climate change|climate change]].

Rotary mechanical device that extracts [[Energy|energy]] from a fluid flow and converts it into useful [[Work (physics)|work]]. The work may drive a generator to produce electricity. A turbine is a turbomachine with at least one moving part. Its moving part, the rotor assembly, is a shaft or drum with attached blades; the fluid acts on the blades to impart rotational energy. Gas, steam and water turbines have a casing that contains and controls the working fluid. [[Impulse (physics)|Impulse]] turbines change the direction of a high-velocity fluid jet, while reaction turbines develop [[Torque|torque]] by reacting to pressure changes in the fluid. The word *turbine* was first applied to the device in 1822 by French mining engineer Claude Burdin, from Latin *turbo*, meaning vortex. [[Hero of Alexandria]] demonstrated the principle with an aeolipile in the first century AD, and early examples include [[Windmill|windmills]] and waterwheels. Modern [[Steam turbine|steam turbines]] often combine reaction and impulse designs.

Fluid motion characterized by chaotic changes in [[Pressure|pressure]] and flow velocity. It contrasts with laminar flow, in which fluid moves in parallel layers with no disruption between them. Most fluid flows in nature and engineering are turbulent, as with surf, fast-flowing rivers and chimney smoke. Turbulence arises when excess [[Kinetic energy|kinetic energy]] overcomes the damping effect of [[Viscosity|viscosity]], producing unsteady vortices that increase [[Drag (physics)|drag]] due to friction. Species of small, biologically immortal [[Jellyfish|jellyfish]], also called the immortal jellyfish. Found worldwide in temperate to tropical waters, it is one of few known animals able to revert from the sexually mature medusa stage to a sedentary polyp colony. The reversal occurs through transdifferentiation, a process that changes existing cells into new types, and can be triggered by stress, injury or age.
