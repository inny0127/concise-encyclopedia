Fluid with [[Viscosity|viscosity]] dependent on stress. Non-Newtonian fluids do not follow Newton's law of viscosity, so their viscosity can change when force is applied. [[Ketchup]] becomes runnier when shaken, and many salt solutions, molten polymers, [[Paint]], [[Blood]] and melted butter are non-Newtonian. A common demonstration is Oobleck, a mixture of corn or potato starch and water, which shows shear thickening: it grows more viscous when disturbed and briefly becomes a near-solid mass under sudden impact. Because viscosity alone cannot fully describe these fluids, they are studied with other rheological properties measured by rheometers.

ENTRY:
Any document or media content that attempts, in good faith, to convey information only about the real world rather than being grounded in imagination. Non-fiction typically aims to present topics objectively on historical, scientific, and empirical information, although some ranges into more subjective territory, including sincerely held opinions on real-world topics. The term often refers specifically to prose writing, where it is one of two fundamental approaches to storytelling, in contrast to narrative [[Fiction|fiction]], which is largely populated by imaginary characters and events.

While specific claims in a non-fiction work may prove inaccurate, the sincere author aims to be truthful at the time of composition. Writers may use features such as graphs, diagrams, timelines, headings, footnotes, maps, and indices to help readers find information. Simplicity, clarity, and directness are among the most important considerations, and the narrative techniques of fiction are generally thought inappropriate.

The boundary with fiction is continually argued over, especially in [[Biography|biography]]; [[Virginia Woolf]] described the biographer's problem of welding the granite-like solidity of truth with the rainbow-like intangibility of personality. Knowingly including untrue information is usually regarded as dishonest. Some works, such as journals, letters, and magazine articles, can legitimately be either fiction or non-fiction. Publishers and booksellers sometimes use the term "creative nonfiction" for works with a more literary or intellectual bent.

By the author's purpose, the main genres are instructional, explanatory, discussion-based, report-based, opinion-based, and chronological recounting. Common forms include academic texts, history books, life writing such as autobiographies and diaries, criticism, news stories, documentary films, essays, reference works such as encyclopedias and [[Atlas|atlases]], textbooks, and [[User guide|user guides]].

Nonlinear programming (NLP), also called nonlinear optimization, is the sub-field of [[Mathematical optimization|mathematical optimization]] dealing with problems in which some constraints are not linear equalities or the objective function is not linear. An optimization problem seeks the extrema (maxima, minima or stationary points) of an objective function over a set of unknown real variables, subject to equalities and [[Inequality (mathematics)|inequalities]], called constraints. The study of the subject began in 1872 with *The Expression of the Emotions in Man and Animals* by [[Charles Darwin|Charles Darwin]]. Encoding is the expression of emotions in ways a receiver can accurately interpret; decoding, called nonverbal sensitivity, is the accurate interpretation of those signals. Continent in the Northern and Western hemispheres, bordered by the [[Arctic Ocean]] to the north, the [[Atlantic Ocean]] to the east, [[South America]] and the [[Caribbean Sea]] to the southeast, and the [[Pacific Ocean]] to the south and west. It covers about 24,709,000 sq km, roughly 16.5% of Earth's land area and 4.8% of its total surface area. It is the third-largest continent by size after [[Asia]] and [[Africa]], and the fourth largest by population, estimated as of 2021 at over 592 million people in 23 independent states and territories, about 7.5% of the world's population.

Definitions vary. The continent includes the subregions of Northern America (Canada, [[Greenland]], the continental United States, Bermuda, and Saint Pierre and Miquelon) and Middle America (Mexico, Central America, and the Caribbean). The UN statistics division recognizes three subregions: Northern America, Central America (which includes Mexico), and the Caribbean. In human geography the term often refers only to Northern America, and in regional trade agreements such as NAFTA it denotes Canada, Mexico, and the United States. Colonization led to most North Americans speaking European languages such as English, Spanish, and French. The continent is divided linguistically and culturally into Anglo-America and Latin America, with large Francophone populations in Louisiana and Quebec. Significant indigenous populations retain pre-colonial traditions, and Latin America lies to the south. Canada and the United States are the largest countries by land area.

Largest alluvial plain of China, in the north of the country.
