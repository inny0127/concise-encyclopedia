ENTRY:
Very rare, silvery-white, hard, corrosion-resistant [[Transition metal|transition metal]] of the [[Platinum|platinum]] group (symbol Rh, atomic number 45). It was discovered in 1803 by William Hyde Wollaston in platinum ore and named for the rose color of one of its chlorine compounds, from the Greek *rhodon*, meaning rose. It is one of the rarest and most valuable precious metals, and it has only one naturally occurring isotope. Its major use, consuming about 80% of world production, is in the [[Catalytic converter|three-way catalytic converters]] of automobiles. Because it resists corrosion, rhodium is usually alloyed with [[Palladium|palladium]] or platinum for high-temperature coatings, and white gold jewelry is often plated with it. Rhodium detectors are used in nuclear reactors to measure neutron flux.

ENTRY:
Regulated succession of strong and weak elements, or of opposite or different conditions, in time. The word comes from Greek *rhythmos*, "any regular recurring motion, symmetry." It most commonly refers to such movement of beats in [[Music|music]], but also applies to poetry, mechanical operations, and biological processes, and to cyclical natural phenomena with periodicities from microseconds to many years. The *Oxford English Dictionary* defines rhythm in verse as the measured flow of words or phrases, forming patterns of sound determined by the relation of long and short or stressed and unstressed syllables.

Rhythm is related to, and distinguished from, pulse, meter, and beats. Most music, dance, and oral poetry maintain an underlying metric level, the pulse or beat, a series of identical periodic stimuli to which listeners entrain as they tap a foot or [[Dance|dance]].

In the performing arts, rhythm is the timing of events on a human scale: musical sounds and silences, the steps of a dance, or the meter of speech and poetry. In [[Hip-hop|hip-hop]], the rhythmic delivery of lyrics is one of the most important elements of the style. Rhythm may also be visual, as "timed movement through space"; architects speak of the rhythm of a building in the spacing of windows, columns, and other elements of the [[Façade|façade]]. Percussion, whose fast-transient sounds define a beat clearly, favors cultures that develop polyrhythm and polymeter, as in the cross-rhythms of Sub-Saharan Africa and the interlocking kotekan rhythms of the [[Gamelan|gamelan]].

Ribonucleoprotein particle found in most cells. It synthesizes [[Protein|proteins]] through [[Translation (biology)|translation]] of genetic information carried in messenger RNA (mRNA). During translation, the ribosome decodes successive codons in an mRNA molecule and, with the aid of transfer RNA (tRNA), links [[Amino acid|amino acids]] into a polypeptide chain. Each ribosome has a small and a large subunit, built from ribosomal RNA (rRNA) and many ribosomal proteins. Ribosomes differ in size and composition among [[Bacteria|bacteria]], [[Archaea|archaea]] and [[Eukaryote|eukaryotes]], but share a core structure that reflects a common evolutionary origin. [[Cereal]] grain, the staple food of over half of the world's population, particularly in [[Africa]] and [[Asia]]. Rice is the seed of the grass species *Oryza sativa* (Asian rice) or, less commonly, *Oryza glaberrima* (African rice). Asian rice was domesticated approximately 13,500 to 8,200 years ago in what is now [[China]]; African rice was domesticated independently in West Africa about 3,000 years ago. In 2023, 800 million tons were produced, third after [[Sugarcane|sugarcane]] and [[Maize|maize]], but only some 8% is traded internationally. China, India, and Indonesia are the largest consumers.

The plant can grow to over 1 m tall, or 5 m in deep water, and takes about six months from seed to harvest. Grain is planted directly in the field, or seedlings are transplanted, as is done by hand for most rice in Asia. Yields are reduced by pests including insects, rodents, and birds, by weeds, and by diseases such as rice blast.

Milling removes the outer layers, giving products from brown rice to white rice. Rice contains no gluten and provides protein but not all essential amino acids; cooked white rice contains 29% carbohydrate and 2% protein, with some manganese. Starch composition determines texture. Long-grain rice from the Indica cultivar is dry and fluffy and includes aromatic varieties such as basmati and jasmine, while short-grain Japonica rice is sticky and used in sushi and mochi. Black rice, colored by [[Anthocyanin|anthocyanins]], was reserved for the royal family in ancient China. Golden rice is a variety [[Genetic engineering|genetically engineered]] to contain vitamin A. Rice production is estimated to have caused over 1% of global greenhouse gas emissions in 2022.

Two- or three-wheeled passenger [[Cart|cart]], originally pulled by one person. The word comes from the Japanese *jinrikisha*, meaning "human-powered vehicle." The first known use of the term in English was in 1879. The most widely accepted account credits Izumi Yosuke, Suzuki Tokujiro and Takayama Kosuke with inventing the vehicle in Tokyo in 1869, although other claims exist and the origin remains unclear. Pulled rickshaws were a popular form of transport and a source of work for male laborers in Asian cities in the 19th century, but they declined as cars and trains became widely available. Cycle rickshaws (also called pedicabs or trishaws), auto rickshaws and electric rickshaws have replaced most pulled rickshaws, which survive mainly in tourism. Bangladesh has the highest number of rickshaws in the world, with 40,000 operating daily in the capital [[Dhaka]]. In 2023, [[UNESCO]] listed rickshaws and rickshaw art as "intangible heritage" of Bangladesh.
