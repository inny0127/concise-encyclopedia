Island country (pop., 2011: 71,293) in the Windward Islands of the [[Lesser Antilles]], in the [[Caribbean]]. Covering 750 sq km, it is the youngest island in the Lesser Antilles and is still being formed by volcanic activity. Known as the "Nature Island of the Caribbean," it has lush rainforests and [[Hot spring|hot springs]], including Boiling Lake, the world's second-largest hot spring. The island was settled by Arawak and later Kalinago peoples, and [[Christopher Columbus]] passed it in 1493. France colonized it from 1690 to 1763, bringing enslaved West Africans to work on coffee plantations, and Britain took control after the [[Seven Years' War]]. Dominica became an independent republic in 1978. The critically endangered Sisserou parrot, found only on the island, appears on the national flag, one of two sovereign national flags featuring purple. The country belongs to the [[Commonwealth of Nations]] and the [[United Nations]].

Family of tile-based games played with rectangular pieces, each divided into two square ends marked with pips (spots) or left blank. The traditional European set has 28 tiles showing every combination of spot counts from zero to six. The earliest mention is in *Former Events in Wulin* by Zhou Mi, from [[Song dynasty|Song dynasty]] China. Modern dominoes first appeared in [[France]] in the mid-18th century, but there is no confirmed link to the Chinese game. The name probably derives from a black-hooded costume worn during the Venetian Carnival. A set, like [[Dice|dice]] or playing cards, can be used for many games. Popular games include Domino Whist, Matador, Muggins (All Fives), Texas 42 and Mexican Train. In Britain, Fives and Threes is the most popular league and pub game. Dominoes have also been used for divination, such as bone throwing in Chinese culture.

[[Roman emperor]] (r. 81–96), the last member of the Flavian dynasty. He was the son of Vespasian and younger brother of Titus, and was declared emperor by the Praetorian Guard after Titus's death. His 15-year reign was the longest since [[Tiberius]]. He revalued the coinage, strengthened the frontiers and began a large building program to restore [[Rome]]. His general Agricola advanced in Caledonia (Scotland), but in Dacia Domitian was unable to defeat King [[Decebalus]] decisively. [[Propaganda]] fostered a [[Cult of personality|cult of personality]], and he named himself perpetual censor. He was popular with the people and army but considered a tyrant by the Senate. Senatorial writers, including [[Tacitus]], Pliny the Younger and Suetonius, portrayed him as a cruel and paranoid tyrant, while modern historians have offered more favorable assessments. He was [[Assassination|assassinated]] by court officials in 96 and succeeded the same day by Nerva.

Two-act *dramma giocoso* by [[Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart]], with an Italian libretto by Lorenzo Da Ponte. It is based on the Spanish legend of the libertine Don Juan, as told in Tirso de Molina's 1630 play *El burlador de Sevilla*. Novel in two parts, published in 1605 and 1615, by [[Miguel de Cervantes]]. Written in Early Modern Spanish, it follows the hidalgo Alonso Quijano of La Mancha, who reads so many [[Chivalry|chivalric]] romances that he becomes a knight-errant under the name Don Quixote to revive chivalry. He recruits the farm laborer Sancho Panza as his squire. The book is considered a founding work of Western literature and the first modern novel, and it is one of the most-translated books in the world. Its influence reached 19th-century French literature, including *[[The Three Musketeers]]* (1844) by [[Alexandre Dumas]], and the word quixotic, meaning "impractically idealistic," entered the language. [[Mark Twain]] said the novel had "swept the world's admiration for the medieval chivalry-silliness out of existence."

=== KEY: Doppler_effect_44a49c

ENTRY:
Change in the frequency, or period, of a wave for an observer moving relative to the wave's source. Named after Christian Doppler, who proposed it in 1842, it explains the change of pitch heard as a vehicle approaches and recedes. A source moving toward the observer compresses successive wave cycles and raises the frequency; a receding source lowers it. For waves in a vacuum, such as light, only relative velocity matters, but for [[Sound|sound]] the speed of the medium also counts. In 1845 Buys Ballot confirmed the effect for sound, and Hippolyte Fizeau found it for light in 1848. In astronomy it measures the radial velocities of stars, and in medicine [[Ultrasound|Doppler ultrasound]] assesses blood flow.

ENTRY:
Small, mostly nocturnal [[Rodent|rodent]] of the family Gliridae. Dormice live in Africa, Asia and Europe and are named for their long [[Hibernation|hibernation]], which in temperate zones can last six months or more. There are nine genera and 28 living species, half of them in the African genus *Graphiurus*. Largely arboreal and omnivorous, dormice are unique among rodents in lacking a cecum. They usually breed once, or occasionally twice, a year, with litters averaging four young. The European edible dormouse was considered a delicacy in ancient Rome and is still a delicacy in [[Slovenia]] and parts of [[Croatia]]. The woodland dormouse is the species most often seen in the pet trade, although dormice remain uncommon as pets.

English chemist and pioneer of X-ray crystallography. Hodgkin used the technique to determine the structure of biomolecules, work that became essential to structural biology. She confirmed the structure of [[Penicillin|penicillin]] and mapped that of [[Vitamin B12|vitamin B12]], and in 1964 she received the [[Nobel Prize]] in Chemistry, becoming the third woman to win it. She is the only British woman scientist to have won a Nobel Prize. She was born in Cairo, Egypt, the oldest of four daughters, and her interest in crystals was first displayed at the age of 10. She also elucidated the structure of [[Insulin|insulin]] in 1969, after 35 years of work.
