Island kingdom in [[Polynesia]], in the southern [[Pacific Ocean]] (pop., 2026: 103,291). Its 171 islands, 45 of them inhabited, cover about 750 sq km. By 888 BC settlers were living at Nukuleka, the earliest known inhabited town, and they were linked to the [[Lapita culture]]. From the rule of the first Tongan king, ʻAhoʻeitu, Tonga became a regional thalassocracy under the [[Tuʻi Tonga Empire]], which controlled much of the Pacific. Tonga was a British protected state from 1900 to 1970, but it never relinquished its sovereignty. In 2010 it became a semi-constitutional monarchy after reforms allowed its first partial representative elections. Captain [[James Cook]] visited in 1773 and gave the islands the name "Friendly Islands." Tonga is a member of the [[Commonwealth of Nations]] and the [[United Nations]].

ENTRY:
Muscular [[Organ (biology)|organ]] in the [[Mouth]] of a typical [[Tetrapod|tetrapod]]. The tongue manipulates food for chewing and swallowing and is the primary organ of [[Taste|taste]]. Its upper surface is covered by taste buds housed in papillae, and it is kept moist by saliva. A major function in humans is speech, and in other animals vocalization. The human tongue has an oral part at the front and a pharyngeal part at the back. It has four intrinsic muscles that alter its shape and four paired extrinsic [[Muscle|muscles]] that change its position. The average human tongue is about 10 cm long, and its average weight is 99 g in adult males and 79 g in adult females. The word derives from [[Old English]] *tunge*.

American novelist and editor. Born in [[Ohio|Lorain, Ohio]], she graduated from Howard University and earned a master's degree from [[Cornell University]]. Her first novel, *The Bluest Eye* (1970), was followed by *Sula* (1973) and *Song of Solomon* (1977), which won the National Book Critics Circle Award. *Beloved* (1987) won the Pulitzer Prize in 1988. She became the first Black female fiction editor at Random House in New York City, and she later taught at [[Princeton University]]. Her works address the harsh consequences of racism in the United States and the Black American experience. She received the [[Nobel Prize]] in Literature in 1993 and the Presidential Medal of Freedom from President [[Barack Obama]] in 2012.

Metric unit of mass equal to [[Kilogram|1,000 kilograms]], symbol t. Also called a metric ton in the United States, it is distinguished from the short ton and long ton. One tonne is about 2,204.6 [[Pound (mass)|pounds]], 1.102 short tons or 0.984 long tons. The exact corresponding SI unit is the megagram (Mg). The symbol t was adopted in 1879. One megatonne of [[TNT equivalent|TNT]] is equivalent to approximately 4.184 petajoules.

British Labour politician and prime minister of the U.K. (1997–2007). He led the [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour Party]] from 1994 to 2007 and rebranded it as "New Labour." He led the party to three consecutive general election victories. His government introduced a minimum wage, devolution in Scotland and Wales, and the [[Good Friday Agreement]] in Northern Ireland. === KEY: Tool_d421fd
HEADWORD: Tool
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Automation | Chimpanzee | Industrial Revolution | Interchangeable parts | Mesopotamia | Metalworking | Simple machine
ENTRY:
Object that extends an individual's ability to modify the surroundings or accomplish a task, typically solid, hand-operated, non-biological, and of a single broad purpose, unlike machines or computers. Humans make and use tools more than any other animal, including using tools to make other tools, but many animals also use tools. In 1981 Benjamin Beck published a widely used definition of tool use, later modified: the external employment of an unattached or manipulable attached object to alter the form, position, or condition of another object, another organism, or the user, with the user holding and directly manipulating it. By extension, concepts that support systematic thought are often called "tools" or "toolkits."

Anthropologists regard tool use as an important step in human evolution. Because humans and wild [[Chimpanzee|chimpanzees]] both use tools extensively, the first routine use is widely assumed to predate their divergence, though early tools were probably perishable, such as sticks. Stone artifacts date back about 2.5 million years, but a 2010 study suggests that *Australopithecus afarensis* carved animal carcasses with stone implements about 3.4 million years ago. Actual tools at least 2.6 million years old have been found in Ethiopia, and the hand axe is one of the earliest distinguishable forms. Early tools of stone, bone, and wood served for food preparation, hunting, weapons, clothing, pottery, and construction. The development of [[Metalworking|metalworking]] made new types possible, and harnessing animal power, wind, or steam allowed increasingly complex tools.

Several of the six classic [[Simple machine|simple machines]] (wheel and axle, lever, pulley, inclined plane, wedge, and screw) were invented in [[Mesopotamia]]. The wheel and axle first appeared with the potter's wheel in the 5th millennium BC, the lever in the shadoof c. 3000 BC, pulleys in the early 2nd millennium BC, and the screw in the Neo-Assyrian period (911–609 BC). Water wheels and watermills date to before 350 BC. Use of mechanical devices expanded in ancient Greece and Rome through waterwheels, and later windmills. Machine tools, with origins among 18th-century clock, watch, and instrument makers, surged during the [[Industrial Revolution]] and enabled precise, economical [[Interchangeable parts|interchangeable parts]]. Widespread [[Automation|automation]] in the 19th and 20th centuries let tools operate with minimal human supervision.

=== KEY: Tooth_77ec14
HEADWORD: Tooth
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Carnivore | Guinea pig | Human | Mammal | Manatee | Snake | Vertebrate
ENTRY:
Hard, calcified structure in the jaws or mouths of many [[Vertebrate|vertebrates]], used to break down food. Some animals, particularly [[Carnivore|carnivores]], also use teeth to capture or wound prey, tear food and defend themselves. Teeth are not made of bone; they consist of tissues of varying density and hardness that originate from the ectoderm, the outermost embryonic germ layer. Most [[Mammal|mammals]], including [[Human|humans]], are diphyodonts, growing one set of deciduous teeth followed by permanent teeth. Elephants, kangaroos and [[Manatee|manatees]] are polyphyodonts, growing many sets over their lives, and sharks grow a new set every two weeks. Rodent incisors grow and wear away continually through gnawing, and voles and [[Guinea pig|guinea pigs]] (but not mice) also have continuously growing molars. Among reptiles, teeth are generally simple and conical, with the venom-injecting fangs of [[Snake|snakes]] a notable exception.
