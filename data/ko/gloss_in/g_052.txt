=== KEY: George_Washington_6c7a49
HEADWORD: Washington, George
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Alexander Hamilton | American Revolutionary War | Constitution of the United States | French Revolution | French and Indian War | Siege of Yorktown | Thomas Jefferson | Treaty of Paris (1783)
ENTRY:
American Founding Father and first president of the United States (1789–97). As commander of the Continental Army, he led Patriot forces to victory in the [[American Revolutionary War]], and he is commonly known as the Father of His Country.

Born in the Colony of Virginia, he learned land surveying and later commanded the Virginia Regiment during the [[French and Indian War]] (1754–63). Appointed commander-in-chief in 1775, he directed a poorly organized and equipped force against disciplined British troops. He won at Trenton in late 1776 and Princeton in early 1777, but lost at Brandywine and Germantown. In 1781 he led a combined French and American force to a decisive victory at [[Siege of Yorktown|Yorktown]], and in the [[Treaty of Paris (1783)|Treaty of Paris]] of 1783 Britain acknowledged American independence. He presided over the Constitutional Convention in 1787, which drafted the [[Constitution of the United States]].

Washington was unanimously elected president by the Electoral College in 1789 and 1792. He established a strong, well-financed national government and remained impartial in the cabinet rivalry between [[Thomas Jefferson]] and [[Alexander Hamilton]]. During the [[French Revolution]] he adopted a policy of neutrality. He set precedents for the presidency, including a peaceful transfer of power, the title "Mr. President", and the two-term tradition, and his 1796 farewell address warned of the dangers that regionalism, partisanship, and foreign influence pose to national unity.

A planter of tobacco and wheat at Mount Vernon, he owned slaves. He began opposing slavery near the end of his life, and his will provided for the eventual manumission of his slaves. His namesakes include the national capital and the State of Washington, and in popular and scholarly polls he is consistently ranked among the greatest American presidents.

=== KEY: Georges_Cuvier_81704a

ENTRY:
French naturalist and zoologist, sometimes called the founding father of paleontology. By comparing living animals with fossils, he helped establish comparative anatomy and vertebrate [[Paleontology|paleontology]], reconstructing extinct animals such as the [[Mastodon|mastodon]] and a giant ground sloth he named *Megatherium*. He established extinction as a scientific fact, and in his *Essay on the Theory of the Earth* (1813) proposed that extinct species had been wiped out by periodic catastrophic floods. He examined [[Sarah Baartman]] while she was held captive in Paris, and his dissection of her after death disparagingly compared her physical features to those of monkeys.

French administrator who renovated Paris under Napoleon III. === KEY: Geostationary_orbit_156e59
HEADWORD: Geostationary orbit
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Arthur C. Clarke | Communications satellite | Geosynchronous orbit
ENTRY:
Circular orbit above Earth's equator, following the direction of Earth's rotation. A satellite in it has an orbital period equal to Earth's rotational period, one sidereal day, so it appears motionless to ground observers. The orbit lies at an altitude of 35,786 km (22,236 mi) and is a special case of [[Geosynchronous orbit|geosynchronous orbit]]. The concept was popularized by [[Arthur C. Clarke]] in a 1945 paper as a way to revolutionize telecommunications. [[Communications satellite|Communications satellites]] use it so that ground antennas can point permanently at them without moving, and weather and navigation satellites also operate there. Satellites are launched into a temporary orbit and placed in a "slot" above a fixed point on Earth's surface, then require periodic station-keeping. Retired satellites are moved to a higher graveyard orbit to avoid collisions.

=== KEY: Geosynchronous_orbit_a1049e
HEADWORD: Geosynchronous orbit
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Arthur C. Clarke | Geostationary orbit
ENTRY:
Earth-centered orbit with a period matching Earth's rotation. Its period is one sidereal day, 23 hours, 56 minutes and 4 seconds, so an observer on the surface sees the object return to the same position in the sky each day. Its path may stay still or trace a figure-eight, depending on inclination and eccentricity. A circular geosynchronous orbit has a constant altitude of 35,786 km (22,236 mi). The [[Geostationary orbit|geostationary orbit]] is a special case, circular and in the equatorial plane, with both inclination and eccentricity equal to 0. Communications satellites are often placed in geostationary or near-geostationary orbits so that their antennas need not move. In a 1945 paper, [[Arthur C. Clarke]] popularized the concept as a means of relaying communications; the collection of satellites in this orbit is called the Clarke Belt.

=== KEY: German_language_9d7176
HEADWORD: German language
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: European Union | Germanic languages | Germany | Martin Luther | Yiddish
ENTRY:
West Germanic language of the Indo-European family, mainly spoken in Western and Central Europe. It is the majority and official or co-official language in [[Germany]], Austria, Switzerland, and Liechtenstein, an official language of Luxembourg, Belgium, and the Italian province of South Tyrol, and a recognized national language in Namibia.

German has nearly 100 million native speakers and over 130 million total speakers as of 2024. It is the most spoken native language in the [[European Union]] and, after English, the second most widely spoken [[Germanic languages|Germanic language]]. It is the fourth most commonly learned second language globally and has been influential in philosophy, theology, science, and technology.

German is most closely related to Afrikaans, Dutch, English, the Frisian languages, and Scots. Modern German developed from Old High German, which in turn developed from Proto-Germanic during the Early Middle Ages. The High German consonant shift separates the High German dialects of the south from the Low German and Low Franconian dialects to the north; an example is Standard German *machen* against Dutch *maken*. Standard German is based on Thuringian-Upper Saxon and Upper Franconian dialects, and its standardization was catalyzed by [[Martin Luther]]'s 16th-century Bible translation.

German is an inflected language, with four cases, three genders, and two numbers, and it has strong and weak verbs. Most of its vocabulary is Germanic, with a smaller share from Latin and Greek; English is the main source of recent loanwords. It is a pluricentric language with three standardized variants: German, Austrian, and Swiss Standard German. Its many dialects include some protected by regional or national governments, and it is closely related to [[Yiddish]] and Luxembourgish. The Council for German Orthography is the main international body regulating its orthography.

[[Hot spring]] that intermittently ejects water and steam turbulently from a surface vent. Geysers are rare because they need heat, water and a plumbing system with the right geometry, conditions found mainly near active volcanic areas where [[Magma|magma]] lies close to the surface. Surface water seeps to an average depth of around 2,000 meters (6,600 ft), where it contacts hot rock. As the water boils, steam pressure forces a superheated column to the surface. ENTRY:
Thermodynamic potential, symbol *G*, measuring the maximum work, other than pressure–volume work, that a closed system can perform at constant temperature and pressure. It is defined as G = H − TS, where H is [[Enthalpy]] and S is [[Entropy]]. This maximum is attained only in a completely reversible process.
