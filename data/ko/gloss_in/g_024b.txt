=== KEY: Cerebrum_f40576
HEADWORD: Cerebrum
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Basal ganglia | Brainstem | Cerebellum | Cerebral cortex | Hippocampus
ENTRY:
Largest part of the brain, also called the telencephalon or endbrain. It contains the [[Cerebral cortex|cerebral cortex]] of the two hemispheres, together with subcortical structures including the [[Hippocampus|hippocampus]], [[Basal ganglia|basal ganglia]] and olfactory bulb. It lies in front of or on top of the [[Brainstem|brainstem]], depending on the animal. In humans it is the uppermost region of the central nervous system and the largest and best-developed of the five major brain divisions. It consists of two C-shaped hemispheres separated by the longitudinal fissure, joined by the corpus callosum, and each hemisphere mainly controls the opposite side of the body. With the assistance of the [[Cerebellum|cerebellum]], it controls all voluntary actions, such as moving or talking. In mammals the pallium becomes the cerebral cortex and the subpallium becomes the basal ganglia.

=== KEY: Ceres_dwarf_planet_3adff7
HEADWORD: Ceres
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Asteroid belt | Jupiter | Mars | Moon | NASA
ENTRY:
Dwarf planet in the main [[Asteroid belt|asteroid belt]] between the orbits of [[Mars]] and [[Jupiter]]. Discovered on 1 January 1801 by Giuseppe Piazzi at the Palermo Astronomical Observatory in Sicily, it was the first object identified in the asteroid belt and was first announced as a new planet. It was later classified as an asteroid and then as a dwarf planet, the only one confirmed in the asteroid belt and the largest without a [[Moon]]. Its diameter is about a quarter that of the Moon, and its apparent magnitude ranges from 6.7 to 9.3. The robotic [[NASA]] spacecraft Dawn approached Ceres for its orbital mission in 2015 and found a surface of water, ice and hydrated minerals such as carbonates and clay. Ceres likely has an internal ocean, and brines reach the surface, allowing cryovolcanoes such as Ahuna Mons to form roughly every fifty million years.

=== KEY: Cerium_32ff8b
HEADWORD: Cerium
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Catalytic converter | Lanthanide | Light-emitting diode
ENTRY:
Soft, ductile, silvery-white metal and chemical element with symbol Ce and atomic number 58. It is the second element of the [[Lanthanide|lanthanide]] series and tarnishes in air. Cerium usually shows an oxidation state of +3, but its stable +4 state does not oxidize water. It is one of the rare-earth elements. Cerium occurs with other rare earths in minerals such as monazite and bastnäsite, but it is easy to extract because it can be oxidized to the +4 state in aqueous solution. It was discovered in 1803 by Jöns Jakob Berzelius and Wilhelm Hisinger in Sweden, and independently by Martin Heinrich Klaproth in Germany. Cerium(IV) oxide is used to polish glass and in [[Catalytic converter|catalytic converters]]. Cerium metal is used in ferrocerium lighters, and cerium-doped YAG phosphor, used with blue [[Light-emitting diode|light-emitting diodes]], produces white light in most commercial white LED light sources.

=== KEY: Cerrado_4e2af9
HEADWORD: Cerrado
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Amazon rainforest | Brazil | Soybean
ENTRY:
Vast tropical savanna ecoregion in central [[Brazil|Brazil]], centered on the Brazilian Highlands. The name means closed or dense. It accounts for 21 percent of Brazil's land area, the second-largest habitat type after the [[Amazon rainforest|Amazonian rainforest]], and extends marginally into Paraguay and Bolivia. Its habitats include forest savanna, wooded savanna, park savanna, gallery forests and savanna wetlands. The World Wide Fund for Nature has named it the biologically richest savanna in the world, with about 10,000 plant species and 10 endemic bird species. Nearly 200 mammal species occur there, 14 of them endemic. About 75% of its 2 million km² is privately owned, which makes protection difficult. More than 50% of its original vegetation has been cleared for [[Soybean|soybean]] cultivation, cattle ranching and other agriculture.

=== KEY: Cervix_d4901b
HEADWORD: Cervix
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Female reproductive system | Vagina
ENTRY:
Fibromuscular organ of the [[Female reproductive system|female reproductive system]] that connects the [[Vagina|vagina]] with the uterine cavity. The cervix is approximately 4 cm (1.6 in) long and 3 cm (1.2 in) in diameter. It dilates during vaginal childbirth to allow the fetus to pass, and its mucus changes consistency during the menstrual cycle, which may signal ovulation. Human papillomavirus (HPV) infections can cause changes in the epithelium that lead to cancer of the cervix. Cervical cytology tests can detect cervical cancer and its precursors. HPV vaccines, developed in the early 21st century, reduce the risk of cervical cancer by preventing infection with the main cancer-causing strains.

American labor leader and political activist. Along with [[Dolores Huerta]] and Gilbert Padilla, he co-founded the National Farm Workers Association in 1962; it merged in 1967 with the Agricultural Workers Organizing Committee to form the United Farm Workers (UFW). === KEY: Cesare_Beccaria_510220
HEADWORD: Beccaria, Cesare
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Criminal law | Criminology | Milan | Montesquieu
ENTRY:
Italian criminologist, jurist, philosopher and economist. Born in [[Milan]], he is best remembered for the treatise *On Crimes and Punishments* (1764), which condemned torture and the death penalty and was a founding work of penology and the classical school of [[Criminology|criminology]]. He is considered the father of modern [[Criminal law|criminal law]] and of criminal justice. The treatise protests against torture to obtain confessions, secret accusations, arbitrary judicial power, unequal sentencing and capital punishment for serious and even minor offenses. Studying [[Montesquieu]] redirected him from mathematics to economics. According to John Bessler, his works had a profound influence on the Founding Fathers of the United States.

=== KEY: Cesare_Borgia_e4433f

ENTRY:
Italian-Spanish cardinal deacon and condottiero. He was the illegitimate son of [[Pope Alexander VI]] and sibling of Lucrezia Borgia. Made a cardinal at 18 after his father's election to the papacy, he resigned on August 17, 1498, to pursue a military career, and Louis XII of France named him duke of Valentinois the same day. He was unable to retain power for long after his father's death. Several contemporaries suggested that he had killed his brother Giovanni in 1497, but his role in the act has never been clear. His quest for power is documented in [[Niccolò Machiavelli]]'s treatise *The Prince*.

=== KEY: Cestoda_5bcf9a
HEADWORD: Cestoda
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Flatworm | Larva | Vertebrate
ENTRY:
Class of parasitic [[Flatworm|flatworms]] in the phylum Platyhelminthes, commonly known as tapeworms. Most species, including the best known, belong to the subclass Eucestoda, whose adults are ribbon-like and whose bodies consist of segments called proglottids, which are packages of eggs shed into the environment. The subclass Cestodaria consists mainly of fish parasites. Many cestodes have complex life histories with one or two intermediate hosts in which the [[Larva|larvae]] develop. Adults typically live in the digestive tracts of [[Vertebrate|vertebrates]]. Some six thousand species have been described. Tapeworms attach to the intestine with a scolex armed with hooks or suckers, and they absorb nutrients directly from the host's gut. Humans can be infected by eating undercooked pork, beef or fish.

=== KEY: Cetacean_3a279f
HEADWORD: Cetacean
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Artiodactyla | Blue whale | Dolphin | Mammal | Porpoise | Whale | Whaling
ENTRY:
Infraorder of marine [[Mammal|mammals]] that includes [[Whale|whales]], [[Dolphin|dolphins]] and [[Porpoise|porpoises]]. Cetaceans have fully aquatic life cycles, streamlined fish-like bodies and tail flukes, and they must surface to breathe. Toothed whales use echolocation. About 90 living species are recognized, 75 toothed whales (Odontoceti) and 15 baleen whales (Mysticeti). The [[Blue whale]] is the largest animal known to have existed, with a maximum confirmed length of 29.9 meters (98 feet). Genetic and fossil evidence place cetaceans within the [[Artiodactyla|even-toed ungulates]], most closely related to hippopotamids. Cetaceans were extensively hunted for meat, blubber and oil after the Industrial Revolution. Although the International Whaling Commission agreed to halt [[Whaling|commercial whaling]], hunting continues under quotas and for scientific research.

Landlocked country in [[Central Africa]]. Its capital and largest city is [[N'Djamena]], and its population is 20 million. === KEY: Chain_532055
HEADWORD: Chain
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Chain mail | Chainsaw | Rope
ENTRY:
Serial assembly of connected links, typically of metal. Like a [[Rope|rope]], a chain is flexible and curved in compression but linear, rigid and load-bearing in tension. Chains designed for lifting, pulling or securing have torus-shaped links that bend in two dimensions. Chains for transferring power in machines have links that mesh with sprocket teeth and bend in only one dimension; these are known as roller chains. Bicycle chains transfer power from the pedals to the drive wheel, and timing chains transfer rotation from the crankshaft to an engine's valves and ignition system. Uses range from jewelry and [[Chain mail]] to [[Chainsaw|chainsaws]]. Two chains may be joined with a quick link, carabiner or shackle.

=== KEY: Chain_mail_89148c
HEADWORD: Chain mail
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Armour | Celts | Firearm | Plate armour | Shark
ENTRY:
Personal [[Armour|armor]] of small metal rings linked together in a pattern to form a mesh. Mail was widely used in European militaries from the 3rd century BC to the 16th century AD, and in Asia, Africa and the Middle East until the 18th century. A coat of mail is often called a hauberk. The earliest surviving examples, dated to the 3rd century BC, were found in the Carpathian Basin, and its invention is commonly credited to the [[Celts]]. Its use declined in the 16th and 17th centuries as [[Plate armour|plate armor]] and more advanced [[Firearm|firearms]] were developed. Mail is still used in industries such as butchery and as protection against the bites of [[Shark|sharks]].

=== KEY: Chain_rule_eb0c84
HEADWORD: Chain rule
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Calculus | Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz
ENTRY:
Formula in [[Calculus|calculus]] for differentiating the composition of two differentiable functions. If z depends on y, and y depends on x, the derivative of z with respect to x is the product of the derivatives dz/dy and dy/dx, so that dz/dx = dz/dy · dy/dx. In integration, the counterpart of the chain rule is the substitution rule. The rule seems to have first been used by [[Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz|Leibniz]], who also devised its common notation.

=== KEY: Chainsaw_21de03
HEADWORD: Chainsaw
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Chain | Saw
ENTRY:
Portable handheld power [[Saw|saw]] that cuts with teeth attached to a [[Chain|chain]] rotating along a guide bar. Modern chainsaws are typically gasoline or electric and are used for tree felling, pruning, firewood harvesting, and cutting concrete and ice. A chainsaw comprises an engine, a drive mechanism, a guide bar, a cutting chain, a tensioning mechanism and safety features. Its precursors were first used in surgery, where Scottish doctors John Aitken and James Jeffray pioneered a flexible chain saw around 1783–1785. Patents for wood chainsaws began in the late 19th century. The first portable chainsaw was developed and patented in 1918 by Canadian millwright James Shand.

Seat typically designed for one person, with one or more legs, a flat or slightly angled seat and a back rest. It may be made of [[Wood|wood]], [[Metal|metal]] or synthetic materials, and may be padded or upholstered. An armchair has fixed armrests; a recliner has a mechanism that lowers the back and raises a footrest; a rocking chair has legs fixed to two curved slats; and a [[Wheelchair|wheelchair]] has wheels fixed to an axis under the seat. Chairs have been used since antiquity, but for centuries they were a symbol of state and dignity rather than ordinary furniture. Chairs became common only in the 16th century, when people had sat on chests, benches and stools. The chair is still used as an emblem of authority in the House of Commons of the [[United Kingdom]] and [[Canada]].

=== KEY: Chalcolithic_d6805d
HEADWORD: Chalcolithic
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Bronze Age | Copper | Neolithic | Serbia | Smelting
ENTRY:
Archaeological period, also called the Copper Age or Eneolithic, that followed the [[Neolithic]] and preceded the [[Bronze Age]]. It was marked by increasing use of [[Smelting|smelted]] [[Copper|copper]], although stone tools were still predominant. Cold hammering of near-pure copper ore began around 6,500 BC, as in the Great Lakes Old Copper complex of North America. At Belovode in [[Serbia]] has been found the world's oldest securely dated evidence of high-temperature copper smelting, from c. 5,000 BC. In Europe the transition to the Bronze Age occurred between the late 5th and late 3rd millennium BC. In Britain the Chalcolithic lasted from about 2,500 to 2,200 BC, a short period marked by the first copper and gold objects.

=== KEY: Chaldea_412b10
HEADWORD: Chaldea
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Babylonia | Euphrates | Levant | Mesopotamia | Tigris
ENTRY:
Region, probably in the marshy land of southern [[Mesopotamia]]. West Semitic-speaking migrants from the [[Levant]] arrived between the 11th and 9th centuries BC. The Kaldu, later known as Chaldeans, settled chiefly on the left bank of the [[Euphrates]] in the far southeast of [[Babylonia]]. Strictly, Chaldea was the plain formed by the Euphrates and [[Tigris]] deposits, about 640 kilometers (400 mi) long and averaging 160 km (100 mi) wide, although Hebrew literature used the name loosely for all of southern Mesopotamia. A Chaldean dynasty ruled Babylonia from 626 BC to 539 BC, at the height of the Neo-Babylonian Empire. The Chaldeans should not be confused with the modern Chaldean Catholic Church, whose adherents are predominantly ethnic Assyrians.
