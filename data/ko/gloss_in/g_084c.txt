=== KEY: Mandrill_d5413f

PERSON: no

ENTRY:
Large [[Old World monkey]] of west central Africa, one of the most colorful mammals, with red and blue skin on its face and rump. Males are larger, with longer canines and brighter coloring. Mandrills mainly live in [[Tropical rainforest|tropical rainforests]], forage on the ground for fruit and seeds, and form hordes that can number in the hundreds, with females at the core of each group. The species is classified as vulnerable by the IUCN, which cites habitat destruction and bushmeat hunting as its main threats. [[Gabon]] is considered its stronghold.

=== KEY: Maned_wolf_d9fd6f
HEADWORD: Maned wolf
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Argentina | Bolivia | Brazil | Canidae | Paraguay | Stem cell
ENTRY:
Large [[Canidae|canid]] of South America, neither a fox nor a wolf despite its foxlike markings. It weighs 20–30 kg and stands up to 110 cm at the withers. The solitary, crepuscular omnivore helps disperse fruits such as the wolf apple (*Solanum lycocarpum*). It lives in open grassland and savanna in [[Brazil]], [[Paraguay]], [[Argentina]], and [[Bolivia]]. The IUCN lists it as near threatened. In 2011 a female hit by a truck received [[Stem cell|stem cell]] treatment at the Zoo Brasília, the first recorded use of stem cells to heal injuries in a wild animal.

=== KEY: Manfred_von_Richthofen_20f1fa
HEADWORD: Richthofen, Manfred von
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Prussia | World War I | Wrocław
ENTRY:
German [[World War I]] fighter pilot, known as the Red Baron. Born near [[Wrocław]] (then Breslau) into the [[Prussia|Prussian]] nobility, he was a *Freiherr*, usually translated as baron. A former cavalryman, he transferred to the air service in 1915 and led the fighter unit Jasta 11 in 1917 before commanding Jagdgeschwader I, known as the Flying Circus. Credited with 80 air combat victories, the highest total of the war, he painted his aircraft red, which gave rise to his nickname. He was shot down and killed near Vaux-sur-Somme, France, and the circumstances of his death have been widely debated.

=== KEY: Manga_89e232
HEADWORD: Manga
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Japan | Osamu Tezuka | Science fiction
ENTRY:
Comics or graphic novels originating in [[Japan]]. Manga are typically printed in black and white, serialized in large anthology magazines, and collected in *tankōbon* volumes. Genres range from action, romance, and horror to [[Science fiction|science fiction]] and fantasy. Manga has been a major part of Japan's publishing industry since the 1950s. The Japanese market was valued at ¥586.4 billion in 1995 and reached a record ¥704.3 billion in 2024, with digital manga accounting for about ¥500 billion. In 2021 manga made up 76% of US comics and graphic novel sales, according to NPD BookScan. Pioneers such as [[Osamu Tezuka]] refined cinematic storytelling in the 1950s and 1960s. Related comics in other languages include manhua in Chinese and manhwa in Korean.

=== KEY: Manganese_b6372f
HEADWORD: Manganese
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Iron | Mineral | Parkinson's disease | Photosynthesis | Stainless steel | Steelmaking | Transition metal
ENTRY:
[[Transition metal]] with atomic number 25 (symbol Mn). Hard, brittle and silvery, manganese is usually found in [[Mineral|minerals]] combined with [[Iron|iron]]; it was first isolated in 1774 by Swedish chemist Johan Gottlieb Gahn. Over 90% of global manganese consumption goes into [[Steelmaking|steelmaking]] as a deoxidizer and desulfurizer, improving strength, hardness and wear resistance in [[Stainless steel|stainless steels]]. Manganese compounds are also used in batteries, fertilizers, pigments and water treatment. It is an essential trace element, a cofactor for enzymes involved in metabolism, antioxidant defense and bone formation. In plants it forms part of the oxygen-evolving complex that splits water in [[Photosynthesis|photosynthesis]]. Excess exposure can cause neurotoxicity resembling [[Parkinson's disease]].

=== KEY: Mango_95f8c2
HEADWORD: Mango
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Bangladesh | Calorie | India | Indian subcontinent | Myanmar | Pakistan | Philippines | Protein (nutrient)
ENTRY:
Tropical stone fruit of the tree *Mangifera indica*. It originated between the northeastern part of the [[Indian subcontinent]] and [[Myanmar]] and has been cultivated in South and Southeast Asia since ancient times. Several hundred cultivars vary in size, shape, sweetness, and skin and flesh color. Mango is the national fruit of [[India]], [[Pakistan]] and the [[Philippines]], and the mango tree is the national tree of [[Bangladesh]]. Trees grow 30–40 meters tall, and some specimens still fruit after 300 years. A 100-gram serving of raw mango provides about 60 [[Calorie|calories]] and 1 gram of [[Protein (nutrient)|protein]].

=== KEY: Mangosteen_4a626b
HEADWORD: Mangosteen
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Borneo | Malay Peninsula | Tannin
ENTRY:
Tropical evergreen tree with an edible fruit, *Garcinia mangostana*. Native to the [[Malay Peninsula]] and [[Borneo]], it has been cultivated across tropical Asia since ancient times and grows 6 to 25 meters tall. The fruit has an inedible purplish rind enclosing sweet, tangy, fragrant white segments. Polyphenols in the rind, including xanthones and [[Tannin|tannins]], make immature fruit astringent and deter pests.

=== KEY: Mangrove_d37a45
HEADWORD: Mangrove
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Brackish water | Carbon | Shrub | Tsunami
ENTRY:
[[Shrub]] or tree growing mainly in coastal saline or [[Brackish water|brackish water]]. Mangroves occur in tropical and subtropical coastal areas, mostly between latitudes 30° N and 30° S, and tolerate salt through a complex salt filtration system and specialized root systems. Their forests stabilize shorelines and protect nearby areas from [[Tsunami|tsunamis]] and extreme weather, and they are effective at [[Carbon|carbon]] sequestration and storage. In 2025 the global area was estimated at 15.9 million hectares, with Indonesia holding the largest extent at 3.40 million ha. Human activity continues to cause losses, with a global annual deforestation rate estimated at 0.16%, and per-country rates as high as 0.70%. Restoration success may depend on engagement with local stakeholders.

=== KEY: Manhattan_1834cd
HEADWORD: Manhattan
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: County (United States) | Nasdaq | New York (state) | New York City | New York Stock Exchange | Statue of Liberty | Wall Street
ENTRY:
Borough of [[New York City]] and [[County (United States)|county]] in [[New York (state)|New York State]] (pop., 2020: 1,694,250). Smallest of the city's five boroughs by area and the most densely populated, it serves as its economic and administrative center. Dutch colonists set up a trading post on Manhattan Island in 1624, which was named New Amsterdam in 1626. New York, based in Lower Manhattan, was the U.S. capital from 1785 to 1790. The [[Statue of Liberty]] in New York Harbor greeted millions of immigrants. The borough houses [[Wall Street]], the [[New York Stock Exchange]] and [[Nasdaq]].

=== KEY: Manhattan_Project_a620e9
HEADWORD: Manhattan Project
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Hiroshima | J. Robert Oppenheimer | Nagasaki | New Mexico | Plutonium | Trinity (nuclear test) | Uranium | World War II
ENTRY:
Allied nuclear weapons program of [[World War II]]. Led by the United States with the United Kingdom and Canada, the project employed nearly 130,000 people at its peak and cost nearly US$2 billion. Major General Leslie Groves directed it from 1942 to 1946, while [[J. Robert Oppenheimer]] directed the Los Alamos Laboratory in [[New Mexico]], where the bombs were designed. [[Uranium|Enriched uranium]] was produced in Tennessee, and [[Plutonium|plutonium]] was made in the world's first industrial-scale nuclear reactors at Hanford in Washington. The first nuclear detonation, the [[Trinity (nuclear test)|Trinity test]], took place in New Mexico on July 16, 1945. Bombs were used against [[Hiroshima]] and [[Nagasaki]] in August 1945, hastening the end of the Pacific War. The project was succeeded by the United States Atomic Energy Commission in January 1947.
