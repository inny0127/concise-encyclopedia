Mutiny aboard the [[Royal Navy]] ship *HMS Bounty* on April 28, 1789. Disaffected crewmen led by Fletcher Christian seized the ship from its captain, William Bligh, and set him and 18 loyalists adrift in an open launch. The *Bounty* had sailed to collect [[Breadfruit|breadfruit]] plants on [[Tahiti]], and a five-month stay there made many men less amenable to naval discipline. Descendants of the mutineers and their Tahitian companions have lived on Pitcairn into the 21st century.

=== KEY: Myanmar_217fa5
HEADWORD: Myanmar
PERSON: no

ENTRY:
Country (pop., 2024: about 51 million) in northwest [[Southeast Asia]]; capital, Naypyidaw. Officially the Republic of the Union of Myanmar and also called Burma (the official English name until 1989), it is the largest country by area in Mainland Southeast Asia. Its largest city is [[Yangon]] (formerly Rangoon).

Early civilizations included the Tibeto-Burman-speaking [[Pyu city-states]] and the Mon kingdoms. The Bamar entered the upper Irrawaddy valley in the 9th century, and after the [[Pagan Kingdom]] was established in the 1050s, Burmese language and culture and Theravada Buddhism became dominant. Reunified by the Taungoo dynasty in the 16th century, it briefly became the largest empire in Southeast Asian history. The [[Konbaung dynasty]] ruled in the early 19th century; after three Anglo-Burmese Wars, Britain made the country a colony. Independence came in 1948. A coup in 1962 brought military rule, and a military junta governed directly until 2011. The National League for Democracy, led by [[Aung San Suu Kyi]], took power after the 2015 election. After the NLD won the 2020 election, the military again seized power in the 2021 coup, citing alleged electoral irregularities; the coup led to widespread protests and an ongoing [[Civil war|civil war]].

In 1989 the military government changed the English name from Burma to Myanmar. Opposition groups and some countries still use Burma, while the UN and [[ASEAN]] use Myanmar.

The country has resources including jade, gems, oil, natural gas, and teak, but suffers from political instability, corruption, and poor infrastructure, and is classified as one of the least developed countries. The UN High Commissioner for Refugees counts over 1.3 million refugees and asylum seekers and over 3.5 million internally displaced people.

=== KEY: Mycelium_ca9a1b
HEADWORD: Mycelium
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Carbon dioxide | Fungus | Hypha | Petroleum | Polystyrene
ENTRY:
Root-like structure of a [[Fungus|fungus]], consisting of a mass of branching, thread-like [[Hypha|hyphae]]. Mycelium may be too small to see, or may span thousands of acres, as in *Armillaria*. A fungus absorbs nutrients through its mycelium in two stages: the hyphae secrete enzymes that break food down into smaller units, which are then absorbed. Mycelia are vital to ecosystems for decomposing plant material, and their growth releases [[Carbon dioxide|carbon dioxide]] into the atmosphere. Mycorrhizal mycelium increases the efficiency of water and nutrient absorption for most plants. Mycelium grown on agricultural waste can be used to produce alternatives to [[Polystyrene|polystyrene]] packaging and artificial leather. Fungi can eradicate some pollutants, such as [[Petroleum|petroleum]] products and some pesticides, in a process called mycoremediation.

=== KEY: Mycenaean_Greece_b69340
HEADWORD: Mycenaean Greece
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Athens | Bronze Age | Crete | Sea Peoples
ENTRY:
Last phase of the [[Bronze Age]] in ancient Greece, c. 1750–1050 BC, and the first advanced, distinctively Greek civilization on the mainland. Its palatial states, named for the prominent site of Mycenae, included Pylos, Tiryns, Midea, Thebes and [[Athens]]. The Mycenaeans were stimulated by contact with [[Crete|Minoan Crete]] and traded across the Mediterranean. A warrior elite ruled palace-centered states under a king called the wanax. Their syllabic script, Linear B, offers the first written records of the Greek language. Their religion included several deities later found in the Olympic pantheon. The civilization collapsed into the Greek Dark Ages, with proposed causes including the Dorian invasion, the [[Sea Peoples]] and natural disasters. Its world became the setting of much ancient Greek literature, including the Trojan Epic Cycle.

=== KEY: Mycology_a3f41e
HEADWORD: Mycology
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Antibiotic | Fungus | Pathogen | Penicillin | Traditional medicine | Yeast
ENTRY:
Branch of biology concerned with the study of [[Fungus|fungi]], including their taxonomy, genetics, biochemical properties and use by humans. Fungi can be a source of tinder, food, [[Traditional medicine|traditional medicine]] and infection. [[Yeast|Yeasts]] are among the most heavily used fungi, particularly in food manufacturing. Mycology overlaps with phytopathology, the study of plant diseases, because most [[Pathogen|plant pathogens]] are fungi. Some fungi produce [[Antibiotic|antibiotics]], such as [[Penicillin]], and others serve as biological control agents against crop diseases. Many fungi break down lignin, a durable component of wood, and so play a critical role in the global carbon cycle. Field meetings to find fungi are called "forays". A biologist who specializes in fungi is called a mycologist.

=== KEY: Myocardial_infarction_e33bb1
HEADWORD: Myocardial infarction
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Artery | Aspirin | Atherosclerosis | Cardiac arrest | Coronary artery disease | Electrocardiography | Heart failure
ENTRY:
Death (infarction) of part of the heart muscle caused by decreased or stopped blood flow in a coronary artery; commonly called a heart attack. The most common symptom is retrosternal chest pain or discomfort that classically radiates to the left shoulder, arm, or jaw. Other symptoms include shortness of breath, nausea, and a cold sweat. Women more often present without chest pain. It may cause [[Heart failure|heart failure]], an irregular heartbeat, cardiogenic shock, or [[Cardiac arrest|cardiac arrest]].

Most cases result from [[Coronary artery disease|coronary artery disease]]; the usual mechanism is complete blockage of a coronary [[Artery|artery]] by the rupture of an [[Atherosclerosis|atherosclerotic]] plaque. Risk factors include high blood pressure, smoking, diabetes, obesity, high cholesterol, poor diet, and excessive alcohol intake. Less commonly, coronary artery spasms caused by cocaine, significant emotional stress (Takotsubo syndrome), or extreme cold are responsible.

Diagnosis uses [[Electrocardiography|electrocardiograms]], blood tests for troponin, and coronary angiography. An ECG distinguishes ST elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) from non-ST elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI). Treatment is time-critical. [[Aspirin]] is an appropriate immediate treatment. In a STEMI, treatment aims to restore blood flow through percutaneous coronary intervention, or through thrombolysis with medications. NSTEMI is often managed with the blood thinner heparin. Coronary artery bypass surgery may be recommended for people with multiple blocked arteries and diabetes. After an infarction, lifestyle changes and long-term aspirin, beta blockers, and statins are typically recommended.

About 15.9 million myocardial infarctions occurred worldwide in 2015. STEMIs occur about twice as often in men as in women, and in the developed world the risk of death after a STEMI is about 10%.

=== KEY: Myriapoda_692a22
HEADWORD: Myriapoda
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Arthropod | Cambrian | Centipede | Fossil | Millipede | Ordovician | Predation
ENTRY:
Subphylum of terrestrial [[Arthropod|arthropods]], including [[Millipede|millipedes]] and [[Centipede|centipedes]]. The group contains about 13,000 species, all of them terrestrial. Molecular evidence and [[Fossil|fossils]] suggest diversification in the [[Cambrian]] period, and trace fossils show myriapods inhabited land by the [[Ordovician]]. The oldest known body fossil dates to approximately 427.0 ± 4.5 million years ago. Millipedes have body segments fused in pairs, so each appears to bear two pairs of legs. Most have a single pair of antennae and simple eyes, and a few centipedes and millipedes are bioluminescent. The phylogenetic classification of myriapods is still debated.

=== KEY: Mystical_or_religious_experience_8612ef
HEADWORD: Mystical or religious experience
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Aldous Huxley | William James
ENTRY:
Subjective experience, also called spiritual or sacred experience, that is interpreted within a religious framework. In a strict sense, the term refers to an ecstatic unitive experience, or nonduality, of self and other objects. The concept developed in the 19th century as a defense against growing rationalism. [[William James]] popularized it in *The Varieties of Religious Experience* and identified four qualities: ineffability, noetic quality, transiency and passivity. [[Aldous Huxley]] and James held that mystical experiences share a common core. Rudolf Otto described numinous experiences as combining fear and trembling with fascination. From the 1970s, a constructionist approach became dominant, holding that experiences are shaped by pre-existing frames of reference.

=== KEY: Mysticism_c1d221
HEADWORD: Mysticism
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Buddhism | Christianity | Hinduism | Judaism | Sufism | Western esotericism
ENTRY:
Religious traditions of human transformation aided by various practices and religious experiences. Popularly, mysticism means an ecstatic unitive experience of becoming one with God or the Absolute. Since the 1970s, scholars have noted that it can also mean insight into ultimate or hidden truths, as in Buddhist awakening, or altered states such as samadhi. Mystical traditions appear in [[Christianity]], [[Judaism]], [[Sufism]], [[Buddhism]], [[Hinduism]] and [[Western esotericism]]. Feminist critiques highlight gender biases in traditional accounts of mysticism.

=== KEY: Myth_5e920a
HEADWORD: Myth
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Ancient Greek | Creation myth | Folklore | Legend | Plato | Ritual
ENTRY:
Genre of [[Folklore|folklore]] consisting primarily of narratives that play a fundamental role in a society. For scholars this differs from the ordinary sense of the term, a belief that is not true: the veracity of a piece of folklore is irrelevant to whether it constitutes a myth.

Myths are often endorsed by religious and secular authorities and may be natural or supernatural in character. [[Creation myth|Creation myths]] take place in a primordial age before the world achieved its later form, origin myths explain how a society's customs, institutions, and taboos were established and sanctified, and national myths symbolize a nation's values. The relationship between the recital of myths and the enactment of [[Ritual|rituals]] is complex.

Main characters are usually gods, demigods, and other supernatural figures; stories of ordinary humans are usually classed as [[Legend|legends]]. Myths are sometimes distinguished from legends in that they deal with gods, usually have no historical basis, and are set in a remote past very different from the present. As faiths change, myths may come to be considered folktales. Definitions vary among scholars. The Finnish folklorist Lauri Honko describes a myth as a religious account of the world's beginning and the exemplary deeds of the gods, which expresses a society's religious values and provides patterns of behavior to imitate.

The word comes from [[Ancient Greek]] *mythos*, meaning "speech", "narrative", or "fiction". [[Plato]] used *mythología* as a general term for fiction or story-telling of any kind. English adopted *mythology* in the 15th century, first attested in John Lydgate's *Troy Book* (c. 1425), and *myth* itself appeared only in 1830.

HEADWORD: Mythologies of the Indigenous peoples of the Americas
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Coyote (mythology) | Creation myth | Indigenous peoples of the Americas
ENTRY:
Narratives, beliefs and sacred oral traditions of the [[Indigenous peoples of the Americas]], which differ widely by region. North American myths commonly feature a close relation to nature and animals, a belief in a Great Spirit, and [[Creation myth|creation stories]]. Animals often shape-shift, and marriages between humans and bears are common. Tricksters such as [[Coyote (mythology)|Coyote]] and the Plains figure Old Man provide humor and moral lessons. In the Andes, the Inca creator god Viracocha shaped humanity from stone at Lake Titicaca.

French civil code established in 1804 during the French Consulate, and still in force in [[France]]. === KEY: Narrative_0c036b
HEADWORD: Narrative
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Biography | Hero | Journalism | Literature
ENTRY:
Account of a series of related events or experiences, real or imagined, presented through words, images, or both. Also called a story or tale. A narrative may be nonfictional, as in memoir, [[Biography|biography]], news report, or documentary, or fictional, as in the fairy tale, fable, legend, thriller, or novel. It differs from a simple description of qualities, states, or situations in which no particular individuals are involved.

Narrative appears in speech, [[Literature|literature]], theater, dance, music and song, comics, [[Journalism|journalism]], animation, film, television, video games, and radio, and potentially even in static visual art such as painting, provided a sequence of events is presented. Narratives range from a single sentence, such as "the cat sat on the mat," to long series spanning multiple books or films.

The social activity of sharing narratives is called storytelling, most of which has historically been oral. Narration, the formal process of constructing a narrative, is one of the four traditional rhetorical modes of discourse, along with argumentation, description, and exposition.

Scholars identify basic elements of narrative, including character and conflict. Characters, who may be imaginary or based on real people, drive the plot. The protagonist is the main character, often opposed by an antagonist. In many traditional narratives the protagonist is a [[Hero|hero]] who fights for morally good causes. Conflict is the tension that drives characters' thoughts and actions, and in the narrow sense the major problem the protagonist faces. The word narrative entered English from French in the 15th century and derives from the Latin *narrare* ("to tell").

Solution concept in non-cooperative [[Game theory|game theory]]. A set of strategies is a Nash equilibrium when no player can gain by changing their own strategy while the others keep theirs unchanged. Confederate general and slave trader. He profited from the interstate slave trade, including a slave jail in Memphis. He enlisted as a private in 1861 and rose to general during the [[American Civil War]] without prior military training, earning a reputation for aggressive [[Cavalry|cavalry]] tactics. At Fort Pillow in April 1864, Confederate forces killed many Union troops, most of them Black soldiers, after the fort had effectively ceased resistance; historians differ on whether this resulted from explicit orders. After the war he became the first Grand Wizard of the [[Ku Klux Klan]] and in 1869 ordered its dissolution.
