PERSON: no

ENTRY:
Siamese kingdom in [[Southeast Asia]] (1351–1767), centered on the city of Ayutthaya. Considered the precursor of modern [[Thailand]], it emerged from a group of maritime city-states in the lower Chao Phraya valley. Its name derives from the [[Sanskrit]] Ayodhya. From 1569 to 1584 it was a vassal of Toungoo Burma under [[Bayinnaung]], then regained independence. In the 17th and 18th centuries it became a center of international trade. Under Narai (r. 1657–1688) Persian and then European influence was strong, and an embassy went to the court of [[Louis XIV]] in 1686. Cultural life centered on [[Theravada]] Buddhism. In 1767, after a 14-month siege, Burmese forces destroyed the city, ending the 417-year-old kingdom. Within fifteen years the seat of authority had moved to Thonburi and [[Bangkok]].

=== KEY: Azalea_19aefc

ENTRY:
Flowering shrub in the genus [[Rhododendron]] and family [[Ericaceae]]. Azaleas bloom in spring, often for several weeks, and tolerate shade. They do best in well-drained acidic soil at 4.5–6.0 pH and need little fertilizer. Breeders have produced thousands of cultivars, which are propagated by cuttings. *Azalea indica* was first introduced to the outdoor landscape in the United States in the 1830s, at a rice plantation near [[Charleston, South Carolina]]. The plant is highly toxic, and in Turkey bees fed on its nectar produce an occasionally lethal honey known as mad honey. The azalea is one of the symbols of [[São Paulo]], and in China it is known as "thinking of home bush", immortalized in the poetry of [[Du Fu]].

Landlocked country in the South Caucasus, at the boundary of Western Asia and Eastern Europe. Its capital and largest city is [[Baku]], on the [[Caspian Sea]]. Turkic language of the Oghuz branch, also known as Azeri. It is the only official language of the [[Azerbaijan|Republic of Azerbaijan]] and one of 14 official languages of [[Dagestan]], a Russian federal subject. It lacks official status in Iran, where most Iranian Azerbaijanis live. North Azerbaijani, based on the Shirvani dialect, has used the [[Latin script]] since independence in 1991, while South Azerbaijani has always used the Perso-Arabic script. === KEY: Aztec_Empire_19970c

Alliance of three Nahua city-states in central Mexico (1428–1521). Also called the Triple Alliance, it was formed from the victorious factions of a civil war against Azcapotzalco, and [[Tenochtitlan]] became militarily dominant. === KEY: Aztec_religion_eaa7b6
HEADWORD: Aztec religion
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Aztec Empire | Day of the Dead | Human sacrifice | Tenochtitlan | Venus
ENTRY:
Polytheistic and monistic pantheism of the [[Aztec Empire]]. Nahua metaphysics centered on teotl, a sacred power construed as the supreme god Ometeotl. Priests and the upper classes held monistic views, while popular religion emphasized the mythological and polytheistic aspects of a pantheon of lesser gods and natural forces. The most important deities, including Huitzilopochtli, were worshiped at the Templo Mayor in [[Tenochtitlan]], and conquered peoples could add the imperial god to their own pantheons. The cosmology divided the world into thirteen heavens and nine earthly layers. The Sun, the Moon and the planet [[Venus]] were the most important celestial entities. Public ritual included food, dance, ceremonial warfare and [[Human sacrifice|human sacrifice]]. After the Spanish conquest the population was obliged to convert to Catholicism, and the two religions syncretized, as seen in the [[Day of the Dead]].

=== KEY: Aztecs_5adc76
HEADWORD: Aztecs
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Aztec Empire | Hernán Cortés | Maize | Mexico City | Sacrifice | Smallpox | Tenochtitlan
ENTRY:
Nahuatl-speaking peoples and Mesoamerican civilization of central Mexico, c. 1300–1521. The term is often restricted to the Mexica of Tenochtitlan but is also used for the Nahua peoples of central Mexico in general. It was not an endonym; some scholars have criticized it and proposed alternatives such as Culhua-Mexica.

Aztec society was organized into city-states (altepetl), divided between nobility (pipiltin) and commoners (macehualtin), and shared Mesoamerican traits including [[Maize]] cultivation, a pantheon, and a calendric system. The Mexica were late arrivals to the Valley of Mexico and founded [[Tenochtitlan]] on islets in Lake Texcoco, around 1325 by one account. In 1427 Tenochtitlan, Texcoco, and Tlacopan formed the Triple Alliance, or [[Aztec Empire]], to defeat the Tepanec state of Azcapotzalco. Texcoco and Tlacopan soon became junior partners. The empire was never a territorial state held by garrisons: it dominated client city-states by installing friendly rulers, arranging marriage alliances, and extracting taxes, and it extended its reach through trade and conquest. Religion included human [[Sacrifice]]; excavations at the Templo Mayor have uncovered skull racks.

The empire reached its greatest extent in 1519, when Spanish forces led by [[Hernán Cortés]] arrived. Cortés allied with city-states hostile to the Mexica, and epidemics including [[Smallpox]] struck the population. Tenochtitlan fell on 13 August 1521 with the capture of the emperor Cuauhtémoc, and the Spanish founded [[Mexico City]] on its ruins, ruling through local nobles. Aztec history is known from archaeology, Indigenous writings, Spanish eyewitness accounts, and works such as the bilingual *Florentine Codex*, compiled by the friar Bernardino de Sahagún with Aztec informants.

=== KEY: B_F_Skinner_6132ac

PERSON: yes

ENTRY:
American psychologist, behaviorist and social philosopher. He founded the experimental analysis of behavior, a school of experimental research [[Psychology|psychology]], and developed the philosophy of radical [[Behaviorism]]. Skinner used operant conditioning to strengthen behavior and considered the rate of response the most effective measure of response strength. To study it he invented the operant conditioning chamber, known as the Skinner box, and the cumulative recorder. With Charles Ferster he produced his most influential experimental work, outlined in *Schedules of Reinforcement* (1957). He was Edgar Pierce Professor of Psychology at [[Harvard University]] from 1948 until his retirement in 1974. His utopian novel *Walden Two* (1948) envisioned a community designed on his ideas, and *Verbal Behavior* (1958) analyzed human behavior. Critics, including [[Noam Chomsky]], challenged the application of operant principles to language. A June 2002 survey listed him as the most influential psychologist of the 20th century.

=== KEY: Bacteria_4c0984
HEADWORD: Bacteria
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Archaea | Cholera | Earth's crust | Hot spring | Hydrothermal vent | Methane | Soil | Tuberculosis
ENTRY:
Domain of mostly single-celled prokaryotic microorganisms, typically a few micrometres long. Bacteria were among the first life forms on Earth, appearing about 4 billion years ago, and for about 3 billion years they and [[Archaea]] were the dominant forms of life. They inhabit nearly every habitat, including air, [[Soil]], water, acidic [[Hot spring|hot springs]], radioactive waste, and the deep biosphere of [[Earth's crust]].

Bacterial cells have no nucleus and rarely contain membrane-bound organelles; their DNA usually forms a circular chromosome. Most reproduce by binary fission. Shapes include spheres, rods, and spirals, and cell-wall structure divides them into Gram-positive and Gram-negative types. The word comes from the Greek for staff or cane, because the first ones discovered were rod-shaped. Once classed as plants, bacteria were recognized in the 1990s as one of two very different prokaryotic groups, the other being archaea. Although an estimated 43,000 species have been named, most have never been studied; 90% of research focuses on less than 1% of species.

Bacteria are essential to the nutrient cycle, recycling nutrients, fixing atmospheric nitrogen, and driving decomposition. Near [[Hydrothermal vent|hydrothermal vents]], extremophile bacteria convert compounds such as hydrogen sulphide and [[Methane]] into energy. Eukaryotic cells arose when ancient bacteria entered endosymbiotic associations with their ancestors, giving rise to mitochondria and, later, chloroplasts. Humans carry approximately 10¹³ to 10¹⁴ bacteria, mostly in the gut; most are harmless or beneficial. Pathogenic species cause [[Cholera]], syphilis, anthrax, leprosy, [[Tuberculosis]], tetanus, and bubonic plague. Antibiotics treat bacterial infections, but their use in farming has made resistance a growing problem. Bacteria also serve in sewage treatment, cheese and yogurt fermentation, mining of gold, palladium, and copper, and biotechnology.

=== KEY: Ballistics_6cab82
HEADWORD: Ballistics
PERSON: no

ENTRY:
Field of [[Mechanics|mechanics]] concerned with the launching, flight behavior and impact effects of projectiles, especially weapon munitions. The word derives from the Greek *ballein*, "to throw." A ballistic missile is guided only during the brief initial powered phase, after which its trajectory is governed by classical mechanics. Ballistics was placed on a mathematical basis by [[Isaac Newton]] in *Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica* (1687), whose laws of motion and gravity made trajectories predictable. Capital and largest city of Thailand (pop., 2024: 9.1 million), in the Chao Phraya River delta in central Thailand. Known in Thai as Krung Thep Maha Nakhon, it covers 1,568.7 sq km. The surrounding Bangkok Metropolitan Region has over 17.4 million people (2021 estimate), about 25% of Thailand's population, making Bangkok a [[Megacity|megacity]] that dwarfs the country's other urban centers.

The site began as a village and customs outpost under Ayutthaya in the early 15th century. Absolute monarchy was abolished in 1932, and the city has since been the stage for coups, uprisings, and protests, notably in 1973, 1976, 1992, 2006–13, and 2020. Bangkok was bombed by the Allies in [[World War II]], and rapid growth followed with U.S. aid and investment.

The Asian investment boom of the 1980s and 1990s drew regional corporate headquarters, and the city is now a regional center of finance, business, health care, and the arts. Landmarks include the Grand Palace and the temples Wat Arun and Wat Pho; it is also known for street life and its [[Red-light district|red-light districts]]. Rapid growth with little [[Urban planning|planning]] left inadequate roads and chronic congestion, which caused severe [[Air pollution]] in the 1990s. The city now operates 10 urban rail lines, but congestion persists.
