HEADWORD: The Birth of Venus
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Florence | Sandro Botticelli | Scallop | Uffizi | Venus (mythology)
ENTRY:
Painting by [[Sandro Botticelli]], probably executed in the mid-1480s, showing the goddess [[Venus (mythology)|Venus]] arriving on the shore after her birth from the sea. She stands nude on a giant [[Scallop]] shell as the wind god Zephyrus blows her toward land, and a Hora holds a cloak to cover her. The painting is in the [[Uffizi]] Gallery in [[Florence]]. Its large scale and the prominence of its nude female figure were virtually unprecedented in Western art since classical antiquity. The title *Birth of Venus* was applied only in the 19th century, although Vasari had identified the subject. Its appeal is sensory and accessible, and its main meaning is a straightforward treatment of a Greek mythological scene.

HEADWORD: The Book of Healing
PERSON: no

ENTRY:
Scientific and philosophical encyclopedia by Avicenna, begun in 1014, completed around 1020 and published in 1027. Intended to cure ignorance of the soul, it is not concerned with medicine, which is treated in the separate [[The Canon of Medicine]]. The book is divided into four parts: [[Logic]], natural sciences, [[Mathematics]] and [[Metaphysics]]. It was influenced by [[Aristotle]], [[Ptolemy]] and earlier thinkers such as [[Al-Kindi]]. Avicenna was the first to divide human perception into five external and five internal senses, and he described the effect of emotions on the body.

=== KEY: The_Buddha_4d616a
HEADWORD: Buddha
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Ashoka | Bihar | Buddhism | Four Noble Truths | Indo-Gangetic Plain | Nepal | Oral tradition | Uttar Pradesh
ENTRY:
Wandering religious teacher and founder of [[Buddhism]], of the eastern [[Indo-Gangetic Plain|Indo-Gangetic Plains]], 6th or 5th century BC. Born Siddhartha Gautama, he is known as the Buddha, "the awakened one"; the word is a title for those who have attained bodhi (awakening), not a personal name.

According to Buddhist tradition, he was born in Lumbini, in what is now [[Nepal]], to royal parents of the Shakya clan, but renounced home life to become a wandering ascetic. After a life of mendicancy, asceticism, and meditation, he attained nirvana at Bodh Gaya, in what is now [[Bihar]], India. He then wandered through the lower Indo-Gangetic Plain, teaching and building a monastic order (sangha). Tradition holds that he died at Kushinagar, in modern [[Uttar Pradesh]], and reached parinirvana, "final release from conditioned existence".

He taught a Middle Way between sensual indulgence and severe asceticism, leading to freedom from ignorance, craving, rebirth, and suffering. His core teachings are summarized in the [[Four Noble Truths]] and the Noble Eightfold Path. Other key concepts are the five skandhas and dependent origination. The earliest attestation of the title Buddha is from the 3rd century BC, when edicts of [[Ashoka]] mention the Buddha and Buddhism.

His teachings were compiled into the Vinaya Pitaka, on monastic discipline, and the Sutra Pitaka, discourses attributed to him, and passed down through an [[Oral tradition|oral tradition]]. Later generations added treatises, biographies, Jataka tales of his past lives, and the Mahayana sutras. Buddhism diversified into Theravada, Mahayana, and Vajrayana traditions, largely disappeared from India after the 8th century, and grew more prominent in Southeast and East Asia.

=== KEY: The_Canon_of_Medicine_d58ac3
HEADWORD: The Canon of Medicine
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Arabic | Aristotle | Galen | Hippocrates | Latin
ENTRY:
Medical encyclopedia in five books, compiled by Avicenna and completed in 1025. It presents the medical knowledge of the Islamic world, which had been influenced by [[Galen]] and by earlier Greco-Roman, Persian, Chinese and Indian medicine. Avicenna sought to fit Galen's tradition, and thereby that of [[Hippocrates]], into [[Aristotle]]'s natural philosophy. Written in [[Arabic]], it was translated into [[Latin]] in 12th-century Toledo and became a standard textbook for teaching in European universities into the early modern period. It also set the standards for medicine in medieval Europe and the Islamic world, and it remained a standard textbook through the 18th century in Europe. It is an important text in Unani medicine, a traditional medicine practiced in India.

=== KEY: The_Canterbury_Tales_2b5b04
HEADWORD: The Canterbury Tales
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: English literature | Geoffrey Chaucer | Latin | Middle English
ENTRY:
Anthology of twenty-four stories in [[Middle English]] by [[Geoffrey Chaucer]]. The stories are mostly in verse and are presented as a storytelling contest among pilgrims traveling from London to Canterbury to visit the shrine of Saint Thomas Becket at Canterbury Cathedral. They were written between 1387 and 1400. Chaucer planned about 120 stories, four from each pilgrim, but most scholars think the work was left incomplete. The Tales are regarded as Chaucer's magnum opus and one of the paramount works of [[English literature]]. They may have helped establish the English vernacular in mainstream literature, as opposed to French or [[Latin]]. The work survives in 84 manuscripts and four incunabula. William Caxton's 1476 printed edition was one of the first books printed in England; only 10 copies are known to exist.

ENTRY:
Political pamphlet by [[Karl Marx]] and [[Friedrich Engels]], first published in 1848. It was commissioned by the Communist League and published in London, amid the [[Revolutions of 1848]] in Europe. It states that "the history of all hitherto existing society is the history of class struggles," and argues that [[Capitalism|capitalism]] is marked by the exploitation of the proletariat by the ruling [[Bourgeoisie|bourgeoisie]]. It predicts that a revolution will bring [[Communism|communism]], a classless society in which "the free development of each is the condition for the free development of all." The work combines philosophical [[Materialism|materialism]] with the Hegelian [[Dialectic|dialectical method]] to analyze European society through successive modes of production. Its closing line, "Workers of the world, unite!," became a famous slogan of working-class solidarity, and the pamphlet has become one of the world's most influential political documents.

He published the concertos with [[Sonnet|sonnets]], possibly written by Vivaldi himself, that describe each season. The concertos are among the earliest and most detailed examples of program music. Each concerto has three movements, fast–slow–fast, and each sonnet has three matching sections. A performance of all four takes about 40–43 minutes. The first proper electrical recording was made in 1942 by Bernardino Molinari. It is the best known of Vivaldi's works.

PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: John Maynard Keynes | Macroeconomics
ENTRY:
Book by English economist [[John Maynard Keynes]], published in February 1936. It argued that an economy does not automatically reach full employment, even in equilibrium, and that employment depends on the level of aggregate demand rather than on the price of labor. The book is a sustained attack on the classical economics of its time. It introduced the consumption function, the principle of effective demand, liquidity preference and the multiplier. Its concepts gave [[Macroeconomics|macroeconomics]] a central place in economic theory and contributed much of its terminology. The book was interpreted as supporting government spending, budget deficits and countercyclical policy, and its influence is known as the "Keynesian Revolution." The work is pervaded by mistrust of the rationality of free-market decision-making.

ENTRY:
1642 painting by [[Rembrandt|Rembrandt van Rijn]], completed at the peak of the [[Dutch Golden Age]]. It depicts the Militia Company of District II moving out under Captain Frans Banninck Cocq and his lieutenant, Willem van Ruytenburch. Rembrandt transformed a conventional group portrait of a civic militia into a dramatic scene energized by light and shadow. The painting was commissioned around 1639 by Cocq and seventeen members of his guard, and Rembrandt was paid 1,600 guilders for it. The title is a misnomer, since the scene is not nocturnal. In 1715 the painting was trimmed on all four sides to fit between columns in the [[Amsterdam]] Town Hall, which removed two figures and part of the arch. It is now displayed in the Rijksmuseum, Amsterdam. Hidden in a cave during [[World War II]] for protection, it has undergone over 25 restorations.

=== KEY: The_Picture_of_Dorian_Gray_4acec7

PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Hedonism | Novel | Oscar Wilde | The Guardian
ENTRY:
1890 philosophical fiction and Gothic horror [[Novel|novel]] by Irish author [[Oscar Wilde]], his only novel. Basil Hallward, an artist infatuated with Dorian's beauty, paints his portrait, and Dorian is drawn into the [[Hedonism|hedonistic]] worldview of Lord Henry Wotton. Dorian sells his soul so that the portrait, rather than he himself, will age, and the portrait records every sin of his libertine life. The story first appeared in *Lippincott's Monthly Magazine* in July 1890, and the novel-length version was published in April 1891. Before publication an editor removed around five hundred words, including passages alluding to homosexuality. The book drew significant criticism, but it is now one of Wilde's best-known works. [[The Guardian]] listed it among the 100 best novels ever written in English.
