ENTRY:
Capital and largest city of [[Albania]], located in the center of the country. The site has been inhabited since the [[Iron Age]] and was annexed by the [[Roman Empire]] after the Illyrian Wars. Tirana was founded in 1614 by the Ottoman Albanian nobleman Sylejman Pasha Bargjini. It remained unimportant until the 20th century, when the Congress of Lushnjë made it the capital. Today it is the country's economic, financial and political center, and it hosts the government and parliament. It is among the wettest and sunniest cities in [[Europe]].

=== KEY: Tire_e4b17d
HEADWORD: Tire
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Aircraft | Axle | Bicycle | Friction | Locomotive | Natural rubber | Wheel
ENTRY:
Ring-shaped component surrounding a [[Wheel|wheel's]] rim, spelled tire in Commonwealth English. A tire transfers a vehicle's load from the [[Axle]] to the ground and provides [[Friction|traction]]. Most modern tires are pneumatic, with a tread for traction and a body that holds compressed air. They are made from synthetic and [[Natural rubber|natural rubber]], fabric and wire, along with carbon black. Early tires were metal bands fitted to wooden wheels, and [[Locomotive|locomotives]] still use metal tires. Pneumatic tires are used on cars, [[Bicycle|bicycles]], motorcycles, buses, trucks and [[Aircraft]]. Unmaintained tires can cause flat tires and blowouts, and waste tires release toxic chemicals if they are not properly recycled. Regular use also produces microplastic particles containing those chemicals.

=== KEY: Tissue_biology_9097d9
HEADWORD: Tissue
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Connective tissue | Epidermis (botany) | Ground tissue | Muscle | Nervous tissue | Organ (biology) | Photosynthesis | Vascular tissue
ENTRY:
Group of similar cells and their extracellular matrix, from the same embryonic origin, that performs a specific function. Tissues lie between cells and [[Organ (biology)|organs]], which are formed by grouping several tissues. The word derives from the French *tissu*, "fabric." The study of tissues is called histology; Xavier Bichat is considered its father. In plants, tissues fall into three systems: the [[Epidermis (botany)|epidermis]], [[Ground tissue]] and [[Vascular tissue]]. Meristematic tissue, found at growing tips, consists of actively dividing cells that increase a plant's length and thickness. Ground tissue makes nutrients by [[Photosynthesis]] and stores reserves. Animal tissues are classified as epithelial, [[Connective tissue]], [[Muscle]] and [[Nervous tissue]].

HEADWORD: Tit
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: North America | Northern Hemisphere | Parrot | Passerine
ENTRY:
Small, stocky woodland [[Passerine|passerine]] bird of the family Paridae, found mainly in the [[Northern Hemisphere]] and Africa. Eurasian and African members are called tits, while [[North America|North American]] species are called chickadees or titmice. The name titmouse is recorded from the 14th century and combines the Old English *mase* with *tit*, meaning something small. Tits are adaptable birds with short, stout bills and a mixed diet of seeds and insects, and many visit bird feeders near homes. After the corvids and [[Parrot|parrots]], they are among the most intelligent birds. They are territorial during the breeding season and often join mixed-species feeding flocks at other times.

=== KEY: Titan_moon_7dac51
HEADWORD: Titan
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Christiaan Huygens | Ganymede (moon) | Methane | Saturn | Solar System | Water cycle
ENTRY:
Largest moon of [[Saturn]] and second largest in the [[Solar System]], after Jupiter's [[Ganymede (moon)|Ganymede]]. [[Christiaan Huygens]] discovered it in 1655. Titan is the only moon known to have a dense atmosphere, denser than Earth's, and the only known object besides Earth with stable bodies of surface liquid. Its atmosphere is mainly nitrogen and [[Methane]], forming hydrocarbon clouds and haze. The Cassini–Huygens mission, which arrived in 2004, revealed hydrocarbon lakes in the polar regions. At about 94 K (−179 °C), its [[Water cycle|methane cycle]] nearly resembles Earth's water cycle, and it is sometimes called the most Earth-like object in the Solar System.

1997 American historical romance film by [[James Cameron]]. Loosely based on the sinking of RMS Titanic in 1912, it stars [[Leonardo DiCaprio]] and [[Kate Winslet]] as lovers from different social classes. With a production budget of $200 million, it was the most expensive film ever made at the time. It won eleven of its fourteen [[Academy Awards]] nominations, including Best Picture and Best Director. The first film to pass $1 billion worldwide, it remained the highest-grossing film until *[[Avatar (2009 film)|Avatar]]* (2009) surpassed it in 2010. In 2017 the [[Library of Congress]] selected it for the National Film Registry.

ENTRY:
British [[Ocean liner|ocean liner]] lost in 1912. Operated by the White Star Line, it sailed from [[Southampton]] for [[New York City]] on its maiden voyage and struck an iceberg on April 15, 1912. Of the 2,208 passengers and crew aboard, approximately 1,500 died (estimates vary), one of the deadliest peacetime sinkings of a single ship. Built by Harland and Wolff in [[Belfast]], it was the second of three Olympic-class liners. Its name derives from the [[Titans]] of Greek mythology. The disaster prompted major changes in maritime safety regulations.

=== KEY: Titanium_6c1aad
HEADWORD: Titanium
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Aluminium | Cornwall | Titanium dioxide | Titans | Transition metal
ENTRY:
Chemical element with atomic number 22 and symbol Ti. Titanium can be reduced to a lustrous [[Transition metal|transition metal]] with a silver color, low density and high strength, resistant to corrosion in sea water, aqua regia and chlorine. It was discovered in [[Cornwall]], Great Britain, by William Gregor in 1791 and named by Martin Heinrich Klaproth after the [[Titans]] of Greek mythology. The metal is extracted from its principal ores, mainly rutile and ilmenite, by the Kroll and Hunter processes. Its most common compound, [[Titanium dioxide]], is used in white pigments. Titanium has the highest tensile-strength-to-density ratio of any metallic element. Alloyed with [[Aluminium|aluminum]], vanadium, molybdenum and iron, it is used in jet engines, missiles, spacecraft, desalination plants, golf clubs and consumer electronics. Its biocompatibility suits it to prostheses, orthopedic implants and dental implants.

=== KEY: Titanium_dioxide_891b34
HEADWORD: Titanium dioxide
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Inorganic compound | Mineral | Oxygen | Paint | Pigment | Refractive index | Titanium
ENTRY:
[[Inorganic compound|Inorganic compound]] of [[Titanium|titanium]] and [[Oxygen|oxygen]] (TiO₂), one of the most important white [[Pigment|pigments]]. Also called titanium white when used as a pigment, it is an insoluble white solid used in [[Paint|paint]], plastics, paper, sunscreen and food coloring (E number E171). It has been estimated that about two-thirds of all pigments use it, and world production exceeded 9 million tonnes in 2014. Synthetic TiO₂ is produced mainly from ilmenite, while the [[Mineral|minerals]] rutile and anatase occur naturally. Its high [[Refractive index|refractive index]] and opacity give paint its whiteness, and it is also a popular photocatalyst. Twelve polymorphs are known. Nanoparticle forms have raised safety debates: the European Food Safety Authority deemed it unsafe as a food additive in 2021, citing genotoxicity concerns, while the U.S. FDA considers it generally recognized as safe for food use up to 1% by weight.

=== KEY: Titanoboa_ceec79
HEADWORD: Titanoboa
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Apex predator | Colombia | Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event | Egypt | Eocene | Genus
ENTRY:
Extinct [[Genus|genus]] of giant boid snakes of the middle to late Paleocene epoch. Its 186 fossils, recovered from the Cerrejón coal mines in [[Colombia]], were described in 2009 as *Titanoboa cerrejonensis*. It is estimated at 12.8 m (42 ft), possibly 14.3 m (47 ft), long and weighed 730 to 1,135 kg, supplanting *Gigantophis garstini* of the [[Eocene]] of [[Egypt]] as the largest snake then known. Evolving after the [[Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event]], it is thought to have been a semi-aquatic [[Apex predator|apex predator]] feeding mainly on fish.
