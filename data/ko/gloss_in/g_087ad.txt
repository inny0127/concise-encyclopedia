State in [[Northeast India|northeastern India]], with Shillong as its capital (pop., 2014 est.: 3,211,474). Formed on January 21, 1972, from districts carved out of [[Assam]], it covers 22,429 sq km. The name means "abode of clouds" from [[Sanskrit]]. Meghalaya is the wettest region of India, and about 70 percent of the state is forested. Its economy is largely agrarian, with crops such as potatoes, rice, maize and pineapples. The state has historically followed a matrilineal system, in which the youngest daughter inherits the family's wealth. In 2018 the International Commission on Stratigraphy named a late [[Holocene]] stage the Meghalayan, after a speleothem in Mawmluh Cave.

ENTRY:
[[Ottoman Empire|Ottoman]] sultan (r. 1444–1446, 1451–1481), known as Mehmed the Conqueror. He was sultan twice. During his first reign he defeated a crusade led by [[John Hunyadi]], which followed Hungarian violations of the Treaties of Edirne and Szeged. Restored to the throne in 1451, he strengthened the navy and at the age of 21 captured [[Constantinople]] in 1453, effectively ending the [[Byzantine Empire]]. He then claimed the title caesar of Rome, a claim recognized by the Patriarchate of Constantinople but rejected by most European monarchs. Mehmed reunified [[Anatolia]], campaigned as far west as Bosnia, implemented political and social reforms and patronized the arts and sciences. He is considered a hero in modern [[Turkey]], and [[Istanbul]]'s Fatih district and the Fatih Mosque are named after him.

PERSON: yes

ENTRY:
Ottoman sultan (r. 1648–1687), nicknamed Mehmed the Hunter for his love of hunting. He came to the throne at the age of six after his father, Sultan Ibrahim, was overthrown in a coup. His reign was the second longest in Ottoman history after that of [[Suleiman the Magnificent]]. Its first and last years brought defeat and instability, but during the Köprülü era, under Grand Vizier Köprülü Mehmed Pasha from 1656, the empire revived. Ottoman forces regained the Aegean islands and Crete from Venice during the Cretan War (1645–1669) and campaigned successfully against Transylvania and Poland. In 1683 the Ottomans suffered a catastrophic defeat at the [[Battle of Vienna]] by Polish-Lithuanian and imperial forces led by [[John III Sobieski]]. Soldiers overthrew Mehmed in 1687 during the War of the Holy League, and he retired to Edirne, where he died. Contemporaries called him a *gazi*, or holy warrior.

ENTRY:
Political event of 1868 that restored imperial rule in [[Japan]] under [[Emperor Meiji]]. On January 3, 1868, the emperor declared that political power had been restored to the Imperial House, ending more than two centuries of rule by the [[Tokugawa shogunate]]. Its origins lay in economic and political difficulties under the shogunate and in pressure from foreign powers, which challenged the policy of sakoku; after the Perry Expedition, Japan was forced to open to the West under unequal treaties. Samurai from domains such as Satsuma and Chōshū overthrew the shogunal system, and the [[Boshin War]] followed. The new government abolished the domain system and eventually the samurai class, and Japan rapidly [[Industrialisation|industrialized]] and adopted Western ideas. The Meiji Constitution of 1889 remained in place until the Allied occupation after World War II.

HEADWORD: Mein Kampf
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Adolf Hitler | Beer Hall Putsch | Munich | Nazi Party | Rudolf Hess
ENTRY:
Autobiographical and political manifesto by [[Adolf Hitler]], leader of the [[Nazi Party]], published in two volumes in 1925 and 1926. Hitler began writing it in Landsberg Prison after the failed [[Beer Hall Putsch]] in [[Munich]] in November 1923. [[Rudolf Hess]] did most of the editing. The book outlines Hitler's political beliefs, ideology and plans for Germany and the world. Sales were slow at first but rose after Hitler became chancellor in 1933. Bavaria held the copyright after World War II and refused reprints in Germany until 2016, when the Institute of Contemporary History in Munich published an annotated edition.
