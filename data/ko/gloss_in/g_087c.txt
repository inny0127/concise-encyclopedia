=== KEY: Mental_representation_51b75a
HEADWORD: Mental representation
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Cognitive psychology | Empiricism | Philosophy of mind | Semantics | Syntax | Thomas Hobbes
ENTRY:
Hypothetical internal cognitive symbol representing external reality or its abstractions, studied in [[Philosophy of mind]] and [[Cognitive psychology]]. Mental representations allow people to imagine things never experienced or that do not exist. The representational theory of mind, in which representations act as intermediaries between observer and world, can be traced to [[Thomas Hobbes]] and was a dominant theme of [[Empiricism|classical empiricism]]. Modern proponents such as Jerry Fodor and Steven Pinker describe an internal language of thought, or mentalese, with [[Syntax]] and [[Semantics]] similar to natural languages. Stephen Kosslyn proposes that images help solve certain problems. How the brain interprets and stores representational content remains debated.

Genus of flowering plants in the mint family, [[Lamiaceae]], with an estimated 18 to 24 species. It is a member of the tribe Mentheae in the subfamily Nepetoideae. Mints are almost exclusively perennial herbs with square, branched stems and spreading stolons, and some are considered invasive. Peppermint, *Mentha* × *piperita*, is a hybrid of *M. aquatica* and *M. spicata*. Mint essential oils are rich in [[Menthol]].

Monoterpenoid organic compound found naturally in the oils of [[Mentha|mint]]-family plants such as peppermint and corn mint. It is a white or clear waxy crystalline solid. Menthol produces a cooling sensation when inhaled, eaten or applied to the skin, and has local anesthetic and counterirritant qualities. === KEY: Mercator_projection_b941eb
HEADWORD: Mercator projection
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Antarctica | Equator | Greenland | Map projection | Navigation | Song dynasty
ENTRY:
Cylindrical conformal [[Map projection|map projection]], first presented by Flemish geographer and mapmaker Gerardus Mercator in 1569. Because it represents rhumb lines as straight lines, it became the standard projection for [[Navigation|navigation]] in the 18th century. On world maps it inflates the size of landmasses the farther they are from the [[Equator|equator]], so that [[Greenland]] and [[Antarctica]] appear far larger than landmasses near the equator. Historian Joseph Needham speculated, without evidence, that some [[Song dynasty]] star charts may have been drafted on the projection, but astronomical historian Kazuhiko Miyajima concluded from cartometric analysis that they used an equirectangular projection instead. Its use for maps other than marine charts declined in the 20th century but resurged in the 21st century because of characteristics favorable for online mapping.

=== KEY: Mercury_element_5dad0e

ENTRY:
Chemical element with symbol Hg (from Latin *hydrargyrum*), atomic number 80, also known as quicksilver. It is the only metal known to be liquid at standard temperature and pressure, and it has the lowest freezing point, the lowest boiling point and the narrowest liquid range of any metal. Mercury occurs mostly as [[Cinnabar|cinnabar]] (mercuric sulfide), from which the red pigment [[Vermilion|vermilion]] is made. Exposure to mercury and its compounds is toxic to the nervous system, immune system and kidneys. It was long used in [[Thermometer|thermometers]], [[Barometer|barometers]] and other instruments, but concerns about toxicity have reduced its use. It remains in scientific research, in dental [[Amalgam (chemistry)|amalgam]] in some locales and in fluorescent lamps. Its melting point is −38.83 °C and its boiling point 356.73 °C.

=== KEY: Mercury_planet_315bca
HEADWORD: Mercury
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Solar System | Sun | Terrestrial planet | Venus
ENTRY:
Innermost and smallest planet of the [[Solar System]], first from the [[Sun]]. It is a rocky [[Terrestrial planet|terrestrial planet]] with a trace atmosphere and a surface gravity slightly lower than that of Mars. The largest crater, Caloris Planitia, is 1,550 km across, about one-third the planet's diameter of 4,880 km. Because it orbits closest to the Sun, it always appears near the Sun in Earth's sky, as a "morning star" or an "evening star."

Mercury's sidereal year (88.0 Earth days) and sidereal day (58.65 Earth days) are in a 3:2 spin–orbit resonance, so one solar day, from sunrise to sunrise, lasts around 176 Earth days. Equatorial temperatures range from −170 °C at night to 420 °C in sunlight, making it the second hottest planet after [[Venus]], since it lacks an atmosphere to trap heat. Its very small axial tilt leaves the poles permanently shadowed, which strongly suggests that water ice may be present in the craters.

The planet is about 70% metallic and 30% silicate material. Its density, 5.427 g/cm³, is the second highest in the Solar System, implying a large iron-rich core. The core radius is estimated at 2,020 ± 30 km and occupies about 57% of the volume, against 17% for Earth. Mercury formed approximately 4.5 billion years ago and is expected to be destroyed when the Sun becomes a red giant in approximately seven or eight billion years.

The ancient Greeks had realized by about 350 BC that the morning and evening "stars" were the same body. Mariner 10 made the first successful flyby in 1974, and the planet has since been explored by the MESSENGER and BepiColombo orbiters.

=== KEY: Meristem_fca067
HEADWORD: Meristem
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Plastid | Stem cell | Vascular cambium
ENTRY:
Type of plant tissue made up of undifferentiated [[Stem cell|stem cells]] that divide continuously. Meristematic cells are totipotent, able to differentiate into any plant cell type, and they are the source of all differentiated plant tissues and organs. Some remain meristematic while others specialize and usually lose the ability to divide. These cells are small, with thin primary cell walls, few or no vacuoles, dense protoplasm, and proplastids instead of mature [[Plastid|plastids]]. Meristems fall into three main types: apical meristems at the tips of roots and shoots, which drive primary growth; intercalary or basal meristems in the middle of stems or leaves, which enable regrowth; and lateral meristems, or [[Vascular cambium|cambium]], which produce secondary growth in woody plants. The term was first used in 1858 by Swiss botanist Carl Wilhelm von Nägeli.

=== KEY: Merovingian_dynasty_600563
HEADWORD: Merovingian dynasty
PERSON: no

Ruling family of the [[Franks]] from around the middle of the 5th century until 751. Under [[Clovis I|Clovis]], who converted to [[Christianity|Nicene Christianity]], the Merovingians united the Franks and conquered most of [[Gaul]], defeating the [[Visigoths]] in 507 and the Burgundians in 534. ENTRY:
[[Prime number|Prime number]] of the form 2ⁿ − 1, one less than a power of two. The exponent n must itself be prime, but not every prime exponent gives a prime, as 2¹¹ − 1 = 2047 = 23 × 89 shows. The primes are named after the French friar Marin Mersenne, who studied them in the early 17th century. The Euclid–Euler theorem links them to [[Perfect number|even perfect numbers]]. === KEY: Mesa_2260b6
HEADWORD: Mesa
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Limestone | Plain | Plateau | Sandstone | Shale | Tibetan Plateau
ENTRY:
Isolated, flat-topped elevation bounded on all sides by steep escarpments and standing distinctly above a surrounding [[Plain|plain]]. A mesa consists of flat-lying soft [[Shale|shales]] capped by a resistant layer of harder rock, such as [[Sandstone|sandstone]] or [[Limestone|limestone]], that forms a caprock protecting the flat summit. Unlike a [[Plateau|plateau]], which may lack horizontal bedrock, as with the [[Tibetan Plateau]], a mesa is defined by flat-lying strata and steep-sided isolation. A butte is a smaller, eroded mesa with a limited summit, while a cuesta has a gentle dip slope and one steep escarpment because its strata are tilted. Large, flat-topped plateaus with horizontal strata are called tablelands.

=== KEY: Mesoamerica_79a4a1
HEADWORD: Mesoamerica
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Aztecs | Cradle of civilization | Maize | Mexico | Smallpox | Spanish colonization of the Americas | Teotihuacan | Tikal
ENTRY:
Historical region and cultural area of central and southern [[Mexico]] and northern Central America, home to indigenous civilizations before the Spanish conquest. It comprises central and southern Mexico, all of Belize, Guatemala, El Salvador, western Honduras, and the Greater Nicoya region of Nicaragua and Costa Rica. Societies flourished there for more than 3,000 years before [[Spanish colonization of the Americas]] began on Hispaniola in 1493. It is one of six regions where ancient civilizations arose independently (see [[Cradle of civilization]]) and one of five where writing developed independently.

Beginning as early as 7000 BCE, the domestication of cacao, [[Maize|maize]], beans, tomato, avocado, vanilla, squash, and chili, along with the turkey and dog, led hunter-gatherers to settle in agricultural villages. Shared traits included a complex mythological and religious tradition, a vigesimal numeric system, a complex calendar, ball playing, and a distinct architectural style. Ceremonial centers were linked by trade in obsidian, jade, cacao, cinnabar, hematite, and ceramics. The wheel and basic metallurgy were known but never became technologically relevant.

The Olmec were among the earliest complex civilizations. In the preclassic period urban polities arose among the Maya, at centers such as [[Tikal]], and among the Zapotec at Monte Albán; the first true writing systems appeared then and reached their height in Classic Maya script. [[Teotihuacan]] built a military and commercial empire and collapsed around 600 CE. The [[Aztecs]] later built a tributary empire covering most of central Mesoamerica.

The Spanish conquest in the 16th century ended the distinct tradition; [[Smallpox|smallpox]] and measles caused the deaths of upwards of 90% of the indigenous people.

=== KEY: Mesoamerican_architecture_b061fa
HEADWORD: Mesoamerican architecture
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Ancient Egypt | Cardinal direction | Mesoamerica | Tenochtitlan | Teotihuacan | Tikal
ENTRY:
Building traditions of pre-Columbian [[Mesoamerica]], best known from public, ceremonial and urban monumental structures. Its pyramids are the largest such structures outside [[Ancient Egypt]]. Many features were governed by religious and mythological ideas, and the layout of most cities was influenced by the [[Cardinal direction|cardinal directions]] and their symbolic meanings. Monumental buildings were decorated with religious images and, in some cases, writing. From about 1800 BCE until the Spanish conquest of the 16th century, builders used stone, stucco and earth, without metal tools or the wheel, to create stepped pyramids, ball courts, palaces and plazas. The [[Teotihuacan]] megacity, with over 100,000 inhabitants, featured the Pyramid of the Sun and Pyramid of the Moon. Maya city-states such as [[Tikal]] built corbelled arches and multi-level temples, while the Aztec capital [[Tenochtitlan]] was a floating city on Lake Texcoco.

=== KEY: Mesolithic_62194b
HEADWORD: Mesolithic
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Hunter-gatherer | Last Glacial Maximum | Neolithic | Neolithic Revolution | Upper Paleolithic
ENTRY:
Archaeological period of the Old World between the [[Upper Paleolithic]] and the [[Neolithic]], also called the Middle Stone Age. It denotes the final period of [[Hunter-gatherer|hunter-gatherer]] cultures in Europe and the Middle East, between the end of the [[Last Glacial Maximum]] and the [[Neolithic Revolution]]. In Europe it spans roughly 15,000 to 5,000 years before present; in the Middle East, roughly 20,000 to 10,000. It is associated with a shift from group hunting of large animals to a broader foraging way of life, with smaller, more sophisticated stone tools, including microliths hafted into composite weapons. Settlements were often near the sea or inland waters, and diets included wild game, fish, shellfish and nuts. Some sites show early use of pottery. Agriculture marks the transition to the Neolithic.

=== KEY: Meson_da40cd
HEADWORD: Meson
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Baryon | Boson | Hadron | Pion | Proton | Quark | Strong interaction
ENTRY:
[[Hadron|Hadronic]] subatomic particle composed of an equal number of [[Quark|quarks]] and antiquarks, usually one of each, bound together by the [[Strong interaction|strong interaction]]. Mesons are about one femtometer in diameter (10⁻¹⁵ m), roughly 0.6 times the size of a [[Proton|proton]] or neutron, and all are unstable. Outside the nucleus they appear only briefly, as products of very high-energy collisions such as those of cosmic rays, and they are routinely produced in particle accelerators. Conventional two-quark mesons are [[Boson|bosons]], whereas [[Baryon|baryons]] are fermions. Each meson has an antiparticle; the positive [[Pion|pion]] is made of an up quark and a down antiquark. The heavier J/psi, first seen in 1974, is about three times as massive as a proton.

=== KEY: Mesopotamia_155f90
HEADWORD: Mesopotamia
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Alexander the Great | Assyria | Babylonia | Iraq | Neo-Assyrian Empire | Neolithic Revolution | Parthian Empire | Sargon of Akkad
ENTRY:
Historical region of West Asia within the Tigris–Euphrates river system, in the northern part of the Fertile Crescent, corresponding roughly to modern [[Iraq]]. The name is Greek for "[land] between rivers." The broader region also includes parts of present-day Iran, Turkey, Syria, and Kuwait.

Mesopotamia is the site of the earliest developments of the [[Neolithic Revolution]] from around 10,000 BC and is recognized as the cradle of some of the world's earliest civilizations. It has been credited with inspiring the invention of the wheel, the planting of the first cereal crops, cursive script, mathematics, astronomy, and agriculture. Sumerians and Akkadians dominated from the beginning of recorded history (c. 3100 BC) to the fall of Babylon in 539 BC.

Empires began with [[Sargon of Akkad]] around 2350 BC. In the early second millennium BC, society polarized into [[Assyria]] in the north and [[Babylonia]] in the south. The [[Neo-Assyrian Empire]] controlled much of the ancient Near East from 900 to 612 BC; the Babylonians then dominated for a century as the last independent Mesopotamian realm until the modern era. Cyrus the Great's Achaemenid Empire conquered the region in 539 BC, and [[Alexander the Great]] in 332 BC, after which the Seleucids prevailed over his other successors. Around 150 BC the [[Parthian Empire]] held it, and it became a battleground between Romans and Parthians. In 226 AD the eastern regions fell to the Sassanid Persians under Ardashir I. The division between the Roman and Sasanian empires lasted until the 7th century Muslim conquest, after which the region came to be known as Iraq.

=== KEY: Mesothelioma_615662
HEADWORD: Mesothelioma
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Asbestos | Biopsy | Chemotherapy
ENTRY:
Cancer developing from the mesothelium, the thin tissue layer that covers many internal organs. It most often affects the lining of the lungs and chest wall. Symptoms may include shortness of breath, a swollen abdomen, chest wall pain and weight loss. More than 80 percent of cases are caused by [[Asbestos|asbestos]] exposure, usually about 40 years earlier. Diagnosis relies on imaging and a tissue [[Biopsy|biopsy]], and treatment often combines surgery, radiation therapy and [[Chemotherapy|chemotherapy]]. The five-year survival rate in the United States averages eight percent.
