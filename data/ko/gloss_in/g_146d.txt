=== KEY: Whooping_cough_e498b7
HEADWORD: Whooping cough
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Antibiotic | Common cold | Vaccination
ENTRY:
Highly contagious bacterial respiratory disease caused by *Bordetella pertussis*, also called pertussis or the 100-day cough. It begins with symptoms like a [[Common cold|common cold]], followed after one or two weeks by violent coughing fits that often end in a high-pitched whoop as the person inhales. The cough usually lasts 1–6 weeks but can last 10 or more. Infants may have little or no cough and instead pause in breathing. Among infected children under one year old, about 50% need hospitalization and nearly 0.5% (1 in 200) die. Prevention is mainly by [[Vaccination|vaccination]] with the pertussis vaccine, given as four doses in the first two years of life, with additional doses for older children and adults. [[Antibiotic|Antibiotics]] are useful if started within three weeks of symptoms but otherwise have little effect. In 2015 an estimated 16.3 million people were infected and 58,700 died, down from 138,000 deaths in 1990.

=== KEY: Wi_Fi_fa524b
HEADWORD: Wi-Fi
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Ethernet | Radio wave | Router (computing)
ENTRY:
Family of wireless network protocols based on the IEEE 802.11 standards. Wi-Fi lets nearby digital devices exchange data by [[Radio wave|radio waves]] and provides Internet access through [[Router (computing)|wireless routers]] and access points in homes, offices and public places. The name is a trademark of the Wi-Fi Alliance, which certifies products that pass interoperability testing. Over 3.05 billion Wi-Fi-enabled devices were shipped globally each year as of 2019. Wi-Fi is designed to work with its wired counterpart, [[Ethernet]]. It usually operates in the 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz bands, with 6 GHz used in newer generations, and an access point's indoor range is about 20 m (66 ft). As of 2025, some versions reach 23 Gbit/s at close range.

=== KEY: Wicca_40a181
HEADWORD: Wicca
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Magic (supernatural) | New religious movement | Western esotericism
ENTRY:
Modern pagan, syncretic, Earth-focused religion. It developed in England during the first half of the 20th century from [[Western esotericism|Western esoteric]] traditions, and Gerald Gardner, a retired British civil servant, introduced it to the public in 1954. Doreen Valiente joined him in the 1950s and helped build its liturgical tradition. Scholars of religion classify Wicca as a [[New religious movement|new religious movement]] and a form of modern paganism. It is typically duotheistic, venerating a goddess and a god, traditionally the Triple Goddess and the Horned God. Celebrations follow the cycles of the Moon, called Esbats, and seasonal festivals of the Sun, called Sabbats. The Wiccan Rede is a popular expression of Wiccan morality, often linked to the ritual practice of [[Magic (supernatural)|magic]]. Some Wiccans prefer the word spirituality to religion.

=== KEY: Wichita_Kansas_522b9d
HEADWORD: Wichita
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Airbus | Boeing | Wyatt Earp
ENTRY:
City (pop., 2020: 397,532), county seat of Sedgwick County in south-central Kansas, on the Arkansas River. Wichita began as a trading post on the Chisholm Trail in the 1860s and was incorporated in 1870. As a stop for cattle drives from Texas, it earned the nickname Cowtown. In 1875 [[Wyatt Earp]] served as a police officer there. In the 1920s and 1930s aircraft makers were established in the city, which became known as The Air Capital of the World. [[Airbus]] and [[Boeing]] still operate facilities there. Its airport, Wichita Dwight D. Eisenhower National Airport, is the largest in Kansas.

Musical with music and lyrics by Stephen Schwartz and a book by Winnie Holzman. === KEY: Wife_319b24
HEADWORD: Wife
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Cohabitation | Common law | Divorce
ENTRY:
Female spouse; a woman in a marital relationship. She remains a wife until her marriage is legally dissolved by divorce or until her partner dies, when she becomes a widow. Her rights, obligations and status vary between cultures and have changed over time. The word derives from Proto-Germanic *wībam*, meaning woman, and the older sense survives in *midwife*. Many cultures expect a married woman to take her husband's surname. The term applies to a woman in a union sanctioned by law, including religious law, but usually not to an informal [[Cohabitation|cohabiting]] partner, though a woman in a [[Common law|common-law]] marriage may use it. An annulment, unlike a [[Divorce|divorce]], is usually retroactive, so the marriage is treated as never having existed. Many countries are replacing wife and husband with spouse in their statute law.

=== KEY: Wig_95e216
HEADWORD: Wig
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: French Revolution | Louis XIII | Louis XIV
ENTRY:
Head covering made from human or animal hair, or a synthetic imitation. Wigs may be worn to disguise baldness, alter appearance, or as part of certain professional uniforms. Ancient Egyptians wore wigs to shield shaved heads from the sun, fixing them with beeswax and resin. After the fall of the Western Roman Empire, wigs fell out of use in the West for about a thousand years. Their revival in the 16th century helped with hair loss and reduced head lice. [[Louis XIII]] began wearing one in 1624 after premature balding, and his successor [[Louis XIV]] promoted the fashion across Europe. Men's wig-wearing dominated for about 140 years, until dress changed in the 1790s under the influence of the [[French Revolution]].
