American actor and producer of film and television. Garner was born James Scott Bumgarner. He became a television star as the gambler Bret Maverick in *Maverick* (1957–62), a role he reprised in *Bret Maverick* (1981–82). In the 1970s he starred as the private investigator Jim Rockford in *The Rockford Files* (1974–80). He also played leading roles in more than 50 theatrical films, including *The Great Escape* (1963) and *Murphy's Romance* (1985), for which he received an Academy Award nomination for Best Actor. He won three Golden Globe Awards and two Primetime Emmy Awards.

French-British financier and politician. His controversial business career led to frequent clashes with British media, often involving litigation or the threat of it. The tabloid press described him as a greenmail corporate raider and asset stripper, a categorization he denied. Born in Paris, he fled France with his family when [[Nazi Germany]] overran the country. In 1994 he was elected to the European Parliament for a French constituency. He founded the short-lived Eurosceptic Referendum Party in the United Kingdom, an early campaigner against Britain's membership of the [[European Union]].

=== KEY: James_Hutton_8ec674
HEADWORD: Hutton, James
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Anthropic principle | Soot
ENTRY:
Scottish geologist, agriculturalist, and chemical manufacturer, often called the "Father of Modern Geology." Hutton inferred the remote history of the Earth from evidence in present-day rocks, studying features such as Salisbury Crags and Siccar Point. He argued that geological features underwent continuing transformation over indefinitely long periods, and he was one of the earliest proponents of uniformitarianism, the view that continuing natural processes shape the Earth's crust. He proposed a "system of the habitable Earth," a deistic mechanism to keep the world suitable for humans, an early form of the [[Anthropic principle]]. He earned a medical doctorate at Leiden University and co-owned a profitable chemical works that made sal ammoniac from [[Soot|soot]]. In Berwickshire he farmed and introduced innovative farming practices.

ENTRY:
King of England, Scotland, and Ireland, 1685–88. The last Catholic monarch of the three kingdoms, James was deposed in the [[Glorious Revolution]]. He was the second surviving son of [[Charles I of England|Charles I]]. Parliaments in England and Scotland refused to pass measures undermining the Protestant religion, and his attempts to impose them by decree met opposition. In 1688 leading English politicians invited William of Orange, his nephew and son-in-law, to take the throne. After his defeat at the Battle of the Boyne in 1690, he returned to France, where [[Louis XIV]] protected him.

PERSON: yes

Irish novelist, poet, and literary critic. *Ulysses* (1922) is a landmark work that parallels the episodes of [[Homer]]'s *Odyssey* in a variety of literary styles, particularly stream of consciousness. Although Joyce spent most of his adult life abroad, in [[Trieste]], [[Zurich]], and [[Paris]], his fiction centers on [[Dublin]]. Publication of *Ulysses* was prohibited in the United Kingdom and the United States for its perceived obscenity until the mid-1930s. His other works include the short-story collection *Dubliners* (1914), the novel *A Portrait of the Artist as a Young Man* (1916), and the experimental *Finnegans Wake* (1939). He died in Zurich after surgery for a perforated ulcer.
