ENTRY:
Pelagic lampriform fish of the family Regalecidae, comprising three species. The giant oarfish (*Regalecus glesne*) is the longest bony fish alive, reaching about 8 m (26 ft). Oarfish have silvery, scaleless bodies, a long crest-like dorsal fin and no swim bladder. They often self-amputate the tail, and their gelatinous flesh is not well regarded for eating. === KEY: Obesity_9f8251
HEADWORD: Obesity
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Body mass index | Cancer | Cardiovascular disease | Diabetes | Osteoarthritis | Sumo | Urbanization | World Health Organization
ENTRY:
Medical condition, considered a disease by multiple organizations, in which excess body fat has accumulated to such an extent that it can have negative effects on health. People are classified as obese when their [[Body mass index|body mass index]] (BMI), a person's weight divided by the square of height, is over 30 kg/m²; the range 25–30 kg/m² is defined as overweight. Obesity is correlated with [[Cardiovascular disease|cardiovascular diseases]], [[Diabetes|type 2 diabetes]], obstructive sleep apnea, certain types of [[Cancer|cancer]], and [[Osteoarthritis|osteoarthritis]].

Causes include diet, low physical activity, automation, [[Urbanization|urbanization]], genetic susceptibility, medications, mental disorders, economic policies, and endocrine disorders. BMI is criticized because it ignores lean body mass: more than half of NFL players are classified as obese by BMI, yet their mean body fat percentage is 14%, and 40% of college-aged [[Sumo|sumo]] wrestlers in one study were no longer classified as obese when body fat percentage was used.

Diet and exercise are the main recommended treatments, and medications, a gastric balloon, or surgery may be used when they fail. The [[World Health Organization]] stresses that healthy dietary choices should be the most available, affordable, and accessible options. Many people lose weight, but maintaining the loss long-term is rare.

In 2022, over 1 billion people lived with obesity (879 million adults and 159 million children), more than double the adult cases and four times the child cases registered in 1990. The share of adults who were obese rose from 12.1% in 2012 to 16.2% in 2024, with 18.6% projected by 2030. Obesity is stigmatized in many parts of the world, while some cultures have viewed it as a symbol of wealth and fertility.

Spherical region of the [[Universe|universe]] containing all [[Matter|matter]] that can be observed from [[Earth]]. Its radius is about 14.26 gigaparsecs (46.5 billion light-years or 4.40×10²⁶ m). === KEY: Observation_c680d4

ENTRY:
Active acquisition of information from a primary source. In living organisms, observation occurs mainly through the senses; in science it often extends to [[Telescope|telescopes]], [[Microscope|microscopes]] and other instruments that detect and record data. Observations are either qualitative, describing characteristics not expressed numerically such as color, texture or behavior, or quantitative, based on counting or measurement. === KEY: Observational_study_cc3fa2
HEADWORD: Observational study
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Bias | Epidemiology | Lung cancer | Medicine | Psychology | Randomized controlled trial | Statistics
ENTRY:
Research design in which outcomes are measured without the investigator manipulating variables or conditions. Observational studies are commonly used in [[Medicine|medicine]], [[Epidemiology|epidemiology]], [[Psychology|psychology]], sociology and [[Statistics|statistics]]. Unlike a [[Randomized controlled trial|randomized controlled trial]] (RCT), the investigator does not assign subjects to treatment or control groups. Such designs suit situations where a trial would be unethical, impractical or beyond the investigator's control. For example, it would be unethical to assign participants at random to smoke in a trial of whether smoking causes [[Lung cancer|lung cancer]]. Observational studies are susceptible to confounding and [[Bias|bias]], give weaker evidence than RCTs, and cannot on their own establish causality. Common designs include cohort, case-control and cross-sectional studies.

=== KEY: Occam_s_razor_90f379
HEADWORD: Occam's razor
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Aristotle | Hypothesis | Isaac Newton | Thomas Aquinas | William of Ockham
ENTRY:
Philosophical problem-solving principle, also called the principle of parsimony. It recommends that, among explanations, the one constructed from the smallest possible set of elements be preferred. It is often cited as "Entities must not be multiplied beyond necessity," although [[William of Ockham]], a 14th-century English philosopher and theologian, never used those words. Similar ideas appear in [[Aristotle]], and [[Thomas Aquinas]] wrote that it is superfluous to suppose that what can be accounted for by a few principles has been produced by many. In science it serves as an abductive heuristic in building models rather than a rigorous arbiter between them, and it does not choose between [[Hypothesis|hypotheses]] that make different predictions.

=== KEY: Ocean_current_768459

ENTRY:
Continuous, directed movement of seawater, generated by [[Wind|wind]], the Coriolis effect, temperature and salinity differences and other forces. Surface currents are driven mainly by wind systems, while deep-water currents are driven by differences in [[Density|density]] caused by variations in temperature and salinity. === KEY: Ocean_f41054
HEADWORD: Ocean
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Arctic Ocean | Atlantic Ocean | Gulf Stream | Hydrosphere | Indian Ocean | Pacific Ocean | Photosynthesis | Southern Ocean
ENTRY:
Body of salt water covering approximately 70.8% of Earth. The ocean is conventionally divided into five large bodies of water, also called oceans; in descending order by area they are the [[Pacific Ocean]], the [[Atlantic Ocean]], the [[Indian Ocean]], the Antarctic or [[Southern Ocean]], and the [[Arctic Ocean]]. These are mostly divided into seas, gulfs, and smaller bodies of water. The ocean's volume exceeds 1.3 billion cubic km, and its average salinity is roughly 35 grams of dissolved salts per liter. It contains 97% of Earth's water and is the primary component of the [[Hydrosphere|hydrosphere]], acting as a huge reservoir of heat for Earth's energy budget, carbon cycle, and water cycle, and forming the basis for climate and weather patterns worldwide. It harbors most of Earth's animals and protist life, originated [[Photosynthesis|photosynthesis]], and still supplies half of Earth's atmospheric oxygen.

In general use "the ocean" and "the sea" are interchangeable. Strictly, a sea is a body of water, generally a division of the world ocean, partly or fully enclosed by land, such as the North Sea or the Red Sea. The term World Ocean was coined in the early 20th century by the Russian oceanographer Yuly Shokalsky. The word ocean comes from Oceanus, the elder of the Titans in classical Greek mythology, whom the ancient Greeks and Romans believed to be the divine personification of an enormous river encircling the world.

Scientists divide the ocean into horizontal and vertical zones. Horizontally, it covers the oceanic crust and, at its margins, the shallow continental shelves. Vertically, the pelagic zone is the open ocean's water column. The photic zone, defined as the depth at which light intensity is only 1% of the surface value (approximately 200 m in the open ocean), is where photosynthesis occurs and is the most biodiverse zone; the deeper ocean is cold and dark.

Surface temperatures can exceed 30 °C in the tropics and fall to about −2 °C near the poles where sea ice forms; deep ocean temperatures range between −2 °C and 5 °C. Temperature and salinity differences, wind, tides, and the Coriolis effect drive currents such as the [[Gulf Stream]], Kuroshio Current, Agulhas Current, and Antarctic Circumpolar Current, which transport water, gases, pollutants, and heat around the world. As the ocean absorbs carbon dioxide from the atmosphere, it undergoes acidification, a drop in pH.

Geological evidence from the Isua Greenstone Belt shows that water existed on Earth 3.8 billion years ago, though the origin of Earth's oceans is unknown. The ocean is the habitat of over 230,000 known species, perhaps over two million in all. It provides ecosystem services, seafood, and transport, but faces marine pollution, overfishing, warming, acidification, and sea level rise.

=== KEY: Ocean_sunfish_f70ab5

ENTRY:
Large ray-finned fish, *Mola mola*, the type species of its genus. Adults typically weigh between 247 and 1,000 kg (545 and 2,205 lb). Sunfish can be as tall as they are long, between 175 and 180 centimeters (5.74 and 5.91 ft) with fins extended. Spanning the Eastern and Western hemispheres, it has a land area of about 9,000,000 sq km and a population of around 44.4 million as of 2022, making it the smallest continent in land area and the second-least populated after Antarctica. [[Australia]] is its largest and most populous country and Sydney its largest city. Puncak Jaya in Indonesia, at 4,884 m, is the highest peak.

Economies range from the highly developed markets of Australia, French Polynesia, Hawaii, New Caledonia, and New Zealand, which rank high in the Human Development Index, to the much less developed economies of Kiribati, Papua New Guinea, Tuvalu, Vanuatu, and Western New Guinea. Most countries are parliamentary democracies, and tourism is a large source of income for the Pacific island nations.

The first settlers of Australia, New Guinea, and the large islands just to the east arrived more than 60,000 years ago. The rock art of [[Aboriginal Australians]] is the longest continuously practiced artistic tradition in the world. Europeans first explored the region from the 16th century onward: between 1512 and 1526 Portuguese explorers reached the Tanimbar Islands, some of the Caroline Islands, and west New Guinea, followed by Spanish, Dutch, British, and French explorers. On his first voyage, [[James Cook]] went to Tahiti and followed the east coast of Australia for the first time; he later reached the Hawaiian Islands. Britain established a penal colony in Australia in 1788. European settlement significantly altered the social and political landscape, and the Pacific theater saw major action in both world wars.

The name derives from the French *Océanie*, from the Greek word for ocean, and was coined by French geographers in the early 19th century; it reflects the ocean's role in linking the region's parts. Definitions vary. The broadest encompasses the islands between mainland Asia and the Americas, with Zealandia usually considered a microcontinent. The United Nations has used a definition since 1947 based on four subregions, Australasia, Melanesia, Micronesia, and Polynesia, which excludes Indonesia, Japan, the Philippines, Singapore, and Taiwan. In 1973 anthropologists Roger Green and Andrew Pawley proposed the terms Near Oceania and Remote Oceania. The islands generally considered the geographic extremes are the Bonin Islands (Japan), Hawaii (United States), Clipperton Island (France), Easter Island (Chile), and Macquarie Island (Australia).

Long, narrow depression of the sea floor, typically 50 to 100 km (30 to 60 mi) wide. Trenches lie 3 to 4 km (1.9 to 2.5 mi) below the surrounding floor and can extend thousands of kilometers. About 50,000 km (31,000 mi) of them exist, mostly around the [[Pacific Ocean]]. They mark convergent [[Plate tectonics|plate boundaries]], where oceanic lithosphere sinks beneath another plate. The deepest point, the [[Challenger Deep]] in the [[Mariana Trench]], lies 10,994 m (36,070 ft) below sea level. Trenches generally lie about 200 km (120 mi) from a volcanic arc. Fluid trapped in subducting sediments returns to the surface at trenches, supporting unique [[Biome|biomes]] based on chemotrophic microorganisms. Unlike troughs, which have flat bottoms, trenches have a V-shaped profile.

ENTRY:
Type of lyric poem that praises or glorifies an event or individual, or describes nature intellectually and emotionally. Odes originated in [[Ancient Greece]], where they were performed to musical accompaniment, usually on the aulos or the [[Lyre|lyre]]. Three typical forms are recognized. === KEY: Odonata_661ac9

ENTRY:
Order of predatory flying insects that includes [[Dragonfly|dragonflies]] and [[Damselfly|damselflies]]. Odonates have large compound eyes, two pairs of long, transparent wings that move independently, and elongated abdomens. Adults catch prey in flight with specialized legs, and they can land and perch. Almost all have aquatic [[Nymph (biology)|nymphs]] called naiads, which are carnivorous and eat anything they can overpower, including small [[Fish|fish]]. === KEY: Odor_93e0bd

ENTRY:
Smell caused by volatilized chemical compounds, usually present in low concentrations, that humans and many animals perceive through the [[Olfactory system|olfactory system]]. Commonwealth English spells it odor. The terms scent, aroma and fragrance usually denote pleasant odors. Odorant molecules bind to receptor proteins on the cilia of olfactory receptor neurons in the olfactory epithelium, a small patch of tissue at the back of the nasal cavity. Women usually outperform men in odor discrimination, although a 2019 meta-analysis found the difference to be small. Pregnant women have heightened sensitivity to smell.

=== KEY: Ohm_643961

Unit of electrical resistance in the [[International System of Units|SI]], symbol Ω, named for German physicist [[Georg Ohm]]. One ohm is the resistance between two points of a conductor when a constant potential difference of one [[Volt|volt]] produces a current of one [[Ampere|ampere]] in it. === KEY: Ohm_s_law_9ac653
HEADWORD: Ohm's law
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Electric current | Georg Ohm | Gustav Kirchhoff | Henry Cavendish | James Clerk Maxwell | Voltage
ENTRY:
Law of electrical current and voltage. In an ohmic conductor, the [[Electric current|electric current]] between two points is directly proportional to the [[Voltage|voltage]] across them. The constant of proportionality is the resistance, giving V = IR, or equivalently I = GV, where G is the conductance. The law is an empirical relation that describes the conductivity of most electrically conductive materials over many orders of magnitude of current; materials that do not obey it are called non-ohmic. The law is named after German physicist [[Georg Ohm]], who described his measurements in a treatise published in 1827. [[Henry Cavendish]] had made similar measurements in 1781, but his results were unknown until [[James Clerk Maxwell]] published them in 1879. The vector form, J = σE, is due to [[Gustav Kirchhoff]].

Viscous, water-insoluble liquid. Oils are nonpolar substances composed primarily of [[Hydrocarbon|hydrocarbons]]. === KEY: Oil_drop_experiment_28234f
HEADWORD: Oil drop experiment
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Capacitor | Electron | Gravity | Nobel Prize | University of Chicago
ENTRY:
Experiment to measure the elementary electric charge. Robert A. Millikan and Harvey Fletcher performed it in 1909 in the Ryerson Physical Laboratory at the [[University of Chicago]]. Tiny charged oil droplets were observed between two horizontal metal plates forming a [[Capacitor|capacitor]]. With no field applied, the fall speed of a droplet gave its radius and therefore its mass. A voltage was then adjusted until the drop hung in balance against [[Gravity|gravity]], which gave its charge. Repeated measurements showed that all charges were small integer multiples of a base value, which the researchers proposed as the charge of a single [[Electron|electron]]. Their value, 1.5924(17)×10⁻¹⁹ C, differs by about 0.6% from the currently accepted value of 1.602176634×10⁻¹⁹ C. Millikan won the [[Nobel Prize]] in Physics in 1923, in part because of this experiment. Later revelations that he selectively reported data have not overturned the core findings.

=== KEY: Old_age_9b1b82
HEADWORD: Old age
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Ageing | Geriatrics | Life expectancy | Loneliness | Retirement | United Nations
ENTRY:
Range of ages for people nearing and surpassing [[Life expectancy|life expectancy]]. Old people are also called the elderly, elders, senior citizens, seniors, or older adults. Old age is not a definite biological stage: the chronological age denoted as old varies culturally and historically. Disciplines concerned with it include the study of the organic processes of [[Ageing|aging]] (senescence), gerontology, [[Geriatrics|geriatrics]], and gerontechnology.

There is no universal official definition. Most developed Western countries set the [[Retirement|retirement]] age around 65, generally taken to mark the transition from middle to old age. The [[United Nations]] considers old age to begin at 60, while a 2001 joint report by the U.S. National Institute on Aging and the World Health Organization's Regional Office for Africa set it at 50 in Sub-Saharan Africa, where old age is determined by a person's ability to contribute to society and life expectancy is lower.

Gerontologists recognize diversity within old age. In developed countries many people in their later 60s and 70s remain fit and active, while after age 80 they generally become increasingly frail; some, called "superagers," retain vitality much longer. One study distinguishes the young-old (60 to 69), the middle-old (70 to 79), and the very old (80+). Scholars list chronological, biological, psychological, and social dimensions of old age, to which Wattis and Curran add a developmental one.

Older people often have limited regenerative abilities and are more susceptible to illness and injury. They face social problems related to retirement, [[Loneliness|loneliness]], and ageism: a 2023 U.S. poll found that about 37% of adults aged 50 to 80 experienced loneliness. In 2011 the United Nations proposed a human-rights convention to protect old people.

Oleaceae is an ancient family, and some of its genera may be relictual populations that persisted in isolation for long periods.

=== KEY: Olfactory_system_1804b3
HEADWORD: Olfactory system
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Epithelium | Sense of smell | Taste
ENTRY:
Sensory system responsible for the [[Sense of smell|sense of smell]] (olfaction). Olfaction is one of the special senses, directly associated with specific organs. Most mammals and reptiles have a main olfactory system, which detects airborne substances, and an accessory system, which senses fluid-phase stimuli. In the nose, odor molecules dissolved in mucus bind to receptors on the cilia of olfactory neurons in the [[Epithelium|olfactory epithelium]]. The resulting electrical signals travel along olfactory nerve fibers through the cribriform plate of the ethmoid bone to the olfactory bulbs. From there, information reaches the olfactory cortex, which includes the piriform cortex, amygdala and olfactory tubercle. The olfactory system and the sense of [[Taste|taste]] are often grouped as the chemosensory system, because both give the brain information about the chemical composition of objects.

=== KEY: Oligarchy_de232c
HEADWORD: Oligarchy
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Ancient Greek | Aristotle | Nobility | Themistocles | Wealth
ENTRY:
Controversial form of government in which power rests with a small number of people. Leaders of oligarchies, called oligarchs, are generally characterized by titles of [[Nobility|nobility]] or high amounts of [[Wealth|wealth]]. The word comes from [[Ancient Greek|Greek]] words meaning rule by few. [[Aristotle]] used the term to mean rule by the rich and treated oligarchy as a corruption of aristocracy. In the early 20th century, Robert Michels argued in his iron law of oligarchy that even democracies tend to become oligarchic, because dividing labor creates a ruling class focused on maintaining its power. In Athens, [[Themistocles]] became archon in 493 BC and greatly increased the power of the navy, allowing the lower classes to influence politics. Business groups may be considered oligarchies if they are the largest private owners in a country, have political power and control several businesses.

=== KEY: Olive_2b8b38

ENTRY:
Fruit of *Olea europaea*, a subtropical evergreen tree in the family [[Oleaceae]]. The olive is a [[Fruit|drupe]] similar in structure to the [[Cherry|cherry]] or [[Peach|peach]]. One of the first fruit trees to be domesticated, it was first cultivated in the Eastern Mediterranean between 6,000 and 4,000 BC and spread around the Mediterranean Basin through trade and human migration. Modern cultivars trace primarily to the Near East, the [[Aegean Sea|Aegean Sea]] and the [[Strait of Gibraltar|Strait of Gibraltar]]. Olive oil is a core ingredient in Mediterranean and Middle Eastern cuisines, and the fruit is also eaten as table olives. The olive branch is a symbol of peace. Spain, Italy and Greece lead world commercial production, and about 80% of harvested olives are processed into oil.

ENTRY:
English military and political leader, Lord Protector (1653–58). Cromwell rose to prominence in the [[English Civil War]] as a Parliamentarian commander. He commanded the New Model Army [[Cavalry|cavalry]] under Thomas Fairfax. He was a leading advocate of the execution of Charles I in January 1649, which led to the establishment of the Commonwealth of England. He was named Lord Protector in December 1653 and held the post until his death. His son Richard succeeded him, but weakness led to a power vacuum and the 1660 Stuart Restoration. Cromwell's body was removed from [[Westminster Abbey|Westminster Abbey]] and hanged at Tyburn on 30 January 1661. His reputation is contested. Critics cite his use of military force to gain power, his role in the execution of Charles I and the brutality of the 1649 conquest of Ireland. [[Winston Churchill]] described him as a military dictator, while others regard him as a hero of liberty.
