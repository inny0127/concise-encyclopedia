=== KEY: Tooth_decay_83eb60
HEADWORD: Tooth decay
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Bacteria | Diabetes | Ibuprofen | Paracetamol | Stimulant
ENTRY:
Breakdown of teeth due to acids produced by [[Bacteria|bacteria]], also known as caries or dental cavities. Bacteria produce acid from food debris and sugar on the tooth surface, dissolving enamel, dentin and cementum. Simple sugars are the bacteria's main energy source, so a diet high in them is a risk factor. Caries results when demineralization outpaces remineralization from sources such as saliva. Risk factors include conditions that reduce saliva, such as [[Diabetes|diabetes]] mellitus, and medications including [[Stimulant|psychostimulants]], antihistamines and antidepressants. Prevention relies on fluoride, a low-sugar diet, and regular brushing and flossing. Treatment depends on the extent of destruction, and no known method regrows large amounts of tooth. [[Paracetamol]] or [[Ibuprofen|ibuprofen]] may be taken for pain. As of 2016, approximately 3.6 billion people (48% of the global population) had caries in their permanent teeth.

=== KEY: Topaz_aedb17
HEADWORD: Topaz
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Fluorine | Gemstone | King James Version | Mineral | Septuagint
ENTRY:
Silicate [[Mineral|mineral]] containing aluminum and [[Fluorine|fluorine]], used as a [[Gemstone|gemstone]]. Common topaz is colorless in its natural state, though trace impurities can make it pale blue or golden-brown to yellow-orange. It is often treated with heat or radiation to produce deep blue, reddish-orange, pale green, pink or purple colors. It is one of the hardest naturally occurring minerals, and major sources include Brazil and Russia. Many English Bible translations, including the [[King James Version]], mention topaz, but they derive from a [[Septuagint]] term for a yellow stone that was probably chrysolite, so topaz is likely not meant.

City (pop., 2020: 126,587), capital of [[Kansas]] and county seat of Shawnee County. The city was laid out in 1854 as a Free-State town by Eastern antislavery men. Its name is a Kansa-Osage word meaning roughly "a good place to dig potatoes." A [[Ferry|ferry]] service across the Kansas River served travelers on the [[Oregon Trail]] at the site. Ferguson* and declared racial segregation in public schools unconstitutional.

=== KEY: Topographic_prominence_14c115
HEADWORD: Topographic prominence
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: K2 | Mount Everest
ENTRY:
Height of a summit above the lowest contour line encircling it that contains no higher summit, a measure of independence. Prominence is the least drop in height needed to get from a summit to higher terrain. The key col is the highest of the lowest points on all paths to higher ground. [[K2]], the second-highest mountain, has a prominence of 4,017 m. The South Summit of [[Mount Everest]] is taller, at 8,749 m, but its prominence is only 11 m, so it is not regarded as an independent mountain. Peaks with high prominence are likely to have extraordinary views.

=== KEY: Topological_space_60ed22
HEADWORD: Topological space
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Distance | Euclidean space | Manifold | Mathematics | Metric space | Open set
ENTRY:
Set equipped with a topology, a structure defining closeness among its points without a numeric [[Distance|distance]]. A topology is most commonly defined through [[Open set|open sets]], though it can also be defined by a set of neighborhoods for each point that satisfy axioms of closeness. Topological spaces are the most general setting in which limits, continuity and connectedness can be defined. Common examples include [[Euclidean space|Euclidean spaces]], [[Metric space|metric spaces]] and [[Manifold|manifolds]]. The study of topological spaces in their own right is called general topology, or point-set topology. Felix Hausdorff first defined topological spaces in 1914 in his *Principles of Set Theory*, and the concept is fundamental to virtually every branch of modern [[Mathematics|mathematics]].

=== KEY: Topology_99571e
HEADWORD: Topology
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz | Graph theory | Henri Poincaré | Homotopy | Leonhard Euler | Metric space | Seven Bridges of Königsberg | Topological space
ENTRY:
Branch of mathematics concerned with the properties of a geometric object that are preserved under continuous deformations such as stretching, twisting, crumpling, and bending, but not closing or opening holes, tearing, or gluing.

A [[Topological space]] is a set with a structure, called a topology, that allows continuous deformation to be defined. Examples include Euclidean spaces and, more generally, [[Metric space|metric spaces]], since any metric defines a topology. The deformations considered are homeomorphisms and homotopies, and a property invariant under them is a topological property. Basic examples are dimension, which distinguishes a line from a surface; compactness, which distinguishes a line from a circle; and connectedness, which distinguishes a circle from two non-intersecting circles. Intuitively, two spaces are homeomorphic if one can be deformed into the other without cutting or gluing, so a topologist cannot distinguish a coffee mug from a doughnut.

The underlying ideas go back to [[Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz]], who in the 17th century envisioned *geometria situs* and *analysis situs*. [[Leonhard Euler]]'s 1736 paper on the [[Seven Bridges of Königsberg]] showed that no route through the town (now Kaliningrad) could cross each bridge exactly once, a result depending only on connectivity, and the problem led to [[Graph theory|graph theory]]. His polyhedron formula, V − E + F = 2, is regarded by some authorities as the first theorem of topology. Johann Benedict Listing introduced the term *Topologie* in 1847, and the English form appeared in 1883 in his obituary in *Nature*. [[Henri Poincaré]] consolidated and extended their work, and his 1895 paper *Analysis Situs* introduced the concepts now known as [[Homotopy|homotopy]] and homology. The idea of a topological space was developed only in the first decades of the 20th century.

=== KEY: Tor_network_970def
HEADWORD: Tor
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Internet | Massachusetts
ENTRY:
Free, open-source overlay network for anonymous communication, based on onion routing. Volunteer-operated relays, numbering over seven thousand, route users' traffic along random paths, so no single point on the [[Internet]] can see both where traffic originates and where it is going. Onion routing was developed in the mid-1990s by employees of the U.S. Naval Research Laboratory, and the network's code was released under a free license in 2004. The [[Massachusetts]]-based nonprofit Tor Project, founded in 2006, maintains it. Users range from activists to criminal enterprises and law enforcement agencies.

=== KEY: Torah_264aaa
HEADWORD: Torah
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Hebrew Bible | Judaism | Moses | Old Testament | Synagogue | Talmud
ENTRY:
Compilation of the first five books of the [[Hebrew Bible]], namely Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers and Deuteronomy, central to [[Judaism]]. The word means "instruction" or "teaching," and the translation "law" is considered misleading. Rabbinic tradition holds that God gave the Torah through [[Moses]], who wrote it down, and that the Oral Torah, embodied in the [[Talmud]], consists of interpretations and amplifications. A Torah scroll is written by a scribe in Hebrew, and a portion is read publicly in a [[Synagogue|synagogue]] on Mondays, Thursdays and Saturdays. Muslims believe God gave the Torah to the prophets, and it is also part of the Christian [[Old Testament]]. Biblical scholarship generally regards the final form of the Torah as largely complete by the Persian period, though this is widely debated.
