=== KEY: Wilhelm_II_3c908a
HEADWORD: Wilhelm II
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Otto von Bismarck | Queen Victoria | World War I
ENTRY:
Last German emperor and king of Prussia (r. 1888–1918). He was the eldest of the 42 grandchildren of [[Queen Victoria|Queen Victoria]]. In 1890 he dismissed Chancellor [[Otto von Bismarck|Otto von Bismarck]] and assumed direct control of policy. His reign saw Germany acquire territory in China and the Pacific and become Europe's largest manufacturer. His tactless foreign policy, including a massive naval buildup, alienated most of Europe, and many consider it a cause of the empire's fall. In 1914 he guaranteed military support to Austria-Hungary during the July Crisis, which helped plunge Europe into [[World War I|World War I]]. He left most war decisions to the German Supreme Army Command, which dominated policy from 1916. Forced to abdicate during the German Revolution, he went into exile in the Netherlands, where he died.

=== KEY: Wilhelm_I_f84a92
HEADWORD: Wilhelm I
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Austro-Prussian War | Franco-Prussian War | Otto von Bismarck | Wilhelm II
ENTRY:
King of Prussia (r. 1861–88) and first German emperor (r. 1871–88). He was not expected to inherit the throne, but became heir presumptive in 1840. He played a major role in crushing the Revolutions of 1848 in Germany and was briefly exiled in England. After coming into conflict with the Landtag over military budgets, he appointed [[Otto von Bismarck|Otto von Bismarck]] minister president, beginning a partnership that lasted his life. Prussia won the [[Austro-Prussian War|Austro-Prussian War]] and, after the [[Franco-Prussian War|Franco-Prussian War]], German unification was achieved in 1871. Wilhelm largely left governance to Bismarck. He was succeeded by his son Frederick, who died after 99 days, and the throne passed to [[Wilhelm II|Wilhelm II]].

=== KEY: Wilhelm_R_ntgen_f1092b
HEADWORD: Röntgen, Wilhelm
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: ETH Zurich | Munich | X-ray
ENTRY:
German experimental physicist. After studies at [[ETH Zurich|ETH Zurich]] and a doctorate in physics from the University of Zurich, he held professorships at several German universities, ending at [[Munich|Munich]]. In 1895, while working at the University of Würzburg, he investigated electromagnetic radiation and found a wavelength range now called [[X-ray|X-rays]] (Röntgen rays in many languages). The rays passed through objects and made images on photographic plates. In 1901 he became the first recipient of the Nobel Prize in Physics. He refused to patent the technology, which he regarded as a public benefit. The element roentgenium is named in his honor, and his legacy endures in radiology.

=== KEY: Wilhelm_Wundt_a38d43
HEADWORD: Wundt, Wilhelm
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Consciousness | Leipzig | Sigmund Freud | William James
ENTRY:
German physiologist and philosopher, widely regarded as the father of experimental psychology. He was the first person to call himself a psychologist, and he distinguished psychology as a science from philosophy and biology. In 1879, at the University of [[Leipzig|Leipzig]], he founded the first formal laboratory for psychological research. He also established the first academic journal for psychological research and wrote the 1874 textbook *Principles of Physiological Psychology*. He studied the elements of [[Consciousness|consciousness]] such as sensation and feeling using introspection. A 1991 survey of American historians ranked him first for all-time eminence, ahead of [[William James|William James]] and [[Sigmund Freud|Sigmund Freud]].

=== KEY: Will_Eisner_4aa0bc
HEADWORD: Eisner, Will
PERSON: yes

ENTRY:
American cartoonist, writer and entrepreneur. Born in [[Brooklyn|Brooklyn]] to Jewish immigrant parents, he grew up poor and was one of the earliest cartoonists in the American comic book industry. His series *The Spirit* (1940–1952) was noted for its experiments in content and form. In 1978 he popularized the term graphic novel with *A Contract with God*. He later wrote *Comics and Sequential Art* (1985), an early contribution to formal comics studies. He co-founded the Eisner-Iger Studio, which packaged content for early comic books. In 1936, at age 19, he began selling cartoons to the new comic book *Wow, What A Magazine!* The Eisner Award is named in his honor.

=== KEY: Will_and_testament_382eef
HEADWORD: Will and testament
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Civil law (legal system) | Solon | Trust (law)
ENTRY:
Legal document expressing a person's wishes for the distribution of property after death. It also names an executor to manage the estate until final distribution, and it may create a testamentary [[Trust (law)|trust]] that takes effect only after death. The phrase comes from English law, when Old English and Law French were used side by side, and records show the terms were used interchangeably for personal and real property. Plutarch credited [[Solon|Solon]] with inventing the written will. [[Civil law (legal system)|Civil law]] systems often restrict freedom of disposition, for example through forced heirship. Holographic wills, handwritten by the testator, need not be witnessed, while nuncupative wills, which are oral, were often limited to sailors or military personnel.

=== KEY: Willem_Barentsz_eb8413
HEADWORD: Barentsz, Willem
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Arctic | Barents Sea | Kara Sea | Novaya Zemlya | Polar bear | Walrus
ENTRY:
Dutch navigator, cartographer, and [[Arctic]] explorer. Born on the island of Terschelling, Barentsz was a mapmaker who co-published an atlas of the Mediterranean with Petrus Plancius before devoting his career to finding a Northeast Passage to China. His first two voyages, in 1594 and 1595, were turned back by ice in the [[Kara Sea]] and near [[Novaya Zemlya]], although his crews met a [[Polar bear|polar bear]] and [[Walrus|walruses]]. On a third expedition, in 1596, the crew discovered Bear Island and Spitsbergen, but was trapped on Novaya Zemlya for almost a year. Barentsz died on the return voyage. The [[Barents Sea]] is named after him.

=== KEY: William_Blackstone_020d79
HEADWORD: Blackstone, William
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Abraham Lincoln | Alexander Hamilton | Common law | John Adams
ENTRY:
English jurist, judge, and Tory politician. Blackstone's lectures on English law, begun in 1753, were a success and were published as *An Analysis of the Laws of England* (1756). In 1759 he became the first Vinerian Professor of English Law at Oxford. His four-volume *Commentaries on the Laws of England*, first published from 1765, became the best-known description of the English [[Common law|common law]], and reprints appeared into the 1870s. He was elected a Tory member of Parliament for Hindon in 1761, and was appointed a justice of the Court of King's Bench in 1770, moving to the Court of Common Pleas that year. In the United States, the *Commentaries* influenced [[Alexander Hamilton]], [[John Adams]], and [[Abraham Lincoln]].
