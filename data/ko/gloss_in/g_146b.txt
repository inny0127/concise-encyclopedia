=== KEY: Whirlpool_Galaxy_e3fe62
HEADWORD: Whirlpool Galaxy
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Active galactic nucleus | Binoculars | Cepheid variable | Constellation | Edwin Hubble | Light-year | Spiral galaxy
ENTRY:
Interacting grand-design [[Spiral galaxy|spiral galaxy]] in the [[Constellation|constellation]] Canes Venatici, 31 million [[Light-year|light-years]] away. Also called Messier 51a or NGC 5194, it is 76,900 light-years in diameter and has a Seyfert 2 [[Active galactic nucleus|active galactic nucleus]]. It interacts with its companion, NGC 5195, and the pair is among the most famous and relatively close interacting systems, a favorite subject of galaxy interaction models. Charles Messier discovered it on October 13, 1773. William Parsons, 3rd Earl of Rosse, found its spiral structure with a 72-inch telescope, the first spiral "nebula" known to have one. [[Edwin Hubble|Edwin Hubble's]] observations of [[Cepheid variable|Cepheid variables]] in some spiral nebulae showed that they were separate galaxies. Amateur astronomers can see the pair with [[Binoculars|binoculars]].

=== KEY: Whirlpool_fc6a7c
HEADWORD: Whirlpool
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Niagara Falls | Rapids | Strait | Tide
ENTRY:
Body of rotating water produced by opposing currents, or by a current running into an obstacle. Miniature whirlpools form in a draining bath or sink. More powerful ones in seas may be called maelstroms, and a whirlpool with a downdraft is a vortex. In narrow ocean [[Strait|straits]] with fast-flowing water, whirlpools are often caused by [[Tide|tides]]. Small whirlpools appear at river [[Rapids|rapids]] and downstream of weirs and dams, while large cataracts such as [[Niagara Falls]] produce strong ones. Many stories describe ships sucked into maelstroms, although only smaller craft are actually in danger.

=== KEY: Whiskers_3091af
HEADWORD: Whiskers
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Arthropod | Crustacean | Hair | Insect | Marsupial | Monotreme | Primate | Triassic
ENTRY:
Specialized sensory [[Hair|hairs]] that help most mammals sense their environment, also called *vibrissae*. Whiskers detect air currents and objects, aiding navigation, hunting and spatial awareness, and can trigger reflexes that protect the eyes. They also convey an animal's emotional state to other members of its species. Whiskers grow in clusters above the eyes, on the chin, on the forelegs, near the ears and above the upper lip. Most mammals have them, including all non-human [[Primate|primates]] and [[Marsupial|marsupials]], but [[Monotreme|monotremes]] lack them. Whiskers are stiffer, thicker and longer than body fur, with richly innervated follicles. Rapid whisking, a cyclic motion of the tips, can reach 25 Hz in mice. Similar structures occur in [[Insect|insects]], [[Arthropod|arthropods]], [[Crustacean|crustaceans]], fish and birds. It has been suggested that they evolved during the [[Triassic]] period.

=== KEY: Whisky_498511
HEADWORD: Whisky
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Barley | Ireland | Liquor | Oak | Rye | Scotland | United States | Wheat
ENTRY:
Distilled [[Liquor|liquor]] made from fermented grain mash, usually [[Barley]], corn, [[Rye]] or [[Wheat]]. It is typically aged in charred white [[Oak|oak]] casks, and all classes share grain fermentation, distillation and aging in wooden casks. The name is an anglicization of the Gaelic word *uisce*, meaning "water"; the spelling whiskey is common in [[Ireland]] and the [[United States]]. Scotch whisky must be distilled and matured in oak casks in [[Scotland]] for at least three years. In the United States, bourbon must contain at least 51% corn in its mash. It is one of the world's most popular spirits, and Scotch exports were valued at £5.4 billion as of 2024.

=== KEY: White_25a817
HEADWORD: White
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Ancient Egypt | Ancient Rome | Black | Color | Pope | Snow | Titanium dioxide
ENTRY:
Lightest [[Color|color]], achromatic, reflecting all visible wavelengths of light. It is the color of [[Snow|snow]], chalk and milk, and the opposite of [[Black|black]]. White pigments, especially [[Titanium dioxide|titanium dioxide]], produce it, while screens create it by mixing red, blue and green light. Priestesses in [[Ancient Egypt|ancient Egypt]] and [[Ancient Rome|ancient Rome]] wore white as a symbol of purity, and Romans wore white togas as symbols of citizenship. From the 18th century white became the most common color of new churches and government buildings, especially in the United States. Surveys in Europe and the United States associate white most often with perfection, honesty, cleanliness and neutrality. The [[Pope|pope]] has worn white since 1566, and white is the color of mourning in many Asian cultures.

=== KEY: White_House_7fee96
HEADWORD: White House
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Dublin | Harry S. Truman | John Adams | Philadelphia | President of the United States | War of 1812 | Washington, D.C.
ENTRY:
Official residence and workplace of the [[President of the United States|president of the United States]] in [[Washington, D.C.]] It has housed every president since [[John Adams]] in 1800, when the national capital moved from [[Philadelphia]]. The Neoclassical design by Irish-born architect James Hoban was modeled on Leinster House in [[Dublin]]. British forces set the mansion ablaze in 1814, during the [[War of 1812]], and it was rebuilt. By 1948 its walls and beams were found close to failure, and [[Harry S. Truman]] oversaw a renovation that rebuilt the interior around a new steel frame.

=== KEY: White_Nile_6096f9
HEADWORD: White Nile
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Blue Nile | Burundi | Lake Albert (Africa) | Lake Victoria | Nile | Rwanda | South Sudan | Uganda
ENTRY:
River in North and East Africa, the longer but less voluminous of the two major tributaries of the [[Nile]], the other being the [[Blue Nile]]. Its name comes from the clay sediment carried in its water, which gives it a pale color. Strictly, the White Nile begins at Lake No, where the Bahr al Jabal and Bahr el Ghazal rivers meet; more broadly it includes the course from [[Lake Victoria]] to the Blue Nile. Its headwaters lie in [[Burundi]] and [[Rwanda]], and the most remote are 3,700 km (2,300 mi) from the Blue Nile. In [[Uganda]] it flows as the Victoria Nile to [[Lake Albert (Africa)|Lake Albert]], then as the Albert Nile to the border with [[South Sudan]], where it continues as the Mountain Nile, or Bahr al Jabal.

=== KEY: White_Sea_Baltic_Canal_422ae1
HEADWORD: White Sea–Baltic Canal
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Baltic Sea | Gulag | Joseph Stalin | Lake Onega | White Sea
ENTRY:
Man-made ship canal in northwestern Russia, linking the [[White Sea]] with [[Lake Onega]], which connects to the [[Baltic Sea]]. Opened on August 2, 1933, it is 227 km (141 mi) long, but its minimum depth of 3.5 m (11.5 ft) is inadequate for most seagoing vessels. The canal was built in twenty months by forced labor of [[Gulag]] inmates. Cost and time constraints of [[Joseph Stalin|Stalin's]] first five-year plan forced a shallower design than the 5.4 m (17.7 ft) originally proposed. Between 12,000 and 25,000 laborers died according to official records, while journalist Anne Applebaum estimates 25,000 deaths. As of 2008 it carried only ten to forty boats per day.

=== KEY: White_Sea_b69de6
HEADWORD: White Sea
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Baltic Sea | Barents Sea | Russia | Tide | White Sea–Baltic Canal
ENTRY:
Southern inlet of the [[Barents Sea]] on the northwest coast of [[Russia]], bordered by Karelia to the west, the Kola Peninsula to the north and the Kanin Peninsula to the northeast. The whole sea is considered part of Russia's internal waters. The port of Arkhangelsk, on Dvina Bay, was for much of Russian history the main center of international maritime trade, conducted by the Pomors. In the modern era the sea became an important Soviet naval and submarine base. It is one of four seas named after colors, along with the Black, Red and Yellow seas. The [[White Sea–Baltic Canal]] links it with the [[Baltic Sea]], and its [[Tide|tidal]] amplitude rises from 1 meter in the south to 10 meters in Mezen Bay.

=== KEY: White_blood_cell_738e66
HEADWORD: White blood cell
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Blood | Disease | Immune system | Infection | Inflammation | Lymphatic system | Red blood cell
ENTRY:
Leukocyte, or immune cell, of the [[Immune system|immune system]], which defends the body against [[Infection|infection]], other [[Disease|diseases]] and foreign entities. Unlike [[Red blood cell|red blood cells]], white blood cells have nuclei and are generally larger. They are produced from hematopoietic stem cells in the bone marrow and circulate through the [[Blood|blood]] and [[Lymphatic system|lymphatic system]]. The main types are granulocytes (neutrophils, eosinophils and basophils), lymphocytes (T cells and B cells) and monocytes. Neutrophils are the most abundant, at 50–70% of leukocytes. In the US the normal count is 4,000 to 11,000 cells per microliter of blood. An excess usually reflects [[Inflammation|inflammation]] or infection, while leukopenia, a count below normal, indicates a weakened immune system.
