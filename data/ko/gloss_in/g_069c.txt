Member of the elite infantry corps and household troops of the [[Ottoman Empire]]. The corps was one of the first infantry forces equipped primarily with [[Firearm|firearms]], adopted under Murad II (r. 1421–1444, 1446–1451). Originally recruited through the devşirme system of child levy, which took mainly Christian boys from the [[Balkans]], the janissaries were forbidden to marry until retirement. By the 17th century civilians had bought their way into the corps, which lost much of its military character. Resistant to reform, the corps was abolished by [[Mahmud II]] in 1826 in the Auspicious Incident, in which 6,000 or more janissaries were executed.

=== KEY: Japan_53a577
HEADWORD: Japan
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki | Tokugawa shogunate
ENTRY:
Island country (pop. almost 123 million) in East Asia, in the Pacific Ocean off the northeast coast of the Asian mainland, with Tokyo as its capital and largest city. It is bordered by the Sea of Japan to the west, the Sea of Okhotsk to the north, and the East China Sea to the south. The archipelago has four major islands and over 14,000 smaller ones, and the country is divided into 47 prefectures.

The first known habitation dates to the Upper Paleolithic, c. 36,000 BC. From the 12th century, real power was held by military aristocrats known as shōgun, enforced by the warrior nobility, the samurai. The [[Tokugawa shogunate]] unified Japan in 1600 and pursued an isolationist foreign policy until an American fleet forced the opening of trade in 1853. Imperial power was restored in 1868. In the Meiji period Japan industrialized rapidly, adopted the Meiji Constitution in 1889, and annexed Taiwan in 1895 and Korea in 1910. It invaded China in 1937 and attacked the U.S. and European colonial powers in 1941, entering World War II as an Axis power. After the [[Atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki|atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki]], it surrendered in 1945 and was under Allied occupation until 1952.

Japan experienced rapid economic growth in the following decades, but since the collapse of its asset price bubble in the early 1990s has had a prolonged stagnation known as the Lost Decades. It is a constitutional monarchy with a bicameral legislature, the National Diet, and the only Asian member of the G7. It is a leader in automotive, electronics, and robotics industries and has one of the world's highest life expectancies, but its population is declining.

=== KEY: Japanese_art_65c4a4
HEADWORD: Japanese art
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Anime | Buddhism | Edo period | Manga | Ukiyo-e
ENTRY:
Visual and material arts produced in Japan, from ancient pottery to modern [[Manga|manga]] and [[Anime|anime]]. The history of Japanese art begins with human habitation in Japan in the 10th millennium BCE. The earliest complex art was produced in the 7th and 8th centuries in connection with [[Buddhism]]. Until modern times the Japanese wrote with a brush, and painting became the preferred artistic expression. In the [[Edo period]], [[Ukiyo-e]] woodblock prints became a major form and proved instrumental in the Western world's 19th-century dialogue with Japanese art. Sculpture declined as a medium, since most large Japanese sculpture was associated with religion. Japanese lacquerware decorated with maki-e was exported to Europe and China until the 19th century. The Meiji period (1868–1912) brought an abrupt influx of Western styles.

=== KEY: Japanese_cuisine_238676
HEADWORD: Japanese cuisine
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Chinese cuisine | Curry | Ramen | Sushi | Tokyo
ENTRY:
Regional and traditional foods of Japan, known as washoku and based on rice with miso soup. Side dishes often feature fish, pickled vegetables, and vegetables cooked in broth. Seafood is commonly grilled or served raw as [[Sushi|sushi]], and deep-fried in batter as tempura. Buddhism long discouraged eating meat, but meat-based dishes such as tonkatsu became common after the modernization of the 1880s. Historically influenced by [[Chinese cuisine]], Japanese cuisine has adapted foreign dishes, including [[Ramen|ramen]], [[Curry|curry]], and hamburgers, to Japanese tastes. In 2011 Japan overtook France as the country with the most three-star Michelin restaurants, and [[Tokyo]] had the most three-star restaurants of any city as of 2018. In 2013 Japanese cuisine was added to the UNESCO Intangible Heritage List.

=== KEY: Japanese_language_825bc2
HEADWORD: Japanese language
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Japan
ENTRY:
Principal language of the Japonic family, with around 123 million speakers, primarily in [[Japan]], the only country where it is the national language. Proposals linking it to Ainu, Austronesian, Koreanic, and the discredited Altaic have not gained wide acceptance. Proto-Japonic is thought to have been brought from the Korean peninsula in the early to mid-4th century BC, during the Yayoi period.

Chinese documents of the 3rd century AD recorded a few Japanese words, but substantial Old Japanese texts date from the 8th century. The *Kojiki*, the earliest text, was written entirely in Chinese characters. Early Middle Japanese is the language of the Heian period (794–1185), when waves of Sino-Japanese vocabulary entered the language and made it mora-timed. Late Middle Japanese (1185–1600) saw extensive grammatical change and the first European loanwords. The basis of the standard dialect moved from the Kansai region to the Edo region (modern Tokyo) in the early modern period. After the end of Japan's isolation in 1853, loanwords from European languages increased significantly, and words from English roots have proliferated.

Japanese is agglutinative and mora-timed, with a pure vowel system, phonemic vowel and consonant length, and a lexically significant pitch accent. Word order is normally subject–object–verb, with particles marking grammatical function and sentence structure that is topic–comment. Nouns have no grammatical number or gender, and there are no articles. Verbs and adjectives are conjugated, verbs primarily for tense and voice but not person. A complex system of honorifics indicates the relative status of the speaker, the listener, and persons mentioned.

The writing system combines Chinese characters, called *kanji*, with two syllabaries derived from them, *hiragana* and *katakana*. Latin script (*rōmaji*) is used in a limited fashion, for example for imported acronyms.

=== KEY: Japanese_literature_450a07
HEADWORD: Japanese literature
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Haiku | Heian period | Kenzaburō Ōe | Kojiki | Murasaki Shikibu | The Tale of Genji | Yasunari Kawabata
ENTRY:
Body of works composed in the Japanese language, shaped by contact with Chinese and Indian literature. Early texts were often written in Classical Chinese. The earliest literary works, including [[Kojiki|*Kojiki*]] (712), date from the Nara period. During the [[Heian period]] (794–1185), literature developed its own style through the use of kana, and [[Murasaki Shikibu]] wrote [[The Tale of Genji|*The Tale of Genji*]] in the early 11th century. Later, [[Haiku|haiku]] was refined by Matsuo Bashō in the 17th century. Western literary influence grew during the Meiji era, and [[Yasunari Kawabata]] and [[Kenzaburō Ōe]] won Nobel Prizes in Literature in 1968 and 1994.

=== KEY: Japanese_mythology_9e8470
HEADWORD: Japanese mythology
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Amaterasu | Buddhism | Folk religion | Kojiki | Shinto
ENTRY:
Traditional stories, folktales, and beliefs that emerged in the islands of the Japanese archipelago. [[Shinto]] traditions are its cornerstone, and its pantheon holds uncountable kami, or spirits, tied to the archipelago's topography and to agriculturally based [[Folk religion]]. Contact with Chinese and Indian myths, including [[Buddhism]], also shaped its beliefs. The two oldest surviving sources are [[Kojiki|*Kojiki*]] (712) and *Nihon Shoki* (720). They relate the origins of the islands and of the imperial family, which claims direct descent from the sun goddess [[Amaterasu]] and her grandson Ninigi. The Edo-period scholar Motoori Norinaga's commentaries on the *Kojiki* are still studied for their influence on the understanding of Japanese myths.

=== KEY: Japanese_spider_crab_7ae526
HEADWORD: Japanese spider crab
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Crab
ENTRY:
Marine [[Crab|crab]] species, the largest crab found in waters around Japan. Its scientific name is *Macrocheira kaempferi*. Reaching up to 3.7 m (12.1 ft) from claw to claw, it has the largest leg span of any arthropod, and its carapace can grow to 40 cm (16 in) wide. It is the sole living member of its genus. The crab is sought by fisheries and considered a delicacy in Japan, and conservation efforts aim to prevent overfishing. It camouflages itself by attaching sponges and other organisms to its shell.

=== KEY: Japanese_writing_system_2a03e6
HEADWORD: Japanese writing system
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Chinese characters | Hiragana | Kanji | Katakana
ENTRY:
Mixed writing system combining logographic [[Kanji|kanji]], adopted from [[Chinese characters]], with the syllabaries [[Hiragana|hiragana]] and [[Katakana|katakana]]. Hiragana is used mainly for native words and grammatical elements, and katakana for foreign names, loanwords, and onomatopoeia. Almost all written sentences mix kanji and kana, which makes the system one of the most complicated in use. There are 2,136 commonly used kanji (*jōyō kanji*) as of 2010, and students must learn 1,026 before graduating sixth grade. Each kana syllabary has 46 basic characters, and both derive from man'yōgana, Chinese characters used for their sounds in the Nara period. Texts without kanji are rare and are mostly children's books.

=== KEY: Japanese_yen_47b477
HEADWORD: Japanese yen
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Euro | United States dollar | World War II
ENTRY:
Official currency of Japan, the third-most traded currency in foreign exchange markets after the [[United States dollar]] and the [[Euro|euro]]. The New Currency Act of 1871 introduced the modern system, divided decimally into 100 sen or 1,000 rin. The Bank of Japan was founded in 1882. After [[World War II]], the yen lost much of its value amid a debt crisis and hyperinflation. It was pegged to the dollar under the Bretton Woods system and has floated since February 1973. The Ministry of Finance and the Bank of Japan have intervened in the market to slow exchange rate movements, including in 1998–2003, 2010–2011, 2022, and 2024.

ENTRY:
Yellowish or greenish discoloration of the skin and eyes caused by high levels of bilirubin. In adults it usually indicates an underlying disease, such as liver dysfunction or a blocked bile duct. It is common in babies, with an estimated 80% affected in their first week of life. Causes range from [[Hepatitis]] and [[Cirrhosis]] to [[Medication|medications]], gallstones, and [[Cancer]]. === KEY: Java_d52387
HEADWORD: Java
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Dutch East Indies | Jakarta
ENTRY:
Island of Indonesia, one of the Greater Sunda Islands, bordered by the Indian Ocean to the south and the Java Sea to the north. It is the world's most populous island, with about 56% of the Indonesian population on only 7% of its land area. It is the world's 13th largest island. [[Jakarta]], Indonesia's capital, lies on its northwestern coast. Java dominates Indonesia politically, economically, and culturally.

The island was formed by volcanic eruptions caused by the subduction of the Australian Plate under the Sunda Plate. A chain of volcanic mountains forms its east–west spine, and the highest, Mount Semeru, reaches 3,676 m. Mount Merapi is the most active volcano in Java and in Indonesia. The mountains split the interior into relatively isolated regions suited to wet-rice cultivation, and its rice lands are among the richest in the world. Indonesian coffee was first grown on Java, beginning in 1699. Java is divided into four provinces, Banten, West Java, Central Java, and East Java, and two special regions, Jakarta and Yogyakarta.

Four main languages are spoken: Javanese, Sundanese, Madurese, and Betawi. Javanese and Sundanese are the most widely spoken; the Javanese live in the central and eastern parts and the Sundanese in the west. The majority are Muslim, but the population includes diverse religious beliefs.

Java was the center of powerful Hindu-Buddhist empires and Islamic sultanates, and the core of the colonial [[Dutch East Indies]]. It was also the center of the Indonesian struggle for independence in the 1930s and 1940s. Five of Indonesia's ten UNESCO World Heritage Sites are on Java, among them Borobudur, Prambanan, Ujung Kulon National Park, and Yogyakarta.

=== KEY: Java_programming_language_d37ced
HEADWORD: Java
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: C (programming language) | C++ | GitHub | Linux
ENTRY:
High-level, general-purpose, memory-safe, object-oriented programming language. Java was designed by James Gosling at Sun Microsystems and released in May 1995. It is intended to let programmers write once, run anywhere, because compiled bytecode runs on any Java virtual machine (JVM). Its syntax resembles [[C (programming language)|C]] and [[C++]], but it has fewer low-level facilities. Oracle, which bought Sun in 2010, offers its own HotSpot JVM, but the official reference implementation is the open-source OpenJDK, the default JVM for almost all [[Linux]] distributions. Java was the third most popular programming language on [[GitHub]] in 2022, although its use has gradually declined as other languages using the JVM have gained popularity.

=== KEY: Javanese_language_c3fbfb
HEADWORD: Javanese language
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Bali | Java
ENTRY:
Austronesian language spoken mainly in the central and eastern parts of the island of [[Java|Java]], Indonesia. It is the native language of more than 68 million people and the largest Austronesian language by number of native speakers. Its closest relatives include Sundanese, Madurese, and [[Bali|Balinese]]. Most speakers also use Indonesian. The language has regional dialects and speech levels, from informal ngoko to formal krama, which speakers adjust to social status and context. Traditionally written in the Javanese script, an abugida derived from the Brahmic Kawi script, it has increasingly been written in the Latin alphabet since the 20th century. Javanese is co-official with Indonesian in the Special Region of Yogyakarta.

=== KEY: Javelin_throw_c88ffa
HEADWORD: Javelin throw
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Ancient Olympic Games | Decathlon
ENTRY:
Track and field event in which a spear-like javelin is thrown as far as possible from a runway into a marked sector. It is one of the four traditional throwing events in athletics and is part of the men's [[Decathlon]] and the women's heptathlon. It originated in ancient Greece, where it was part of the pentathlon at the [[Ancient Olympic Games]] in 708 BC, and it entered the modern Olympic program for men in 1908 and for women in 1932. Athletes must not step beyond the throwing arc, and a throw is valid only if the javelin lands tip-first. The longest valid throw wins. The men's world record is 98.48 meters, set by Jan Železný in 1996, and the women's is 72.28 meters, set by Barbora Špotáková in 2008.
