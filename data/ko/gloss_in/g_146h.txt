ENTRY:
27th president of the United States (1909–1913) and tenth chief justice of the United States (1921–1930), the only person to hold both offices. Born in [[Cincinnati]], Taft was appointed civilian governor of the [[Philippines]] by [[William McKinley]] and became secretary of war under [[Theodore Roosevelt]], his hand-picked successor. His administration was torn between Republican conservatives and progressives; when Roosevelt challenged him for the 1912 nomination, [[Woodrow Wilson]] won the presidency. Taft later taught at [[Yale University|Yale]], and as chief justice culminated his court administrative reform in the Judiciary Act of 1925. Historians rank him near the middle of U.S. presidents.

=== KEY: William_III_of_England_f4a28b
HEADWORD: William III
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Glorious Revolution | Louis XIV
ENTRY:
Stadtholder of the Dutch Republic from 1672 and king of England, Scotland, and Ireland from 1689 to 1702. Born Prince of Orange, William led resistance to the expansionism of [[Louis XIV]] and rebuilt the Dutch army after the French invasion of 1672. Backed by English leaders opposed to the policies of his uncle and father-in-law, James II, he invaded England in November 1688. James was deposed in the [[Glorious Revolution]], and William ruled jointly with his wife, Mary II, until her death in 1694. The event led to the English Bill of Rights. William's role in ordering the Massacre of Glencoe in Scotland remains notorious. Having no children, he was succeeded by Anne, Mary's sister.

=== KEY: William_IV_6a16ea
HEADWORD: William IV
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: British Empire | George III | Royal Navy
ENTRY:
King of the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland and of Hanover (1830–1837). The third son of [[George III]], William served in the [[Royal Navy]] as a young man and was nicknamed the "Sailor King." His reign saw the Reform Act 1832, the restriction of child labor, and the abolition of slavery in nearly all of the [[British Empire]]. He was the last British monarch to appoint a prime minister against the will of Parliament. With no surviving legitimate children, he was succeeded by his niece Victoria in the United Kingdom and by his brother Ernest Augustus in Hanover.

=== KEY: William_James_3f90d6
HEADWORD: James, William
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Charles Sanders Peirce | Harvard University | John Dewey | Pragmatism | Wilhelm Wundt
ENTRY:
American philosopher and psychologist, often called the father of American psychology. James taught anatomy at [[Harvard University|Harvard]] and was the first educator to offer a psychology course in the United States. *The Principles of Psychology* (1890) was a groundbreaking text in the field. With [[Charles Sanders Peirce]], he established [[Pragmatism|pragmatism]], and he also developed radical empiricism. *The Varieties of Religious Experience* examined religious experience, including mysticism, from a psychological standpoint. A 1991 survey ranked him second among psychologists, after [[Wilhelm Wundt]], the founder of experimental psychology. His work influenced [[John Dewey]] and Ludwig Wittgenstein.

ENTRY:
25th president of the United States (1897–1901). A Republican and [[American Civil War|Civil War]] veteran who rose to brevet major, McKinley championed high protective tariffs and the gold standard. He defeated William Jennings Bryan in 1896 and 1900. His presidency oversaw the [[Spanish–American War]] and the annexation of [[Hawaii]], [[Puerto Rico]], [[Guam]], and the [[Philippines]]. He was shot by an anarchist in September 1901 and succeeded by [[Theodore Roosevelt]].

=== KEY: William_Pitt_the_Younger_5b6891
HEADWORD: Pitt, William, the Younger
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: French Revolution | Napoleonic Wars | Robert Walpole | United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland
ENTRY:
British statesman and prime minister, known as Pitt the Younger to distinguish him from his father. He became prime minister at 24, the youngest in British and world history, and served eighteen years and 343 days in total, second only to [[Robert Walpole]]. He was the last prime minister of Great Britain and the first of the [[United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland|United Kingdom]]. His premiership was dominated by the [[French Revolution]] and the [[Napoleonic Wars]]; he raised taxes to fund the war against France and cracked down on radicalism at home. To counter Irish support for France, he engineered the Acts of Union 1800, but his attempt to secure Catholic emancipation as part of the Union failed. He was also Chancellor of the Exchequer throughout his premiership.

=== KEY: William_Randolph_Hearst_cc3a29
HEADWORD: Hearst, William Randolph
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Joseph Pulitzer | Nazi Germany | San Francisco
ENTRY:
American newspaper publisher and Democratic politician, who built the largest newspaper and magazine business in the world. Born in [[San Francisco]], he took over the *San Francisco Examiner* and then bought the *New York Journal*, fighting a circulation war with [[Joseph Pulitzer]] through giant headlines and sensational stories. His chain eventually numbered nearly 30 newspapers. Twice elected to the U.S. House of Representatives as a Democrat, he ran unsuccessfully for president in 1904, and for mayor of New York City and governor of New York. His papers sensationalized Spanish atrocities in Cuba and pressed for war in 1898; he later claimed to have started the war, a claim historians reject as exaggerated. After World War I he promoted isolationism, and in the 1930s he ordered favorable coverage of [[Nazi Germany]]. Heavy debts during the Great Depression forced him to liquidate most of his assets in the late 1930s.

=== KEY: William_Rowan_Hamilton_d6b637
HEADWORD: Hamilton, William Rowan
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Algebra | Classical mechanics | Lagrangian mechanics | Linear algebra | Optics | Quaternion
ENTRY:
Irish mathematician, physicist, and astronomer. As a boy Hamilton learned about a dozen languages, and by age 16 he had covered much of Newton's *Principia*. He made major contributions to [[Algebra|algebra]], [[Classical mechanics|classical mechanics]], and [[Optics|optics]]. His invention of [[Quaternion|quaternions]], a system of four-dimensional numbers, made him one of the founders of modern [[Linear algebra|linear algebra]]. His reformulation of [[Lagrangian mechanics]] is considered fundamental to modern theoretical physics. He was Andrews Professor of Astronomy at Trinity College Dublin and director of Dunsink Observatory from 1827 until his death, and was knighted in 1835. He is regarded as arguably the most influential Irish physicist, along with Ernest Walton.

=== KEY: William_S_Burroughs_54c22c
HEADWORD: Burroughs, William S.
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Harvard University | Jack Kerouac | Jonathan Swift | Mexico City | Morphine
ENTRY:
American writer and visual artist, a central figure of the Beat Generation. Born into a wealthy family in St. Louis, he attended [[Harvard University|Harvard]] and then medical school in Vienna before turning to [[Morphine|morphine]] and then heroin, an addiction that shaped much of his work. His experimental novels, including *Junkie* (1953) and *Naked Lunch* (1959), feature unreliable narrators and the cut-up technique he popularized with Brion Gysin. In 1951 he killed his second wife, Joan Vollmer, in [[Mexico City]]. [[Jack Kerouac]] called him the "greatest satirical writer since [[Jonathan Swift]]."
