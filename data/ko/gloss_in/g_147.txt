=== KEY: Windmill_35aec0
HEADWORD: Windmill
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: China | Flanders | France | Hammurabi | Iran | Mesopotamia | Yorkshire
ENTRY:
Machine powered by wind acting on vanes or sails, used chiefly to mill grain. Wind-powered machines appeared earlier: the Babylonian ruler [[Hammurabi]] had used wind power for irrigation in [[Mesopotamia]] in the 17th century BC. The first practical windmills were horizontal, or panemone, machines with six to 12 sails covered in reed matting or cloth. They first appeared in [[Iran|Persia]] in the 9th century and spread through the Middle East and Central Asia to [[China]]. Vertical windmills first appeared in northwestern Europe in the 12th century, in an area that included [[Flanders]] and northern [[France]]. The earliest certain reference in northern Europe dates from 1185, at Weedley in [[Yorkshire]]. Vertical-axle windmills were also built in small numbers in Europe during the 18th and 19th centuries as independent inventions.

=== KEY: Window_c89686

ENTRY:
Opening in a [[Wall|wall]], [[Door|door]], [[Roof|roof]] or [[Vehicle|vehicle]] that admits [[Light|light]] and sometimes allows the passage of sound and air. Modern windows are usually glazed, with a sash set in a frame, and many can be opened for ventilation or closed to exclude [[Weather|inclement weather]]. Modern floor-to-ceiling windows became possible only after industrial plate glass processes were fully perfected.

=== KEY: Wing_a42dc0
HEADWORD: Wing
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Aerodynamics | Aircraft | Bird | Drag (physics) | Fluid mechanics | Lift (force) | Submarine
ENTRY:
Structure, shaped as an airfoil, that produces both [[Lift (force)|lift]] and [[Drag (physics)|drag]] while moving through air. Wing efficiency is expressed as the lift-to-drag ratio. The design and analysis of aircraft wings is a principal application of [[Aerodynamics]], a branch of [[Fluid mechanics]]. Wings with an asymmetrical cross-section are the norm in subsonic flight, while symmetrical airfoils are used in [[Aircraft|aerobatic aircraft]] because they fly the same whether upright or inverted. For a wing to produce lift, it must be at a suitable angle of attack; it deflects air downward, and the air exerts an equal and opposite force on the wing. This force arises from higher pressure beneath the wing than above it. The word wing comes from Old Norse *vængr*, and for centuries it referred mainly to the foremost limbs of [[Bird|birds]]. Equivalent foils that move through water are found on hydrofoil vessels and on [[Submarine|submarines]].

=== KEY: Winter_b18e66

PERSON: no

ENTRY:
Coldest and darkest season of the year in temperate and polar climates, between [[Autumn|autumn]] and [[Spring (season)|spring]]. Winter occurs when a hemisphere is tilted away from the [[Sun]] because of the [[Axial tilt|tilt of Earth's axis]]. When it is winter in the [[Northern Hemisphere]], it is summer in the Southern Hemisphere. Winter typically brings precipitation that is mainly rain or snow, depending on climate. The shortest day and longest night fall at the [[Winter solstice|winter solstice]]. The severity of winter varies with elevation, exposure to marine winds and precipitation. [[Winnipeg]], on the landlocked Great Plains, has a January high of −11.3 °C (11.7 °F), while [[Vancouver]], on Canada's Pacific coast, has a January low of 1.4 °C (34.5 °F). Meteorologists define winter as the three calendar months with the lowest average temperatures.

=== KEY: Winter_solstice_c2f0f3
HEADWORD: Winter solstice
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Earth | England | Ireland | Stonehenge | Sun
ENTRY:
Astronomical event when either pole of [[Earth]] reaches its maximum tilt away from the [[Sun]]. It is the shortest day and longest night of the year in its hemisphere, when the Sun is at its lowest arc in the sky. The date is December 21 or 22 in the Northern Hemisphere and June 20 or 21 in the Southern. Daylight hours then begin to increase again. Since prehistory, many cultures have marked the solstice with festivals and rites. Neolithic sites such as [[Stonehenge]] in [[England]] and Newgrange in [[Ireland]] are aligned with the solstice sunrise or sunset.

Use of magic by a person called a witch, traditionally to inflict supernatural harm or misfortune on others. === KEY: Wolf_Rayet_star_435356

PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Planetary nebula | Star | Ultraviolet
ENTRY:
Rare class of [[Star|star]] with unusual spectra showing broad emission lines of ionized helium and highly ionized nitrogen or carbon. Classic Wolf–Rayet stars are evolved, massive stars that have lost their outer hydrogen and are fusing helium or heavier elements in the core. Their surface temperatures range from 20,000 K to around 210,000 K, hotter than almost all other stars. They are highly luminous, though not especially bright visually because most of their output is [[Ultraviolet|ultraviolet]]. Strong stellar winds drive their mass loss. A separate group are the central stars of [[Planetary nebula|planetary nebulae]], which have shed their atmospheres to reveal a bare carbon-oxygen core.

Member of the [[Spider|spider]] family Lycosidae, named for the robust and agile hunting skills of its members. === KEY: Wolfgang_Amadeus_Mozart_1c82c2
HEADWORD: Mozart, Wolfgang Amadeus
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Chamber music | Concerto | Don Giovanni | Holy Roman Empire | Joseph II | Opera | Salzburg
ENTRY:
Salzburg-born composer and musician of the Classical period. He completed more than 800 works, including outstanding examples of most genres of his time: symphonies, [[Concerto|concertos]], [[Chamber music|chamber music]], [[Opera|opera]], and choral music. He is regarded as one of the greatest composers in Western music.

Born in [[Salzburg]], then within the [[Holy Roman Empire]], Mozart was the youngest of seven children, five of whom died in infancy. He emerged as a child prodigy under his father Leopold, an experienced teacher. At five he was competent on keyboard and violin, had begun to compose, and had performed before European royalty. His father took him on a grand tour of Europe and then three trips to Italy. At 17 he was a musician at the Salzburg court. A fruitless search for employment (1777–1779) took him to Paris, Mannheim, and Munich; during this time he wrote his five violin concertos, and the opera *Idomeneo*.

In 1781, while visiting Vienna, his quarrels with his Salzburg employers came to a head and he was dismissed. He remained in Vienna for the rest of his life, achieving fame and some financial success but no long-term security. He married Constanze Weber on August 4, 1782, and was appointed chamber composer to Emperor [[Joseph II]] in 1787.

His early Vienna years produced the opera *Die Entführung aus dem Serail*, the Great Mass in C minor, and he wrote more than a dozen piano concertos there, many considered masterpieces. In his final years he composed his last three symphonies, culminating in the *Jupiter*, the serenade *Eine kleine Nachtmusik*, the operas *Le nozze di Figaro*, [[Don Giovanni]], *Così fan tutte*, and *Die Zauberflöte*, and the Requiem, largely unfinished at his death at 35.

Austrian theoretical physicist and pioneer of [[Quantum mechanics|quantum mechanics]]. Anatomically, female characteristics of human sexual dimorphism center on the [[Female reproductive system|reproductive system]], which includes the ovaries, fallopian tubes, uterus, vagina, and vulva. Common accompanying characteristics are larger breasts, a generally wider pelvis, less facial and body hair, higher body fat, shorter stature, and less muscle mass; these facilitate pregnancy, childbirth, and breastfeeding. Women have occupied positions at every level of society, though patriarchal gender roles have often limited them. Feminist movements aim at [[Gender equality|gender equality]].

=== KEY: Wood_6e4dd7
HEADWORD: Wood
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Cellulose | Construction | Fuel | Furniture | Paper | Vascular cambium
ENTRY:
Natural lignocellulosic material derived primarily from the secondary xylem of trees and other woody plants. In living plants it provides mechanical support and, in the functional sapwood, conducts water and dissolved minerals. It is a natural composite of [[Cellulose|cellulosic]] fibers, strong in tension, embedded in a matrix of lignin and hemicelluloses that resists compression. Dry wood is roughly 40–50% cellulose, 20–35% hemicelluloses, and 15–35% lignin.

A tree's diameter increases by secondary growth: cell division in the [[Vascular cambium|vascular cambium]] adds layers that envelop the whole stem, branches, and roots. Where seasons are distinct, growth appears as rings; the part of a ring formed early in the season is usually lighter, called earlywood, and the later part is latewood. Softwoods, from conifers, are dominated by tracheids, while hardwoods have vessel elements for water transport. Density ranges from about 0.20 g/cm³ in balsa to over 1.0 g/cm³ in lignum vitae.

The earliest known plants to have grown wood, found in New Brunswick in 2011, lived approximately 395 to 400 million years ago. Humans have used wood since prehistoric times for [[Fuel|fuel]], tools, weapons, and shelter, and later for [[Construction|construction]], [[Furniture|furniture]], and [[Paper|paper]]. The earliest spears were crafted from wood, and Neolithic European longhouses were built primarily of wood. Known wooden constructions date back ten thousand years.

Forests worldwide hold a growing stock of about 630 billion cubic meters, and almost 4 billion cubic meters of wood were harvested in 2023, mostly for furniture and building construction. Wood science emerged as a distinct field in the early 20th century.

=== KEY: Word_07a094
HEADWORD: Word
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Morpheme | Syntax
ENTRY:
Basic element of language that carries meaning, can be used on its own, and is uninterruptible. Speakers usually have an intuitive grasp of what a word is, but linguists have no consensus on a definition, and attempts to find specific criteria remain controversial. Some linguists propose avoiding the term in favor of better-defined ones such as morphemes.

A word is distinguished from a [[Morpheme|morpheme]], the smallest unit of language with meaning, even if it cannot stand alone. Words consist of at least one morpheme. In English they generally include at least one root (*rock*, *god*, *write*) and possibly affixes (*-s*, *un-*, *-ly*, *-ness*). Words with more than one root, such as *typewriter*, are compound words.

Definitions can be consistent at particular levels. Phonologically, a word is the smallest segment of sound that can be isolated by word accent and boundary markers. Orthographically, it is a segment set off by blank spaces. Morphologically, it is the basic element of grammatical paradigms such as inflection. Semantically, it is the smallest relatively independent carrier of meaning in a lexicon, and in [[Syntax|syntax]], the smallest permutable and substitutable unit of a sentence. In some languages these types coincide; in others they correspond to elements of different size.

Phonological words can differ from intuition. In Walmatjari, an Australian language, a phonological word must have at least two syllables. The Finnish compound *pääkaupunki* "capital" counts as two phonological words because it does not conform to Finnish vowel harmony within words.

Dictionaries list a language's lexicon as lemmas, indicating what its writers regard as words.

=== KEY: Work_human_activity_6b70d4
HEADWORD: Work
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: First aid | Goods | Hobby | Hunter-gatherer | Mark Twain
ENTRY:
Intentional human activity undertaken to meet one's own needs and wants, or those of others or of organizations. In economics, work is human labor that, alongside other factors of production, contributes to the creation of [[Goods|goods]] and services. It exists in all human societies, paid or unpaid, from gathering resources by hand in [[Hunter-gatherer|hunter-gatherer]] groups to operating complex technologies in industrial and post-industrial societies. A person's regular role in work is an occupation, or job. Societies coordinate work through institutions such as government programs, nonprofit organizations, cooperatives, and corporations.

What counts as work depends on context. Sport is a job for a professional athlete but a [[Hobby|hobby]] for someone playing for fun, and a paramedic on duty is working while a bystander giving [[First aid|first aid]] is not. [[Mark Twain]]'s whitewashed-fence scene in *The Adventures of Tom Sawyer* expresses the view that the label is somewhat subjective.

Hunter-gatherers varied their effort with the seasons; agriculture brought more sustained effort, peaking at harvest. In the early modern era, Protestantism and proto-capitalism emphasized the moral advantages of hard work, and industrialization intensified demands on workers to keep pace with machines. Restrictions on working hours and the ages of workers followed. According to a 2024 study, most current U.S. employment is in occupations introduced since 1940. Economists commonly divide economies into primary (extraction), secondary (manufacturing), and tertiary (services) sectors.

Work has been tied to power, class, tradition, and rights, and dirty, dangerous, or demeaning occupations can threaten survival. Paul Lafargue, David Graeber, and Bob Black are among those who have urged reducing or abolishing it. Retirement and, more recently, experiments with universal basic income aim to end the economic necessity of lifelong work.

Work is positive when the force has a component in the direction of displacement and negative when it opposes the displacement. === KEY: World_War_II_fbb78b

Global conflict (September 1, 1939–September 2, 1945) between two coalitions, the Allies and the [[Axis powers]]. Nearly all the world's countries took part, many in total war on an unprecedented scale. It was the deadliest conflict in history; estimates of deaths range from 60 million to 85 million, a majority civilians.

Causes included unresolved tensions after [[World War I]], the rise of fascism in Europe, and Japanese militarism. === KEY: World_War_I_f32658

Global conflict (July 28, 1914–November 11, 1918), also called the Great War, between the Allies (or Entente) and the [[Central Powers]]. === KEY: Writing_af96ab
HEADWORD: Writing
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Cuneiform | Email | Literacy | Mesopotamia | Papyrus | Reading | Typewriter | Writing system
ENTRY:
Act of creating a persistent, usually visual representation of language on a surface. As a structured system of communication, it is also known as written language. A set of symbols, called a script, together with the rules by which it encodes a spoken language, is a [[Writing system|writing system]]. In rare cases writing is tactile rather than visual. Its physical output, whether inscribed, mechanically transferred, or digitally represented, is called a text; construing a text is [[Reading|reading]], and the ability to read and write is [[Literacy|literacy]].

Writing systems are not languages in themselves but durable means of representing language so that it can be understood later. Language is universal across human societies, but most spoken languages are not written. Writing extends speech across space, as in correspondence, and stores it for future reading, as in libraries. It also lets people externalize their thinking in forms that are easier to reflect on, elaborate, and revise.

Writing arose independently in the ancient Near East, Egypt, China, and Mesoamerica. The earliest attested systems appeared around 3200 BC in [[Mesopotamia]] with proto-[[Cuneiform|cuneiform]], first used for accounting on clay tokens and tablets, and nearly simultaneously in Egypt with hieroglyphs. Systems later diversified into logographic, syllabic, and alphabetic forms. Writing emerged to meet the needs of societies growing in economic and social complexity: tracking produce and wealth, recording history, and administering through legal codes, census records, contracts, deeds, taxation, and treaties.

Implements have included fingers, styluses, ink brushes, pencils, and pens; surfaces have included stone and clay tablets, bamboo slips, [[Papyrus|papyrus]], wax tablets, vellum, parchment, paper, copperplate, and slate. The [[Typewriter|typewriter]] and the digital word processor let writers produce visually consistent text mechanically. Natural language generation software first produced formulaic texts such as weather forecasts and sports summaries; generative pre-trained transformers can now produce longer texts from a prompt.

Individual motivations include going beyond the limits of memory (lists, recipes, maps), spreading ideas (essays, petitions, manifestos), storytelling, maintaining social networks, business correspondence, and diaries. Digital communication such as [[Email|email]] and social media has made writing an increasingly important part of daily life. In many occupations, including law, accounting, and software design, written documentation is both the main product and the mode of work. Commerce, banking, and government rest on written documents, from contracts and tax records to national constitutions.

=== KEY: Writing_system_f38234
HEADWORD: Writing system
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Alphabet | Chinese characters | Latin alphabet | Morpheme | Orthography | Phoneme | Vietnamese language
ENTRY:
Conventional system for representing a particular language using a set of symbols, called a script, and the rules those symbols encode. The earliest writing systems appeared in the late 4th millennium BC. Each independently invented system emerged from proto-writing, in which a few ideographs were used in a way that could not fully encode language.

Systems are classified by how their symbols, called graphemes, relate to units of language. Phonetic systems, which include alphabets and syllabaries, use graphemes that correspond to sounds. [[Alphabet|Alphabets]] use letters that generally correspond to spoken [[Phoneme|phonemes]]: pure alphabets represent consonants and vowels, abjads generally only consonants, and abugidas consonant–vowel pairs. Syllabaries use syllabograms representing whole syllables or moras. Logographic systems use graphemes that represent units of meaning such as words or [[Morpheme|morphemes]]. Alphabets typically use fewer than 100 symbols, syllabaries and logographies hundreds or thousands.

Under most contemporary definitions, writing is a visual and tactile notation whose symbols correspond systematically to functional units of a spoken or signed language, which excludes drawings and maps. Most spoken languages have not been written, and all written languages rest on a spoken one. One language, such as Hindustani, can be written in several systems, and one system can represent several languages. [[Chinese characters]] were used to write [[Vietnamese language|Vietnamese]] from at least the 13th century until the Latin-based Vietnamese alphabet replaced them in the 20th century.

The grapheme is the basic functional unit of a writing system, comparable to the phoneme in speech; visually distinct variants of one grapheme are called allographs. [[Orthography]] is the set of rules and conventions for writing shared by a community, including spelling. English orthography, for example, uses uppercase and lowercase forms of 26 letters of the [[Latin alphabet]].

It is made by puddling, melting high-carbon cast iron in a [[Charcoal|charcoal]] or coke hearth so that the excess carbon oxidizes. The iron is never fully molten, so impurities remain, and its fibrous slag inclusions give it a wood-like grain. It is tough, malleable, ductile and easily forge welded. Before effective steelmaking, wrought iron was the most common malleable iron, used for [[Rivet|rivets]], [[Nail (fastener)|nails]], wire, [[Chain|chains]], rails, pipes and horseshoes. Its use peaked in the 1860s, then declined as cheaper [[Steel|steel]] became available. Commercial production has ceased, and products sold as wrought iron are usually mild steel.

=== KEY: Wu_Chinese_f9c221
HEADWORD: Wu Chinese
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Chinese language | Jiangsu | Shanghai | Yangtze | Zhejiang
ENTRY:
Group of [[Chinese language|Chinese]] varieties spoken at and south of the [[Yangtze]] delta. Wu is spoken primarily in [[Shanghai]], [[Zhejiang]] province and parts of [[Jiangsu]] province, and it is at times simply called Shanghainese. The Suzhounese variety was the prestige dialect of Wu in the 19th century, but Shanghainese had replaced it by the turn of the 20th century. Northern Wu varieties form a mutually intelligible language family, while Southern Wu varieties are not mutually intelligible. Linguists value Wu for preserving all voiced initials of medieval Chinese and the checked tone as a glottal stop. Wu, especially the Suzhou variety, is traditionally perceived as soft, leading to the idiom "the tender speech of Wu." Most speakers refer only to their local variety rather than to the group as a whole.

American hip-hop group formed in Staten Island, [[New York City]], in 1992. Members include RZA, GZA, Method Man, Raekwon, Ghostface Killah, Inspectah Deck, U-God and Masta Killa, plus Ol' Dirty Bastard until his death in 2004. Its debut album, *Enter the Wu-Tang (36 Chambers)* (1993), is widely considered one of the greatest hip-hop albums. *Wu-Tang Forever* (1997) debuted atop the *Billboard* 200. The only copy of *Once Upon a Time in Shaolin* was bought for $2 million by [[Martin Shkreli]]. The group was inducted into the [[Rock and Roll Hall of Fame]] in 2026.
