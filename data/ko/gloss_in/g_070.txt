=== KEY: Jellyfish_28f4e6
HEADWORD: Jellyfish
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Cnidaria | Desalination | Larva | Molecular biology
ENTRY:
Gelatinous, soft-bodied marine animal, the medusa stage of certain members of the phylum [[Cnidaria]]. A jellyfish has a bell-shaped body with trailing tentacles armed with stinging cells, which it uses to capture prey. Pulsating contractions of the bell propel it through open water. The medusa is normally the sexual stage of a complex life cycle, which produces planula [[Larva|larvae]] that settle as sedentary polyps. Jellyfish live from surface waters to the deep sea and have existed for at least 500 million years, possibly 700 million, making them the oldest multi-organ animal group. The green fluorescent protein of some species is widely used in [[Molecular biology|molecular biology]] research. Swarms can clog fishing nets and the cooling systems of [[Desalination|desalination]] plants, and thousands of swimmers worldwide are stung every year.

=== KEY: Jensen_s_inequality_3bf3da
HEADWORD: Jensen's inequality
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Probability theory | Random variable
ENTRY:
Theorem relating the value of a convex function at a mean to the mean of the function's values. Named after the Danish mathematician Johan Jensen, who proved it in 1906, it states that for a convex function f and non-negative weights λ₁, …, λₙ summing to 1, f(λ₁x₁ + … + λₙxₙ) ≤ λ₁f(x₁) + … + λₙf(xₙ). In [[Probability theory|probability theory]], for a [[Random variable|random variable]] X and convex φ, it is stated as φ(E[X]) ≤ E[φ(X)]. The difference between the two sides is called the Jensen gap.

=== KEY: Jerusalem_0a5ed4
HEADWORD: Jerusalem
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Abrahamic religions | Church of the Holy Sepulchre | Israeli–Palestinian conflict | Six-Day War | Suleiman the Magnificent | Temple Mount | World Heritage Site
ENTRY:
City (pop., 2024: 1,050,153) on a plateau in the Judaean Mountains, in the Southern Levant between the Mediterranean and the Dead Sea. One of the oldest cities in the world, it is holy to the three [[Abrahamic religions]]: Judaism, Christianity, and Islam.

The City of David shows first signs of settlement in the 4th millennium BC, in the form of encampments of nomadic shepherds. Egyptian tablets of the 14th century BC name the city Urusalim, probably meaning "foundation of [the god] Shalem." Radiocarbon dating of 103 samples shows Iron Age settlement in the 12th–10th centuries BC and growth until the Babylonian destruction in 586 BC. According to the Hebrew Bible, King David made it the capital of the United Kingdom of Israel and his son Solomon commissioned the First Temple. Jerusalem has been destroyed at least twice, besieged 23 times, captured and recaptured 44 times, and attacked 52 times. The walls that define the Old City were rebuilt in 1538 under [[Suleiman the Magnificent]]. The Old City became a [[World Heritage Site]] in 1981.

Despite covering only 0.9 km², the Old City contains the [[Temple Mount]], with the Western Wall, Dome of the Rock, and Al-Aqsa Mosque, and the [[Church of the Holy Sepulchre]]. Christians associate the city with the crucifixion and resurrection of Jesus. In Islam it is the third-holiest city, after Mecca and Medina, and was the first direction of Muslim prayer.

Israel occupied East Jerusalem from Jordan in 1967 following the [[Six-Day War]] and annexed it. The 1980 Jerusalem Law calls the city Israel's undivided capital, and the Knesset and Supreme Court sit there. Neither Israel's nor Palestine's claim to the city as capital is widely recognized internationally, and its status remains a core issue in the [[Israeli–Palestinian conflict]].

=== KEY: Jesus_e98296

Jewish preacher and religious leader active in the Roman province of Judaea in the 1st century, and the central figure of [[Christianity]]. Most branches of Christianity consider him the incarnation of God the Son and the awaited [[Messiah]].

=== KEY: Jet_aircraft_df4df7

[[Fixed-wing aircraft|Fixed-wing]] aircraft propelled by one or more [[Jet engine|jet engines]]. === KEY: Jet_engine_3ec90e
HEADWORD: Jet engine
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Turbofan
ENTRY:
Reaction engine that produces thrust by expelling a fast-moving jet of heated gas, usually air. Air-breathing jet engines, including turbojets, [[Turbofan|turbofans]], ramjets and scramjets, are internal combustion engines. Typically they have a rotating compressor powered by a turbine, with leftover power providing thrust through a nozzle, a process known as the Brayton thermodynamic cycle. Early jets used relatively inefficient turbojets for subsonic flight, so most modern subsonic jet aircraft use high-bypass turbofans, which give higher speed and greater fuel efficiency than propeller engines over long distances. Ramjets and scramjets instead use the ram effect of the vehicle's speed rather than a mechanical compressor. Thrust in a typical jetliner engine grew from 5,000 lbf (22 kN) in the 1950s to 115,000 lbf (510 kN) in the 1990s.

=== KEY: Jet_stream_a3104c
HEADWORD: Jet stream
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: El Niño–Southern Oscillation
ENTRY:
Fast-flowing, narrow air current in the atmosphere. The main terrestrial jet streams are westerly winds near the tropopause, flowing west to east around the globe. Each hemisphere has a polar jet, typically traveling at about 110 mph (180 km/h) at around 30,000 ft (9.1 km), and a weaker subtropical jet lies closer to the equator. The [[El Niño–Southern Oscillation]] affects their location, which in turn affects weather over the tropical Pacific Ocean. Meteorologists use jet stream positions in weather forecasting, and airlines use them to reduce flight times and fuel consumption. During World War II, Japan used the jet stream to carry Fu-Go balloon bombs across the Pacific to North America. Jet streams have also been detected on Venus, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune.

=== KEY: Jewellery_4a52f2
HEADWORD: Jewelry
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Amber | Coral | Diamond | Gemstone | India
ENTRY:
Decorative items worn for personal adornment, such as brooches, rings, necklaces, earrings, pendants, bracelets, and cufflinks. The American spelling is *jewelry*. The word derives from *jewel*, which entered English from Old French *jouel* and ultimately from Latin *jocale*, meaning "plaything."

From a Western perspective the term is restricted to durable ornaments. For many centuries gold and silver, often combined with [[Gemstone|gemstones]], have been the standard materials, while [[Amber|amber]], [[Coral|coral]], glass, shells, wood, bone, ivory, clay, and enamel are also used. Platinum alloys range from 900 (90% pure) to 950 (95% pure), and the silver used is usually sterling silver, 92.5% fine. Makers employ forging, casting, soldering or welding, cutting, carving, and "cold-joining" with adhesives, staples, and rivets. Beads, including the small seed beads used in woven styles, are common, and beadwork is prominent in many African and indigenous North American cultures.

Jewelry is among the oldest types of archaeological artefact, and perforated shell beads are cited as early evidence of personal decoration. Its uses include fixing clothing or hair in place, marking social status (as with wedding rings and chains of office), signifying ethnic, religious, or social affiliation (as with the Christian crucifix or the Jewish Star of David), providing talismanic protection through amulets, artistic display, and carrying personal meaning such as love, mourning, or luck. Many items, such as brooches and buckles, began as purely functional objects and became decorative. Some cultures store wedding dowries as jewelry, and it has served as currency or a trade good.

[[Diamond|Diamonds]] were first mined in [[India]]. In 2005 Australia, Botswana, Russia, and Canada ranked among the primary sources of gemstone diamond production.

Jurchen-led imperial dynasty of China, ruled by the Wanyan clan (1115–1234). The dynasty emerged from Wanyan Aguda's rebellion against the [[Liao dynasty]], and its official name, Great Jin, means "gold." After conquering Liao territory, the Jin waged a century-long campaign against the [[Song dynasty]] in southern China. === KEY: Jin_dynasty_266_420_125539

PERSON: no

ENTRY:
Imperial dynasty of China (266–420), founded by Sima Yan. It succeeded the Cao Wei kingdom and in 280 conquered Eastern Wu, ending the [[Three Kingdoms]] period and reuniting China proper for the first time since the [[Han dynasty]] ended. Civil wars from 291 to 306, the War of the Eight Princes, weakened the state. Non-Han rebellions followed, ushering in the [[Sixteen Kingdoms]] era. The Han-Zhao state sacked [[Luoyang]] in 311 and executed Emperor Min in 318, ending the Western Jin. Sima Rui then re-established the dynasty at Jiankang, modern [[Nanjing]]. The Eastern Jin remained in near-constant conflict with northern states until General Liu Yu usurped it in 420.

Mongol prince and military commander. Jochi was the son of Börte, wife of Temüjin, later known as [[Genghis Khan]]. Because Börte had been held captive by the Merkit tribe, his paternity was doubted, and his brother Chagatai and others viewed him as illegitimate. His son Batu was appointed to rule his territories, and Jochi's descendants ruled the [[Golden Horde]].
