HEADWORD: Blake, William
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Church of England | Romanticism | Thomas Paine
ENTRY:
English poet, painter, and printmaker. Largely unrecognized during his lifetime, Blake is now regarded as a seminal figure in the poetry and visual art of the [[Romanticism|Romantic]] Age. Contemporaries considered him mad for his idiosyncratic views, but later critics praised the expressiveness of his work and its mystical undercurrents. He was hostile to the [[Church of England]] and to most organized religion, and was influenced by the French and American revolutions, though he later rejected many of his political beliefs. He maintained an amicable relationship with the political activist [[Thomas Paine]]. His wife, Catherine Boucher, worked as a printmaker and colorist on many of his books. Apart from three years in Felpham, he lived in London his entire life.

=== KEY: William_Ewart_Gladstone_84826d
HEADWORD: Gladstone, William Ewart
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Liverpool | Robert Peel
ENTRY:
British statesman and four-time prime minister of the United Kingdom, from 1868 to 1894. Born in [[Liverpool]], Gladstone entered Parliament in 1832 as a Tory, served under [[Robert Peel]], and after 1846 joined the Peelite faction that merged into the Liberal Party. His first ministry disestablished the Church of Ireland and introduced the secret ballot. His Midlothian campaign of 1879–1880 is considered the first modern political campaign in Britain. His second ministry passed the Third Reform Act but ended in his resignation after public outrage over the death of General Gordon at Khartoum. His 1886 proposal for Irish home rule split the Liberal Party, and he resigned in 1894, aged 84, as the oldest prime minister ever. Historians often rank him among Britain's greatest prime ministers.

=== KEY: William_Faulkner_390fd7
HEADWORD: Faulkner, William
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Howard Hawks | Mississippi | Raymond Chandler | World War I
ENTRY:
American novelist and short-story writer, best known for works set in the fictional Yoknapatawpha County, [[Mississippi]]. Faulkner was born in New Albany and raised in Oxford, Mississippi. He joined the Royal Canadian Air Force during [[World War I]] but did not see combat. His novels include *The Sound and the Fury* (1929), *As I Lay Dying* (1930), and *Light in August*. He also worked as a screenwriter on films by [[Howard Hawks]], including *To Have and Have Not* and *The Big Sleep*, the latter adapted from [[Raymond Chandler]]'s novel. Awarded the 1949 Nobel Prize in Literature, he is the only Mississippi-born Nobel laureate. Two of his works, *A Fable* and *The Reivers*, won the Pulitzer Prize for Fiction. He died of a heart attack a month after a fall from his horse.

American director, producer, and screenwriter of the [[New Hollywood]] movement. He is best known for *The French Connection* (1971), which won five [[Academy Awards]], including Best Picture and Best Director, and for the horror film *The Exorcist* (1973), which earned him another Best Director nomination. His other films include *The Boys in the Band* (1970), regarded as a milestone of queer cinema, and *Sorcerer* (1977). From 1998 he worked extensively as an opera director. His last feature, *The Caine Mutiny Court-Martial* (2023), was released two months after his death.

=== KEY: William_Gilbert_physicist_440ea9
HEADWORD: Gilbert, William
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Amber | Compass | Elizabeth I | Magnetism
ENTRY:
English physician, physicist, and natural philosopher. Gilbert rejected both Aristotelian philosophy and the scholastic method of university teaching. His book *De Magnete* (1600), based on experiments with a model Earth he called a terrella, concluded that the Earth is itself [[Magnetism|magnetic]], which explains why [[Compass|compasses]] point north. He was the first to argue that the Earth's center was iron. Studying the attraction of [[Amber|amber]] when rubbed, he coined the Neo-Latin term *electricus* ("like amber") and built the first electrical measuring instrument, the electroscope. A unit of magnetomotive force, the gilbert, was named in his honor and has since been superseded by the ampere-turn. He was elected president of the Royal College of Physicians in 1600 and was physician to [[Elizabeth I]] from 1601 until her death.

=== KEY: William_Harvey_3d4680
HEADWORD: Harvey, William
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Blood | Heart | University of Cambridge
ENTRY:
English physician, anatomist, and physiologist. Harvey was the first known physician to describe completely, and in detail, pulmonary and systemic circulation and the pumping of [[Blood|blood]] by the [[Heart|heart]]. Earlier writers, including Realdo Colombo and Michael Servetus, had anticipated some of his theories. He studied at [[University of Cambridge|Cambridge]] and then at the University of Padua, took his medical degree in 1602, and from 1609 was physician at St Bartholomew's Hospital in London for almost the rest of his life. His *Exercitatio Anatomica de Motu Cordis et Sanguinis in Animalibus* (1628) used vivisection and quantitative measurement to show that blood flows in one direction, from arteries to veins, through a circuit driven by the heart, with one-way valves in the veins.

=== KEY: William_Henry_Bragg_0ce5b4
HEADWORD: Bragg, William Henry
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Adelaide | Ernest Rutherford | Nobel Prize | Wilhelm Röntgen | X-ray
ENTRY:
British [[X-ray]] crystallographer. Bragg was Elder Professor of Mathematics and Physics at the University of [[Adelaide]] from 1885, where he improved the teaching facilities by apprenticing himself to instrument makers. In 1896 he demonstrated X-rays to local doctors, following [[Wilhelm Röntgen|Röntgen]]'s discovery of them. His lifelong friendship with [[Ernest Rutherford]] began when Rutherford visited him in 1895. With his son Lawrence Bragg, he shared the 1915 [[Nobel Prize]] in Physics "for their services in the analysis of crystal structure by means of X-rays," an important step in the development of X-ray crystallography. He uniquely shared a Nobel Prize with his son.

=== KEY: William_Herschel_8f25cb
HEADWORD: Herschel, William
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Caroline Herschel | Infrared | Saturn | Uranus
ENTRY:
German-born British astronomer and composer. Herschel composed 24 symphonies and many concertos before taking up astronomy. In 1781, while making observations, he discovered the planet [[Uranus]], the first planet found since antiquity, and George III appointed him Court Astronomer. Using prisms and thermometers, he discovered [[Infrared|infrared]] radiation. He also discovered the moons Enceladus and Mimas of [[Saturn]], and the moons Titania and Oberon of Uranus. He also found that the Martian polar ice caps vary seasonally. He worked closely with his sister, [[Caroline Herschel]], and published catalogs of nebulae listing 2,500 objects in 1802 and 5,000 in 1820. He was the first president of the Royal Astronomical Society.

=== KEY: William_Hogarth_8b49a5
HEADWORD: Hogarth, William
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: City of London | Engraving | Realism (arts)
ENTRY:
English painter, engraver, cartoonist, and satirist. Born in the [[City of London]] into a lower-middle-class family, Hogarth took up an apprenticeship with an [[Engraving|engraver]] but did not complete it. Early on he engraved coats of arms and shop bills. His works are mostly satirical caricatures, ranging from [[Realism (arts)|realistic]] portraiture to comic series he called "modern moral subjects," among them *A Harlot's Progress*, *A Rake's Progress*, and *Marriage A-la-Mode*. Widely reproduced as prints in his lifetime, they made him by far the most significant English artist of his generation. Satirical political illustrations in his style are often called "Hogarthian." Charles Lamb wrote that Hogarth's pictures are read rather than looked at.

=== KEY: William_Holden_9418f4
HEADWORD: Holden, William
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Billy Wilder | Boxing | Wildlife conservation
ENTRY:
American actor. Holden played his first starring role in the [[Boxing|boxing]] drama *Golden Boy* (1939) and became one of the biggest box-office draws of the 1950s. He gained critical recognition as the screenwriter Joe Gillis in [[Billy Wilder]]'s *Sunset Boulevard* (1950), and won the Academy Award for Best Actor for *Stalag 17*. He co-starred in *Sabrina* (1954), *Picnic* (1955), and *The Bridge on the River Kwai* (1957). Later he devoted much of his life to [[Wildlife conservation|wildlife conservation]] in Africa.
