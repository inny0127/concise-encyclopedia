ENTRY:
Condition of lacking stable, safe and adequate housing. Definitions vary by country, but a 2009 United Nations census guideline distinguishes primary homelessness, living on the streets without shelter, from secondary homelessness, moving between temporary shelters such as the houses of friends and family, [[Hotel|hotels]] and emergency accommodation. ENTRY:
Pseudoscientific system of [[Alternative medicine|alternative medicine]], conceived in 1796 by German physician Samuel Hahnemann. Its practitioners believe that a substance causing symptoms in healthy people can cure similar symptoms in the sick. Remedies are diluted repeatedly, often until not a single molecule of the original substance remains. Clinical trials have generally demonstrated no objective effect from its preparations. It was introduced to the [[United States]] in 1825. It revived in the 1970s alongside the [[New Age]] movement. The scientific and medical communities have characterized it as [[Quackery|quackery]] and [[Fraud|fraud]]. Meta-analyses in the 21st century have shown its therapeutic claims lack scientific justification.

=== KEY: Homer_680848
HEADWORD: Homer
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Alexander Pope | Ancient Greek literature | Iliad | Odyssey | Plato | Trojan War | Troy | Virgil
ENTRY:
Ancient Greek poet traditionally credited as the author of the [[Iliad|*Iliad*]] and the [[Odyssey|*Odyssey*]], two epic poems foundational to [[Ancient Greek literature]]. The *Iliad* centers on a quarrel between King Agamemnon and the warrior Achilles during the last year of the [[Trojan War]]. The *Odyssey* chronicles the ten-year journey of Odysseus, king of Ithaca, back home after the fall of [[Troy]]. Most researchers believe they were originally transmitted orally, and they are thought to have been composed around the late eighth or early seventh century BC.

Homer's identity and existence, the "Homeric Question," remain debated, and scholars generally regard the ancient conception of a single author as a fictional narrative. Many accounts of his life circulated in antiquity, the most widespread portraying him as a blind bard from Ionia, a region on the western coast of Anatolia, or from the islands of Chios and Samos. Other traditions named Smyrna, Argos, and Athens as his birthplace. Modern scholars consider these accounts apocryphal or "legendary." In antiquity many other works were attributed to him, including the Homeric Hymns, but these claims are not considered authentic today.

The poems shaped Greek culture and education, fostering ideals of heroism, glory, and honor. [[Plato]] described Homer as the one who "has educated Greece." In Dante's *Divine Comedy*, [[Virgil]] calls him "Poet sovereign," and [[Alexander Pope]] acknowledged in the preface to his translation of the *Iliad* that Homer has always been considered the "greatest of poets." The epics have inspired many works of literature, music, art, and film.

Tasks assigned to students by teachers to be completed at home. Common assignments include required reading, writing projects, math problems and review before tests. Its effects are debated. Homework has not been shown to improve academic performance among young children, though proponents claim it builds good study habits. === KEY: Homicide_a9f24b
HEADWORD: Homicide
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Americas | Assassination | Capital punishment | Draco (legislator) | Euthanasia | Mental disorder | Murder | War
ENTRY:
Act by which one person causes the death of another. Homicide requires only a volitional act, or an omission, that causes death, so it may result from accidental, reckless, or negligent acts even without intent to cause harm. Its overlapping legal categories include [[Murder|murder]], manslaughter, justifiable homicide, [[Assassination|assassination]], killing in [[War|war]] (lawful or as a war crime), [[Euthanasia|euthanasia]], and [[Capital punishment|capital punishment]].

Criminal homicide is divided into murder and manslaughter according to the perpetrator's state of mind and intent. Murder is the most serious charge. First-degree murder is the premeditated, unlawful, intentional killing of another person; second-degree murder is intentional and unlawful but without premeditation. In the United States, under the felony murder rule, a person committing a felony may be guilty of murder if someone dies as a result, regardless of intent. Manslaughter is voluntary, an intentional but unpremeditated killing in heat of passion, or involuntary, an unintentional killing through recklessness or negligence. The distinction between murder and manslaughter is sometimes said to have first been made by the Athenian lawmaker [[Draco (legislator)|Draco]] in the 7th century BC.

Not all homicides are prosecutable. Common defenses and excuses include self-defense, [[Mental disorder|mental incapacity]], the defense of infancy, and justifiable homicide, such as a lawful killing in battle.

A report by the United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime in July 2019 documented nearly 464,000 homicide deaths worldwide in 2017, against 89,000 killed in armed conflicts. Another estimate gives approximately 458,000 in 2021, with the [[Americas]] showing the highest rates.

=== KEY: Homo_erectus_cefb99
HEADWORD: Homo erectus
PERSON: no

ENTRY:
Extinct archaic human species of the [[Pleistocene]], spanning nearly 2 million years. It was the first human species to evolve a humanlike body plan and gait, to leave Africa and colonize Asia and Europe. Some populations were ancestors of later human species, including *Homo heidelbergensis*. It was first described in 1893 by Eugène Dubois from a skullcap, molar and femur found on [[Java]], Indonesia. Reconstructed adults ranged from 141–167 cm (4 ft 8 in – 5 ft 6 in) in height and weighed about 50 kg (110 lb). East Asian populations averaged a brain volume of 1,000 cc (61 cu in). The species invented the Acheulean tool industry and is usually characterized as the first [[Hunter-gatherer|hunter-gatherer]]. Its last known occurrence is 108,000 to 117,000 years ago, in Southeast Asia.

Extinct archaic human species from the Early Pleistocene of East and [[South Africa]]. The name means handy man. === KEY: Homological_algebra_271417
HEADWORD: Homological algebra
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Algebraic geometry | Algebraic topology | Category theory | Commutative algebra | David Hilbert | Henri Poincaré | Homology (mathematics) | Representation theory
ENTRY:
Branch of mathematics concerned with [[Homology (mathematics)|homology]] in a general algebraic setting. Its origins lie in investigations in combinatorial topology and abstract algebra at the end of the 19th century, chiefly by [[Henri Poincaré]] and [[David Hilbert]]. Its development was closely intertwined with the emergence of [[Category theory|category theory]]. A central concept is that of chain complexes, which are studied through their homology and cohomology, and a spectral sequence is a powerful tool for extracting information from them. The subject has played an enormous role in [[Algebraic topology]] and has expanded into [[Commutative algebra]], [[Algebraic geometry]], [[Representation theory]] and the theory of partial differential equations. === KEY: Homology_mathematics_f2fbcd
HEADWORD: Homology
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Abelian group | Algebraic topology | Homological algebra | Topological space
ENTRY:
Algebraic structure associated with a [[Topological space|topological space]]. The term, originally introduced in [[Algebraic topology]], has three closely related usages. The first is the homology of a chain complex, a sequence of [[Abelian group|abelian groups]] whose homology groups are regarded as fundamental invariants of the complex. Homology theories associate a chain complex to another mathematical object and take its homology, examples being singular homology, Morse homology and Hochschild homology. Under suitable conditions, distinct homology theories applied to one topological space give the same groups, allowing a single homology of that space to be defined. This notion is closely related to ideas such as the holes of a surface or the cycles of a graph. The nth homology group is the quotient of cycles by boundaries, and [[Homological algebra]] relates the homology groups of different chain complexes.

=== KEY: Homotopy_2fff4d
HEADWORD: Homotopy
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Algebraic topology | Continuous function | Equivalence relation | Topological space | Torus
ENTRY:
Continuous deformation between two [[Continuous function|continuous functions]]. Two continuous functions from one [[Topological space|topological space]] to another are homotopic if one can be continuously deformed into the other, and such a deformation is a homotopy between them. Formally, a homotopy is a continuous function H from X × [0,1] to Y with H(x,0) = f(x) and H(x,1) = g(x) for all x in X. Homotopy is an [[Equivalence relation|equivalence relation]] on continuous functions, and its equivalence classes are called homotopy classes. A [[Torus|torus]] shaped like a doughnut can be continuously deformed into a coffee-mug shape. Homotopy groups and cohomotopy groups, important invariants in [[Algebraic topology]], are defined using homotopies.

ENTRY:
Sweet, viscous foodstuff made by several species of bees, the best known of which are [[Honey bee|honey bees]]. === KEY: Hong_Kong_8b476f
HEADWORD: Hong Kong
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: First Opium War | Human Development Index | London | New York City | Qin dynasty | Shenzhen | World War II
ENTRY:
Special administrative region of China (pop., mid-2025: c. 7.53 million), on the southern coast just south of [[Shenzhen]]. It comprises Hong Kong Island, the Kowloon Peninsula, the New Territories, and over 260 outlying islands, with an area of 1,114 sq km, making it the fourth-most densely populated region in the world.

The area was first incorporated into China under the [[Qin dynasty]] in 214 BC. After the Qing dynasty lost the [[First Opium War]], it ceded Hong Kong Island to Britain in 1841–1842, and the colony expanded to Kowloon in 1860 and, through a 99-year lease of the New Territories, in 1898. Japan occupied the territory from 1941 to 1945 during [[World War II]]. Sovereignty passed from the United Kingdom to China in 1997 under the Sino-British Joint Declaration. Under "one country, two systems," Hong Kong keeps separate governing and economic systems, with autonomy in all affairs except foreign affairs and defense pledged until 2047.

Originally farming and fishing villages, Hong Kong is one of the world's most significant financial centers, ranked third globally behind [[New York City]] and [[London]]. The Hong Kong dollar is the ninth-most traded currency, and the city has more skyscrapers than any other. It has a [[Human Development Index]] of 0.955, ranking eighth in the world, the highest life expectancy in the world, though severe income inequality persists.

Political change followed the 2019–2020 protests and the 2020 National Security Law, which the National People's Congress enacted in place of local legislation and which criminalizes secession, subversion, terrorism, and foreign collusion. Critics argue that it has diminished civil liberties and judicial independence, while Chinese and Hong Kong officials describe it as restoring stability.

Founding emperor of the [[Ming dynasty]] of China, reigning from 1368 to 1398. === KEY: Hooke_s_law_db12d7

ENTRY:
Empirical law of physics relating the force needed to stretch or compress a [[Spring (device)|spring]] to its displacement, written F = kx, where k is the spring's [[Stiffness|stiffness]]. === KEY: Hormone_2b23df
HEADWORD: Hormone
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Diabetes | Endocrine system | Ethylene | Insulin | Nitric oxide | Plant | Steroid | Vertebrate
ENTRY:
Class of signaling molecules in multicellular organisms, sent to distant organs or tissues to regulate physiology and behavior. The term derives from Ancient Greek *hormôn*, "setting in motion." Because the definition is broad, many kinds of molecules qualify, including eicosanoids, [[Steroid|steroids]], amino acid derivatives such as epinephrine and auxin, proteins and peptides such as [[Insulin|insulin]], and gases such as [[Ethylene|ethylene]] and [[Nitric oxide|nitric oxide]].

In [[Vertebrate|vertebrates]], hormones, produced by glands of the [[Endocrine system]], regulate processes including digestion, metabolism, respiration, sensory perception, sleep, excretion, lactation, stress, growth, reproduction, and mood. In [[Plant|plants]], they modulate almost all aspects of development, from germination to senescence. Plants lack specialized hormone-secreting organs.

A hormone acts by binding to specific receptor proteins in a target cell, activating a signal transduction pathway that typically triggers gene transcription and increased expression of target proteins. Water-soluble hormones, such as peptides and amines, generally act on the cell surface through second messengers. Lipid-soluble hormones, such as steroids, generally pass through the plasma membrane to act within the nucleus.

In vertebrates, endocrine glands secrete hormones directly into the bloodstream, whereas the exocrine system uses ducts. Secretion is often subject to negative feedback: high blood sugar promotes insulin synthesis, and insulin then lowers glucose levels, leading to reduced insulin levels. Imbalances can cause disorders such as [[Diabetes|diabetes]] and hypothyroidism. In 1849 the German physiologist Arnold Adolph Berthold observed that castrated roosters had reduced secondary sexual organs and no sexual or aggressive behavior.

=== KEY: Horse_38069b
HEADWORD: Horse
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Central Asia | Domestication | Equidae | Herbivore | Mammal | Saddle
ENTRY:
Domesticated, one-toed, hoofed [[Mammal|mammal]] (*Equus ferus caballus*) of the family [[Equidae]]. The horse evolved over the past 45 to 55 million years from a small multi-toed creature, *Eohippus*, originally in North America. Humans began [[Domestication|domesticating]] horses around 4000 BC in [[Central Asia]], primarily associated with the Botai culture, and domestication is believed to have been widespread by 3000 BC. Genetic evidence indicates, however, that modern breeds descend not from this lineage but from horses of the lower Volga-Don region, which spread across Eurasia from 2000 BC.

Horses are non-ruminant [[Herbivore|herbivores]] adapted to run, and can sleep both standing and lying down. Mares carry their young for approximately 11 months. Most domesticated horses begin training under a [[Saddle|saddle]] or in harness at two to four years, and live on average 25 to 30 years; the oldest verifiable record is a 19th-century horse that lived to 62. Light-riding horses usually stand 14 to 16 hands (142 to 163 cm; one hand is 101.6 mm) at the withers and weigh 380 to 550 kg.

More than 300 breeds exist. They are broadly classed by temperament as spirited "hot-blooded" horses known for speed and endurance, "cold-blooded" draft horses and some ponies suited to heavy work, and "warm-bloods" produced by crossing the two. A young horse is a foal, a female four or older a mare, an uncastrated male four or older a stallion, and a castrated male a gelding.

Horses take part in sport, recreation, police work, agriculture, and therapy, and were historically used in warfare. Products derived from them include meat, milk, hide, hair, bone, and pharmaceuticals extracted from the urine of pregnant mares.

=== KEY: Hotspot_geology_514e04
HEADWORD: Hotspot
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Basalt | Earth's mantle | Geology | Hawaiian Islands | Hypothesis | Volcano
ENTRY:
Volcanic locale in [[Geology|geology]], fed by underlying [[Earth's mantle|mantle]] that is anomalously hot compared with the surrounding mantle. Hotspots are independent of tectonic plate boundaries, so as plates move over them they may create a chain of [[Volcano|volcanoes]], as in the [[Hawaiian Islands]]. Two [[Hypothesis|hypotheses]] explain their origin. One holds that mantle plumes rise as thermal diapirs from the core–mantle boundary; the other holds that the crust above is unusually weak or thin, so that melt rises passively from shallow depths. ENTRY:
American film director, producer and screenwriter of the classic Hollywood era. Hawks worked across comedies, dramas, gangster films, science fiction, film noir, war films and [[Western (genre)|Westerns]]. His popular films include *Scarface* (1932), *Bringing Up Baby* (1938), *Only Angels Have Wings* (1939), *His Girl Friday* (1940), *The Big Sleep* (1946), *Red River* (1948) and *Rio Bravo* (1959). He was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Director for *Sergeant York* (1941) and later received an honorary Academy Award. His frequent portrayals of strong, tough-talking women defined the "Hawksian woman". His influence reached directors including [[Martin Scorsese]], [[John Carpenter]], [[Quentin Tarantino]] and [[Clint Eastwood]]. [[Jean-Luc Godard]] called him "the greatest of all American artists".

=== KEY: Howard_Hughes_215ea3
HEADWORD: Hughes, Howard
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Las Vegas | Obsessive–compulsive disorder
ENTRY:
American aviator, aerospace engineer, business magnate and film producer. Hughes first became prominent in Hollywood with big-budget films such as *Hell's Angels* (1930) and *Scarface* (1932). In 1932 he founded Hughes Aircraft Company, and over the next two decades he set multiple world air speed records and built landmark planes, including the H-1 Racer (1935) and the H-4 Hercules (1947), known as the Spruce Goose and the largest flying boat in history. He acquired and expanded Trans World Airlines. Hughes won the Collier Trophy (1938) and the Congressional Gold Medal (1939) for his aviation achievements. In later years his eccentric, reclusive lifestyle was caused partly by [[Obsessive–compulsive disorder|obsessive–compulsive disorder]], chronic pain from a near-fatal plane crash and deafness. In his final years he extended his business empire to [[Las Vegas]], where he is largely credited with transforming the city into a more refined, cosmopolitan place.

=== KEY: Howitzer_e3640f
HEADWORD: Howitzer
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Artillery | Cannon | Hussite Wars | Mortar (weapon) | World War I | World War II
ENTRY:
[[Artillery]] piece between a [[Cannon|cannon]] and a [[Mortar (weapon)|mortar]], capable of both low-angle and high-angle fire. Howitzers fire explosive shells and incendiary materials into fortifications, and they can be used in battery formations with long-barreled guns and rocket artillery. The English word derives from the Czech *houfnice*, a short-barreled cannon used by the [[Hussite Wars|Hussites]]. By the early 20th century, howitzers were classified as field, siege, super-heavy siege and defense types. They played major roles in [[World War I]] and [[World War II]], especially in trench warfare. Modern howitzers are often self-propelled, mounted on tracked or wheeled vehicles.
