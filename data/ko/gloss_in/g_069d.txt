=== KEY: Jaw_2155a8
HEADWORD: Jaw
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Mouth | Vertebrate
ENTRY:
Pair of articulated structures at the entrance of the [[Mouth|mouth]], used for grasping and manipulating food. In [[Vertebrate|vertebrates]], the jaw is a bony framework made up of the upper jaw, or maxilla, and the lower jaw, or mandible. The maxilla forms the central part of the facial skeleton, supports the upper teeth, and contains the maxillary sinus, the largest paranasal sinus. The mandible is the largest and strongest bone of the skull, and its condylar processes form the temporomandibular joint with the temporal bone. Muscles including the masseter, temporalis, and pterygoids drive chewing. Jaw fractures most commonly occur at the condylar neck or parasymphysis, and temporomandibular joint dysfunction affects about 5% to 12% of adults.

ENTRY:
Indian lawyer, independence leader, and first prime minister of [[India]]. Nehru became a leader of the [[Indian National Congress]] and was designated the political heir of [[Mahatma Gandhi]]. As Congress president in 1929, he called for complete independence from the [[British Raj]]. He served as prime minister for 16 years and 286 days, from independence in 1947 until his death in office. He promoted parliamentary democracy, secularism, and science and technology, and led the establishment of the [[Non-Aligned Movement]], whose members did not join either bloc of the [[Cold War]]. Critics argue that his referral of the [[Kashmir]] dispute to the United Nations in 1948 internationalized a bilateral issue. India suffered territorial losses in the 1962 Sino-Indian War.

ENTRY:
King of the [[Khmer Empire]] (r. 1181–1218). He was the first Khmer king devoted to [[Buddhism]], and as a Mahayana Buddhist he built the Bayon as a monument to the faith. Historians generally consider him the most powerful Khmer monarch. After the [[Champa|Cham]] invaders occupied Cambodia, he led a Khmer army to oust them and was crowned in 1181. He conquered Champa in 1190–1191, which remained a dependency for 30 years, and extended Khmer control of the Mekong Valley north to [[Vientiane]]. His construction program included hospitals, highways, rest houses, reservoirs and temples, among them Ta Prohm for his mother and Preah Khan for his father. He is credited with introducing a [[Welfare state|welfare state]] serving the physical and spiritual needs of the Khmer people.

=== KEY: Jazz_9a284e
HEADWORD: Jazz
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Blues | Duke Ellington | Ragtime
ENTRY:
Music genre that originated in the African-American communities of New Orleans, Louisiana, in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Its roots lie in [[Blues|blues]], [[Ragtime|ragtime]], European harmony, African rhythmic rituals, spirituals, hymns, marches, vaudeville song, and dance music. It is characterized by swing and blue notes, complex chords, call-and-response vocals, polyrhythms, and improvisation. Since the 1920s Jazz Age it has been recognized as a major form of musical expression in both traditional and popular music.

The origin of the word is much researched. It is believed to be related to *jasm*, a slang term dating to 1860 meaning "pep, energy." The earliest written record, in a 1912 *Los Angeles Times* article, concerns a baseball pitch called a "jazz ball." The American Dialect Society named it the Word of the 20th Century.

Jazz is difficult to define because it spans more than 100 years, from ragtime to rock-infused fusion. [[Duke Ellington]] said, "It's all music."

Jazz did not begin as a single tradition. New Orleans jazz began in the early 1910s, combining brass band marches, French quadrilles, biguine, ragtime, and blues with collective polyphonic improvisation. In the 1930s the prominent styles were swing big bands, Kansas City jazz, and gypsy jazz. Bebop emerged in the 1940s, moving jazz from danceable popular music to a more challenging "musician's music" played at faster tempos. The mid-1950s brought hard bop, and the late 1950s modal jazz and free jazz, which explored playing without regular meter, beat, and formal structures. Jazz fusion, combining improvisation with rock's rhythms and electric instruments, appeared in the late 1960s and early 1970s; its commercial form, smooth jazz, succeeded in the early 1980s.

=== KEY: Jazz_dance_26d388
HEADWORD: Jazz dance
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Bob Fosse | Pop music | Ragtime | United States | Vaudeville
ENTRY:
Performance dance style that arose in the [[United States]] in the early 20th century. It expanded on African American vernacular dances that arose with jazz music, and vernacular jazz dance incorporates [[Ragtime]] moves, the Charleston, the Lindy hop and mambo. Dramatic jazz dance on the stage was promoted by choreographers including Jack Cole and [[Bob Fosse]], whose style drew on [[Vaudeville]], striptease and Broadway musicals. Katherine Dunham, an anthropologist and choreographer, introduced isolations into jazz dance. Commercial jazz, popular since the 1980s, combines hip-hop and jazz and is often danced to [[Pop music|pop music]]. The term has also been applied to college classes in which students dance to pop music, rarely jazz.

=== KEY: Jealousy_49400c
HEADWORD: Jealousy
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Anger | Disgust | English language | Fear | William Shakespeare
ENTRY:
Emotion of insecurity, [[Fear|fear]] and concern over a relative lack of possessions or safety. Jealousy can consist of [[Anger|anger]], resentment, inadequacy, helplessness or [[Disgust|disgust]]. In its original meaning it was distinct from envy, though the two terms have popularly become synonymous in [[English language|English]]. Jealousy has been observed in infants as young as five months. Some researchers regard it as universal and others as culture-specific, and sociologists have shown that cultural beliefs shape what triggers it. Psychologists describe romantic jealousy as a response to threats to self-esteem or to the relationship posed by a perceived rival. Since [[William Shakespeare]] wrote of the "green-eyed monster", green has been associated with jealousy and envy.

=== KEY: Jean_Auguste_Dominique_Ingres_29e5bb
HEADWORD: Ingres, Jean-Auguste-Dominique
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Florence | Henri Matisse | Jacques-Louis David | Neoclassicism | Nicolas Poussin | Pablo Picasso | Paris | Rome
ENTRY:
French [[Neoclassicism|Neoclassical]] painter. Ingres considered himself a painter of history in the tradition of [[Nicolas Poussin]] and [[Jacques-Louis David]], but his portraits are recognized as his greatest legacy. From 1806 to 1824, working in [[Rome]] and [[Florence]], he sent paintings to the [[Paris]] Salon, where critics found his style bizarre and archaic. He was acclaimed there in 1824 with *The Vow of Louis XIII* and acknowledged as the leader of the Neoclassical school in France. His *Portrait of Monsieur Bertin* was a popular success in 1833. He directed the French Academy in Rome from 1835 and returned to Paris in 1841. His expressive distortions of form and space influenced [[Henri Matisse]] and [[Pablo Picasso]].

=== KEY: Jean_Baptiste_Lamarck_50bb49
HEADWORD: Lamarck, Jean-Baptiste
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Biology | Botany | Evolution | Invertebrate | Prussia | Seven Years' War | Zoology
ENTRY:
French naturalist and biologist. An early proponent of the idea that biological [[Evolution|evolution]] proceeds in accordance with natural laws. After fighting in the [[Seven Years' War]] against [[Prussia]], he studied medicine and [[Botany|botany]], publishing the three-volume *Flore française* in 1778. He was appointed to the chair of botany in 1788 and became a professor of [[Zoology|zoology]] in 1793. In 1801 he published *Système des animaux sans vertèbres*, a major classification of [[Invertebrate|invertebrates]], a term he coined. In 1802 he was among the first to use [[Biology|biology]] in its modern sense. His *Philosophie zoologique* (1809) set out the inheritance of acquired characteristics, later called Lamarckism, which was partly aligned with Darwin's natural selection.

=== KEY: Jean_Froissart_10228d
HEADWORD: Froissart, Jean
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Chivalry | Edward III | Hundred Years' War | Milan | Petrarch | Poetry
ENTRY:
Medieval French-speaking author and court historian from Hainaut in the Low Countries. Froissart entered the service of Philippa of Hainault, queen consort of [[Edward III]] of England, in 1361 or 1362. He traveled widely in England, Scotland, France, Flanders and Spain gathering material for his *Chronicles*, and he went to [[Milan]] to chronicle the wedding of Lionel, Duke of Clarence, where [[Petrarch]] was also present. His *Chronicles* are recognized as the chief expression of [[Chivalry|chivalric]] revival in the 14th-century kingdoms of England, France and Scotland, and they are an important source for the first half of the [[Hundred Years' War]]. The text survives in more than 100 illuminated manuscripts. He also wrote the Arthurian romance *Meliador* and a large body of [[Poetry|poetry]].

=== KEY: Jean_Jacques_Rousseau_bd96a1
HEADWORD: Rousseau, Jean-Jacques
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Age of Enlightenment | French Revolution | Geneva | Political philosophy | Romanticism
ENTRY:
Genevan philosopher, writer and composer. His [[Political philosophy|political philosophy]] was influential during the [[Age of Enlightenment]] and the [[French Revolution]]. His *Discourse on Inequality* argued that private property is the source of social inequality, and *The Social Contract* outlines his ideas for a legitimate political order. The sentimental novel *Julie, or the New Heloise* (1761) contributed to preromanticism and [[Romanticism|romanticism]] in fiction. *Émile, or On Education* (1762) is a treatise on the place of the individual in society. His posthumously published *Confessions* and the unfinished *Reveries of the Solitary Walker* gave increased focus to subjectivity and introspection. Born in [[Geneva]], then a city-state, he was proud that his family had voting rights there.

=== KEY: Jean_Le_Rond_d_Alembert_d2369a
HEADWORD: d'Alembert, Jean Le Rond
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Denis Diderot | Encyclopédie | Frederick the Great | Fundamental theorem of algebra | Refraction | Tacitus | Wave equation
ENTRY:
French mathematician, physicist and philosopher. Together with [[Denis Diderot]] he co-edited the [[Encyclopédie]] until 1759. His most famous achievements include the [[Wave equation|wave equation]], known as d'Alembert's equation, and his formula for solving it. In his *Traité de dynamique* (1743) he developed his own laws of motion. He contributed to fluid mechanics and explained [[Refraction|refraction]] theoretically in 1740. He was elected to the Académie des Sciences in 1741, and [[Frederick the Great]] of Prussia proposed naming a suspected moon of Venus after him, an honor he declined. He also worked on a translation of [[Tacitus]] in later life. In French, the [[Fundamental theorem of algebra|fundamental theorem of algebra]] is named in his honor.

=== KEY: Jean_Paul_Marat_650c4f
HEADWORD: Marat, Jean-Paul
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Criminal law | French Revolution | Jacques-Louis David
ENTRY:
French physician, scientist and radical political journalist. Before the [[French Revolution]], Marat worked in medicine and science, publishing on optics, electricity, political theory and [[Criminal law|criminal law]]. Through his newspaper *L'Ami du peuple* he publicized complaints brought to him by ordinary citizens against officials. Elected to the National Convention in 1792, he aligned with the Montagnards against the Girondins. His journalism became notorious for calls for the punishment, and sometimes the killing, of those he regarded as enemies of the Revolution. His writings remain central to debates about political violence, including the September Massacres. He was assassinated by Charlotte Corday, a Girondin sympathizer, while taking a bath. Popular societies and the Montagnards then represented him as a martyr of the Revolution, and [[Jacques-Louis David]] helped organize his funeral.

=== KEY: Jean_Paul_Sartre_8a875e

PERSON: yes

ENTRY:
French philosopher, writer and political activist. A leading figure in [[Existentialism]] and [[Phenomenology (philosophy)|phenomenology]], he made the conflict between conformity and authenticity, known as *bad faith*, the dominant theme of his early work. His principal philosophical work, *Being and Nothingness* (1943), developed this theme. During [[World War II]] he was drafted, captured and later released, and he co-founded the resistance group Socialisme et Liberté. After the war he co-founded the journal *Les Temps modernes*, supported anti-colonial movements and condemned French policy in [[Algeria]]. He had an open relationship with [[Simone de Beauvoir]], a fellow existentialist philosopher. He aligned himself at various times with [[Marxism]]. He was awarded the [[Nobel Prize]] in Literature in 1964 but declined it, saying a writer should not become an institution.

=== KEY: Jean_Piaget_9c1f75

ENTRY:
Swiss psychologist known for his work on child development. His theory of cognitive development and his [[Epistemology|epistemological]] view are together called genetic epistemology. Piaget proposed that young children's thinking is inherently different from that of adults, and he set out a global theory of cognitive developmental stages. His ideas, widely popularized in the 1960s, influenced [[Education|education]] and the study of development as a major subdiscipline of psychology. In 1955 he founded the International Center for Genetic Epistemology in [[Geneva]], which he directed until his death. By the end of the 20th century he was second only to [[B. F. Skinner]] as the most-cited psychologist.

=== KEY: Jean_Racine_d947d4
HEADWORD: Racine, Jean
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Roman mythology | Tragedy | Trojan War
ENTRY:
17th-century French dramatist, primarily a [[Tragedy|tragedian]]. He was one of the three great playwrights of 17th-century France, with Molière and Corneille. His tragedies, including *Andromaque* (1667), which follows Andromache after the [[Trojan War]], and *Phèdre*, are regarded as examples of neoclassical perfection. His plays display mastery of the French alexandrine, a twelve-syllable verse line, and his writing is noted for its elegance, purity, speed and fury. Themes from Greek and [[Roman mythology]] play large roles in his work, and his dramaturgy is marked by psychological insight. He was educated by the Jansenists at Port-Royal, an influence that lasted throughout his life.

=== KEY: Jean_Renoir_1e01ff
HEADWORD: Renoir, Jean
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: India | Pierre-Auguste Renoir | World War I
ENTRY:
French filmmaker, actor and producer, the son of the Impressionist painter [[Pierre-Auguste Renoir]]. He served as a reconnaissance pilot in [[World War I]] before turning to film in the 1920s. His *La Grande Illusion* (1937) and *The Rules of the Game* (1939) are often cited by critics among the greatest films ever made, and he was one of the first filmmakers known as an auteur. He made *The River* (1951), the first color film shot in [[India]], with his nephew, the cinematographer Claude Renoir. He later directed the color spectacle *French Cancan* (1955) and received an Academy Award for lifetime achievement in 1975.
