HEADWORD: Callas, Maria
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Aegean Sea | Aristotle Onassis
ENTRY:
American-born Greek soprano and one of the most influential opera singers of the 20th century. Critics praised her bel canto technique and dramatic interpretations, and she was known as "La Divina" ("The Divine One"). Born in Manhattan to Greek immigrant parents, she trained in Greece and established her career in Italy. Her repertoire ranged from bel canto operas by Bellini, Donizetti and Rossini to works by Verdi and Puccini. She performed 180 times across 22 operas at La Scala in Milan between 1950 and 1962 and recorded extensively for EMI. Leonard Bernstein called her "the Bible of opera." Vocal challenges led to her gradual retirement from the stage by 1965. Her widely publicized relationship with shipping magnate [[Aristotle Onassis]] overshadowed her later years. Her ashes were scattered over the [[Aegean Sea]] in 1979.

=== KEY: Maria_Montessori_88d518
HEADWORD: Montessori, Maria
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Sapienza University of Rome
ENTRY:
Italian physician and educator known for her writing on scientific pedagogy and for the Montessori method of education. She was one of the first women to attend medical school in Italy, graduating with honors from the [[Sapienza University of Rome]] in 1896. Her interest in child development grew from work with children with intellectual disabilities in psychiatric clinics. In 1907 she opened the first Casa dei Bambini ("Children's House") in Rome's San Lorenzo district, applying her method to ordinary preschool children. Her approach, which emphasizes independence, sensory learning and prepared environments, spread across Europe, the Americas and Asia through training courses, books and schools. Critics argue that it gives insufficient emphasis to structured academic instruction, and empirical studies show mixed outcomes compared with conventional schooling.

=== KEY: Maria_Theresa_4c5c8a
HEADWORD: Maria Theresa
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Catholic Church | Frederick the Great | Joseph II | Marie Antoinette | Silesia
ENTRY:
Habsburg monarch, the only woman to rule the Habsburg monarchy in her own right. Maria Theresa reigned from 1740 until her death in 1780 as sovereign of Austria, Hungary, Bohemia and other territories, and was Holy Roman Empress by marriage. She inherited the lands under the Pragmatic Sanction of 1713 from her father, Charles VI. Prussia under [[Frederick the Great]] seized [[Silesia]] during the War of the Austrian Succession (1740–1748), and she never regained it. She introduced institutional, financial, medical and educational reforms, reorganized the army and promoted commerce and agriculture, ruling as an autocrat with the counsel of advisers. A pious [[Catholic Church|Catholic]], she despised Freemasons, Jews and Protestants and on occasion ordered their expulsion. She bore 16 children who survived infancy, including [[Joseph II]] and [[Marie Antoinette]].

=== KEY: Mariana_Islands_86f30a
HEADWORD: Mariana Islands
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Ferdinand Magellan | Guam | Micronesia | Pacific Ocean | Tourism
ENTRY:
Archipelago of mostly dormant volcanic islands in the western [[Pacific Ocean]], part of [[Micronesia]]. The islands are divided between the U.S. territory of [[Guam]] and the Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands. Chamorro people settled the islands, possibly after making the longest uninterrupted ocean voyage in human history. Spanish expeditions, beginning with one led by [[Ferdinand Magellan]], were the first Europeans to arrive, and the islands were named after Queen Mariana of Austria. Spain colonized the islands in the 17th century; Germany and Japan later controlled them. U.S. forces invaded in 1944. The economy relies heavily on [[Tourism|tourism]] and the U.S. military.

=== KEY: Mariana_Trench_4d425b
HEADWORD: Mariana Trench
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Challenger Deep | Mariana Islands | Mount Everest | Plate tectonics
ENTRY:
Deepest oceanic trench on Earth, a crescent-shaped depression in the western Pacific Ocean about 200 km east of the [[Mariana Islands]]. It measures about 2,550 km long and 69 km wide. Its deepest point, the [[Challenger Deep]] at the southern end of the trench, has a maximum known depth of 10,935 meters, more than 2 km farther from sea level than the peak of [[Mount Everest]]. The water pressure at the bottom is 111 MPa (16,100 psi), approximately 1,100 times the standard atmospheric pressure at sea level. The trench forms where the Pacific plate is subducted beneath the Mariana plate, reflecting [[Plate tectonics|plate tectonics]]. Single-celled organisms have been recorded at a depth of 10.6 km (35,000 ft). In 2009 the United States established the Mariana Trench Marine National Monument, which excludes Challenger Deep.

=== KEY: Marie_Antoinette_edb6fb
HEADWORD: Marie Antoinette
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: French Revolution | Guillotine | Louis XVI | Maria Theresa | Vienna
ENTRY:
Queen consort of France from 1774 to 1792 as the wife of [[Louis XVI]], and the last queen before the monarchy was abolished during the [[French Revolution]]. Born in [[Vienna]], she was the youngest daughter of [[Maria Theresa]] and Francis I, Holy Roman Emperor. She was betrothed to the dauphin Louis-Auguste in 1769 to strengthen the Franco-Austrian alliance, and they married in 1770. Blamed for the nation's financial crisis, she was nicknamed "Madame Déficit." She was falsely accused of defrauding the Crown's jewelers in the Affair of the Diamond Necklace, but the accusations still damaged her reputation. After the royal family was imprisoned in 1792, she was convicted of high treason and executed by [[Guillotine|guillotine]] in 1793.

=== KEY: Marie_Curie_8ee140
HEADWORD: Curie, Marie
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Henri Becquerel | Nobel Prize | Pierre Curie | Polonium | Radium | Warsaw | World War I | X-ray
ENTRY:
Polish-born French physicist and chemist who pioneered research on radioactivity, a term she coined. Born Maria Salomea Skłodowska in [[Warsaw]], then in the Russian Empire, she studied at the clandestine Flying University, which admitted women, and in 1891 followed her elder sister Bronisława to Paris, where she earned her higher degrees. In 1895 she married [[Pierre Curie]], with whom she conducted her early research. She shared the 1903 [[Nobel Prize]] in Physics with Pierre and [[Henri Becquerel]] for their joint researches on the radioactivity phenomena Becquerel had discovered. Pierre died in a Paris street accident in 1906, and she became the first woman to hold a professorship at the University of Paris that year.

In 1911 she won the Nobel Prize in Chemistry for the discovery of the elements [[Radium|radium]] and [[Polonium|polonium]], by the isolation of radium and the study of its nature and compounds. She was the first woman to win a Nobel Prize, the first person to win twice, and the only person to win in two different scientific fields; she and Pierre were the first married couple to win. She named polonium after her native Poland and never lost her sense of Polish identity, teaching her daughters Polish and taking them on visits there.

Under her direction the first studies of treating neoplasms with radioactive isotopes were conducted. She founded the Curie Institute in Paris in 1920 and another in Warsaw in 1932; both remain major medical research centers. During [[World War I]] she developed mobile radiography units, known as *petites Curies*, to provide [[X-ray]] services to field hospitals.

She died in 1934 of aplastic anemia, likely caused by radiation exposure in her research and radiological work. In 1995 she became the first woman entombed in the Paris Panthéon on her own merits. The synthetic element curium is named in her honor.

=== KEY: Marilyn_Monroe_60b701
HEADWORD: Monroe, Marilyn
PERSON: yes

ENTRY:
American actress and model, one of the most popular sex symbols of the 1950s and early 1960s and an emblem of the era's [[Sexual revolution|sexual revolution]]. Known for comic "blonde bombshell" roles, she starred in 1953 in *Niagara* and the comedies *Gentlemen Prefer Blondes* and *How to Marry a Millionaire*, which established her star image as a "dumb blonde." Her nude images appeared on the first issue of [[Playboy]] that year. Feeling typecast and underpaid by the studio, she founded her own production company, and her later films included *The Seven Year Itch* (1955) and *Some Like It Hot* (1959), for which she won a Golden Globe. Her marriages to [[Joe DiMaggio]] and playwright [[Arthur Miller]] ended in divorce. Her death from a barbiturate overdose was ruled a probable suicide.

=== KEY: Marina_Abramovi_ba7c2a
HEADWORD: Abramović, Marina
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Belgrade | Great Wall of China | Museum of Modern Art
ENTRY:
Serbian conceptual and performance artist, who calls herself the "grandmother of performance art." Her work explores body art, endurance, the relationship between performer and audience, and the limits of the body. Born in [[Belgrade]], she worked with the German artist Ulay on relational pieces, including walking the length of the [[Great Wall of China]] before their separation. Her 2010 retrospective *The Artist Is Present* at the [[Museum of Modern Art]] had her sit silently opposite visitors for up to 736 hours over three months. In 2007 she founded the Marina Abramović Institute, a nonprofit foundation for performance art.
