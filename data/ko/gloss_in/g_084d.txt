HEADWORD: Mania

ENTRY:
Mood disorder marked by abnormally elevated mood, hyperactivity and risky behavior. Symptoms include irritable or euphoric mood, flight of ideas, decreased need for sleep and hyperactivity. In severe episodes of [[Bipolar disorder|bipolar I disorder]], [[Delusion|delusions]] or [[Hallucination|hallucinations]] may occur, and [[Psychosis|psychosis]] appears in 57% of manic episodes compared with 13% of depressive ones. In a mixed state, with concurrent depressive symptoms, the risk of [[Suicide|suicide]] is greater. More than 5% of people have experienced mania or hypomania to some degree.

=== KEY: Manichaeism_10b5aa
HEADWORD: Manichaeism
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Abbasid Caliphate | Christianity | Jesus | Religion | Sasanian Empire | The Buddha | Uyghur Khaganate | Zoroaster
ENTRY:
Dualistic [[Religion|religion]] founded in the third century AD by the Iranian prophet Mani in the [[Sasanian Empire]]. Its cosmology describes a struggle between a good world of light and an evil material world of darkness, through which light is gradually returned to the divine. Most forms of the faith regarded Mani as the final prophet after [[Zoroaster]], [[The Buddha|Buddha]] and [[Jesus]]. Missionaries spread it through Aramaic-speaking regions, the Mediterranean and the Middle East, and it was briefly the main rival to early [[Christianity]]. Persecution by the Roman state and the Christian church largely removed it from Roman lands by the end of the sixth century. The [[Abbasid Caliphate]] repressed it in the 10th century, and it was the official religion of the [[Uyghur Khaganate]] until its collapse in 840.

=== KEY: Manifest_destiny_db09a3
HEADWORD: Manifest destiny
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Abraham Lincoln | American Civil War | Democratic Party (United States) | Mexican–American War | North America | Ulysses S. Grant | William McKinley
ENTRY:
19th-century U.S. belief in westward expansion across [[North America]]. Rooted in American exceptionalism, it held that the nation had a divinely ordained mission to spread republican government. [[Democratic Party (United States)|Democrats]] used it to justify the 1846 Oregon boundary dispute, the 1845 annexation of Texas as a slave state and the [[Mexican–American War]]. Opponents, including [[Abraham Lincoln]] and [[Ulysses S. Grant]], rejected the concept and opposed the war. It is also associated with settler expansion onto Indigenous lands. After the [[American Civil War]], the U.S. acquired Alaska in 1867, and in the 1890s [[William McKinley]] annexed Hawaii, the Philippines, Puerto Rico, Guam and American Samoa.

=== KEY: Manifold_76dd9d
HEADWORD: Manifold
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Calculus | Euclidean space | General relativity | Sphere | Topological space | Topology | Torus
ENTRY:
[[Topological space]] locally resembling [[Euclidean space]] near each point. An *n*-dimensional manifold has a neighborhood around each point that is homeomorphic to an open subset of *n*-dimensional Euclidean space. One-dimensional manifolds include lines and circles, and two-dimensional manifolds, called surfaces, include the [[Sphere|sphere]], the [[Torus|torus]] and the Klein bottle. Manifolds are central to geometry and modern physics: differentiable manifolds allow calculus, a Riemannian metric allows distances and angles to be measured, and four-dimensional Lorentzian manifolds model spacetime in [[General relativity|general relativity]]. Studying them requires knowledge of [[Calculus|calculus]] and [[Topology|topology]].

=== KEY: Manila_b07c84
HEADWORD: Manila
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Americas | Luzon | Metro Manila | Mexico City | New Spain | Philippines | Tokyo | Typhoon
ENTRY:
Capital of the [[Philippines]] (pop., 2024: 1,902,590), on the eastern shore of Manila Bay in [[Luzon]]. A Tagalog-fortified polity called Maynila existed on the site by 1258. On June 24, 1571, after the defeat of its last indigenous ruler, Rajah Sulayman, Miguel López de Legazpi began building the walled fortification of Intramuros. Manila was the capital of the Spanish East Indies, administered from [[Mexico City]] within the Viceroyalty of [[New Spain]], and its galleon trade linked Asia with the [[Americas]]. Under American occupation it became the country's first chartered city in 1901. Manila is the world's second most natural disaster-exposed capital after [[Tokyo]]. [[Typhoon|Typhoons]] and poor drainage cause recurrent flooding. It forms the core of [[Metro Manila]].

=== KEY: Manipur_9f405b
HEADWORD: Manipur
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: British Raj | Northeast India | Polo
ENTRY:
State in [[Northeast India|northeastern India]], with Imphal as its capital. Mostly hilly, it is home to the Meitei people, who make up around 53% of the population and live mainly in the Imphal Valley. Manipur was a princely state under the [[British Raj]] and merged with India in 1949, an agreement many Meitei feel violated their self-determination. A long-running insurgency against Indian rule has been accompanied by ethnic violence, which displaced over 60,000 people after May 2023. The state is credited with introducing [[Polo]] to Europeans.

=== KEY: Manitoba_bcbcce
HEADWORD: Manitoba
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Arctic | Hudson Bay | Hudson's Bay Company | Louis Riel | Prairie | Tundra | Winnipeg
ENTRY:
Province of Canada at the country's longitudinal center. Its capital and largest city is [[Winnipeg]]. The landscape ranges from [[Arctic]] [[Tundra|tundra]] and the coast of [[Hudson Bay]] in the north to boreal forest, lakes and [[Prairie|prairie]] in the south. Under the [[Hudson's Bay Company]], Rupert's Land, which included the present province, was administered from 1670 until 1869. Armed resistance led by [[Louis Riel]] preceded Manitoba's entry into Confederation as the fifth province on July 15, 1870. French holds official legislative and judicial status alongside English.

=== KEY: Manmohan_Singh_8f78e6
HEADWORD: Singh, Manmohan
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: BRICS | Indian National Congress | Sonia Gandhi | United Nations | University of Oxford
ENTRY:
Indian economist and politician, prime minister of India from 2004 to 2014. A member of the [[Indian National Congress]], he was the first and remains the only Sikh prime minister. Born in Gah, in present-day Pakistan, he earned a doctorate in economics at the [[University of Oxford]] and worked for the [[United Nations]] from 1966 to 1969. As finance minister from 1991 he carried out structural reforms that liberalized India's economy. In 2004 [[Sonia Gandhi]] unexpectedly gave up the prime ministership to him. His first ministry carried out key legislation, including the Right to Information Act. His coalition was returned with an increased mandate in 2009, but its second term was marked by political scandals. In 2009 [[BRICS]] was established with India as a founding member.

=== KEY: Mannerism_53ba6c
HEADWORD: Mannerism
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Baroque | El Greco | Leonardo da Vinci | Michelangelo | Raphael | Renaissance
ENTRY:
Style in European art, emerging around 1520 and lasting in Italy until about the end of the 16th century. Mannerists reacted against the ideals of the High [[Renaissance]], exaggerating the proportion and balance of [[Leonardo da Vinci]], [[Raphael]] and [[Michelangelo]] to create asymmetrical or unnaturally elegant compositions. The term derives from the Italian *maniera*, meaning style or manner. Its definition remains debated, and some scholars apply it to late 16th- and 17th-century poetry and music. The [[Baroque]] style largely replaced it in Italy, while in Spain, [[El Greco]] adapted it in his late works.
