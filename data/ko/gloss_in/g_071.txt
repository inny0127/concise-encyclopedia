=== KEY: Johann_Gottlieb_Fichte_ac9491

PERSON: yes

ENTRY:
German philosopher. A founding figure of German idealism, which developed from the writings of [[Immanuel Kant]], Fichte was motivated, like Descartes and Kant, by the problem of subjectivity and [[Consciousness|consciousness]]. He originated the thesis–antithesis–synthesis scheme, an idea often wrongly attributed to Hegel. He taught philosophy at Jena until 1799, when he was dismissed amid the "atheism controversy" over one of his essays. He later lectured in Berlin, where he wrote on [[Political philosophy|political philosophy]] and is regarded as one of the fathers of German nationalism. His *Addresses to the German Nation* (1807–1808), delivered during the [[Napoleon|Napoleonic]] occupation of Prussia, called for educational reform and national unity. Critics charge that the lectures fostered ethnic exclusivity, while others stress their emphasis on voluntary ethical commitment.

=== KEY: Johann_Sebastian_Bach_86b1e3
HEADWORD: Bach, Johann Sebastian
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Brandenburg Concertos | Counterpoint | Felix Mendelssohn | Fugue | Leipzig | Mass in B minor | The Well-Tempered Clavier | Thuringia
ENTRY:
German composer and musician of the late Baroque period.

Bach is known for his prolific output across many instruments and forms: the orchestral [[Brandenburg Concertos|*Brandenburg Concertos*]]; the *Cello Suites* and the Sonatas and Partitas for solo violin; keyboard works such as the *Goldberg Variations* and [[The Well-Tempered Clavier|*The Well-Tempered Clavier*]]; organ works including the Toccata and Fugue in D minor; and choral works such as the *St. Matthew Passion* and the [[Mass in B minor|*Mass in B minor*]]. His mastery of [[Counterpoint|counterpoint]] is heard in *The Musical Offering* and *The Art of Fugue*, and many of his works use techniques such as canon and [[Fugue|fugue]].

Born in Eisenach, the youngest child of the town musician Johann Ambrosius Bach, he was orphaned at age 10 and lived for five years with his eldest brother, Johann Christoph, before continuing his education in Lüneburg. From 1703 he worked as a church musician in [[Thuringia]], at Arnstadt and Mühlhausen. By 1723 he was Thomaskantor, responsible for music in four Lutheran city churches and for the St. Thomas School in [[Leipzig]]. There he composed annual cycles of church cantatas, wrote for the university's Collegium Musicum, and began publishing his keyboard music in 1726. In 1736 Augustus III of Poland named him court composer of the Elector of Saxony. Four of his twenty children, Wilhelm Friedemann, Carl Philipp Emanuel, Johann Christoph Friedrich, and Johann Christian, became composers.

He blended German traditions with rhythms, forms, and textures from Italy and France, and wrote hundreds of cantatas, Passions, oratorios, motets, concertos, and suites. Few works were printed in his lifetime, and he was known mainly as an organist. [[Felix Mendelssohn]] precipitated the Bach Revival with a performance of the *St. Matthew Passion* in 1829.

ENTRY:
German composer and pianist of the mid-Romantic period. === KEY: Johannes_Diderik_van_der_Waals_5efab5
HEADWORD: Waals, Johannes Diderik van der
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Amsterdam | Molecule | Superconductivity | Van der Waals force
ENTRY:
Dutch theoretical physicist who received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1910 "for his work on the equation of state for gases and liquids." He began as a schoolteacher and became the first physics professor of the University of [[Amsterdam]] in 1877. He is best known for the van der Waals equation, which describes the behavior of gases and their condensation to the liquid phase. His 1873 thesis noted the non-ideality of real gases and attributed it to intermolecular interactions, and his equation accounted for the finite volume of [[Molecule|molecules]]. The forces between stable molecules are named for him: [[Van der Waals force|van der Waals forces]]. His work influenced Heike Kamerlingh Onnes, who first liquefied helium in 1908, a step that led directly to his 1911 discovery of [[Superconductivity|superconductivity]].

=== KEY: Johannes_Gutenberg_04f7b8
HEADWORD: Gutenberg, Johannes
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Alloy | Antimony | Gutenberg Bible | Ink | Printing press | Reformation | Renaissance | Strasbourg
ENTRY:
German inventor and craftsman who created the movable-type printing press in Europe.

Born in Mainz into a patrician family, he trained as a goldsmith, metallurgist, and gem cutter. A letter of March 1434 places him in [[Strasbourg]], where he appears to have been enrolled in the militia as a goldsmith, and in 1437 he was teaching a wealthy tradesman to polish gems. Movable type was already in use in East Asia, but his methods allowed a much faster rate of printing.

His contributions include a process for mass-producing movable type, adjustable molds, oil-based [[Ink|ink]], and a wooden [[Printing press|printing press]] modeled on the agricultural screw presses of the period. His method of making type is traditionally held to have used a hand mold and a type-metal [[Alloy|alloy]] of lead, tin, and [[Antimony|antimony]], which melted at a relatively low temperature, cast well, and produced durable type. By about 1450 he had established a printing workshop in Mainz. His major work, the [[Gutenberg Bible]] (c. 1455), was the first printed version of the Bible and has been acclaimed for its aesthetic and technical quality. Financial difficulties followed: a lawsuit from the investor Johann Fust led to the loss of his press and equipment in 1456.

The printing press later spread across the world and led to an information revolution and the mass spread of literature in Europe, with a profound effect on the [[Renaissance]], the [[Reformation]], and humanist movements. To mark the 500th anniversary of his birth, the Gutenberg Museum was founded in Mainz in 1900. In 1997 Time Life picked his invention as the most important of the second millennium.

=== KEY: Johannes_Kepler_cf659a
HEADWORD: Kepler, Johannes
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Scientific Revolution | Telescope | Tycho Brahe
ENTRY:
German astronomer and mathematician who was a key figure of the [[Scientific Revolution]]. He is best known for three laws of planetary motion, published in *Astronomia nova* (1609) and *Harmonices Mundi* (1619). The first two laws state that planets follow elliptical paths with the Sun at one focus, and that a line from the Sun to a planet sweeps equal areas in equal times. The third law relates the square of a planet's orbital period to the cube of its semi-major axis. Kepler worked as an assistant to [[Tycho Brahe]], whose observations of Mars he used, and later as imperial mathematician in Prague. His *Astronomiae Pars Optica* (1604) described how lenses form images, and he invented an improved version of the [[Telescope|refracting telescope]]. He was also an astrologer, and his novel *Somnium* earned him the description "father of science fiction."

=== KEY: John_Dalton_54c244

ENTRY:
English chemist, physicist, and meteorologist. He proposed that each chemical element consists of identical [[Atom|atoms]] of characteristic weight, and that compounds form when atoms of different elements combine in fixed whole-number proportions. Building on earlier ideas about the indivisibility of matter, his work laid the foundations of modern atomic theory and stoichiometric chemistry. His investigations into his own [[Color blindness|color blindness]] produced the first scientific description of the condition, which in several languages is still known as Daltonism.

=== KEY: John_Locke_8f60c7
HEADWORD: Locke, John
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Empiricism | Francis Bacon | Glorious Revolution | Liberalism | Netherlands | Slavery | Thomas Jefferson
ENTRY:
English philosopher and physician, among the most influential thinkers of the Enlightenment and commonly called the "father of [[Liberalism|liberalism]]."

His major works, all of 1689–90, are *A Letter Concerning Toleration*, *Two Treatises of Government*, and *An Essay Concerning Human Understanding*. The *Letter* contends that religion is a matter for the individual and that churches are voluntary associations, ruling out religious coercion and uniformity. The *Two Treatises* argue for government based on the consent of the governed and for a right to revolt against tyranny. They are believed to have influenced the language [[Thomas Jefferson]] chose for the 1776 Declaration of Independence.

In the *Essay*, Locke argued that the mind at birth is a blank slate, or *tabula rasa*, and that knowledge comes only from experience through sense perception, a position known as [[Empiricism|empiricism]] and associated with the tradition of [[Francis Bacon]]. He is also credited with describing private property as a natural right arising when a person mixes labor with nature.

Born in Wrington, Somerset, to Puritan parents, he attended Westminster School and Christ Church, Oxford, where he spent 15 years as student and tutor and pursued medicine. From 1666 he was an associate of Lord Shaftesbury and held government posts through him. He lived in exile in the [[Netherlands]] in 1683–85, returned in 1689 after the [[Glorious Revolution]], and later served as a Commissioner of Trade and Plantations.

His ties to [[Slavery|slavery]] remain debated: he was a shareholder in the Royal Company of Adventures Trading to Africa, which traded in slaves, and was involved in the Carolina proprietorship, which permitted slavery.
