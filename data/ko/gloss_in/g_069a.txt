Film, literary, and media franchise centered on the British Secret Service agent [[James Bond (literary character)|James Bond]], code-named 007. HEADWORD: James Bond
PERSON: no

ENTRY:
Fictional British Secret Service officer, code-named 007. [[Ian Fleming]] created the character in 1953 and wrote twelve Bond novels and two short-story collections. Other authors later wrote Bond novels. Bond was a composite of commandos Fleming knew in the Naval Intelligence Division during the Second World War, to whom Fleming added his own style and tastes. His traits include a love of cars, food, drink, and sex, and an average intake of sixty custom-made cigarettes a day. His name may come from the American ornithologist James Bond, though Fleming may instead have taken it from a Welsh agent he served with.

American singer, songwriter, and record producer known as the "Godfather of Soul." === KEY: James_Buchanan_7d1f07

PERSON: yes

ENTRY:
15th president of the United States (1857–61). A Democrat from Pennsylvania, Buchanan upheld a pro-slavery interpretation of the Constitution. Before taking office, he sought to secure the pro-slavery ruling in Dred Scott v. Sandford, and he supported admitting Kansas as a slave state under the Lecompton Constitution. In his last months in office, seven Southern states seceded, and on April 12, 1861, Confederate forces attacked Fort Sumter, starting the [[American Civil War]]. Critics in the North and South faulted his limited response to the secession crisis. Historians and scholars rank him among the worst presidents in American history.

=== KEY: James_Chadwick_04e214

PERSON: yes

ENTRY:
British experimental physicist who discovered the [[Neutron|neutron]]. Chadwick studied under [[Ernest Rutherford]] at Manchester and then at Cambridge, where he became Rutherford's assistant director of research. He spent four years in the Ruhleben internment camp in Germany during [[World War I]]. In 1941 he wrote the final draft of the MAUD Report, which inspired the U.S. government to begin serious atomic bomb research, and he headed the British team on the [[Manhattan Project]] during [[World War II]]. He won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1935 for discovering the neutron and was knighted in 1945. In 1935 he also became a professor of physics at Liverpool, where he installed a cyclotron.

=== KEY: James_Clerk_Maxwell_9a8628
HEADWORD: Maxwell, James Clerk
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Control theory | Isaac Newton | Maxwell's demon | Maxwell's equations | Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution | Quantum mechanics | Radio wave | Statistical mechanics
ENTRY:
Scottish physicist and mathematician who formulated the classical theory of electromagnetic radiation, the first theory to describe electricity, magnetism, and light as different manifestations of the same phenomenon. Born in Edinburgh, he graduated from Trinity College, Cambridge, in 1854, earning distinction in mathematics and the Smith's Prize. He later held the chair of natural philosophy at Marischal College in Aberdeen, where he proposed that the rings of Saturn were composed of numerous small particles, work that earned him the Adams Prize. From 1860 to 1865 he was professor of natural philosophy at King's College London. His 1865 paper "A Dynamical Theory of the Electromagnetic Field" showed that electric and magnetic fields travel through space as waves at the speed of light, and led to his prediction of the existence of [[Radio wave|radio waves]]. The four equations now known as [[Maxwell's equations]] appear in his 1873 *A Treatise on Electricity and Magnetism* and are regarded as the second great unification in physics, after that achieved by [[Isaac Newton]].

Maxwell was the first to derive the [[Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution]], describing the speeds of gas molecules, and was key in the creation of [[Statistical mechanics|statistical mechanics]]. In 1861 he presented the first durable color photograph, and showed that any color can be produced by mixing three primary colors: red, green, and blue. His 1863 paper "On Governors" is a foundation of [[Control theory|control theory]] and cybernetics. In 1867 he proposed the thought experiment known as [[Maxwell's demon]].

In 1871 he returned to Cambridge as the first Cavendish Professor of Physics and oversaw construction of the Cavendish Laboratory. He is regarded as a founder of modern electrical engineering, and his work laid foundations for relativity and [[Quantum mechanics|quantum mechanics]].

=== KEY: James_Cook_8b6d01
HEADWORD: Cook, James
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Hawaiian Islands | Indigenous peoples | Kingdom of Great Britain | New Zealand | Pacific Ocean | Scurvy | Southern Ocean
ENTRY:
British Royal Navy officer, explorer, and cartographer. Cook led three voyages of exploration in the [[Pacific Ocean]] and [[Southern Ocean]] between 1768 and 1779. He completed the first recorded circumnavigation of the main islands of [[New Zealand]], and his voyages included the first recorded European visit to Australia's east coast and the [[Hawaiian Islands]]. He claimed several territories for the [[Kingdom of Great Britain]] and made contact with numerous [[Indigenous peoples]]. His contributions to preventing [[Scurvy|scurvy]] earned him the Copley Gold Medal of the Royal Society. In 1779, during his second visit to Hawaii, he was killed in a violent dispute with Native Hawaiians. There is debate over the extent of his responsibility for violent encounters with Indigenous peoples, the transmission of infectious diseases, and his claims to Indigenous land without consent.

=== KEY: James_Hutton_8ec674
HEADWORD: Hutton, James
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Anthropic principle | Soot
ENTRY:
Scottish geologist, agriculturalist, and chemical manufacturer, often called the "Father of Modern Geology." Hutton inferred the remote history of the Earth from evidence in present-day rocks, studying features such as Salisbury Crags and Siccar Point. He argued that geological features underwent continuing transformation over indefinitely long periods, and he was one of the earliest proponents of uniformitarianism, the view that continuing natural processes shape the Earth's crust. He proposed a "system of the habitable Earth," a deistic mechanism to keep the world suitable for humans, an early form of the [[Anthropic principle]]. He earned a medical doctorate at Leiden University and co-owned a profitable chemical works that made sal ammoniac from [[Soot|soot]]. In Berwickshire he farmed and introduced innovative farming practices.

King of England, Scotland, and Ireland, 1685–88. The last Catholic monarch of the three kingdoms, James was deposed in the [[Glorious Revolution]]. He was the second surviving son of [[Charles I of England|Charles I]]. Parliaments in England and Scotland refused to pass measures undermining the Protestant religion, and his attempts to impose them by decree met opposition. After his defeat at the Battle of the Boyne in 1690, he returned to France, where [[Louis XIV]] protected him.

Irish novelist, poet, and literary critic. *Ulysses* (1922) is a landmark work that parallels the episodes of [[Homer]]'s *Odyssey* in a variety of literary styles, particularly stream of consciousness. Although Joyce spent most of his adult life abroad, in [[Trieste]], [[Zurich]], and [[Paris]], his fiction centers on [[Dublin]]. Publication of *Ulysses* was prohibited in the United Kingdom and the United States for its perceived obscenity until the mid-1930s. His other works include the short-story collection *Dubliners* (1914), the novel *A Portrait of the Artist as a Young Man* (1916), and the experimental *Finnegans Wake* (1939). He died in Zurich after surgery for a perforated ulcer.

11th president of the United States (1845–49). A Democrat and protégé of [[Andrew Jackson]], Polk was an advocate of expansionism. Texas joined the Union in his first year, and the [[Mexican–American War]] that followed ended with Mexico ceding the American Southwest. Founding Father and fourth president of the United States (1809–17). Madison was acclaimed the "Father of the Constitution" for his central role in drafting the [[Constitution]] and the Bill of Rights. He joined [[Alexander Hamilton]] and John Jay in writing *The Federalist Papers*, pro-ratification essays. Alongside [[Thomas Jefferson]], he organized the Democratic–Republican Party in opposition to Hamilton's [[Federalist Party]]. As Jefferson's secretary of state, he helped convince him to submit the [[Louisiana Purchase]] treaty to the Senate. He led the United States into the [[War of 1812]]. Born into a slave-owning planter family in [[Virginia]], he owned slaves at his plantation, Montpelier.

=== KEY: James_McNeill_Whistler_4d4a54
HEADWORD: Whistler, James McNeill
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Impressionism | Venice
ENTRY:
American painter and printmaker based mainly in the United Kingdom. Whistler rejected sentimentality and moral allusion in painting and was a leading proponent of "art for art's sake." He titled many paintings as "arrangements," "harmonies," and "nocturnes" to emphasize tonal harmony. His best-known work, *Arrangement in Grey and Black No. 1* (1871), is commonly known as *Whistler's Mother*. He produced nearly 500 etchings, many of them of the Thames River. In 1878 he brought a libel suit against critic John Ruskin, winning a farthing in damages that left him bankrupt. His decorative harmony influenced [[Impressionism]], and he taught in [[Venice]] and Paris late in his career.

=== KEY: James_Monroe_6e65f8
HEADWORD: Monroe, James
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Federalist Party | Louisiana Purchase | Monroe Doctrine | Monrovia | Thomas Jefferson
ENTRY:
Founding Father and fifth president of the United States (1817–25). He was the last Founding Father to serve as president, and his presidency coincided with the Era of Good Feelings after the collapse of the [[Federalist Party]]. As President [[Thomas Jefferson]]'s envoy, he helped negotiate the [[Louisiana Purchase]]. In 1819 the Adams–Onís Treaty gave the United States Florida, and in 1823 Monroe announced the [[Monroe Doctrine]], opposing European intervention in the Americas. He signed the Missouri Compromise, which admitted Missouri as a slave state and banned slavery from territories north of the 36°30′ parallel. Liberia's capital, [[Monrovia]], is named in his honor.

=== KEY: James_Prescott_Joule_fe812e
HEADWORD: Joule, James Prescott
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Calorie | Conservation of energy | First law of thermodynamics | John Dalton | Joule | Lord Kelvin | Resistor
ENTRY:
English physicist and brewer. Joule studied the nature of heat and showed its relationship to mechanical work, leading to the law of [[Conservation of energy|conservation of energy]] and the [[First law of thermodynamics|first law of thermodynamics]]. He estimated the mechanical equivalent of heat as 4.1868 joules per [[Calorie|calorie]]. He worked with [[Lord Kelvin]] to develop an absolute thermodynamic temperature scale, which became known as the Kelvin scale. His finding on the relationship between the current through a [[Resistor|resistor]] and the heat dissipated is known as Joule's first law. Tutored as a young man by [[John Dalton]], he managed the family brewery as an adult, and the SI unit of energy, the [[Joule|joule]], is named after him.
