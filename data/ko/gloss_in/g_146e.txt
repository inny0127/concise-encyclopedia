HEADWORD: Wii
PERSON: no

ENTRY:
Home video game console by [[Nintendo]]. Released on November 19, 2006, in North America, it was Nintendo's fifth major home console and a seventh-generation console alongside the [[Xbox 360]] and [[PlayStation 3]]. Its wireless Wii Remote detects motion and gestures. Nintendo's focus on play rather than raw computing power kept production costs low. The bundled game Wii Sports became its killer app. Lifetime sales exceed 101 million units, making it the seventh-best-selling console of all time as of April 2026. Production ended in October 2013. Its successor, the Wii U, became Nintendo's worst-selling home console.

=== KEY: WikiLeaks_2398fe
HEADWORD: WikiLeaks
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Hillary Clinton | Julian Assange
ENTRY:
Non-profit media organization publishing leaked documents. Founded in 2006 by [[Julian Assange]], it says it has released more than ten million documents. Its releases include a video of a 2007 US helicopter strike in Baghdad, titled Collateral Murder, which killed Reuters journalists, and thousands of US military logs from Afghanistan and Iraq. During the 2016 US presidential campaign it released emails from the Democratic National Committee and from [[Hillary Clinton]]'s campaign manager, which contributed to the chairwoman's resignation. Critics cite inadequate curation, privacy violations and bias. In 2023 Assange said WikiLeaks could no longer publish because of his imprisonment and surveillance pressures.

=== KEY: Wiki_bf111e
HEADWORD: Wiki
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Wikipedia
ENTRY:
Type of website whose pages its audience can edit directly through a web browser, with little structure imposed by a defined owner or leader. The name derives from the Hawaiian word meaning quick, and from WikiWikiWeb, the first user-editable website. Ward Cunningham, developer of the first wiki software, described a wiki as the simplest online database that could possibly work. Wikis are used for knowledge management, note-taking and community sites. The most popular is [[Wikipedia]], which is a collection of hundreds of wikis in different languages. Wiki engines range from free and open-source software to proprietary products.

=== KEY: Wikipedia_9c6772
HEADWORD: Wikipedia
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Bias | Wiki
ENTRY:
Free online encyclopedia written and maintained by volunteer editors. It was founded by Jimmy Wales and Larry Sanger in 2001 as a [[Wiki|wiki]] complement to Nupedia, an expert-written encyclopedia project. It has been hosted since 2003 by the Wikimedia Foundation, an American nonprofit funded mainly by reader donations. It is the largest and most read reference work in history. As of November 2026 the English edition has more than seven million articles, and all editions together exceed 68 million. Its reliability was often criticized in the 2000s but has earned more praise since the late 2010s. Critics point to systemic [[Bias|bias]], including gender bias against women and geographic bias against the Global South. Some national governments have censored it, from specific pages to the entire site.

=== KEY: Wild_Bill_Hickok_43f3bb
HEADWORD: Hickok, Wild Bill
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: American Civil War | Illinois | Poker | South Dakota
ENTRY:
American frontier scout, lawman and gambler. Raised on an [[Illinois|Illinois]] farm, he headed west and worked as a stagecoach driver and lawman in Kansas. He served as a scout and spy for the Union army in the [[American Civil War]]. Many tales of his exploits were exaggerated or fabricated, some by himself. Biographer Joseph G. Rosa credits him with killing six or seven men between 1861 and 1871. In Deadwood, Dakota Territory (now [[South Dakota]]), he was shot and killed by Jack McCall while playing [[Poker|poker]]. The hand he supposedly held, black aces and eights, became known as the dead man's hand.

=== KEY: Wild_boar_57afd8
HEADWORD: Wild boar
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Hybrid (biology) | Invasive species | Pig | Wolf
ENTRY:
Species of suid (*Sus scrofa*), also known as the wild swine. It is native to much of Eurasia and North Africa, has been introduced to the Americas and Oceania, and is one of the widest-ranging mammals. It is listed as least concern by the IUCN but has become [[Invasive species|invasive]] in part of its introduced range. As of 2005, up to 16 subspecies are recognized. Wild boars live in matriarchal groups of related females and their young, while adult males are usually solitary outside the breeding season. The [[Wolf|wolf]] is its main predator across most of its range. The species is the ancestor of most domestic [[Pig|pig]] breeds. Boar–pig [[Hybrid (biology)|hybrids]] have become serious pests in the Americas and Australia.

=== KEY: Wild_turkey_3cbf2c
HEADWORD: Wild turkey
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Domestic turkey | Galliformes
ENTRY:
Upland game bird native to North America, one of two extant turkey species and the heaviest member of the order [[Galliformes]]. It is the ancestor of the [[Domestic turkey|domestic turkey]], which derives from a southern Mexican subspecies. Adult males, called toms, normally weigh 5 to 11 kg (11 to 24 lb), and the record male weighed 17.06 kg (37.6 lb). Mature males have a reddish, featherless head and a fleshy snood on the bill. Overhunting brought the bird near extinction by the early 20th century. Reintroductions from the 1940s restored it to all U.S. states except Alaska.

=== KEY: Wildebeest_de294b
HEADWORD: Wildebeest
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Antelope | Cattle | Zebra
ENTRY:
[[Antelope|Antelope]] of the genus *Connochaetes*, native to eastern and southern Africa. There are two species: the blue wildebeest, also called the brindled gnu, and the black wildebeest or white-tailed gnu. The two diverged about one million years ago. In East Africa the blue wildebeest is the most abundant big-game species, and some populations make an annual migration to new grazing grounds. Calves are born at the end of the rainy season and must move with the herd soon after birth, and some fall prey to spotted hyenas. Wildebeest often graze with [[Zebra|zebras]], which heightens awareness of predators. They compete with livestock for pasture, and farmers sometimes blame them for spreading disease to [[Cattle|cattle]]. Both species are listed as least concern by the IUCN. The name is Afrikaans for wild beast, wild ox or wild cattle.

=== KEY: Wildfire_d2b796
HEADWORD: Wildfire
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Carbon dioxide | Climate change | Drought | Lightning
ENTRY:
Unplanned, uncontrolled fire in an area of combustible vegetation. Some natural forest ecosystems depend on wildfire, and many plant species rely on fire for growth and reproduction. Forest managers use prescribed burns to reduce fire risk, although controlled burns can escape containment. Ignition comes from natural causes such as [[Lightning|lightning]], volcanic eruptions and spontaneous combustion, or from human activity, which is responsible for over 85% of wildfires in the United States. Severe fires often follow wet periods that build fuel and then [[Drought|drought]] and heat, and [[Climate change|climate change]] has intensified these cycles. Human practices have caused a doubling in land area burned compared with natural levels. Wildfire releases [[Carbon dioxide|carbon dioxide]], which adds to the greenhouse effect and creates a climate feedback.

=== KEY: Wildlife_conservation_3c26ea
HEADWORD: Wildlife conservation
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Habitat
ENTRY:
Protection of wild species and their [[Habitat|habitats]] to maintain healthy populations and to restore or enhance natural ecosystems. Major threats include habitat destruction, overexploitation, poaching, pollution, climate change and the illegal wildlife trade. The IUCN estimates that 42,100 of the species it has assessed are at risk of extinction, and a 2019 UN report on biodiversity put the figure at a million species. Deforestation for agriculture or logging removes habitat for more than 80% of the world's species, which live in forests. Key agreements include the 1973 Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora (CITES) and the 1992 Convention on Biological Diversity. Nongovernmental groups include the Nature Conservancy, World Wildlife Fund and Conservation International.
