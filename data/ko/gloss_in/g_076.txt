Volcanic caldera in the Sunda Strait between [[Java]] and [[Sumatra]], [[Indonesia]]. The eruption of August 26–27, 1883, created the caldera and destroyed two-thirds of the island of Krakatoa. === KEY: Krill_320900

ENTRY:
Small, shrimp-like marine [[Crustacean|crustaceans]] of the order Euphausiacea, found in all the world's oceans. Krill feed mainly on phytoplankton and are a key link near the bottom of the food chain. The Antarctic krill makes up an estimated biomass of around 379 million tonnes, and over half of that biomass is eaten each year by whales, seals, [[Penguin|penguins]], [[Seabird|seabirds]], squid and fish. Krill are the main food of baleen whales, including the [[Blue whale]]. The total global harvest of 150,000 to 200,000 tonnes a year is used mainly for [[Aquaculture|aquaculture]] and [[Aquarium|aquarium]] feeds, bait and human consumption. The name comes from the Norwegian word for "small fry of fish".

=== KEY: Krypton_abac9b

ENTRY:
Chemical element with symbol Kr and atomic number 36, a colorless, odorless [[Noble gas|noble gas]] found in trace amounts in Earth's atmosphere. The name comes from the Greek *kryptos*, meaning "the hidden one." Scottish chemist William Ramsay and English chemist Morris Travers discovered krypton in 1898 in the residue left after nearly all components of liquid air had evaporated. Like other noble gases, it is chemically inert, but its bright spectral lines make it useful in fluorescent lamps, photography and high-powered [[Laser|lasers]]. From 1960 to 1983 the official definition of the meter was based on a spectral line of krypton-86. Krypton-85, a radioactive isotope produced by the fission of uranium and plutonium in nuclear reactors, is released when fuel rods are reprocessed.

=== KEY: Kuiper_belt_afb807

PERSON: no

ENTRY:
Disc of small bodies in the outer [[Solar System]], extending from the orbit of [[Neptune]] at 30 astronomical units (AU) to about 50 AU from the Sun. It is similar to the [[Asteroid belt|asteroid belt]] but about 20 times as wide, and most of its objects are composed largely of frozen volatiles such as [[Methane|methane]], ammonia and water. The belt is named for Dutch astronomer Gerard Kuiper, who conjectured its existence in 1951. The belt contains most of the objects generally accepted as [[Dwarf planet|dwarf planets]], including [[Pluto]], Haumea, Quaoar and Makemake. Pluto was reclassified as a dwarf planet in 2006. Studies since the mid-1990s show that [[Comet|comets]]' true place of origin is the scattered disc.

Hindu philosopher and scholar of the Mimamsa school in early medieval India. === KEY: Lagrange_multiplier_755d4b

ENTRY:
Method in [[Mathematical optimization|mathematical optimization]] for finding the local maxima and minima of a function subject to equation constraints. Named after [[Joseph-Louis Lagrange]], it converts a constrained problem into an unconstrained one through a Lagrangian function, which combines the objective with each constraint weighted by a multiplier λ. === KEY: Lagrange_point_2a7846

PERSON: no

ENTRY:
Equilibrium point for a small object under the gravitational influence of two massive orbiting bodies. Five such points exist for any pair of bodies, labeled L1 through L5 and lying in their orbital plane. At these points the gravitational forces of the two bodies balance the centrifugal pseudo-force, so satellites there need minimal fuel to hold position. The [[James Webb Space Telescope]] is located at the Sun–Earth L2 point.

=== KEY: Lagrangian_mechanics_6ac856

PERSON: no

ENTRY:
Formulation of [[Classical mechanics|classical mechanics]] founded on the d'Alembert principle of virtual work. Introduced by [[Joseph-Louis Lagrange]] in 1760 and presented in his 1788 work *Mécanique analytique*, it describes a system by its configuration space and a Lagrangian, which for many systems is the kinetic energy minus the potential energy. Its influence extends to relativity and [[Quantum field theory|quantum field theory]], and symmetries in the Lagrangian yield conserved quantities through [[Noether's theorem]].
