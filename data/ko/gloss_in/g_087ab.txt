HEADWORD: Meerkat
PERSON: no

ENTRY:
Small social [[Mongoose|mongoose]] of [[Southern Africa|southern Africa]], also called the suricate (*Suricata suricatta*). Meerkats live in packs of two to 30 individuals that occupy home ranges of about 5 sq km. Dominant animals usually breed, while subordinate members help care for the pups; litters of three to seven are born after a gestation of 60 to 70 days. Meerkats feed mainly on insects, with small birds, reptiles and plants also eaten, and they retreat to burrows when danger appears. The species is listed as Least Concern by the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN).

=== KEY: Megabat_fd01db
HEADWORD: Megabat
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Africa | Bat | Eurasia | Oceania | Public health
ENTRY:
Family of fruit-eating [[Bat|bats]] (Pteropodidae), also called flying foxes. The family contains about 197 species, as of 2018, and is found in tropical and subtropical areas of [[Eurasia]], [[Africa]] and [[Oceania]]. It likely evolved in Australasia. Megabats include the largest bats, with some species weighing up to 1.45 kg and having wingspans of up to 1.7 m, although nearly a third of species weigh less than 50 g. With few exceptions they cannot echolocate, relying instead on sight and smell to find mainly fruit and nectar. Most species bear one offspring at a time after a pregnancy of four to six months, and a quarter of species are threatened, mainly by habitat destruction and overhunting. As natural reservoirs of several viruses that affect humans, megabats are of interest to [[Public health|public health]] researchers.

=== KEY: Megacity_de4909
HEADWORD: Megacity
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: China | India | Jakarta | OECD | Tokyo | United Nations
ENTRY:
Urban area with a total population of more than 10 million people. The [[United Nations]] defines megacities as urban agglomerations with over 10 million inhabitants, although some sources use a threshold of five to eight million with a density of at least 2,000 per sq km. Counts differ: 32 according to the EU's Global Human Settlement Layer (2024), 33 according to the UN (2025), 39 according to the [[OECD]] (2020) and 42 according to Demographia (2025). Many are in [[China]] and [[India]]. In 2025 the UN listed [[Jakarta]] as the world's largest megacity, whereas the OECD's 2020 figures listed [[Tokyo]].

ENTRY:
American heavy metal band formed in [[Los Angeles]] in 1983 by vocalist and guitarist Dave Mustaine. Megadeth is one of the "big four" of American thrash metal, with [[Slayer]], Anthrax and [[Metallica]], and its technically complex music helped develop and popularize the genre. Its debut album, *Killing Is My Business... and Business Is Good!* (1985), was followed by *Peace Sells... but Who's Buying?* (1986) and platinum-selling albums such as *Rust in Peace* (1990). The final studio album, *Megadeth* (2026), was the band's first to reach No. 1 on the *Billboard* 200, and Mustaine has said the band will disband after its tour.

=== KEY: Megafauna_43c82b
HEADWORD: Megafauna
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Alfred Russel Wallace | Human | Pleistocene
ENTRY:
Large animals, commonly defined as those weighing about 45 kg or more. Megafauna suppress the growth of woody vegetation, reducing wildfire frequency, and help regulate the abundance of smaller animals. During the [[Pleistocene]], megafauna were diverse across the globe. From about 50,000 years ago, most large mammal species became extinct, including 80% of mammals over 1,000 kg, while small animals were largely unaffected. [[Human|Humans]] and climatic change are the most likely causes, though their relative importance is disputed. One of the earliest uses of the term appeared in [[Alfred Russel Wallace]]'s 1876 work on animal distribution.
