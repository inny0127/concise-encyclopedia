=== KEY: Karst_ea4833
HEADWORD: Karst
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Aquifer | Cave | Italy | Limestone | Sinkhole | Slovenia
ENTRY:
Topography formed by the dissolution of soluble carbonate rocks such as [[Limestone|limestone]] and dolomite. Its features include [[Sinkhole|sinkholes]], [[Cave|caves]] and underground drainage systems, which may leave few rivers or lakes on the surface. Karst is most strongly developed in thinly bedded, highly fractured limestone and is poorly developed in chalk, which is porous rather than dense. The term derives from a limestone plateau on the border between [[Italy]] and [[Slovenia]]. Karst [[Aquifer|aquifers]] are highly permeable, which makes them vulnerable to pollutants entering the water supply. Buried karst is important in petroleum geology, because as much as 50% of the world's hydrocarbon reserves are hosted in carbonate rock.

=== KEY: Kartvelian_languages_42e3fe
HEADWORD: Kartvelian languages
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Bethlehem | Georgia (country) | Georgian language
ENTRY:
Language family indigenous to the South Caucasus, spoken primarily in [[Georgia (country)|Georgia]]. It comprises four closely related languages: [[Georgian language|Georgian]], Svan, Mingrelian and Laz. The family is not related to any other language family in the world. Georgian, with about 4 million speakers, is the most widely spoken member and the only literary language in the family. Svan has approximately 35,000–40,000 native speakers, while Laz is endangered as its speakers shift to Turkish. Georgian scripts are used to write all Kartvelian languages. The earliest known written record of any Kartvelian language is an Old Georgian inscription near [[Bethlehem]], dated to c. 430 AD.

=== KEY: Kashmir_96fb0f
HEADWORD: Kashmir
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Akbar | Buddhism | Indian subcontinent | Partition of India
ENTRY:
Northernmost geographical region of the [[Indian subcontinent]]. Until the mid-19th century the name referred only to the Kashmir Valley; it now also covers territory administered by India, Pakistan and China, including Ladakh, Azad Kashmir, Gilgit-Baltistan and Aksai Chin. The region was an important center of [[Buddhism]] in the first millennium, and Shah Mir became its first Muslim ruler in 1339. Mughal rule began with [[Akbar]]'s conquest of Kashmir in 1586. Neutral sources use the terms Pakistan-administered Kashmir and India-administered Kashmir. In 2019 India revoked the special autonomy of Jammu and Kashmir, a step that Pakistani and separatist perspectives have accused of demographic engineering and suppression.

=== KEY: Kashrut_3d984c
HEADWORD: Kashrut
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Fish | Frog | Pork | Talmud | Torah
ENTRY:
Jewish dietary laws governing which foods are permitted and how they must be prepared. Kosher foods come from permitted mammals, birds and [[Fish|fish]] that meet specific criteria; forbidden animals include [[Pork|pork]], [[Frog|frogs]] and shellfish. Because blood may not be eaten, mammals and birds must be slaughtered by a certified ritual slaughterer (*shochet*) in a process called shechita, and the meat must then be koshered by broiling, roasting, or soaking and salting. Meat and dairy may never be mixed, and separate equipment is used for meat-based and dairy-based foods. Kosher products are classified as meat (fleishig), dairy (milchig) or pareve (neutral). Most basic laws derive from Leviticus and Deuteronomy in the [[Torah]], and details are set down in the Oral Torah, codified in the [[Talmud]].

=== KEY: Katabatic_wind_ab1e6f
HEADWORD: Katabatic wind
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Air mass | Antarctica | Greenland | Southern California
ENTRY:
Downslope wind caused by a flow of dense, cool [[Air mass|air]] into lighter air below. Katabatic winds are usually cool or cold, because cold air is denser than warm air. The most common type forms at night as slopes cool by radiation. Examples include the piteraq of [[Greenland]], the Bora in the Adriatic and the Santa Ana winds of [[Southern California]]. Off the ice sheets of [[Antarctica]] and Greenland, they can blow well over hurricane force in coastal valleys. In parts of continental Antarctica they scour away snow, creating dry valleys such as the McMurdo Dry Valleys.

=== KEY: Katakana_edb097
HEADWORD: Katakana
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Hiragana | Kanji
ENTRY:
One of two Japanese syllabaries, used mainly for foreign loanwords. The name means *fragmentary kana*, because its characters derive from fragments of more complex [[Kanji|kanji]]. It evolved from Japanese Buddhist monks' transliterations of Chinese texts. Katakana transcribes foreign words (*gairaigo*) and foreign names, and is used for onomatopoeia, emphasis, technical and scientific terms, and the names of plants, animals, minerals and many Japanese companies. It contrasts with [[Hiragana|hiragana]], which writes native words and grammatical inflections. Its usage is comparable to italics in English.

=== KEY: Katana_a54248
HEADWORD: Katana
PERSON: no

ENTRY:
Japanese sword with a curved, single-edged blade and a long grip for two hands. Developed later than the tachi, it was worn by [[Samurai|samurai]] in feudal Japan with the edge facing upward. The name is a compound of *kata* ("one side") and *na* ("blade") and first appears in the *Nihon Shoki* of 720. Its blade is generally longer than 2 shaku, or about 60 cm. In the [[Sengoku period]] of the 16th century, the main battlefield weapons were the bow, the spear and the gun, while the katana was used mainly for close combat. The Portuguese adopted the word *catana* for a [[Machete|machete]].

=== KEY: Kathasaritsagara_a27e41
HEADWORD: Kathāsaritsāgara
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Akbar | Brahmin | Sanskrit
ENTRY:
Eleventh-century [[Sanskrit]] collection of Indian legends and folk tales, compiled by Somadeva, a Shaiva [[Brahmin|Brahman]] of Kashmir. It was written between 1063 and 1081 for the amusement of Queen Sūryavatī, wife of King Ananta of Kashmir. The work is framed by the adventures of Prince Naravahanadatta, son of King Udayana, and as many as 350 tales are built around this central story, making it the largest existing collection of Indian tales. Somadeva described it as an abridged translation of the lost *Bṛhatkathā* by Guṇāḍhya. Its tales, like those of the *Panchatantra*, traveled widely, and a Persian version was commissioned by [[Akbar]] in 1589.

=== KEY: Kazakh_Khanate_b104c3
HEADWORD: Kazakh Khanate
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Aral Sea | Caspian Sea | Central Asia | Golden Horde | Russian Empire | Ural Mountains
ENTRY:
Nomadic Turkic state in [[Central Asia]], c. 1465 to the early 19th century. It emerged after the fragmentation of the [[Golden Horde]]. By the end of the 15th century the Kazakhs controlled the steppe east of the [[Caspian Sea]] and north of the [[Aral Sea]], and under Kasym Khan their authority reached the [[Ural Mountains]]. The khanate declined in the 17th and 18th centuries and, after the death of Tauke Khan, split into three independent khanates known as zhuzes. Ablai Khan reunited the Kazakhs, but after his death in 1781 the zhuzes fractured again, and their khans were appointed by the Russian government. The institution of the khanate was abolished in the [[Russian Empire]] in 1822–1824. It was the first nation-state in Central Asia created by the modern Kazakh ethnic group itself.

=== KEY: Kazakh_language_7e63ad

ENTRY:
Kipchak branch [[Turkic languages|Turkic]] language and the official language of [[Kazakhstan]]. It is closely related to Nogai, Kyrgyz and Karakalpak, and is agglutinative, with vowel harmony. Ethnologue recognizes three mutually intelligible dialect groups: North-Eastern Kazakh, the most widely spoken and the basis of the official language; Southern Kazakh; and Western Kazakh. Kazakhs wrote in the [[Arabic script]] until about 1929, and the Soviet regime later imposed Latin and then [[Cyrillic script|Cyrillic]] scripts. A 2017 decree ordered a change to Latin script, scheduled to be phased in from 2023 to 2031.

=== KEY: Kelvin_8cdaea

ENTRY:
Base unit of temperature in the [[International System of Units]] (SI), symbol K. The kelvin scale is an absolute temperature scale that starts at [[Absolute zero]], taken to be 0 K. A rise of one kelvin equals a rise of one degree [[Celsius]], and a Celsius temperature is converted to kelvins by adding 273.15. The scale was first proposed in 1848 by William Thomson, later [[Lord Kelvin]], whose paper gave a value of −273 °C for absolute zero. The kelvin was formally added to the SI in 1954, when 273.16 K was defined as the triple point of water. The 2019 revision of the SI instead fixes the Boltzmann constant at exactly 1.380649×10⁻²³ joules per kelvin.

=== KEY: Kepler_s_laws_of_planetary_motion_1a2a07
HEADWORD: Kepler's laws of planetary motion
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Ellipse | Isaac Newton | Johannes Kepler | Mars | Solar System | Tycho Brahe
ENTRY:
Three laws describing planetary orbits around the Sun. Published by [[Johannes Kepler]] from 1609 to 1621, they replaced the circular orbits and epicycles of Copernicus's heliocentric model with elliptical orbits, based on the accurate observations of [[Tycho Brahe]]. The first law states that each planet's orbit is an [[Ellipse|ellipse]] with the Sun at one focus. The second law states that equal areas are swept in equal times, so a planet moves faster when closer to the Sun. The third law states that the square of a planet's orbital period is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis of its orbit. Kepler's study of the orbit of [[Mars]] led him to infer that other bodies in the [[Solar System]] also move in ellipses. [[Isaac Newton]] later used the third law in deriving universal gravitation.

Core component of a computer [[Operating system|operating system]]. Organic compound containing a carbonyl group (C=O) bonded to two carbon atoms. The simplest ketone is [[Acetone|acetone]], (CH₃)₂CO. Strong, heat-resistant synthetic aramid fiber related to Nomex. Developed by Stephanie Kwolek at [[DuPont]] in 1965, Kevlar was first used commercially in the early 1970s as a replacement for steel in racing tires. It is spun into ropes or fabric, or used as an ingredient in [[Composite material|composite]] components. A similar fiber, Twaron, was developed by Akzo in the 1970s.

=== KEY: Keyboard_instrument_e1ed91
HEADWORD: Keyboard instrument
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Accordion | Harpsichord | Musical instrument | Piano | Pipe organ | Synthesizer
ENTRY:
[[Musical instrument]] played using a keyboard, a row of levers pressed by the fingers. The most common are the [[Piano|piano]], [[Pipe organ|organ]] and electronic keyboards, including [[Synthesizer|synthesizers]] and digital pianos. The term classifies instruments by how they are played rather than how their sound is produced, grouping them into families such as aerophones, including the pipe organ and [[Accordion|accordion]], and chordophones, including the [[Harpsichord|harpsichord]] and clavichord. In musicology, the term can also mean an instrument of uncertain identity, since the harpsichord, clavichord and early piano competed in the 18th century. The earliest known keyboard instrument was the ancient Greek *hydraulis*, a pipe organ invented in the third century BC. The first template for the modern piano was introduced in 1698 by Bartolomeo Cristofori in Italy.

=== KEY: Khazars_fb39bc

ENTRY:
Semi-nomadic Turkic people who built a major commercial empire on the [[Eurasian Steppe]]. For about three centuries (c. 650–965) the Khazars dominated the area from the Volga-Don steppes to the eastern [[Crimea]] and the northern [[Caucasus]]. === KEY: Khmer_language_e78e67

ENTRY:
[[Austroasiatic languages|Austroasiatic]] language and the official language of [[Cambodia]]. Spoken by some 13 million people in Cambodia, it is also used by Khmer communities in [[Thailand]] and [[Vietnam]]. Most speakers use Central Khmer, though the speech of [[Phnom Penh]] is considered a separate dialect. Khmer is an analytic, isolating language with no inflections, and its word order is subject–verb–object. Unlike neighboring Burmese, Thai, Lao and Vietnamese, it is not tonal. === KEY: Khoisan_languages_bb451e

ENTRY:
Group of African languages known for click consonants, once classified as one family. Joseph Greenberg proposed the grouping, but linguists now reject its genetic unity and treat Khoisan as a *sprachbund*, a set of languages that share features through contact. It is now held to comprise three distinct families and two language isolates, spoken mainly in the [[Kalahari Desert]] of [[Namibia]] and [[Botswana]] and in central [[Tanzania]]. Illegal relocation and confinement of a person against their will. === KEY: Kidney_4ff2bb
HEADWORD: Kidney
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Bladder | Hormone | Transplantation (medicine) | Urinary system | Vitamin D
ENTRY:
Paired, bean-shaped, blood-filtering organ of the [[Urinary system|urinary system]]. Adult kidneys lie on either side of the spine and are about 12 cm (4½ inches) long. Each receives blood from the renal arteries and is attached to a ureter that carries urine to the [[Bladder|bladder]]. Filtration in the glomerulus removes one-fifth of the blood volume entering the kidney, regulating fluid volume, electrolytes, acid–base balance and toxins. The nephron is the structural and functional unit, and each adult kidney contains around 1 million nephrons. The kidneys also convert a precursor of [[Vitamin D|vitamin D]] to its active form and synthesize the [[Hormone|hormones]] erythropoietin and renin. Kidney failure is treated by dialysis or [[Transplantation (medicine)|transplantation]], almost always once renal function drops below 15%.

=== KEY: Kiel_Canal_e29532
HEADWORD: Kiel Canal
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Adolf Hitler | Baltic Sea | Dreadnought | Elbe | North Sea | Schleswig-Holstein | Treaty of Versailles | World War II
ENTRY:
Canal in northern Germany linking the [[North Sea|North Sea]] and the [[Baltic Sea|Baltic Sea]]. The 98 km (61 mi) freshwater canal runs through [[Schleswig-Holstein]], from Brunsbüttel at the mouth of the [[Elbe]] to Holtenau on the Kiel Fjord. Built between 1887 and 1895 and widened between 1907 and 1914 to admit [[Dreadnought|dreadnought]] battleships, it shortens the sea journey by 460 km (290 mi), bypassing the Jutland peninsula and the Danish Straits. It carries an annual average of 32,000 ships (90 daily), transporting approximately 100 million tonnes of goods. The [[Treaty of Versailles]] required it to be open to vessels of any nation at peace with Germany, but the government under [[Adolf Hitler]] repudiated this status in 1936. The canal was reopened to all traffic after [[World War II]] and took its current name in 1948.

=== KEY: Kievan_Rus_34c010

ENTRY:
[[Slavs|East Slavic]] state in [[Eastern Europe]], from the late 9th to the mid-13th century. Ruled by the Rurik dynasty, founded by the Varangian prince Rurik, it was an amalgam of principalities that at its greatest extent, in the mid-11th century, stretched from the White Sea to the [[Black Sea|Black Sea]]. The modern nations of [[Belarus]], [[Russia]] and [[Ukraine]] all claim it as their cultural ancestor.
