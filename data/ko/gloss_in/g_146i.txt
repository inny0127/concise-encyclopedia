ENTRY:
English playwright, poet, and actor, widely regarded as the greatest writer in the English language and the world's pre-eminent dramatist. His extant works, including collaborations, consist of some 39 plays, 154 sonnets, 3 long narrative poems, and a few other verses, some of uncertain authorship. His plays have been translated into every major living language and are performed more often than those of any other playwright.

Shakespeare was born and raised in Stratford-upon-Avon, Warwickshire, the son of John Shakespeare, a glover, and Mary Arden. At 18 he married 26-year-old Anne Hathaway; their children were Susanna and the twins Hamnet and Judith. The years between 1585 and 1592, known as the "lost years," are poorly documented. By 1592 he was part of the [[London]] theater scene as an actor, writer, and part-owner of the Lord Chamberlain's Men, later called the King's Men. Around 1613 he appears to have retired to Stratford.

Most of his known works date from 1589 to 1613. His early plays were primarily comedies and histories. He then wrote mainly tragedies until 1608, among them [[Hamlet]], [[Othello]], [[King Lear]], and [[Macbeth]]. In his last phase he wrote tragicomedies, also called romances, such as *The Winter's Tale* and [[The Tempest]]. His sonnets were published in 1609, and the narrative poems *Venus and Adonis* (1593) and *The Rape of Lucrece* (1594) were his most popular works in his lifetime.

In 1623 fellow actors John Heminges and Henry Condell published the First Folio, a collected edition including 36 plays. Its preface has a poem by [[Ben Jonson]], a rival, who hailed him as "not of an age, but for all time."

Canadian actor, best known for playing Captain James T. Kirk in the [[Star Trek]] franchise. From 1966 to 1994 he played Kirk across the franchise, including the original series and seven films. He also starred as attorney Denny Crane in *The Practice* and *Boston Legal*, winning two Emmy Awards. As a recording artist he is known for dramatic recitations of songs, such as versions of [[The Beatles]]' "Lucy in the Sky with Diamonds." In 2021, at age 90, he became the oldest person to fly in space, on a Blue Origin suborbital flight.

=== KEY: William_Shockley_7784ae
HEADWORD: Shockley, William
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Bell Labs | Eugenics | John Bardeen | Silicon Valley | Stanford University | Transistor | World War II
ENTRY:
American physicist and co-inventor of the [[Transistor|transistor]]. Shockley managed a research group at [[Bell Labs]] that included [[John Bardeen]] and Walter Brattain; the three shared the 1956 Nobel Prize in Physics for their work on semiconductors and the transistor effect. During [[World War II]] he worked on radar research. His attempts to commercialize a new transistor design helped make [[Silicon Valley]] a hub of electronics innovation, although his autocratic management led many employees to leave and found major companies. As a professor at [[Stanford University]] in later life, he became known for his outspoken promotion of racism and [[Eugenics|eugenics]].

=== KEY: William_Wallace_e7f507
HEADWORD: Wallace, William
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Edward I | Glasgow | Scotland
ENTRY:
Scottish knight and leader in the First War of Scottish Independence. With Andrew Moray, Wallace defeated an English army at the Battle of Stirling Bridge in 1297 and was appointed Guardian of Scotland, a post he held until his defeat at Falkirk in 1298. Captured near [[Glasgow]] in 1305, he was handed to [[Edward I]] of England, who had him hanged, drawn, and quartered for treason. Since then he has legendary status beyond [[Scotland]]: he is the protagonist of Blind Harry's 15th-century epic *The Wallace*, and the subject of the Academy Award-winning film *Braveheart*.

=== KEY: William_Wilberforce_ee8c8a
HEADWORD: Wilberforce, William
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Atlantic slave trade | Kingston upon Hull | Sierra Leone | Westminster Abbey
ENTRY:
English politician, philanthropist and leader of the movement to abolish the [[Atlantic slave trade]]. A native of [[Kingston upon Hull]], Yorkshire, he became an independent Member of Parliament (MP) for Yorkshire (1784–1812). After an evangelical conversion in 1785, he headed the parliamentary campaign against the British slave trade for 20 years, until the Slave Trade Act 1807. His causes also included British missionary work in India, the creation of a free colony in [[Sierra Leone]] and the foundation of the Church Mission Society. His conservatism led him to support repressive legislation, which drew criticism that he ignored injustices at home. He supported the campaign for complete abolition of slavery, which led to the Slavery Abolition Act 1833. He was buried in [[Westminster Abbey]].

=== KEY: William_Wordsworth_652349
HEADWORD: Wordsworth, William
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Alps | Lake District | Samuel Taylor Coleridge
ENTRY:
English Romantic poet who, with [[Samuel Taylor Coleridge]], helped launch the Romantic Age in English literature through their joint publication *Lyrical Ballads* (1798). His magnum opus is generally considered to be *The Prelude*, a semi-autobiographical poem of his early years that he revised and expanded a number of times; it was titled and published posthumously by his wife. Born in Cockermouth, Cumberland, he studied at St John's College, Cambridge. In 1790 he went on a walking tour of Europe, touring the [[Alps]] extensively and visiting France, Switzerland and Italy. He is associated with the [[Lake District]], whose landscapes his works celebrate. He was Poet Laureate from 1843 until his death.

=== KEY: William_Wyler_3b3b39
HEADWORD: Wyler, William
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Academy Awards | German Empire | United States
ENTRY:
German-born American film director and producer. Born in Mulhouse, Alsace-Lorraine, then part of the [[German Empire]], he emigrated to the [[United States]] in 1921, worked first for Universal Studios and by 1925 was its youngest director. He won the [[Academy Awards|Academy Award]] for Best Director three times, for *Mrs. Miniver* (1942), *The Best Years of Our Lives* (1946) and *Ben-Hur* (1959), all of which also won Best Picture. He received 12 nominations for Best Director, more than any other director. He directed a string of classic literary adaptations, among them *Wuthering Heights* (1939), which were box-office and critical successes, and he was known as an actors' director who often propelled performers to stardom.

=== KEY: William_of_Ockham_dc08e3
HEADWORD: Ockham, William of
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Logic | Munich | Occam's razor | Physics | Theology | University of Oxford
ENTRY:
English Franciscan friar, scholastic philosopher and theologian. Probably born in Ockham, Surrey, he joined the Franciscan Order at an early age and studied at the [[University of Oxford]] but never became a regent master. He is widely known for [[Occam's razor]], the methodological principle that bears his name, and he wrote significant works on [[Logic]], [[Physics]] and [[Theology]]. Summoned to the papal court at Avignon in 1324 to defend his commentary on Peter Lombard's *Sentences*, he became involved in the dispute over Apostolic poverty. He later fled Avignon, sought protection under Holy Roman Emperor Louis IV in [[Munich]], and wrote treatises denouncing the pope as heretical, which led to his excommunication.

=== KEY: William_the_Conqueror_d70612
HEADWORD: William the Conqueror
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Battle of Hastings | Domesday Book | Edward the Confessor | Norman Conquest | Normandy
ENTRY:
Norman king of England (r. 1066–1087) and duke of [[Normandy]] (from 1035). Illegitimate son of Duke Robert I of Normandy, he consolidated his power in Normandy by about 1060 and secured control of the county of Maine by 1062. Claiming the throne after the death of [[Edward the Confessor]] in January 1066, he invaded England and defeated Harold Godwinson at the [[Battle of Hastings]] on 14 October 1066. He suppressed subsequent English revolts in what became known as the [[Norman Conquest]]. His reign was marked by castle-building and the settlement of a new Norman nobility on English land. In 1086 he ordered the compilation of the [[Domesday Book]], a survey of English landholdings. He died while leading a campaign in northern France and was buried in Caen. His lands were divided: Normandy went to his son Robert and England to his second surviving son, William Rufus.

=== KEY: William_the_Silent_0e6385
HEADWORD: William the Silent
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Eighty Years' War | Netherlands | Protestantism
ENTRY:
Dutch nobleman, Prince of Orange and leader of the Dutch revolt against Spanish Habsburg rule, which set off the [[Eighty Years' War]] (1568–1648). He is the founder of the Orange-Nassau branch and ancestor of the monarchy of the [[Netherlands]]. Initially a servant of the Habsburgs at the court of Margaret of Parma, governor of the Spanish Netherlands, he turned against Spain over the centralization of power and the persecution of Dutch [[Protestantism|Protestants]]. The most influential and politically capable of the rebels, he led the Dutch to several successes against Spain. The Spanish king declared him an outlaw in 1580, and he was assassinated in Delft. He is widely considered a heroic figure and the principal founding father of the Netherlands, and is known as *Pater Patriae* (Father of the Fatherland).
