=== KEY: China_ae54a5
HEADWORD: China
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: 1911 Revolution | Beijing | Chinese Communist Party | Confucianism | Han dynasty | Shanghai | Taiwan | Xi Jinping
ENTRY:
Country (pop. exceeding 1.4 billion), East Asia, officially the People's Republic of China (PRC). Area 9.6 million sq km. The second most populous and third largest country, it is divided into 33 province-level divisions, including the special administrative regions of Hong Kong and Macau. [[Beijing]] is the capital and [[Shanghai]] the most populous city by urban area. Major rivers include the Yangtze and Yellow.

The name derives, through Portuguese, Malay, and Persian, from the Sanskrit *Cīna*; the Chinese name *Zhōngguó* means "central state." Humans first arrived in the Paleolithic era. Dynastic states emerged in the 2nd millennium BC. China was unified under the Qin in 221 BC, followed by the [[Han dynasty]], beginning two millennia of imperial rule. The imperial era saw the invention of gunpowder, paper, printing, and the compass. The empire was succeeded by the Republic of China after the [[1911 Revolution]]. In 1949 the [[Chinese Communist Party]] (CCP) proclaimed the PRC, and the Republic of China retreated to [[Taiwan]]; both sides still claim political legitimacy. Reform and opening-up from 1978 moved China toward a socialist market economy and spurred rapid growth.

China is a unitary state with the CCP as its sole ruling party, led by [[Xi Jinping]]. It is a permanent member of the UN Security Council, the world's largest manufacturer, and has the second-largest economy by nominal GDP. International organizations have alleged authoritarianism and human rights abuses.

China is the birthplace of [[Confucianism]] and Daoism, which, with Buddhism, form the traditional three teachings. World Heritage Sites include the Great Wall and the Grand Canal. The Han are the dominant ethnic group, and 55 minorities, including the Hui, Mongols, Tibetans, Uyghurs, and Zhuang, are officially recognized.

=== KEY: Chinchilla_98292e

ENTRY:
Genus of two species of crepuscular [[Rodent|rodents]] native to the [[Andes]] of [[South America]]. The short-tailed *Chinchilla chinchilla* and the long-tailed *C. lanigera* live in colonies called [[Herd|herds]] at elevations up to 4,270 m (14,000 ft). Chinchillas have around 20,000 hairs per square centimeter, the densest fur of any extant terrestrial mammal. After being hunted for their soft fur, they had become rare by the end of the 19th century. Wild colonies are now known only in [[Chile]]. Most chinchillas used by the [[Fur|fur]] industry are farm-raised, and domestic chinchillas descended from *C. lanigera* are kept as pets.

Sole governing party of the People's Republic of China. Founded in 1921, the party won the [[Chinese Civil War]] against the [[Kuomintang]] and proclaimed the People's Republic in October 1949 under the chairmanship of [[Mao Zedong]]. Mao remained its most influential member until 1976. [[Deng Xiaoping]] then directed the party toward reform and opening up in the 1980s. [[Xi Jinping]] has led the party since 2012. As of 2025, the party had more than 101 million members, the second-largest political party by membership in the world. === KEY: Chinese_New_Year_0979c8
HEADWORD: Chinese New Year
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Chinese calendar | Fireworks | Malaysia | Philippines | Singapore | Spring (season) | Thailand
ENTRY:
Traditional holiday in Chinese culture, also called the Spring Festival. It marks the start of the year on the lunisolar [[Chinese calendar]], falling on the new moon between January 21 and February 20. In more than 96 percent of years, it falls on the new moon closest to the beginning of [[Spring (season)|spring]]. The celebration runs from New Year's Eve to the Lantern Festival on the 15th day. Families hold a reunion dinner, clean their homes to sweep away ill fortune, display red paper-cuts and couplets, set off [[Fireworks|firecrackers]], and give money in red envelopes. The holiday is also widely observed in [[Singapore]], [[Malaysia]], the [[Philippines]], and [[Thailand]], and it has influenced Lunar New Year celebrations in Korea, Vietnam, and Tibet.

=== KEY: Chinese_architecture_51f5dd
HEADWORD: Chinese architecture
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: East Asia | Forbidden City | Pagoda | Palace | Tang dynasty
ENTRY:
Architectural style of China, influential across [[East Asia]]. Chinese architecture uses a timber-frame system whose structural principles have remained largely unchanged since the early ancient era, while decorative details have varied. Buildings are typically bilaterally symmetrical, arranged around enclosed courtyards, and oriented horizontally. Their form varies by rank and purpose, from [[Palace|palaces]] such as the [[Forbidden City]] to [[Pagoda|pagodas]]. Starting with the [[Tang dynasty]], the style strongly influenced architecture in Japan, Korea, Vietnam, and Mongolia. Since the 20th century, urban development has favored high-rises over traditional buildings, which are normally less than three levels, while traditional carpentry and masonry continue in rural vernacular architecture.

=== KEY: Chinese_art_c5a4f0
HEADWORD: Chinese art
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Architecture | Calligraphy | Cultural Revolution | Garden | Porcelain | Sculpture
ENTRY:
Visual art originating in China, Greater China, or practiced by Chinese artists. Major forms include [[Calligraphy|calligraphy]], painting, [[Sculpture|sculpture]], [[Architecture|architecture]], [[Garden|garden]] design, [[Porcelain|porcelain]], bronze work, lacquerware, and textiles. Chinese art strongly influenced the arts of Korea, Japan, and Vietnam, and imported porcelain, lacquer, and silk prompted imitations in Europe. Much of the finest work, especially ceramics, was produced in imperial and large workshop factories by largely unknown artists. Ink wash painting, practiced mainly by scholar-officials, developed aesthetic values similar to those of the West. After 1949, Socialist Realism became the dominant officially sanctioned style, and the [[Cultural Revolution]] (1966–1976) saw much of the artistic heritage suppressed or destroyed. Since the 1980s, traditional forms have been revived and avant-garde movements have emerged.

=== KEY: Chinese_calendar_f133da
HEADWORD: Chinese calendar
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Beijing | Chinese New Year | Chinese zodiac | Gregorian calendar | Nanjing | Shang dynasty
ENTRY:
Lunisolar calendar used by the Chinese people. Its twelve months are aligned with the phases of the moon, with an intercalary month added as needed to keep the calendar in step with the seasons, and each month has 29 or 30 days. It also has 24 solar terms, and the winter solstice must occur in the eleventh month. A total of 102 calendars have been officially recorded in classical texts, and results vary by location, so calculations for [[Beijing]] and [[Nanjing]] can differ. Originating in the [[Shang dynasty]] as the Yin calendar, it still fixes the date of [[Chinese New Year]] and is linked to the [[Chinese zodiac]]. Modern China officially uses the [[Gregorian calendar]].

=== KEY: Chinese_characters_cabb17
HEADWORD: Chinese characters
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Han dynasty | Henan | Kanji | Shang dynasty | Unicode
ENTRY:
Logographic writing system used for the Chinese languages and, historically, for others influenced by Chinese culture. Chinese characters generally represent morphemes, units of meaning, rather than sounds. They are one of four independently invented writing systems accepted by scholars, and the only one still in continuous use. The earliest attested characters are oracle bone inscriptions from the 13th century BCE, made for divinations by the [[Shang dynasty]] in present-day Anyang, [[Henan]]. Clerical script, matured by the [[Han dynasty]], abstracted the forms of characters. Writing everyday vocabulary requires roughly 2,000 to 3,000 characters. Simplified characters are used in mainland China, Singapore, and Malaysia, and traditional characters in Taiwan, Hong Kong, and Macau. In Japanese they are called [[Kanji|kanji]], and more than 100,000 characters have been included in the [[Unicode]] Standard.

=== KEY: Chinese_cuisine_0c1c3a
HEADWORD: Chinese cuisine
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Song dynasty | Soy sauce | Tofu | Traditional Chinese medicine | Zhejiang
ENTRY:
Culinary traditions originating in China and among Chinese communities abroad. Cuisine varies with geography, from the tropical south to the subarctic northeast, and with social class and religion. Northern dishes rely on wheat, millet, noodles, and bread, while rice was cultivated in the south. Restaurants in the modern sense emerged in [[Song dynasty]] China in the 11th and 12th centuries. A 1980 grouping of the Eight Cuisines of China named Anhui, Guangdong, Fujian, Hunan, Jiangsu, Shandong, Sichuan, and [[Zhejiang]]. Chinese food is intertwined with [[Traditional Chinese medicine|traditional Chinese medicine]], and staples such as [[Soy sauce|soy sauce]], [[Tofu|tofu]], and tea are now found worldwide. Cooks judge a dish by its ingredients, knife work, cooking time, and seasoning.

=== KEY: Chinese_folk_religion_3ef91a
HEADWORD: Chinese folk religion
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Chinese mythology | Han Chinese | Qi | Qing dynasty | Song dynasty | Taoism | Traditional Chinese medicine | Yin and yang
ENTRY:
Range of traditional religious practices of the [[Han Chinese]], including the Chinese diaspora. It includes veneration of *shen* ("spirits") and ancestors and worship of deities and immortals, who may be deities of places or natural phenomena, of human behavior, or progenitors of family lineages. Stories about these gods form a loose canon of [[Chinese mythology]]. By the [[Song dynasty]] (960–1279), these practices had blended with Buddhist, Confucian, and [[Taoism|Taoist]] teachings into the popular religious system that has lasted in many ways to the present.

The tradition is diverse, but a common core can be summarized as four concepts: *Tian*, the transcendent source of moral meaning; [[Qi|*qi*]], the breath or energy that animates the universe; ancestor veneration; and *bao ying*, moral reciprocity. [[Yin and yang]] describes the order of the universe, with *yang* usually preferred over *yin* in common religion. Feng shui, acupuncture, and [[Traditional Chinese medicine|traditional Chinese medicine]] reflect this worldview.

The Chinese language historically had no overarching term for "religion"; the modern word *zongjiao* developed around 1900.

The Chinese government formally recognizes five religions: Buddhism, Taoism, Catholic Christianity, Protestant Christianity, and Islam. It does not classify folk religion as a religion, because of its ambiguous boundaries and poorly defined structure, and treats it as part of cultural heritage. After the fall of the [[Qing dynasty]] in 1911, governments and modernizing elites condemned "feudal superstition" and opposed practices they believed conflicted with modern values. By the late 20th century these attitudes began to change in both mainland China and Taiwan, and many scholars now view folk religion positively. Revival has included Yellow Emperor worship and other local cults. The government generally tolerates popular religious movements but has suppressed or persecuted large organizations it fears would undermine social stability.

=== KEY: Chinese_language_0ad2b7
HEADWORD: Chinese language
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Beijing | Cantonese | Chinese characters | Shang dynasty | Singapore | Sino-Tibetan languages | Taiwan | United Nations
ENTRY:
Group of Sinitic language varieties natively spoken by the Han Chinese and some ethnic minorities in Greater China, as well as by overseas Chinese communities. Approximately 1.39 billion people, or 17% of the global population, speak one of the varieties as a first language. Together they form the largest branch of the [[Sino-Tibetan languages]], a family that also includes Burmese and Tibetan. The Chinese government defines all spoken varieties as dialects of a single language, but the frequent lack of mutual intelligibility, especially outside the northern varieties, has led linguists to treat them as separate languages. Most classifications posit 7 to 13 main regional groups. Mandarin is by far the most spoken, with 66%, or around 800 million speakers, followed by Min (75 million), Wu (74 million), and Yue (68 million), which includes [[Cantonese]]. All varieties are tonal at least to some degree and are largely analytic.

Chinese is written with logographic [[Chinese characters]]. The earliest attested written Chinese consists of oracle bone inscriptions created during the [[Shang dynasty]] c. 1250 BC. Standard Chinese, based on the [[Beijing]] dialect and first officially adopted in the 1930s, is the official language of both the People's Republic of China and the Republic of China ([[Taiwan]]), one of the four official languages of [[Singapore]], and one of the six official languages of the [[United Nations]]. It is written mainly in modern written vernacular Chinese, which educated readers share even when their spoken varieties are mutually unintelligible. The government of the People's Republic has promoted simplified characters since the 1950s, and Singapore adopted them officially in 1976. Traditional characters remain in use in Taiwan, Hong Kong, Macau, and among other overseas Chinese communities.

=== KEY: Chinese_literature_74845c
HEADWORD: Chinese literature
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Classical Chinese | Du Fu | Great Leap Forward | Li Bai | Lu Xun | Tang dynasty
ENTRY:
One of the world's oldest and most extensive literary traditions. It begins with the earliest recorded inscriptions and court archives and includes major works of philosophy and history from the Axial Age, mostly written in [[Classical Chinese]]. The Confucian Four Books and Five Classics became the basis of the imperial examinations in the 12th century. The [[Tang dynasty|Han and Tang dynasties]] were golden ages of poetry, with [[Li Bai]] and [[Du Fu]] among the best known. Ming and Qing writers produced mature novels such as *Dream of the Red Chamber*, written in vernacular Chinese. The early 20th-century vernacular movement made [[Lu Xun]] an influential voice. In the People's Republic era, literature has operated under state oversight, and critics argue that censorship has constrained writers and limited depictions of events such as the [[Great Leap Forward]] famines.

=== KEY: Chinese_martial_arts_be94c3
HEADWORD: Chinese martial arts
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Qi | Yellow Emperor
ENTRY:
Fighting styles developed over centuries in China, commonly called kung fu or wushu. Styles are classified as internal (*neijia*), focusing on [[Qi|qi]] manipulation, or external (*waijia*), building muscle and cardiovascular fitness; northern and southern traditions form another classification. Legend credits the [[Yellow Emperor]] with the earliest fighting systems, and the *Spring and Autumn Annals* (5th century BC) mention a hand-to-hand combat theory. Since the 1950s the People's Republic has organized the art as an exhibition and full-contact sport called wushu. Debate continues over combat effectiveness: some studies report benefits such as improved balance, while critics note that traditional forms have shown little advantage in mixed-rules fights.

=== KEY: Chinese_mythology_c5a1ed
HEADWORD: Chinese mythology
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Chinese folk religion | Qi | Taoism | Yellow Emperor
ENTRY:
Body of myths passed down orally and in literature throughout Greater China. Creation myths include Pangu creating the world from his body and Nuwa mending the sky. Culture heroes are credited with teaching skills ranging from building houses to writing, and some are revered as ancestors of ethnic groups or dynasties, such as the [[Yellow Emperor]]. Like many mythologies, it was once regarded at least partly as a factual record of the past. Mythology forms part of [[Chinese folk religion]] and of religious [[Taoism]], and it is intertwined with the concepts of li and [[Qi|qi]]. Literary tales include *Journey to the West*.

=== KEY: Chinese_philosophy_57a53f
HEADWORD: Chinese philosophy
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Buddhism | Confucianism | Confucius | Laozi | Legalism (Chinese philosophy) | Mencius | Taoism | Zhu Xi
ENTRY:
Philosophical traditions of China, spanning more than two millennia. Philosophy began in the Spring and Autumn and Warring States periods, known as the "Hundred Schools of Thought", when [[Confucius]], [[Mencius]], and [[Laozi]] laid foundations for [[Confucianism]], [[Taoism]], Mohism, and [[Legalism (Chinese philosophy)|Legalism]]. Confucianism became the official ideology under the Han dynasty. Later, Indian [[Buddhism]] influenced Chinese thought, and [[Zhu Xi]] helped systematize Neo-Confucianism in the Song and Ming dynasties. In the modern era, Chinese thinkers engaged with Western thought, producing Chinese Marxism and New Confucianism. Confucianism emphasizes ethical cultivation and political responsibility, while Daoism advocates living in accordance with nature.

ENTRY:
Scheme assigning an animal to each year in a repeating twelve-year cycle. The animals are the Rat, Ox, Tiger, Rabbit, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog, and Pig. The system was standardized in the [[Han dynasty]], when it was combined with ten Heavenly Stems to form a 60-year cycle. According to legend, the [[Jade Emperor]] held a race to choose the animals. Nigerian novelist, poet, and critic regarded as a central figure of modern African literature. Born in Ogidi in colonial Nigeria, he drew on [[Igbo people|Igbo]] traditions and Christian influences, and defended writing in English as a means to reach a broad audience. His first novel, *Things Fall Apart* (1958), is the most widely studied and translated African novel. With *No Longer at Ease* (1960) and *Arrow of God* (1964) it forms the "African Trilogy". He supported Biafran independence during the [[Nigerian Civil War]] and later taught in the United States, at Bard College and [[Brown University]]. He rejected the label "father of modern African literature".

=== KEY: Chitin_fe09dc
HEADWORD: Chitin

ENTRY:
Long-chain [[Polymer|polymer]] of N-acetylglucosamine, an amide derivative of glucose. Chitin is the second most abundant polysaccharide in nature, behind only [[Cellulose|cellulose]]; an estimated 1 billion tons are produced each year. It is a primary component of [[Cell wall|cell walls]] in fungi and of the exoskeletons of [[Arthropod|arthropods]] such as [[Crustacean|crustaceans]] and [[Insect|insects]]. Commercially it is extracted from the shells of crabs, shrimps and lobsters, which are by-products of the seafood industry. In pure form chitin is translucent, pliable and tough; combined with [[Calcium carbonate|calcium carbonate]] in crustacean and mollusc shells, it forms a much harder composite. Chitosan, produced by deacetylating chitin with [[Sodium hydroxide|sodium hydroxide]], has biomedical uses including wound healing and drug delivery.

=== KEY: Chivalry_1392cb
HEADWORD: Chivalry
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Carolingian Empire | Cavalry | Charlemagne | Crusades | Francia | King Arthur | Knight
ENTRY:
Informal and varying code of conduct associated with medieval European [[Knight|knighthood]]. It developed in France between 1170 and 1220, combining military bravery, individual training and service to others with knightly piety and courtly manners. Its roots lie in the idealization of the [[Cavalry|cavalryman]] in the [[Carolingian Empire]], especially in [[Francia]]. It was popularized in medieval literature, notably the legends of [[King Arthur]] and the paladins of [[Charlemagne]]. The code was largely a product of the Late Middle Ages, evolving after the [[Crusades]] from an idealization of knights fighting in the Holy Land and from ideals of courtly love. The word derives from the Old French *chevalerie*, meaning horse soldiery.

Common [[Sexually transmitted infection|sexually transmitted infection]] caused by the bacterium *Chlamydia trachomatis*. Most infected people have no symptoms. When they occur, symptoms in women may include vaginal discharge or burning with urination, and in men discharge from the penis or pain and swelling of the testicles. Chlamydia is cured with [[Antibiotic|antibiotics]], typically [[Azithromycin|azithromycin]] or [[Doxycycline|doxycycline]], and is prevented by consistent [[Condom|condom]] use. It affects about 4.2% of women and 2.7% of men worldwide.

=== KEY: Chloride_214dea
HEADWORD: Chloride
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Chlorine | Sodium chloride
ENTRY:
Anion of [[Chlorine|chlorine]] (Cl⁻) and the main anion present in seawater. Chloride salts such as [[Sodium chloride|sodium chloride]] are often soluble in water. Chloride is an essential electrolyte in all body fluids, helping maintain acid–base balance, transmit nerve impulses and regulate fluid flow in and out of cells. Seawater contains about 19,400 mg/L of chloride, while chloride minerals such as halite are abundant mainly in dry climates or deep underground. Chloride ions can be detected with silver nitrate, which forms a white silver chloride precipitate. Chloride can be oxidized, for example to chlorine gas in the chlor-alkali process, but it cannot be reduced.

=== KEY: Chlorine_dca055
HEADWORD: Chlorine
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Chemical weapon | Chloride | Electrolysis | Fluorine | Halogen | Humphry Davy | Polyvinyl chloride | World War I
ENTRY:
Chemical element with atomic number 17 and symbol Cl, a yellow-green gas at room temperature. The second-lightest [[Halogen|halogen]], it lies between [[Fluorine|fluorine]] and bromine in the periodic table. Chlorine is extremely reactive and a strong oxidizing agent, with the highest electron affinity of any element. Free chlorine gas was recognized as a separate substance around 1630, and [[Humphry Davy|Humphry Davy]] confirmed it as an element in 1810. It is produced from brine by [[Electrolysis|electrolysis]], mainly in the chloralkali process. It is used to make a wide range of consumer products, about two-thirds of them organic chemicals such as [[Polyvinyl chloride|polyvinyl chloride]], and as a disinfectant in swimming pools. Chlorine was first used as a [[Chemical weapon|chemical weapon]] in [[World War I|World War I]]. In the form of [[Chloride|chloride ions]], it is necessary to all known species of life.

Organelle, a type of [[Plastid|plastid]], that conducts photosynthesis mostly in plant and algal cells. The chemical energy is used in the Calvin cycle to make sugar and other organic molecules from [[Carbon dioxide|carbon dioxide]]. Chloroplasts also carry out fatty acid and amino acid synthesis. === KEY: Christopher_Columbus_4c2ec8
HEADWORD: Columbus, Christopher
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Americas | Columbian exchange | Ferdinand II of Aragon | Hispaniola | Isabella I of Castile | Lisbon | Republic of Genoa | The Bahamas
ENTRY:
Italian explorer and navigator from the [[Republic of Genoa]]. He completed four transatlantic voyages in the name of the Spanish Catholic Monarchs, opening the way for widespread European exploration and colonization of the [[Americas]]; his expeditions were the first known European contact with the Caribbean and Central and South America.

Columbus went to sea young, traveled widely, and was based for several years in [[Lisbon]]. He planned a western sea passage to the East Indies, hoping to profit from the spice trade. After the Granada War and his persistent lobbying, Queen [[Isabella I of Castile|Isabella I]] and King [[Ferdinand II of Aragon|Ferdinand II]] agreed to sponsor him. He left Palos in August 1492 with three ships and made landfall on 12 October on an island in [[The Bahamas]]. He then visited Cuba and [[Hispaniola]], where he founded the short-lived fort of La Navidad. Further voyages explored the Lesser Antilles in 1493, Trinidad and the northern coast of South America in 1498, and the east coast of Central America in 1502. He mistakenly called the people he met *indios*, believing he had reached the East Indies, and never clearly renounced the belief that he had reached the Far East.

As colonial governor, Columbus was accused by some contemporaries of significant brutality; he was arrested and removed from Hispaniola in 1500 and later fought litigation over the privileges he claimed from the Crown. His voyages began the transfer of plants, animals, diseases, people, and ideas between the Old and New Worlds known as the [[Columbian exchange]]. Diseases, warfare, enslavement, and other mistreatment contributed to the decline of Hispaniola's American Indians. Growing awareness of these actions has brought increased scrutiny of a figure long idealized as a heroic discoverer.

Set of [[Laboratory|laboratory]] techniques for separating [[Mixture|mixtures]] into their components.
