ENTRY:
Holiday on the day before Ash Wednesday, the last day of [[Carnival]]. Mardi Gras is French for "Fat Tuesday," marking the final day of eating rich foods before the Lenten fast. Also called Shrove Tuesday, it is known in the [[United Kingdom]] as Pancake Day, when pancakes use up eggs, milk and butter before Lent. In [[New Orleans]], [[Louisiana]], the Carnival season begins on January 6 and peaks on Fat Tuesday with parades, masked balls and king cake.

=== KEY: Margaret_Atwood_9a6370
HEADWORD: Atwood, Margaret
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Booker Prize | Climate change | Genetic engineering | Myth | Religion | Toronto
ENTRY:
Canadian novelist, poet and literary critic. Since 1961 she has published 18 books of poetry, 18 novels, 11 books of nonfiction and nine collections of short fiction. Her best-known work is the dystopian novel *The Handmaid's Tale* (1985), set in a theocratic regime that subjugates women. Her writing addresses gender, Canadian identity, [[Religion|religion]] and [[Myth|myth]], the power of language, [[Climate change|climate change]] and [[Genetic engineering|genetic engineering]]. She has won two [[Booker Prize|Booker Prizes]] and numerous other awards, and many of her works have been adapted for film and television. Atwood co-founded the Griffin Poetry Prize and the Writers' Trust of Canada, and she is a senior fellow of Massey College in [[Toronto]]. She also invented the LongPen, a device for remote robotic writing of documents.

=== KEY: Margaret_Court_fd15f6
HEADWORD: Court, Margaret
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Grand Slam (tennis) | Same-sex marriage
ENTRY:
Australian former tennis player and Pentecostal minister. Court won 24 women's singles major titles, the most in women's tennis history, and 64 major titles in all, including 19 women's doubles and 21 mixed doubles titles. She won the first of seven consecutive Australian Open singles titles in 1960 and completed the singles [[Grand Slam (tennis)|Grand Slam]] in 1970 by winning all four major titles. She retired in 1977 after taking breaks to have children. Court became associated with Pentecostalism in the 1970s, became a Christian minister in 1991 and founded Margaret Court Ministries. She has argued that children benefit from both maternal and paternal influences and has opposed [[Same-sex marriage|same-sex marriage]]. Her views have made her a polarizing figure in Australia, and proposals to rename the Margaret Court Arena have been debated.

=== KEY: Margaret_I_of_Denmark_5e8261
HEADWORD: Margaret I
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Hanseatic League | Scandinavia
ENTRY:
Queen regnant of Denmark, Norway and Sweden from the late 1380s until her death, and founder of the Kalmar Union, which joined the [[Scandinavia|Scandinavian]] kingdoms for over a century. The youngest daughter of Valdemar IV of Denmark, she married Haakon VI in 1363 at age ten and was queen consort of Norway until 1380. After the deaths of her husband and son, she was proclaimed queen of the Scandinavian kingdoms. She intended to unite Scandinavia against the power of the [[Hanseatic League]]. She was succeeded by her grandnephew, Erik of Pomerania, who came of age in 1401, but she remained sole ruler in all but name until her death, and her subjects called her "Lady King." Some Norwegian and Swedish historians criticize her for favoring Denmark and for autocratic rule. She is generally thought to have been highly regarded in Norway and respected in Denmark and Sweden.

=== KEY: Margaret_Mead_77a3b8
HEADWORD: Mead, Margaret
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: American Museum of Natural History | Columbia University | Franz Boas | Papua New Guinea | Sexual revolution
ENTRY:
American cultural anthropologist, author and speaker who became prominent in the mass media. Her first ethnography, *Coming of Age in Samoa* (1928), addressed adolescence and sexuality and catapulted her to national visibility. Her book *Sex and Temperament in Three Primitive Societies* (1935) examined gender roles based on fieldwork in [[Papua New Guinea]]. Mead studied with [[Franz Boas]] at [[Columbia University]] and was curator of ethnology at the [[American Museum of Natural History]] from 1946 to 1969. Her reports on sexual attitudes in Pacific and Southeast Asian cultures influenced the [[Sexual revolution]] of the 1960s, and her association with cultural relativism drew sharp criticism from conservatives. Anthropologist Derek Freeman later challenged her Samoan findings, while others defended her framework. Mead served as president of the American Association for the Advancement of Science in 1975.

=== KEY: Margaret_Sanger_c82f08
HEADWORD: Sanger, Margaret
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Birth control | Eugenics
ENTRY:
American birth control activist, sex educator, writer and nurse. Sanger opened the first birth control clinic in the United States and founded Planned Parenthood. Working as a nurse in New York City's slums, she campaigned to legalize [[Birth control|contraception]] on the ground that women should decide if and when to have children. She was arrested eight times for distributing information about contraceptives. Court rulings that followed her activism legalized birth control, including *Griswold v. Connecticut*, which legalized contraception without a prescription for couples nationwide. As an adherent of [[Eugenics|eugenics]], she argued that birth control would reduce the number of unfit people; her eugenics views remain controversial. Sanger discouraged abortion, and her clinics never offered abortion services during her lifetime. In the early 1950s she persuaded philanthropists to fund biologist Gregory Pincus's development of the first birth control pill.

=== KEY: Margaret_Thatcher_64bdfa
HEADWORD: Thatcher, Margaret
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Conservative Party (UK) | Falklands War | House of Lords | John Major
ENTRY:
British Conservative politician and prime minister of the United Kingdom from 1979 to 1990. She was the first woman to hold the office and the longest-serving British prime minister of the 20th century. As leader of the [[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservative Party]] from 1975 to 1990, she pursued policies known as Thatcherism, including privatization and curbs on trade unions. Victory in the [[Falklands War]] in 1982 and an economic recovery brought a landslide in 1983 after early unpopularity. Her support for the Community Charge, or poll tax, was widely unpopular, and her Eurosceptic views were not shared by others in her cabinet. She resigned in 1990 after a leadership challenge and was succeeded by [[John Major]]. After leaving the House of Commons in 1992, she was given a life peerage and sat in the [[House of Lords]]. A polarizing figure, she is nonetheless viewed favorably in historical rankings of British prime ministers.

=== KEY: Margarine_0915d6
HEADWORD: Margarine
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Napoleon III | Unilever
ENTRY:
Spread, usually made from vegetable oil, used as a substitute for butter. Margarine is a water-in-fat emulsion used for flavoring, baking and cooking. The name combines Latin *oleum* ("olive oil") and Greek *margarite* ("pearl"). Responding to a challenge from [[Napoleon III]], Hippolyte Mège-Mouriès invented margarine in 1869 from beef tallow. He sold the patent in 1871 to the Dutch company Jurgens, later part of [[Unilever]]. Dairy interests opposed margarine, and the 1886 Margarine Act imposed prohibitive taxes in the United States. Hydrogenated vegetable oils became the main ingredient after shortages of beef fat, and by 1950 U.S. manufacturers had switched almost entirely to vegetable oils. Partially hydrogenated oils, which produce trans fats linked to cardiovascular disease, have since been restricted in many countries.

Canadian-American actress and activist. Born in Yellowknife, Northwest Territories, Kidder is best known for playing Lois Lane in the *Superman* films (1978–1987) opposite [[Christopher Reeve]]. She won the Saturn Award for Best Actress for *Superman* and also starred in the horror films *Black Christmas* (1974) and *The Amityville Horror* (1979). A manic episode in 1996 stemmed from [[Bipolar disorder|bipolar disorder]], and she continued in independent films and television afterward. She was an outspoken political, environmental and antiwar activist.
