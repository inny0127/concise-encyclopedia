ENTRY:
[[Indo-Aryan languages|Indo-Aryan]] language of India, spoken mainly in the state of [[Maharashtra]]. Derived from Maharashtri Prakrit, it is the official language of Maharashtra and an additional official language in [[Goa]]. It is one of the 22 scheduled languages of India, with 83 million speakers as of the 2011 census. Marathi ranks third among Indian languages by number of native speakers, after Hindustani (Hindi-Urdu) and Bengali, and 15th in the world. It is written in [[Devanagari]] and has some of the oldest literature of any modern Indian language. It was probably first attested in a 739 CE copper-plate inscription from Satara. The [[Government of India|Government of India]] designated it a classical language in October 2024. Marathi has three genders: masculine, feminine and neuter.

=== KEY: Marathon_0c7320
HEADWORD: Marathon
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Athens | Battle of Marathon | Herodotus | Olympic Games | Sparta
ENTRY:
Long-distance foot race of 42.195 kilometers (c. 26.22 mi), usually run as a road race, though the distance can also be covered on trails. It was one of the original events of the modern [[Olympic Games]], first held in 1896 in [[Athens]], and the distance was not standardized until 1921. It is included in the World Athletics Championships, which began in 1983, and is the only running road race in both championships. More than 800 marathons are held worldwide each year, mostly by recreational athletes. The name derives from a legend of the Greek messenger Pheidippides, said to have run from the [[Battle of Marathon|Battle of Marathon]] to Athens in 490 BC to announce victory, then died. Historians dispute the account, since the earliest source, [[Herodotus]], describes a run from Athens to [[Sparta]] and makes no mention of this one.

=== KEY: Marble_7fb268
HEADWORD: Marble
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Acid rain | Calcite | Chert | Limestone | Metamorphic rock | Quarry
ENTRY:
[[Metamorphic rock]] of recrystallized carbonate minerals, most commonly [[Calcite|calcite]] or dolomite. It forms when [[Limestone|limestone]] or dolomite is subjected to heat and pressure, and it has a crystalline texture and is typically not layered. Pure white marble results from very clean limestone or dolomite; its characteristic veins and swirls come from impurities such as clay, sand, iron oxides or [[Chert|chert]] in the original rock. Its hardness, durability and polishable surface make it valued in sculpture and architecture, and sculptors prize its uniform grain for fine detail. Marble is extracted by [[Quarry|quarrying]]. Production is dominated by China, Italy, India and Spain, which together account for almost half of world production of marble and decorative stone. Outdoor marble is damaged by [[Acid rain|acid rain]].

=== KEY: Marble_toy_9c5047
HEADWORD: Marble
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Agate | Mohenjo-daro | Nuremberg
ENTRY:
Small spherical toy, often made of glass, clay, steel, plastic or [[Agate|agate]]. Marbles are usually 0.5 to 3.5 cm (0.2 to 1.4 in) in diameter and are used in games, in marble runs and races, or as art objects. Collectors value them for their colors. Small stone balls from about 2500 BCE, found near [[Mohenjo-daro]] in the Indus Valley Civilization, are identified as marbles. Many examples come from sites associated with Mesopotamia and ancient Egypt. Marbles arrived in Britain from the Low Countries in the medieval era. In 1503 the town council of [[Nuremberg]] limited marble games to a meadow outside town, and the name marble, for balls once made of polished stone, dates from this era. Ceramic marbles entered inexpensive mass production in the 1870s. The British and World Marbles Championship has been held at Tinsley Green, West Sussex, every year since 1932.

=== KEY: Marc_Chagall_e57235
HEADWORD: Chagall, Marc
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Belarus | Cubism | New York City | World War II
ENTRY:
Russian and French artist of Jewish ancestry and an early modernist. Born into a Jewish family near Vitebsk, in what is now [[Belarus]], he worked in painting, stained glass, stage sets, ceramics and tapestries. His style blended [[Cubism|Cubism]], Symbolism and Fauvism with Eastern European and Jewish folklore. He left for Paris in 1923 and, during [[World War II|World War II]], escaped occupied France to the United States, where he lived in [[New York City]] for seven years before returning to France in 1948. His commissions include stained-glass windows for the cathedrals of Reims and Metz, windows for the United Nations, and part of the ceiling of the Paris Opéra. Art critic Robert Hughes called him the quintessential Jewish artist of the twentieth century.

=== KEY: Marcel_Duchamp_fc2ca0

PERSON: yes

ENTRY:
French-American artist, chess player and inventor, a key figure in the avant-garde. He is best known for [[Fountain (Duchamp)|Fountain]] (1917), an anti-art work in which he signed the name R. Mutt 1917 on a urinal. Duchamp spent the last 25 years of his life in New York City. Commonly regarded, along with [[Pablo Picasso]] and Georges Braque, as one of three artists who established the post-industrial perspective in art history, he is considered the progenitor of [[Conceptual art|conceptual art]]. His definition of the art coefficient influenced appropriation artists such as [[Andy Warhol]]. During his military service from 1905 he learned typography and printing processes, skills he later used in his work.

=== KEY: Marcel_Marceau_0d9079
HEADWORD: Marceau, Marcel
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Auschwitz concentration camp | Charlie Chaplin | French Revolution | Mime artist
ENTRY:
French [[Mime artist|mime artist]] and actor, most famous for his stage persona, Bip the Clown. He called mime the art of silence and performed professionally worldwide for more than 60 years. Born Marcel Mangel in Strasbourg to a Jewish family, he took the surname Marceau during the German occupation of France, in tribute to a general of the [[French Revolution]]. During World War II he and his brother joined the French Resistance in Limoges and helped rescue Jewish children. His father was captured by the Gestapo and killed at [[Auschwitz concentration camp|Auschwitz]]. Marceau created Bip in 1947, a clown in a striped pullover and battered opera hat whose misadventures made him his alter ego, as the Little Tramp was for [[Charlie Chaplin]].
