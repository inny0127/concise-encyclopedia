Film, literary, and media franchise centered on the British Secret Service agent [[James Bond (literary character)|James Bond]], code-named 007. Ian Fleming created the character in 1953, and other authors have written authorized Bond novels since Fleming's death. The Eon Productions film series began in 1962 with *Dr. No*, starring [[Sean Connery]], and had twenty-five films as of 2021. Its films have grossed over US$7.04 billion at the box office, making it the fifth-highest-grossing film series to date. Recurring features include the theme songs, Bond's cars, guns, and gadgets from Q Branch, and his relationships with women, known as "Bond girls." Three theme songs have received [[Academy Awards]].

Fictional British Secret Service officer, code-named 007. [[Ian Fleming]] created the character in 1953 and wrote twelve Bond novels and two short-story collections. Other authors later wrote Bond novels. Bond was a composite of commandos Fleming knew in the Naval Intelligence Division during the Second World War, to whom Fleming added his own style and tastes. His traits include a love of cars, food, drink, and sex, and an average intake of sixty custom-made cigarettes a day. His name may come from the American ornithologist James Bond, though Fleming may instead have taken it from a Welsh agent he served with.

American singer, songwriter, and record producer known as the "Godfather of Soul." Brown began as a gospel singer in Toccoa, [[Georgia (U.S. state)|Georgia]], and rose to prominence in the mid-1950s as lead singer of the [[Rhythm and blues|rhythm and blues]] group the Famous Flames. His late-1960s music emphasized stripped-down, interlocking rhythms that influenced the development of [[Funk|funk]], which he fully established in the early 1970s with records such as "Get Up (I Feel Like Being a) Sex Machine." He was also noted for songs of social commentary, including "Say It Loud – I'm Black and I'm Proud" (1968). He recorded 17 singles that reached No. 1 on the Billboard R&B charts and was inducted into the [[Rock and Roll Hall of Fame]] in 1986. His music has been heavily sampled by [[Hip-hop|hip-hop]] musicians. He continued to perform until his death from pneumonia.

PERSON: yes

ENTRY:
15th president of the United States (1857–61). A Democrat from Pennsylvania, Buchanan upheld a pro-slavery interpretation of the Constitution. Before taking office, he sought to secure the pro-slavery ruling in Dred Scott v. Sandford, and he supported admitting Kansas as a slave state under the Lecompton Constitution. In his last months in office, seven Southern states seceded, and on April 12, 1861, Confederate forces attacked Fort Sumter, starting the [[American Civil War]]. Critics in the North and South faulted his limited response to the secession crisis. Historians and scholars rank him among the worst presidents in American history.

American actor. He rose to prominence as Sonny Corleone in *The Godfather* (1972), earning Academy Award and Golden Globe nominations for Best Supporting Actor. He received a star on the [[Hollywood Walk of Fame]] in 1978. Earlier roles included films by [[Howard Hawks]] and [[Francis Ford Coppola]], and his portrayal of Brian Piccolo in the television movie *Brian's Song* (1971) earned a Primetime Emmy Award nomination. After a five-year break from acting, he returned in films including *Misery* (1990), *Eraser* (1996), and *Elf* (2003).
