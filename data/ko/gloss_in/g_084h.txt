=== KEY: Marcel_Mauss_ded528
HEADWORD: Mauss, Marcel
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Claude Lévi-Strauss | Sacrifice | Émile Durkheim
ENTRY:
French sociologist and anthropologist, known as the father of French ethnology. A nephew of [[Émile Durkheim]], he crossed the boundaries between sociology and anthropology. He is best known for his analyses of magic, [[Sacrifice|sacrifice]] and gift exchange in *The Gift* (1925), and he had a significant influence on [[Claude Lévi-Strauss]], the founder of structural anthropology. He studied linguistics, Sanskrit and the history of religions, and passed the agrégation in 1893. In 1901 he was appointed to the chair of the history of religions of non-civilized peoples at the École pratique des hautes études. He founded research institutions, including the Institut d'Ethnologie in 1926, which stimulated fieldwork-based anthropology among young academics.

=== KEY: Marcel_Proust_071cb8
HEADWORD: Proust, Marcel
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Asthma | Bourgeoisie | Cholera | Paris | Pneumonia
ENTRY:
French novelist, literary critic and essayist, best known for *In Search of Lost Time*. Published in seven volumes between 1913 and 1927, the novel totals around 1.25 million words, and critics and writers consider it one of the most influential works of the twentieth century. Its themes include desire, artistic creativity, sexuality and class, and it helped pioneer the stream of consciousness technique. Proust was born in [[Paris]] to a wealthy [[Bourgeoisie|bourgeois]] family; his father, Adrien Proust, was a prominent pathologist who studied [[Cholera|cholera]]. Severe [[Asthma|asthma]] attacks from childhood disrupted his education, and illness confined him during the last three years of his life. He died of [[Pneumonia|pneumonia]] in 1922, and his brother Robert published the remaining volumes posthumously.

=== KEY: Marcello_Mastroianni_700a08
HEADWORD: Mastroianni, Marcello
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Federico Fellini | Sophia Loren
ENTRY:
Italian actor, regarded as one of Italy's most iconic male performers of the 20th century. He appeared in 147 films between 1939 and 1996 and played leading roles for many of the country's top directors. He became an international celebrity through his films with [[Federico Fellini]], including *La Dolce Vita* (1960) and *8½* (1963). He formed a notable on-screen partnership with [[Sophia Loren]], co-starring with her in eleven films. He was the first actor to receive an Academy Award nomination for a non-English-language performance, and he was nominated for Best Actor three times, for *Divorce Italian Style*, *A Special Day* and *Dark Eyes*. He largely shunned Hollywood.

British chef, restaurateur and television personality. In 1995 he became the first British chef to earn three Michelin stars and, at age 33, the youngest chef to do so. === KEY: Marco_Polo_3384a0
HEADWORD: Polo, Marco
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Christopher Columbus | Genoa | Kublai Khan | Paper money | Porcelain | Silk Road
ENTRY:
Venetian merchant, explorer and writer, known for an account of travels in Asia. He traveled the [[Silk Road|Silk Road]] between 1271 and 1295 and reached the court of [[Kublai Khan]], who appointed him an emissary. He undertook diplomatic missions across the empire and Southeast Asia, to present-day Myanmar, India, Indonesia, Sri Lanka and Vietnam, and he lived in China for 17 years. After returning to Venice, he was captured in the war between Venice and [[Genoa]]. While imprisoned, he dictated his travels to Rustichello da Pisa, producing *The Travels of Marco Polo* (c. 1300). The book gave Europeans a detailed view of China, Persia and India, and was the first Western record of [[Porcelain|porcelain]], gunpowder and [[Paper money|paper money]]. Its narrative inspired [[Christopher Columbus]].

=== KEY: Marcus_Aurelius_2a1937
HEADWORD: Marcus Aurelius
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Commodus | Roman Empire
ENTRY:
Roman emperor (r. 161–180) and Stoic philosopher. He was the last of the so-called Five Good Emperors and the last emperor of the Pax Romana, an age of relative peace in the [[Roman Empire|Roman Empire]] lasting from 27 BC to 180 AD. He ruled jointly with Lucius Verus until Verus died in 169, and he was succeeded by his son [[Commodus]]. His reign was marked by the Parthian War and the Marcomannic Wars against the Marcomanni, Quadi and Iazyges. The Antonine Plague, which broke out in 165 or 166, caused the deaths of five to ten million people. His *Meditations*, private reflections written in Greek, is one of the most important sources for understanding ancient Stoic philosophy.

=== KEY: Marcus_Garvey_4ce41b
HEADWORD: Garvey, Marcus
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Ku Klux Klan | Nation of Islam | Rastafari
ENTRY:
Jamaican black nationalist, Pan-Africanist activist and orator. Garvey founded the Universal Negro Improvement Association (UNIA) in Jamaica in 1914 and built its membership after moving to New York City in 1916. He promoted racial separatism, economic self-reliance and the Back-to-Africa movement; his ideas became known as Garveyism. His Black Star Line shipping company, launched in 1919, sold stock to supporters. In 1923 Garvey was convicted of mail fraud over those sales and imprisoned, a charge many contemporaries viewed as politically motivated. He was deported to Jamaica in 1927 and founded the People's Political Party in 1929. In 1964 his body was returned to Jamaica for reburial, and he was the first person formally recognized as a National Hero. His links with the [[Ku Klux Klan|Ku Klux Klan]] and his prejudice against Jews and mixed-race people drew criticism from many in the African diaspora. His ideas influenced [[Rastafari|Rastafarianism]] and the [[Nation of Islam]].

=== KEY: Marcus_Licinius_Crassus_142f06
HEADWORD: Crassus, Marcus Licinius
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Julius Caesar | Parthian Empire | Pompey | Spartacus | Sulla
ENTRY:
Roman general and statesman of the late Republic. Often called "the richest man in Rome," Crassus amassed a large fortune through property speculation during [[Sulla|Sulla's]] dictatorship and through silver mines. He rose to prominence by defeating the slave revolt led by [[Spartacus]] and shared the consulship with his rival [[Pompey]] in 70 BC. He joined [[Julius Caesar]] and Pompey in the First Triumvirate, an unofficial alliance that dominated Roman politics. As governor of Syria he invaded [[Parthian Empire|Parthia]] in 53 BC; his army was defeated at the Battle of Carrhae, and he was killed. His death ended the balance between Caesar and Pompey, and within four years Caesar crossed the Rubicon and began a civil war against Pompey.
