=== KEY: Mansa_Musa_376f8f
HEADWORD: Mansa Musa
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Ghana Empire | Hajj | Ibn Khaldun | Mali Empire | Timbuktu
ENTRY:
Ruler of the [[Mali Empire]] (r. c. 1312–c. 1337). The empire reached its territorial peak during his reign, which is often regarded as the zenith of Mali's power and prestige, although he features less in Mandinka oral traditions than his predecessors.

Contemporaries described Musa as inconceivably rich. His wealth came principally from the empire's control and taxing of the trade in salt from northern regions and especially from gold panned and mined in Bambuk and Bure to the south. It is estimated that two thirds of the gold circulating in the medieval Mediterranean came from West Africa. At his accession, Mali consisted largely of the territory of the former [[Ghana Empire]], which had become a vassal of Mali. The empire comprised land now in Guinea, Senegal, Mauritania, the Gambia, and modern Mali, an area of 1,300,000 km².

Musa went on the [[Hajj]] to Mecca in 1324, traveling with an enormous entourage and a vast supply of gold. In Cairo, his lavish gift-giving is said to have noticeably affected the value of gold in Egypt and drew the attention of the wider Muslim world. He expanded the empire's borders, incorporating the cities of Gao and [[Timbuktu]], and sought closer ties with the Mamluk and Marinid Sultanates. He recruited scholars from the wider Muslim world, such as the Andalusian poet Abu Ishaq al-Sahili, and helped establish Timbuktu as a center of Islamic learning. His reign is associated with numerous construction projects, including part of the Djinguereber Mosque in Timbuktu.

Much of what is known about him comes from Arabic sources written after his pilgrimage, especially [[Ibn Khaldun]] and Al-Umari.

=== KEY: Manta_ray_b3683d
HEADWORD: Manta ray
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Genus | Plankton | Pollution
ENTRY:
Large ray of the [[Genus|genus]] *Mobula*. *M. birostris* is the largest, reaching 7 meters (23 feet) across, and *M. alfredi*, at 5.5 meters, is smaller. Manta rays have triangular pectoral fins, horn-shaped cephalic fins and large, forward-facing mouths, and they filter-feed on [[Plankton|plankton]] as they swim. They live in warm temperate, subtropical and tropical waters and have one of the highest brain-to-body ratios of any fish. *M. birostris* and *M. alfredi* are listed as vulnerable by the International Union for Conservation of Nature. Threats include [[Pollution|pollution]], entanglement in fishing nets and harvesting of gill plates, which are traded internationally. Their slow reproductive rate worsens these pressures.

=== KEY: Mantis_05b86b
HEADWORD: Mantis
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Ancient Egypt | Ancient Greece | Assyria | Cockroach | Insect | Pet | Termite
ENTRY:
Order of [[Insect|insects]] containing over 2,400 species, including the praying mantises. Mantises are found worldwide in temperate and tropical habitats. They have triangular heads on flexible necks and greatly enlarged forelegs for catching prey, and their upright posture with folded forearms resembles prayer. Most are ambush predators, and they normally live for about a year. Their closest relatives are [[Termite|termites]] and [[Cockroach|cockroaches]]. Females sometimes practice sexual cannibalism. Early civilizations, including [[Ancient Greece]], [[Ancient Egypt]] and [[Assyria]], believed mantises had supernatural powers, and they are among the insects most commonly kept as [[Pet|pets]].

=== KEY: Mantis_shrimp_3362b3
HEADWORD: Mantis shrimp
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Club (weapon) | Compound eye | Crustacean | Mantis | Polarization (waves) | Ultraviolet
ENTRY:
Carnivorous marine [[Crustacean|crustacean]] of the order Stomatopoda, with more than 520 living species. They are among the most important predators in many shallow tropical and subtropical habitats, though they are poorly understood because many species spend most of their lives in burrows. Their raptorial appendages are either spearing types, which impale prey on spiny structures, or smashing types, which strike with a heavily mineralized [[Club (weapon)|club]]. Their [[Compound eye|compound eyes]] detect [[Ultraviolet|ultraviolet]] and [[Polarization (waves)|polarized]] light. Their name comes from forelimbs that resemble those of [[Mantis|mantises]].

=== KEY: Mantra_7671f2
HEADWORD: Mantra
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Buddhism | Hinduism | Jainism | Om | Sanskrit | Sikhism | Vedas | Zoroastrianism
ENTRY:
Sacred utterance, syllable or phrase, often repeated in meditation. The word derives from [[Sanskrit]] roots meaning mind and instrument, so a mantra is understood as a mental tool to focus thought. Its earliest mentions appear in the [[Vedas]] and the Avesta of ancient Iran, and practitioners believe mantras have religious or spiritual power. The seed syllable [[Om]] is a mantra in various Indian religions, and in [[Hinduism]] it is believed to be the first sound. A common practice is japa, the meditative repetition of a mantra, often with prayer beads. Mantras are central to [[Buddhism]], [[Jainism]], [[Zoroastrianism]] and [[Sikhism]], although their use and meaning vary among traditions.

=== KEY: Manuel_I_Komnenos_395cb2
HEADWORD: Manuel I Komnenos
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Anatolia | Byzantine Empire | Crusader states | Egypt | Kingdom of Jerusalem | Kingdom of Sicily | Pope
ENTRY:
[[Byzantine Empire|Byzantine]] emperor from 1143 to 1180. His reign saw the last flowering of the Komnenian restoration, when the empire regained military and economic power. He allied with [[Pope]] Adrian IV, unsuccessfully invaded the [[Kingdom of Sicily]], and established a Byzantine protectorate over the [[Crusader states]]. He joined a combined invasion of [[Egypt]] with the [[Kingdom of Jerusalem]] and placed Hungary under Byzantine hegemony. The campaign that ended at Myriokephalon against the Seljuk Turks was the last unsuccessful effort to recover inner [[Anatolia]]. Some historians argue that his power reflected the Komnenos dynasty rather than his own achievements.

=== KEY: Manufacturing_e86883
HEADWORD: Manufacturing
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Assembly line | Bronze Age | Handicraft | Henry Ford | Industrial Revolution | Iron Age | Mesopotamia | Smelting
ENTRY:
Production of goods with the help of equipment, labor, machines, tools, and chemical or biological processing. Manufacturing is the essence of the secondary sector of the economy. The term covers activity from [[Handicraft|handicraft]] to high technology but is most commonly applied to industrial production, in which raw materials from the primary sector are transformed into finished goods on a large scale. Such goods may be sold to other manufacturers for making more complex products, such as aircraft, household appliances, furniture, sports equipment, or automobiles, or distributed through the tertiary sector to consumers, usually via wholesalers and retailers. Manufacturing engineering designs and optimizes the steps by which raw materials become a final product, beginning with product design and materials specification. Some industries, such as semiconductor and steel manufacturing, use the term fabrication instead.

The English word probably derives from Middle French *manufacture* ("process of making"), from Latin *manū* ("hand") and French *facture* ("making"). Its earliest recorded English use, in the mid-16th century, referred to making products by hand.

Human ancestors made stone tools long before *Homo sapiens* appeared about 200,000 years ago. The earliest method, the Oldowan industry, dates to at least 2.3 million years ago, and the earliest direct evidence of tool use, from Ethiopia, dates to 2.5 million years ago. Stone toolmaking advanced through the prepared-core technique (about 300,000 years ago) and pressure flaking (beginning about 40,000 years ago) to the polished stone tools of the Neolithic. [[Smelting|Smelting]] of copper is believed to have begun when pottery kilns reached sufficiently high temperatures. In the [[Bronze Age]], bronze improved on stone because of its strength and ductility and because it could be cast in molds; the [[Iron Age]] is defined by the widespread making of weapons and tools from iron and steel. Several of the six classic simple machines were invented in [[Mesopotamia]], where the potter's wheel, the first wheel and axle mechanism, appeared in the 5th millennium BC. Egyptian papyrus paper and pottery were mass-produced and exported across the Mediterranean basin.

Papermaking, a 2nd-century Chinese technology, reached the Middle East in the 8th century and Europe through Spain; a paper mill was established in Sicily in the 12th century. The blast furnace came into widespread use in France in the mid 15th century because of cannon casting. The stocking frame, invented in 1598, raised a knitter's output from 100 to 1000 knots per minute.

The [[Industrial Revolution]], from 1760 to the 1830s in Europe and the United States, shifted production from hand methods to machines, with steam and water power, machine tools, and the mechanized factory system. Textiles dominated in employment, output value, and capital invested, with mechanized spinning beginning in Britain in the 1780s. Mass production on [[Assembly line|assembly lines]] was pioneered by [[Henry Ford]] in 1913, followed by automation and digital integration.

Manufacturing accounts for about 15% of world GDP. China produced 32% of global manufacturing value added in 2024 and the United States 15%. Concerns include environmental costs, job losses in high-wage nations, and vulnerable concentrated supply chains, as shown by disruptions during the COVID-19 pandemic.

=== KEY: Manx_cat_4392f2
HEADWORD: Manx cat
PERSON: no

ENTRY:
Breed of [[Cat|domestic cat]] originating on the [[Isle of Man]]. A dominant [[Mutation|mutation]] in a [[Gene|gene]] shortens or removes the tail, and the breed is best known for being entirely tailless, with elongated hind legs and a rounded head. Manx were prized as skilled hunters of [[Rodent|rodents]] and were a preferred ship's cat breed. The mutation can cause Manx syndrome, a form of spina bifida that may lead to spinal deformities, incontinence and [[Arthritis|arthritis]].

=== KEY: Many_worlds_interpretation_a64294
HEADWORD: Many-worlds interpretation
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Quantum mechanics | Schrödinger's cat
ENTRY:
Interpretation of [[Quantum mechanics|quantum mechanics]] holding that all possible measurement outcomes occur in different worlds. Hugh Everett proposed it in 1957 as the relative state formulation, and Bryce DeWitt popularized the name many-worlds in the 1970s. Apparent wave function collapse is explained by decoherence, which produces branches that correspond to different outcomes. It is intended to resolve paradoxes such as [[Schrödinger's cat]], and it is considered mainstream, though critics object to the proliferation of unobservable worlds.
