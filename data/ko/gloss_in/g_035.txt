=== KEY: Deformation_engineering_7324f9
HEADWORD: Deformation
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Concrete | Force | Steel | Stiffness | Young's modulus
ENTRY:
Change in the size or shape of an object when subjected to [[Force|force]]. Deformation is elastic if the object returns to its original shape when the force is removed, and plastic if permanent distortion remains. An object's resistance to deformation is its [[Stiffness|stiffness]] or rigidity. Within the elastic range, stress and strain are generally linear, and the linear relationship for a material is known as [[Young's modulus]]. Small-strain theory suits materials such as [[Steel|steel]] and [[Concrete|concrete]], which deform only slightly under engineering loads. Elastomers, rubber and some shape-memory metals such as Nitinol show large elastic deformation, but their elasticity is nonlinear, so other strain definitions are needed.

=== KEY: Degenerate_matter_751561
HEADWORD: Degenerate matter
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Absolute zero | Astrophysics | Fermion | Neutron star | Pauli exclusion principle | White dwarf
ENTRY:
Dense state of matter at low temperature, in which the [[Pauli exclusion principle]] significantly alters behavior. In [[Astrophysics|astrophysics]], the term refers to dense stellar remnants such as [[White dwarf|white dwarfs]] and [[Neutron star|neutron stars]], whose thermal pressure alone cannot prevent gravitational collapse. It is usually modeled as an ideal Fermi gas of non-interacting [[Fermion|fermions]] that fill the lowest quantum states. This degeneracy pressure remains nonzero even at [[Absolute zero]] and depends on the density of the fermions rather than on temperature, which keeps dense stars in equilibrium. The term also applies to metals in the Fermi gas approximation. The concept of degenerate stars was developed jointly by Arthur Eddington, Ralph Fowler and Arthur Milne.

=== KEY: Degree_angle_befd97

ENTRY:
Unit of plane angle in which one full rotation equals 360 degrees, usually denoted by °. The [[International System of Units|SI]] unit of angular measure is the [[Radian|radian]], and one degree equals π/180 radians. Theories link the choice of 360 to the approximately 360 days of the year and to Babylonian sexagesimal numbers. The number's divisibility also suits the division of the world into 24 [[Time zone|time zones]] of 15° of longitude each. Subdivisions include the arcminute (1/60 of a degree) and the arcsecond; maritime charts use decimal minutes, where one minute of [[Latitude|latitude]] equals one nautical mile.

ENTRY:
Belief in a creator God who does not intervene in the universe after creating it. === KEY: Deity_33184b
HEADWORD: Deity
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Atheism | Augustine of Hippo | Deism | Monotheism | Pantheism | Polytheism | Theism | Zeus
ENTRY:
Supernatural being considered to have authority over some aspect of the universe or life, and often regarded as sacred and worthy of worship. English-speakers call a male deity a god and a female deity a goddess. There is no universally accepted consensus on what a deity is, and concepts vary considerably across cultures, from an infinite transcendent creator (God) to a finite entity or experience of special significance (god).

The English word derives from Old French *deité* and Latin *deitatem*, "divine nature," a form coined by [[Augustine of Hippo]] from *deus* ("god"). *Deus* shares a Proto-Indo-European root with Sanskrit *deva*, "shining one," Greek *dios*, "divine," and [[Zeus]]. The word "god" comes from Proto-Germanic *guthan*; one proposed origin is a root meaning "that which is invoked," another a root meaning "poured," referring to libations. Germanic cognates were originally neuter and became generally masculine after Christianization, whereas all ancient Indo-European cultures recognized both masculine and feminine deities.

Belief in at least one deity who may interact with the world is called [[Theism|theism]]. [[Monotheism]] accepts only one deity. [[Polytheism]] accepts many, usually assembled into a pantheon with accompanying rituals; the gods often represent forces of nature or ancestral principles. Monolatry accepts that many deities exist but holds that only one may validly be worshipped, and henotheism accepts several while regarding them as aspects of the same highest divine principle. Nontheistic religions deny any supreme eternal creator but may accept deities that live, die, and are reborn like other beings. Most monotheistic religions traditionally envision their god as omnipotent, omnipresent, omniscient, omnibenevolent, and eternal, but none of these qualities is essential to the definition of a deity. Monotheistic religions typically refer to their god in masculine terms, while other religions describe deities as male, female, hermaphroditic, or genderless.

[[Deism]] holds that a single deity created the universe but does not usually intervene in it, and was particularly popular among western intellectuals in the 18th and 19th centuries. [[Pantheism]] holds that the universe itself is God. Panentheism holds that divinity pervades the universe and also transcends it. Agnosticism holds that it is impossible to know whether any deity exists, and [[Atheism|atheism]] is the non-belief in any deity.

Many cultures, including the ancient Mesopotamians, Egyptians, Greeks, Romans, and Germanic peoples, personified natural phenomena. Some Avestan and Vedic deities were viewed as ethical concepts. The Upanishads of Hinduism characterize Atman (soul, self) as *deva*, asserting that the supreme principle (Brahman) is part of every living creature. Archaeologists once commonly interpreted prehistoric female figurines as a single primordial goddess, ancestor of goddesses such as Inanna and Aphrodite; this approach has been generally discredited.

=== KEY: Del_5526d6

ENTRY:
Vector differential operator, usually written ∇ and called nabla, used in [[Vector calculus|vector calculus]]. Applied to a field, it denotes one of three operations: the [[Gradient|gradient]] (steepest slope) of a scalar field, the divergence of a vector field, or the curl (rotation) of a vector field. The gradient of a scalar field points in the direction of greatest increase, and its magnitude equals the maximum rate of increase at that point.

=== KEY: Delusion_b282a9

ENTRY:
Fixed belief resistant to conflicting evidence. Unlike beliefs based on false or incomplete information, [[Hallucination|hallucinations]], or other misleading perceptions, delusions are not revised when evidence contradicts them. Delusions occur in many physical and mental disorders and are of particular diagnostic importance in psychotic disorders, including [[Schizophrenia|schizophrenia]], paraphrenia, manic episodes of [[Bipolar disorder|bipolar disorder]], and psychotic depression. === KEY: Dementia_488369
HEADWORD: Dementia
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Alzheimer's disease | Blood test | Hallucination | Huntington's disease | Hypothyroidism | Parkinson's disease | Prion | Stroke
ENTRY:
Syndrome of general decline in cognitive processes that impairs the ability to perform everyday activities, often associated with neurodegenerative diseases. It typically involves problems with memory, thinking, behavior, and motor control. Common symptoms also include emotional problems, difficulties with language, and decreased motivation. Dementia is incurable and progressive, ranging from mild to major severity, and is distinct from age-related decline in cognition and memory.

[[Alzheimer's disease]] is the most common cause, contributing to an estimated 60–70% of cases, followed by vascular dementia from impaired blood flow to the brain. Dementia can also be caused by brain injuries and [[Stroke|stroke]]. Subtypes are named for the underlying disorder, including [[Parkinson's disease]] dementia, [[Huntington's disease]] dementia, HIV dementia, frontotemporal dementia, dementia with Lewy bodies, and [[Prion|prion]] diseases.

Symptoms fall into cognitive, neuropsychiatric, and motor groups. Cognitive symptoms typically involve memory plus another region such as language, attention, problem solving, or perception. Neuropsychiatric symptoms affect more than 90% of cases at different stages and include agitation, disinhibition, depression, [[Hallucination|hallucinations]], delusions, apathy, and personality change.

Diagnosis is usually based on the history of the illness and cognitive testing with imaging. [[Blood test|Blood tests]] rule out reversible causes such as [[Hypothyroidism|hypothyroidism]], and fluid biomarkers and PET scans can identify Alzheimer's disease. Aging is the greatest risk factor, but dementia is not a normal part of aging.obesity]], hypertension, and diabetes.|obesity]], hypertension, and diabetes.

In 2021 approximately 57 million people worldwide were affected, and 10 million new cases are reported every year as of 2025. There is no known cure. Acetylcholinesterase inhibitors such as donepezil are used in some subtypes and may help in mild to moderate stages, though the overall benefit may be minor.

=== KEY: Democracy_2bc694
HEADWORD: Democracy
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Civil liberties | Classical Athens | Constitution | Freedom of speech | Human rights | Suffrage
ENTRY:
Form of government in which political power is vested in the people of a state. Under a narrow definition, rulers are elected through competitive elections; broader definitions also require guarantees of [[Civil liberties|civil liberties]] and [[Human rights|human rights]]. No consensus exists on a precise definition.

In a direct democracy, the people deliberate and decide legislation themselves. In a representative democracy, the dominant form today, they elect officials to govern on their behalf, as in parliamentary or presidential systems. In liberal democracy, majority power is exercised within a representative framework, but a [[Constitution|constitution]] and supreme court limit the majority and protect minorities through individual rights such as [[Freedom of speech]] and freedom of association.political freedom]], and the Rule of law.|political freedom]], and the Rule of law.

The term appeared in the 5th century BC in Greek city-states, notably [[Classical Athens]], to mean "rule of the people," in contrast to aristocracy, "rule of an elite." Citizenship in democracies was initially restricted to an elite class and later extended to all adult citizens, in most modern democracies through the [[Suffrage|suffrage]] movements of the 19th and 20th centuries. Democratic systems have become more prevalent since the 19th century, in waves of democratization.

Abraham Lincoln described democracy as "Government of the people, by the people, for the people." Supporters point to improved health, education, and economic outcomes, and public opinion across regions tends to favor democratic systems. Critics cite vulnerability to demagoguery, short-term decision-making, and policy gridlock. According to the V-Dem indices and *The Economist* Democracy Index, less than half the world's population lived in a democracy as of 2022, and dissatisfaction with democratic performance is common even in established democracies.

=== KEY: Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo_b2ce07

ENTRY:
Country in [[Central Africa]], formerly named [[Zaire]], covering 2,344,858 sq km. It is the second-largest country in Africa by land area and the most populous Francophone country in the world. The capital, largest city, and economic center is [[Kinshasa]].

The [[Kingdom of Kongo]] ruled near the mouth of the Congo River from the 14th to the 19th century. [[Leopold II of Belgium]] acquired rights to the territory in 1885 and called it the Congo Free State. After international pressure over widespread atrocities, he ceded it in 1908, and it became a Belgian colony. Independence came in 1960, followed by secessionist movements, the assassination of Prime Minister [[Patrice Lumumba]], and the seizure of power by [[Mobutu Sese Seko]] in 1965. Mobutu renamed the country Zaire in 1971 and imposed a personalist dictatorship.

Instability from an influx of refugees from the Rwandan Civil War led to the First Congo War (1996–97), which ended with Mobutu's overthrow. The [[Second Congo War]] (1998–2003) caused an estimated several million deaths and the assassination of President Laurent-Désiré Kabila. Joseph Kabila restored relative stability and stepped down in 2019 after Félix Tshisekedi won the contentious 2018 election, the first peaceful transition of power since independence. Over 120 armed groups remain active, concentrated in the east, and fighting has displaced 7 million people.

The country is rich in natural resources, including cobalt, copper, gold, and diamonds, yet remains one of the world's poorest. Raw minerals and metal accounted for 80% of exports in 2023, and China is the largest trade partner. It ranked 171 out of 193 countries on the Human Development Index in 2023.

=== KEY: Demography_b9aa7d

ENTRY:
Statistical study of human populations, covering their size, composition (such as ethnic group and age), and change through fertility, mortality, and migration. Formal demography measures these processes, while social demography, or population studies, also analyzes their links to economic, social, institutional, cultural, and biological factors. Educational institutions usually treat it as a field of [[Sociology|sociology]], although independent departments exist. Demographic analysis uses administrative records to produce estimates often considered a reliable standard for judging [[Census|census]] accuracy. In 1662 John Graunt's study of mortality records found that one-third of London's children died before their sixteenth birthday, and mathematicians such as [[Edmond Halley]] developed the life table as the basis for life insurance mathematics.

=== KEY: Dengue_fever_1a0cbe

PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Antibody | Blood plasma | Climate change | Iberian Peninsula
ENTRY:
Mosquito-borne disease caused by any of four dengue virus serotypes. Most infections are asymptomatic or mild, with fever, headache, muscle and joint pains, and a rash appearing 3 to 14 days after infection. In a small proportion of cases the disease becomes severe, with bleeding, [[Blood plasma|blood plasma]] leakage, and dangerously low blood pressure. Infection with one serotype usually gives lifelong immunity to it but only short-term immunity to the others, and a later infection with another serotype raises the risk of severe disease through [[Antibody|antibody]]-dependent enhancement. There is no specific medication, and severe cases require hospitalization for fluids. Partly attributed to [[Climate change|climate change]], the disease is spreading to the [[Iberian Peninsula]] and the southern United States. In 2023 more than 5 million infections were reported, with more than 5,000 dengue-related deaths.

=== KEY: Denisovan_c3863d

PERSON: no

ENTRY:
Extinct archaic human species or subspecies of Asia. Denisovans ranged across Asia during the Middle to Late Pleistocene, approximately 200,000–32,000 years ago, and most knowledge of them comes from DNA evidence. The first individual was identified in 2010 from mitochondrial DNA extracted from a juvenile finger bone excavated from [[Siberia|Siberian]] Denisova Cave in the [[Altai Mountains]]. Nuclear DNA shows close affinities with [[Neanderthal|Neanderthals]]. Other remains come from sites including the [[Tibetan Plateau]], Laos, [[Taiwan]] and [[Yunnan]]. Denisovans interbred with modern humans: Melanesians, Aboriginal Australians and Filipino Negritos carry roughly 5% Denisovan DNA, compared with 0.2% in mainland Asians and Native Americans. In June 2025 the [[Harbin]] cranium, earlier named *Homo longi*, was identified as Denisovan. Their taxonomy remains unsettled: they may be a new species of [[Homo]] or an archaic subspecies of *Homo sapiens*.

=== KEY: Density_7e6d11
HEADWORD: Density
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Alloy | Archimedes | Buoyancy | Osmium
ENTRY:
Mass per unit volume of a substance, usually symbolized ρ. It is calculated as ρ = m/V, where m is mass and V is volume. Density is an intensive property: adding more of a substance increases its mass but not its density. Different materials usually have different densities, which matter for [[Buoyancy]], purity and packaging. [[Osmium]] is the densest known element at standard conditions for temperature and pressure. Density varies with temperature and pressure, far more for gases than for solids and liquids. Relative density, or specific gravity, compares a material with water, so a value below one means the substance floats. In a well-known but probably apocryphal tale, [[Archimedes]] was asked to detect whether a goldsmith had substituted a cheaper [[Alloy|alloy]] in a gold wreath, and is said to have measured the wreath's volume by the rise of water.

=== KEY: Derivative_70ae6e

ENTRY:
Instantaneous rate of change of a [[Function (mathematics)|function]]'s output with respect to its input. For a function of one variable, the derivative at a point is the slope of the tangent line to the graph there, and the tangent is the best linear approximation of the function near that point. Derivatives are defined as a [[Limit (mathematics)|limit]], and the process of finding one is called [[Differential calculus|differentiation]]. === KEY: Desalination_16b873

ENTRY:
Artificial process converting salt water, generally seawater, to fresh water for human consumption or irrigation. Along with recycled wastewater, desalination is one of the few water resources independent of rainfall, but it is generally more costly than surface water, groundwater or water recycling because of its energy use. Processes are thermal, as in [[Distillation|distillation]], or membrane-based, as in [[Reverse osmosis|reverse osmosis]], and both produce [[Brine|brine]] as a by-product. === KEY: Desert_000c01
HEADWORD: Desert
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Antarctica | Aquifer | Dune | Flash flood | Irrigation | Sahara | Weathering
ENTRY:
Landscape receiving little precipitation, and therefore supporting distinctive biomes and ecosystems. About one-third of Earth's land surface is arid or semi-arid. Deserts generally receive less than 250 mm (10 in) of precipitation a year. The category includes polar regions, sometimes called "cold deserts"; [[Antarctica]], about 98% thick continental ice sheet and 2% barren rock, is the world's largest.

[[Weathering]] forms deserts as large day–night temperature swings strain rocks until they break. Occasional downpours cause [[Flash flood|flash floods]], and wind carries sand and dust, sometimes forming sand or dust storms, and abrades surfaces. Sand ends up as level sheets or high [[Dune|dunes]]; other deserts are stony plains whose surfaces form a mosaic of smooth stones known as desert pavement. Temporary lakes and salt pans form where waters evaporate, and oases occur where springs or [[Aquifer|aquifers]] supply water. Deserts reflect more incoming light than forests or the sea, which moderates Earth's temperature.

Plants are tough and wiry, with small or no leaves, water-resistant cuticles, and often spines; some annuals germinate, bloom, and die within weeks of rain, while long-lived plants have deep roots. Many animals are nocturnal, shelter in shade or underground, conserve water, and may lie dormant for long periods before breeding rapidly after rain.

Nomads move flocks to available grazing, and oases support settled life. Cultivating semi-arid regions encourages erosion and desertification, but [[Irrigation|irrigation]] allows farming, as in California's Imperial Valley. Camel caravans carried salt, gold, ivory, and other goods along trade routes across the [[Sahara]], and large numbers of slaves were also taken north.solar energy]].|solar energy]].

The word comes from ecclesiastical Latin *dēsertum*, "an abandoned place."

=== KEY: Design_1afa74
HEADWORD: Design
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Bauhaus | Industrial Revolution | Positivism
ENTRY:
Concept or proposal for an object, process, or system, intentionally created by a thinking agent. A design is expected to have a purpose within a specific context, typically satisfying goals and constraints while accounting for aesthetic, functional, and experiential considerations. Traditional examples are architectural and engineering drawings, circuit diagrams, sewing patterns, and less tangible artifacts such as business process models.

People who produce designs are designers, a term usually qualified by field, such as fashion, product, web, or interior designer. A designer's sequence of activities is the design process, which can be a quick sketch or a lengthy effort involving research, negotiation, modeling, and re-design. Designing is not confined to professionals: Herbert A. Simon wrote that "Everyone designs who devises courses of action aimed at changing existing situations into preferred ones." Design researcher Nigel Cross likewise held that design ability is embedded in the brain as a natural cognitive function.

Historians differ on where design history begins. John Heskett and others look to the [[Industrial Revolution]] and mass production, while others begin with prehistoric artifacts. The discipline of design history coalesced in the 1970s. The [[Bauhaus]], founded in 1919, greatly influenced modern design education.

Researchers disagree about how designers work. The rational model, developed by Simon and by Gerhard Pahl and Wolfgang Beitz, treats design as plan-driven optimization for known constraints through discrete stages. The alternative view, called reflection-in-action or coevolution, treats design as an interactive process; the two views have been linked to [[Positivism|positivism]] and constructionism respectively.

=== KEY: Design_of_experiments_5cfeec
HEADWORD: Design of experiments
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Analysis of variance | Experiment
ENTRY:
Construction of procedures for [[Experiment|experiments]] that attempt to explain how changes in one aspect of a system affect others. Designers decide which aspects of a system to change and which to control, based on hypotheses about sources of variance. A design selects independent, dependent and control variables and plans the experiment under statistically optimal conditions within available resources. === KEY: Determinant_92eb79
HEADWORD: Determinant
PERSON: no

ENTRY:
Scalar value computed from the entries of a square [[Matrix (mathematics)|matrix]]. A square matrix is invertible if and only if its determinant is nonzero, and the determinant of a product of matrices is the product of their determinants. For real linear transformations, the determinant gives the factor by which volume changes, and its sign shows whether orientation is preserved. Determinants can be computed efficiently by [[Gaussian elimination]], and Cramer's rule expresses the solutions of linear systems in terms of determinants. The concept emerged in the late 17th century with [[Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz]], who studied values associated with arrays of numbers for solving equations. The characteristic polynomial, whose roots are the eigenvalues of a matrix, is defined as a determinant.

=== KEY: Determinism_700de0

PERSON: no

ENTRY:
Metaphysical view that all events in the [[Universe|universe]] can occur only in one possible way. Determinism is often contrasted with [[Free will|free will]], although some philosophers argue that the two are compatible. Causal determinism holds that every event results from preceding events and the laws of nature. The antonym is indeterminism, the view that events are not deterministically caused. === KEY: Dew_1c3c80
HEADWORD: Dew
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Condensation | Dew point | Frost | Infrared | Sun
ENTRY:
Droplets of water that form on exposed objects in the morning or evening. As an exposed surface cools by radiating its heat, atmospheric moisture condenses faster than it can evaporate, forming droplets through [[Condensation|condensation]]. Dew forms most easily on surfaces not warmed by heat from deep ground, such as grass, leaves, railings and car roofs. Cooling is strongest on clear nights, when a surface loses more energy by [[Infrared|infrared]] radiation than it receives from the [[Sun]]. Calm nights favor dew, because wind carries warmer air to cold surfaces. Dew is related to the [[Dew point|dew point]], and when temperatures are low enough it freezes into [[Frost|frost]]. Measured dew rarely exceeds 0.5 mm per night. In regions with long dry seasons, plants such as lichen and pine seedlings benefit from it.
