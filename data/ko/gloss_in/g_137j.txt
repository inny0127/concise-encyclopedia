=== KEY: Tornado_36cbc4
HEADWORD: Tornado
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Cumulonimbus cloud | Cumulus cloud | Earth | Waterspout
ENTRY:
Violently rotating column of air extending from the surface of the [[Earth]] to the base of a [[Cumulonimbus cloud|cumulonimbus]] or [[Cumulus cloud|cumulus]] cloud. Most tornadoes have wind speeds below 180 km per hour (110 mph), are about 80 m (250 ft) across and travel a few miles before dissipating; the most extreme exceed 480 km per hour (300 mph). The United States has by far the most tornadoes of any country, concentrated in "Tornado Alley." Intensity is rated on the Fujita scale, replaced in some countries by the Enhanced Fujita Scale, from EF0, which damages trees, to EF5, which can rip buildings off their foundations. Over water, [[Waterspout|waterspouts]] are generally classified as non-supercellular tornadoes.

=== KEY: Toronto_948ce7
HEADWORD: Toronto
PERSON: no

ENTRY:
Most populous city in [[Canada]] and capital of [[Ontario]], on the northwestern shore of [[Lake Ontario]] (pop., 2021: 2,794,356). Indigenous peoples have lived in the area for more than 10,000 years. The British established the town of York in 1793, and during the [[War of 1812]] it was occupied for two weeks by American troops. York was renamed and incorporated as the City of Toronto in 1834, and it became the capital of Ontario in 1867. The Greater Toronto Area has a population of 6,712,341. About half of its residents were born outside Canada, and over 200 ethnic origins are represented. Toronto is Canada's largest financial center and home to the Toronto Stock Exchange. It is the fourth-most populous city in North America, behind [[Mexico City]], [[New York City]] and [[Los Angeles]].

=== KEY: Toronto_Maple_Leafs_a3b39e
HEADWORD: Toronto Maple Leafs
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Ice hockey | Montreal Canadiens | National Hockey League | Toronto
ENTRY:
Professional [[Ice hockey|ice hockey]] team based in [[Toronto]] and a member of the [[National Hockey League]] (NHL). Founded as the Toronto Arenas for the 1917–18 NHL season, it was renamed the Maple Leafs by Conn Smythe after he bought it in 1927. The club has won more Stanley Cup championships than any NHL team other than the [[Montreal Canadiens]]. Its dynasties from 1946–47 to 1950–51 and from 1961–62 to 1966–67 won a combined eight of eleven Stanley Cups. The championship drought that followed, at 57 seasons, is the longest in league history. The team plays at Scotiabank Arena.

=== KEY: Torpedo_6e51aa
HEADWORD: Torpedo
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Aircraft | Austria-Hungary | Electric ray | Naval mine | Submarine
ENTRY:
Self-propelled underwater weapon launched from above or below the water surface, with an explosive warhead for contact or proximity detonation. The word first named [[Electric ray|electric rays]], and the term originally covered many devices now called [[Naval mine|mines]]. In 1866 Robert Whitehead developed the first practical self-propelled torpedo, at Fiume in [[Austria-Hungary]]. From the 1860s onward, torpedoes allowed small [[Submarine|submarines]], torpedo boats and even light [[Aircraft|aircraft]] to destroy large ships without large guns. Modern torpedoes are lightweight or heavyweight, straight-running, homing or wire-guided, and can be launched from many platforms. In modern warfare, a submarine-launched torpedo is almost certain to hit its target, and the best defense is a counterattack with another torpedo.

=== KEY: Torque_1aaf5a
HEADWORD: Torque
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Cross product | Engine | Force | Lever | Right-hand rule | Siméon Denis Poisson
ENTRY:
Rotational equivalent of linear [[Force|force]], a twist applied to an object about an axis, also called the moment of force. Torque is the product of the perpendicular component of a force and the distance from the axis to the line of action, known as the [[Lever|lever]] arm. In vector form it is the [[Cross product|cross product]] of the position and force vectors, with direction given by the [[Right-hand rule|right-hand rule]]. Its SI unit is the newton-meter (N·m). Torque causes angular acceleration in rigid bodies, appearing in applications from levers and pulleys to [[Engine|engines]]. The term comes from the Latin *torquēre*, "to twist," and was suggested by James Thomson, appearing in print in 1884. In mechanical engineering in the UK and the U.S., it is usually called the moment of force, a usage traced to [[Siméon Denis Poisson]] in 1811.

=== KEY: Tort_04162e
HEADWORD: Tort
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Assault | Battery (crime) | Civil law (legal system) | Contract | Criminal law | Damages | Philippines | Thailand
ENTRY:
Civil wrong, other than breach of [[Contract|contract]], causing loss or harm to a claimant and resulting in legal liability. Tort law aims to compensate victims, whereas [[Criminal law|criminal law]] aims to punish individuals who commit crimes. Some acts, such as [[Assault|assault]] and [[Battery (crime)|battery]], can result in both a civil lawsuit and a criminal prosecution. The main remedy is [[Damages|damages]], or money, though courts may also grant an injunction. Common law jurisdictions derive tort law from English custom and precedent, while [[Civil law (legal system)|civil law]] jurisdictions largely derive it from Roman law. Some countries, including the [[Philippines]] and [[Thailand]], have codified a mixture of common and civil law. The word was first used in a legal context in the 1580s.

=== KEY: Tortoise_f32eee
HEADWORD: Tortoise
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Galápagos tortoise | Predation | Reptile | Turtle
ENTRY:
Land-dwelling [[Turtle|turtle]] of the family Testudinidae, a [[Reptile|reptile]] with a hard shell that protects it from [[Predation|predation]] and retracts its head and neck into the shell. Tortoise species vary widely in size. The [[Galápagos tortoise]] exceeds 1.2 m (3.9 ft) in length, while the speckled Cape tortoise's shell measures only 6.8 cm (2.7 in). Several lineages independently evolved bodies over 100 kg (220 lb). Tortoises are the longest-living land animals, and Galápagos tortoises are noted to live over 150 years, while most species live 80–150 years. In 1968 two Russian tortoises aboard *Zond 5* became the first Earthlings to circle the Moon. Most species seldom lay more than 20 eggs, and incubation averages 100 to 160 days.

=== KEY: Torture_e4d0d6
HEADWORD: Torture
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Human rights | International law | Post-traumatic stress disorder | State (polity) | World War II
ENTRY:
Deliberate infliction of severe pain or suffering on a person to punish, extract a confession, gather information or intimidate third parties. Some definitions restrict torture to acts by the [[State (polity)|state]], while others include non-state actors. Most victims are poor and marginalized people suspected of crimes. Torturers more often act out of fear or limited resources than sadism, and beating is the most common physical method. Since the twentieth century, many torturers have preferred non-scarring or psychological methods. Torture can cause chronic pain and mental harm, and survivors have some of the highest rates of [[Post-traumatic stress disorder|post-traumatic stress disorder]]. Many Western countries abolished official judicial torture in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. It is prohibited under [[International law]] for all states in all circumstances, and opposition to it helped stimulate the [[Human rights|human rights]] movement after [[World War II]]. Despite its decline, torture is still practiced in or by most countries.

=== KEY: Torus_5c22b8
HEADWORD: Torus
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Circle | Geometry | Manifold | Topology
ENTRY:
Doughnut-shaped [[Geometry|geometric]] surface generated by rotating a [[Circle|circle]] one full revolution about an axis coplanar with it. If the axis does not touch the circle, the result is a ring torus, sometimes called a doughnut. A horn torus results when the axis is tangent to the circle, and a spindle torus when the axis passes twice through it. Real-world approximations of a ring torus include swim rings and inner tubes. In [[Topology|topology]], a ring torus is homeomorphic to the Cartesian product of two circles, a compact 2-[[Manifold|manifold]] of genus 1.

=== KEY: Toshiro_Mifune_230f29
HEADWORD: Mifune, Toshiro
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Akira Kurosawa | Hollywood Walk of Fame | Isoroku Yamamoto | Miyamoto Musashi | Seven Samurai
ENTRY:
Japanese actor and film producer, noted for hypermasculine or heroic roles. He appeared in more than 180 screen credits, including 16 films with director [[Akira Kurosawa]], among them *Rashomon* (1950) and *[[Seven Samurai]]* (1954). He won Best Actor at the Venice Film Festival for *Yojimbo* (1961) and again for *Red Beard* (1965). He played [[Miyamoto Musashi]] in the *Samurai Trilogy* (1954–1956) and Admiral [[Isoroku Yamamoto]] in three films. In 1962 he established Mifune Productions, which achieved success with large-scale works such as *The Sands of Kurobe* (1968). He later worked in Hollywood, debuting in *Grand Prix* (1966). In 2016 his name was inscribed on the [[Hollywood Walk of Fame]].
