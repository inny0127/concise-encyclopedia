Country in West Africa. Officially the Togolese Republic, Togo is bordered by [[Ghana]] to the west, [[Benin]] to the east and [[Burkina Faso]] to the north. It is one of the least developed countries, with a lower-middle-income economy that depends mostly on agriculture and [[Phosphate|phosphate]] mining. The coast served as a European slave-trading outpost from the 16th to the 18th centuries. In 1884, during the [[Scramble for Africa]], Germany established a protectorate known as Togoland, which passed to France after [[World War I]]. Togo became independent in 1960. Gnassingbé Eyadéma seized power in a 1967 coup and was president for 38 years, until his death; his son Faure Gnassingbé was elected president in 2005. French is the official language, and about half the population is Christian, with Muslims and adherents of traditional religions also present. Togo is a member of the [[United Nations]] and the [[African Union]].

Japanese film production, distribution and theater company, based in [[Tokyo]]. Founded in 1932 as the Tokyo-Takarazuka Theatre Company, Toho is best known for producing [[Kaiju|kaiju]] films, including [[Godzilla]], which appears in 36 of its films, and the films of [[Akira Kurosawa]]. It has also distributed animated films from [[Studio Ghibli]]. Its [[Doraemon]] series, distributed since 1980, is the highest-grossing film series in Japan. Toho is the largest of Japan's Big Four film studios and the only film studio in the Nikkei 225 index.

=== KEY: Toilet_151ba3
HEADWORD: Toilet
PERSON: no

ENTRY:
Sanitary hardware that collects human urine and feces, usually for disposal. Flush toilets use water and are usually connected to a sewer system in urban areas or a septic tank in isolated areas, while dry toilets use no water. Sitting toilets are popular in Europe and North America, while squatting toilets are more popular in [[Asia|Asia]]. Toilets are commonly made of [[Ceramic|ceramic]] (porcelain), concrete, plastic or wood, and Japan is known for its toilet technology. [[Sanitation|Sanitation]] has concerned [[Human settlement|human settlements]] since their earliest stages. Poor sanitation spreads diseases through the fecal-oral route, including [[Cholera]] and [[Dysentery]]. An estimated 419 million people have no toilet at all and must defecate in the open, a problem addressed by UN Sustainable Development Goal 6.

ENTRY:
Dependent territory of [[New Zealand]] in the [[Pacific Ocean|South Pacific Ocean]]. It consists of three tropical coral [[Atoll|atolls]], with a combined land area of 10 sq km and about 2,500 people. It has the smallest economy of any nation, yet it is the first 100% solar-powered nation in the world and a leader in [[Renewable energy|renewable energy]]. New Zealand and the Tokelauan government both officially call it a nation. In 2007 the UN General Assembly listed it as non-self-governing, but Tokelauans have twice narrowly failed to reach a two-thirds majority in [[Self-determination|self-determination]] referendums.

=== KEY: Tokhtamysh_6d701e

ENTRY:
Mongol khan of the [[Golden Horde]]. He belonged to the House of Borjigin, tracing his ancestry to [[Genghis Khan]]. After years as a fugitive, during which he sought help from the Turco-Mongol warlord [[Timur]], he consolidated power in both wings of the Golden Horde and briefly unified the Blue and White Hordes into a single polity. In 1382 he besieged and sacked [[Moscow]], reasserting Tatar–Mongol hegemony over the [[Russia|Russian]] principalities and restoring tribute payments. His former protector Timur later invaded the Golden Horde and defeated him twice, undoing his achievements. Tokhtamysh is often called the last great ruler of the Golden Horde.

=== KEY: Tokugawa_Ieyasu_4d8d2f
HEADWORD: Tokugawa Ieyasu
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Battle of Sekigahara | Oda Nobunaga | Shogun | Tokugawa shogunate | Tokyo | Toyotomi Hideyoshi
ENTRY:
Japanese samurai and daimyo, founder and first [[Shogun|shogun]] of the [[Tokugawa shogunate]]. Born Matsudaira Takechiyo, he was one of the three Great Unifiers of Japan, along with [[Oda Nobunaga]] and [[Toyotomi Hideyoshi]]. After Hideyoshi's death and Ieyasu's victory at the [[Battle of Sekigahara]] in 1600, he seized power. He was appointed shogun in 1603 and resigned in 1605, but retained de facto control of government until his death. He built his castle at Edo, now [[Tokyo]], and implemented the bakuhan system, which used graded rewards and punishments to keep the daimyo and samurai at peace. His successors enacted the sakoku policy of isolation in 1639, closing Japan off from most outside contact for over 200 years, with some exceptions.

=== KEY: Tokugawa_shogunate_b136dc
HEADWORD: Tokugawa shogunate
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Battle of Sekigahara | Boshin War | Edo period | Meiji Restoration | Sengoku period | Tokugawa Ieyasu
ENTRY:
Military government of Japan during the [[Edo period]], 1603–1868. Founded by [[Tokugawa Ieyasu]] after his victory at the [[Battle of Sekigahara]] in 1600, it ended the civil wars of the [[Sengoku period]]. The Tokugawa governed from Edo Castle in Edo, now Tokyo, together with daimyo lords who each administered a han, or feudal domain. Society was organized into a strict class system of samurai, farmers, artisans and merchants. Daimyo were required to keep their wives and children in the capital as hostages. Under the isolationist sakoku policy, most foreigners were barred and Japanese subjects could not leave the country. Growth and urbanization fostered a merchant class and ukiyo culture. The shogunate declined from 1853 and was overthrown in the [[Meiji Restoration]] of 1868; loyalists continued the [[Boshin War]] until 1869.

=== KEY: Tokyo_62413a
HEADWORD: Tokyo

ENTRY:
Capital and most populous city of [[Japan]] (pop., 2023: over 14 million), at the head of Tokyo Bay on the central coast of [[Honshu]]. Officially the Tokyo Metropolis, it is Japan's economic center and the seat of its government and emperor. The Greater Tokyo Area, which includes parts of six neighboring prefectures, is the third most populous metropolitan area in the world, with 33 million residents as of 2025. Its central 23 special wards, including Chiyoda, Shinjuku, and Shibuya, formerly made up Tokyo City; since 1943 its governing structure has resembled that of a prefecture.

Originally called Edo, the city rose to prominence in 1603 as the seat of the [[Tokugawa shogunate]]. By the mid-18th century it had grown from a small fishing village into one of the world's largest cities, with over one million people. After the [[Meiji Restoration]] (1868), the imperial capital was moved there from [[Kyoto]] and the city was renamed Tokyo, meaning "Eastern Capital." It was greatly damaged by the 1923 Great Kantō earthquake and by Allied bombing in [[World War II]]. Rapid reconstruction beginning in the late 1940s fueled the Japanese economic miracle.

Tokyo was the first Asian city to host the Summer Olympics and Paralympics, in 1964, and hosted them again in 2021. Its gross domestic output was 120.2 trillion yen (US$887.9 billion) in fiscal year 2022, 21.2% of Japan's total. It remains a leading financial hub, and the Tokyo Stock Exchange is among the world's top five. The [[University of Tokyo]] is Japan's top-ranking university. Tokyo Station is the central hub of the [[Shinkansen]] high-speed railway, and Shinjuku Station is the world's busiest train station.

=== KEY: Tokyo_Story_2a7492
HEADWORD: Tokyo Story
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Setsuko Hara | Tokyo | Yasujirō Ozu
ENTRY:
1953 Japanese drama film by [[Yasujirō Ozu]]. Chishū Ryū and Chieko Higashiyama play an aging couple from Onomichi who travel to [[Tokyo]] to visit their grown children. The children are too busy to entertain them, and only their widowed daughter-in-law, Noriko, played by [[Setsuko Hara]], makes time for them. Japanese film exporters initially considered it too Japanese to be marketable abroad, but it won the inaugural Sutherland Trophy in London in 1958. Critics regard it as Ozu's magnum opus. It portrays postwar Japanese family life and the growing distance between parents and children. In the 2012 *Sight and Sound* poll of film experts, it was voted the third-greatest film of all time.

=== KEY: Tokyo_subway_sarin_attack_06129e
HEADWORD: Tokyo subway sarin attack
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Aum Shinrikyo | Tokyo
ENTRY:
Terrorist attack by the [[Aum Shinrikyo]] cult in 1995. On March 20, 1995, members of Aum Shinrikyo released the nerve agent sarin on three [[Tokyo]] Metro lines during rush hour, in five coordinated attacks. Fourteen people were killed, about 50 were severely injured and nearly 1,000 had temporary vision problems. Police raids followed, and more than 200 members were arrested. Thirteen senior members, including Shoko Asahara, were sentenced to death and later executed. The attack remains the deadliest modern terrorist incident in Japan.
