=== KEY: Joint_b14c4e
HEADWORD: Joint
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Cartilage | Gout | Osteoarthritis | Skeleton | Skull
ENTRY:
Connection between bones, ossicles or other hard structures of the [[Skeleton|skeletal system]] that permits movement. Joints are classified structurally, by the binding tissue that connects the bones, and functionally, by the degree of movement they allow. Joints such as the knee, elbow and shoulder are self-lubricating and can withstand compression and heavy loads. Sutures between the bones of the [[Skull]] permit very little movement, which protects the brain and sense organs. [[Osteoarthritis]] involves the breakdown of [[Cartilage]], causing pain and reduced mobility. Rheumatoid arthritis is an autoimmune disorder that causes chronic inflammation in the joints, and [[Gout]] arises from the accumulation of uric acid crystals in them.

=== KEY: Joke_066252
HEADWORD: Joke
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Ancient Egypt | Sumer
ENTRY:
Short humorous story, usually with dialogue, that ends in a punch line. The linguist Robert Hetzron defined a joke as a short humorous piece of oral literature in which the funniness culminates in the final sentence. Jokes benefit from brevity, and the shaggy dog story is an anti-joke that deliberately withholds a punch line. Jokes are passed along anonymously, in private conversation, in scripted entertainment and, more recently, online. The oldest identified joke is a [[Sumer|Sumerian]] proverb from about 1900 BC with toilet humor. The second oldest, from [[Ancient Egypt]] c. 1600 BC, was discovered on the Westcar Papyrus. Slapstick, practical jokes and situational humor are forms of humor that are not verbal jokes.

American virologist and medical researcher. Born in [[New York City]], he attended the City College of New York and New York University School of Medicine. For the next seven years he devoted himself to developing a [[Polio vaccine|vaccine]] against polio. The vaccine's success was made public in April 1955, and Salk chose not to patent it. Unit of energy in the [[International System of Units|International System of Units]] (SI), symbol J. One joule is the work done when a force of one [[Newton (unit)|newton]] moves a body one meter in the direction of the force. It is also the energy dissipated as [[Heat|heat]] when an electric current of one ampere passes through a resistance of one [[Ohm|ohm]] for one second. ENTRY:
Production and distribution of reports on events, facts, ideas, and people that constitute the "[[News|news]] of the day," with a commitment to accuracy and verification. The word applies to the occupation (professional or not), the methods of gathering information, and the organizing literary styles.

Its role varies across countries. In some nations news media are controlled by the government and lack independence; in others they operate as independent private enterprises. Legal frameworks also differ, particularly regarding [[Freedom of speech|freedom of speech]], freedom of the press, slander, and libel. Journalism is said to act as a "fourth estate," a watchdog on government.

In the United States, many credible news organizations are incorporated, have editorial boards, and keep separate editorial and advertising departments. Newspapers separate fact from opinion by section, with opinion pieces written by columnists or placed in an op-ed section. Whether ethics require journalists to be objective and neutral is widely debated. Critics cite studies finding ideological skew in major outlets, and one survey figure put U.S. trust in mass media at 28% in 2025.

Forms include access, advocacy, broadcast, business, citizen, data, and drone journalism.

The spread of the [[Internet|internet]] and [[Smartphone|smartphones]] since the early 21st century has changed how audiences obtain news, with growing use of mobile devices and digital platforms rather than print or television. Print newspapers have declined or disappeared in some regions, and print revenues have fallen faster than digital income has grown, leaving news organizations struggling to monetize digital content.

=== KEY: Judaism_f6f61e
HEADWORD: Judaism
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Conservative Judaism | Halakha | Hebrew Bible | Jews | Orthodox Judaism | Reform Judaism | Talmud | Torah
ENTRY:
Abrahamic, monotheistic, ethnic religion comprising the spiritual, cultural, and legal traditions of the [[Jews|Jewish people]]. Religious Jews regard it as their means of observing the Mosaic covenant, believed to have been established between God and the Jewish people. It is considered one of the earliest monotheistic religions.

Its core texts are the [[Torah]] ("Teaching"), the Nevi'im ("Prophets"), and the Ketuvim ("Writings"), which together form the [[Hebrew Bible]], also called the Tanakh. The Oral Torah comprises the Mishnah and the [[Talmud]], Midrashim, [[Halakha]] (Jewish law), Aggadah, and later rabbinic commentary. Rabbinic Judaism holds that God revealed commandments to Moses on Mount Sinai in both written and oral form.

The largest movements are [[Orthodox Judaism]], [[Conservative Judaism]], and [[Reform Judaism]]. They differ in their approaches to Halakha, rabbinic authority, and rabbinic literature, and in the significance they give to the State of Israel. Orthodox Judaism maintains that the Torah and Halakha are divine, eternal, and unalterable. Conservative Judaism generally takes a more traditionalist line than Reform, whose typical position treats Halakha as general guidelines. Authority is vested not in any one person or body but in the texts and in the rabbis and scholars who interpret them.

The term derives from Latin *Iudaismus*, from Greek *Ioudaismos*, first attested in 2 Maccabees in the 2nd century BC, and ultimately from the Hebrew *Yehudah* (Judah). Scholars debate the religion's origins: many critical historians describe an evolution from Yahwism, the ancient Israelite worship of Yahweh in the Iron Age, while others argue for an earlier distinct covenant. Jewishness may pass by matrilineal descent or conversion, so secular individuals may still belong to the people. Judaism strongly influenced Christianity and Islam. In 2025 the world Jewish population was estimated at 14.8 million.

=== KEY: Julian_calendar_a53c1f
HEADWORD: Julian calendar
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Anno Domini | Gregorian calendar | Julius Caesar | Roman Empire
ENTRY:
Solar calendar of 365 days, with a leap day every fourth year. It was proposed by [[Julius Caesar]] in 46 BC and took effect in 45 BC, replacing the earlier Roman calendar, a lunisolar system whose irregular intercalations had drifted out of alignment with the seasons. The Julian calendar became the predominant calendar in the [[Roman Empire]] and most of the Western world for more than 1,600 years. In 1582 Pope Gregory XIII promulgated the [[Gregorian calendar]], which eliminated occasional leap days and sharply reduced the drift. The Julian calendar's average year of 365.25 days is longer than the solar year, so it gains one day every 128 years. The [[Anno Domini]] system of numbering years was not devised until 525. Most Orthodox countries adopted the Gregorian calendar for civil use in the early 20th century but retain the Julian calendar for religious purposes.

=== KEY: Julius_Caesar_56a71d
HEADWORD: Caesar, Julius
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: Augustus | Cicero | Gallic Wars | Julian calendar | Pompey | Roman Empire | Roman Republic
ENTRY:
Roman general, statesman, and author, dictator of the [[Roman Republic]] almost continuously from 49 BC until his assassination in 44 BC.

Born into the patrician gens Julia, which claimed descent from Venus, Caesar rose through factional politics. In 60 BC he formed the First Triumvirate, an informal alliance with Crassus and [[Pompey]] that dominated Roman politics for several years and was opposed by many senators, including Cato the Younger. As proconsul of Gaul from 58 BC he conducted the [[Gallic Wars]], completed by 51 BC, which greatly expanded Roman territory. During them he invaded Britain and built a bridge across the Rhine.

When the Senate ordered him to give up his command, Caesar crossed the Rubicon in early January 49 BC and marched on Rome, beginning a civil war that he won, leaving him in near-unchallenged power by 45 BC. After pardoning many enemies he carried out reforms and public works. He created the [[Julian calendar]] to replace the republican lunisolar calendar, reduced the grain dole, settled veterans in new overseas colonies, greatly increased the size of the Senate, and extended citizenship to communities in Spain and what is now northern Italy.

In early 44 BC he was proclaimed "dictator in perpetuity." Fearing his power and the possibility that he would make himself king, a group of senators led by Brutus and Cassius assassinated him on the Ides of March, 44 BC. His great-nephew and adoptive heir Octavian, later [[Augustus]], eventually transformed the Republic into the [[Roman Empire]].

Much of his life is known from his own campaign accounts, the letters and speeches of [[Cicero]], and the biographies of Suetonius and Plutarch. His cognomen became a synonym for emperor, giving rise to *Kaiser* and *Tsar*.
