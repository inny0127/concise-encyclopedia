Turco-Mongol conqueror, first ruler of the Timurid dynasty and founder of the [[Timurid Empire]], which ruled over modern-day Afghanistan, Iran, and Central Asia. Also known as Tamerlane, Timur was born into the Turkicized Mongol confederation of the Barlas in Transoxiana. By 1370 he controlled the western Chagatai Khanate, and from there led campaigns that defeated the Khans of the [[Golden Horde]], the [[Mamluk Sultanate]] in Egypt and Syria, the emerging [[Ottoman Empire]], and the [[Delhi Sultanate]] in the Indian subcontinent. He made [[Samarkand]] his capital.

His origins are debated. Historian Peter Jackson considers it generally accepted that Timur was of "unimpeachable Mongol ancestry," and Timurid histories trace his paternal descent to a male-line ancestor of [[Genghis Khan]]. No written evidence from Timur's lifetime or earlier, however, makes such a link to him directly. He sought to revive Genghis Khan's legacy and saw himself as restorer of the Mongol Empire, and by the end of his reign he controlled the remnants of the Chagatai Khanate, the Ilkhanate, and the Golden Horde and had even attempted to restore the Yuan dynasty in China.

Timur's armies were multi-ethnic and much feared, and his campaigns laid waste to large parts of Asia, Africa, and Europe. Scholars estimate that they caused the deaths of millions of people. He also patronized scholars and poets such as Ibn Khaldun and Hafez, and his reign led to the Timurid Renaissance. He died while on the way to invade Ming China, and his empire fragmented shortly afterward. He was the grandfather of [[Ulugh Beg]], an astronomer and mathematician who ruled Central Asia from 1411 to 1449, and the great-great-great-grandfather of Babur, founder of the Mughal Empire.

=== KEY: Timurid_Empire_38bad6

Late medieval Turco-Mongol, culturally Persianate Muslim empire (1370–1507). It was founded by [[Timur]] (r. 1370–1405), also called Tamerlane, who saw himself as the restorer of the [[Mongol Empire]] of [[Genghis Khan]]. At its height it dominated [[Iran]], Iraq, Afghanistan and much of [[Central Asia]]. Under Shahrukh Mirza and his son [[Ulugh Beg]], an astronomer and mathematician, the empire saw a Timurid Renaissance. After the dynasty lost most of Persia to the [[Aq Qoyunlu]], it survived in smaller states until Uzbeks under Shaybani Khan conquered almost all of them by 1507. Babur, a Timurid prince, used his kingdom in Afghanistan as a base to invade the Delhi Sultanate in 1526 and established the [[Mughal Empire]].

Soft, silvery-white [[Metal|metal]] with the symbol Sn and atomic number 50. Tin is a post-transition metal in group 14 of the [[Periodic table]], obtained chiefly from the [[Mineral|mineral]] cassiterite. It is malleable and bends by hand, producing a "tin cry" from the twinning of its crystals. Tin has the largest number of stable [[Isotope|isotopes]] in the periodic table, 10 in all. The first large-scale alloy was [[Bronze]], made with copper as early as 3000 BC. [[Pewter]], an alloy of 85–90% tin, has been used for flatware since the Bronze Age. Inorganic tin has low toxicity and resists [[Corrosion]], so tin-plated steel is widely used for food packaging, as "tin cans."

Family of nine genera of ground-dwelling [[Bird|birds]] native to Mexico, Central America and [[South America]]. Tinamous are the only living palaeognaths able to fly, though they prefer hiding or running away from danger. Recent work places them among the ratites, most closely related to the extinct [[Moa]] of New Zealand. Males usually hold a territory and nest, which a succession of females visits to lay glossy eggs; the males incubate them. Tinamous live in habitats ranging from semi-arid alpine [[Grassland]]s to [[Tropical rainforest]]s. Their predators include [[Jaguar]]s and [[Vampire bat]]s, and habitat destruction is their main threat, with seven species listed as vulnerable. They have never been successfully domesticated.

=== KEY: Tinnitus_fbbaa3
HEADWORD: Tinnitus
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Brain tumor | Hearing loss | Latin | Magnetic resonance imaging
ENTRY:
Perception of ringing or other sounds with no corresponding external source. The word comes from [[Latin]] *tinnire*, "to ring." It affects about 10–15% of people, but only 1–2% find it a severe problem. Tinnitus is a symptom rather than a disease, most often caused by hearing damage or age-related [[Hearing loss|hearing loss]], and it can also result from ear infections or [[Brain tumor|brain tumors]]. Diagnosis rests on the patient's description and an audiogram, with [[Magnetic resonance imaging]] used if other problems are suspected. No drug treats it; management relies on sound generators, hearing aids and counseling.

=== KEY: Tipu_Sultan_9f4228
HEADWORD: Tipu Sultan
PERSON: yes
LINK TARGETS: East India Company | Hyder Ali | Mysore | Rocket | Silk
ENTRY:
Sultan of [[Mysore]] (1782–1799), known as the Tiger of Mysore. He succeeded his father, [[Hyder Ali]], and was a pioneer of [[Rocket|rocket]] artillery, expanding the iron-cased Mysorean rockets used against British forces in the Anglo-Mysore Wars. He commissioned the military manual *Fathul Mujahidin* and introduced a new coinage, a calendar and a land revenue system that initiated growth of the Mysore [[Silk|silk]] industry. He was killed on May 4, 1799, while defending Seringapatam against a combined force of [[East India Company]] troops, Marathas and the Nizam of Hyderabad. His religious policies remain contested: accounts of forced conversions and temple destruction in Malabar and Coorg contrast with portrayals of him as a tolerant ruler.

=== KEY: Tiramisu_839ab8
HEADWORD: Tiramisu
PERSON: no
LINK TARGETS: Coffee | Italy | Rum | Sugar
ENTRY:
Italian dessert of [[Coffee|coffee]]-soaked ladyfingers (savoiardi) covered with a cream of egg yolks, [[Sugar|sugar]], mascarpone and cocoa powder. It originated in northeastern [[Italy]], and modern versions were popularized in restaurants from the 1970s. The name comes from the Italian *tirami su*, meaning "pick me up." Its origin is disputed, but obituaries say the modern version was invented at Le Beccherie, a restaurant in Treviso, on December 24, 1969, and added to the menu in 1972. Variations often incorporate [[Rum|rum]] or Marsala.
